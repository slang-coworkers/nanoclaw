# Don't infer a causal link the failure log never states

**Rule:** When triaging a CI/test failure, anchor the root cause to what the failing job's own log actually shows. Do not stitch a nearby error from a *different* job/step into the failing one as its cause without evidence linking them. A plausible code-trace that "would explain" a failure is a hypothesis, not a diagnosis, until the log of the failing unit corroborates it — especially when you cannot reproduce locally.

**Why:** On shader-slang/slang#11985 (intermittent macOS CI failure), the coworker chain diagnosed the failure as a Metal-4.0 capability-attribute rejection (`[[required_threads_per_threadgroup]]` emitted unconditionally, rejected on <4.0 Metal), traced it through the compiler, and posted it as the verdict. It was wrong on the operative cause. On honest re-read of the CI log: the actual failing example (`gpu-printing`) exited 255 with **zero output**, failing silently at `createComputePipeline` — the metal-4.0 attribute error appeared **only in separate slang-test unit tests that cleared on retry**. The attribute→gpu-printing causal link was **inferred, never supported by the log**. The maintainer's own agent, correlating 12+ real runs, found the true operative cause: a flake on GitHub's new `macos-26` ("Tahoe") runner image (passing on macos-15) — a Metal driver/runtime race, the inverse of "fails deterministically on older Metal." The capability mismatch was a real *latent* inconsistency but not this ticket's cause. Two predictions also diverged and should have been a red flag: our theory → deterministic on OLDER runners; the data → flaky on the NEWEST image.

**How to apply:** (1) Root-cause from the failing unit's own output first; note when the failing unit produced *no* diagnostic at all (that itself is a signal — silent crash ≠ compiler diagnostic). (2) Treat errors from adjacent steps/jobs as separate until evidence connects them; "cleared on retry" means flake, not the deterministic bug you're tracing. (3) When you can't reproduce locally (e.g. no Metal/GPU on a Linux host), label the code-trace a hypothesis and weight empirical run-correlation (which image? deterministic vs flaky? which runners pass?) above it. (4) If your mechanism predicts deterministic-on-old but the data is flaky-on-new, stop and reconcile before posting. (5) When contradicted, retract with a fresh comment that explicitly corrects the prior one — don't silently edit or defend.
