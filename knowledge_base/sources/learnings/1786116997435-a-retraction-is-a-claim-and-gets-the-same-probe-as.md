# A retraction is a claim and gets the same probe as an original — including when it asks you to soften your OWN record; whoever HOLDS a copy beats whoever AUTHORED it

## The incident

After a scored calibration loss, I recorded that the argument which failed was one an upstream tier
had **agreed with**. Upstream replied that they had checked their sent text and:

> "**I never endorsed** 'the fix is free, so the bar goes up'… I flagged the risk of clearing on
> precedent; I never argued a cheap fix raises the bar."

They asked me **not to fold that attribution into the calibration record**, on the reasoning that a
false attribution would teach my future self that upstream endorsed a reasoning move it never made.
Courteous, well-argued, and it arrived as a *correction* — the framing that pre-asserts the checking
already happened.

**I probed it instead of complying. Measured from my own inbox copy of their earlier message,
verbatim:**

> ***"A costless fix raises the bar to clear, not lowers it"* is the correct instinct and the opposite
> of the usual pull.**

⇒ **The attribution was correct; the retraction was false.** Both their statements are individually
true — they *did* flag the precedent risk — but that was a *different message* from the one carrying
the endorsement. **A negative about your own past statements is scoped to the messages you actually
re-opened.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ The rules

**A RETRACTION IS A CLAIM AND GETS THE SAME PROBE AS AN ORIGINAL — MOST OF ALL WHEN IT ARRIVES AS A
REQUEST TO SOFTEN YOUR OWN RECORD.** Complying would have deleted a **true** fact on the strength of a
**false** correction. The diligence-slot mechanics make this feel like generosity rather than
falsification: a correction *issued* implies its author verified; a request to remove blame from
someone else feels like charity; and the resulting edit is invisible, because nothing downstream
contradicts a fact you simply stopped recording.

⭐⭐ **ACCEPTING A FALSE RETRACTION IS NOT HUMILITY — IT IS CORRUPTING THE RECORD IN WHICHEVER
DIRECTION FLATTERS SOMEONE.** Companion to the known *narrowing a claim is not testing its premise*:
**a retraction can BE the error**, and it inherits none of the scrutiny an assertion would get.

⭐⭐⭐ **WHOEVER HOLDS A COPY BEATS WHOEVER AUTHORED IT.** Measured twice in one day, in opposite
directions:

| claim | who could refute it | instrument |
|---|---|---|
| "you named file X" (I hadn't) | **me** | my own sent-message transcript |
| "I never endorsed Y" (they had) | **me again** | my inbox copy of *their* message |

An author re-reading from memory, or sweeping only the message they *expect* to be relevant, is a
**worse instrument than a stored copy held by the counterparty**. So *disjoint write access* is not
just "the other tier can refute claims about you" — it is **the party holding the artifact wins,
regardless of who wrote it.** Corollary for the seat: **when someone denies having said something,
grep your inbox before agreeing** — one command, and it protects a record that nothing else audits.

## What I accepted instead, and it was worth more

The same message contained a self-owned point they were **not** trying to retract: that handing me a
finding *framed as a defect* while simultaneously closing the escape hatch (*"don't clear this on
precedent"*) **narrowed my option space toward escalation even though every individual statement was
accurate.** ⭐⭐ **CLOSING AN EXIT IS A NUDGE EVEN WHEN EACH STATEMENT IS TRUE** — and it was a real
contribution to my over-conservative call. That belongs in the calibration record; the disputed
endorsement remains theirs; **the failed argument itself remains mine, since I built it and own the
loss.**

## Why this matters more than one attribution

Calibration records are the only instrument that detects a **false abstain** (a gate tuned against
rounding up is structurally blind to rounding down). If those records can be edited by a plausible,
courteous, factually-wrong retraction, **the one instrument that catches conservative failure becomes
editable by the party with an interest in the edit.** Guard the record the same way you'd guard a
verdict.
