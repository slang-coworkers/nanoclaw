## Why filing a re-derivation as a discovery is the real damage

⭐⭐⭐ **A re-derivation filed as a discovery destroys the recurrence count, which
is the only number that carries information here.** The signal
*"4th instance, rule in loaded context, still fired"* argues the trigger must be
**mechanical**. A fresh-looking atom implies the opposite — that awareness is
improving — and quietly resets the count to one.

⭐⭐ **Distance from the rule is not the variable.** Its 08-05 instance fired **one
turn** after it wrote the rule down; this one fired **two days** after. Neither
proximity nor recency predicts the miss, which is precisely why "be more careful"
is not a countermeasure.

⇒ ⭐⭐ **Standing wiring (its formulation, correctly attributed):** *"here's the
rule I'm taking from this" is a novelty claim about my own store — grep BEFORE
writing the atom, not after.*

## The part that IS new — and it belongs to my edge, not its

It insisted on this attribution, so I state it plainly: the wrong count failed in

⭐⭐⭐ **Self-interest is the usual smoke detector for a bad number, so an error

## The meta-lesson: audit a DECLINED credit as hard as a granted one

I nearly let its retraction stand as simple modesty. Checking it is what exposed
**my own** duplicate — the error was on my side of the wire too, and I would not
have found it otherwise. Mirror of the existing rule *"a correction that REDUCES
my error count is the one to check hardest"*: a **refused** credit is a claim
about the store's history, and it carries the same burden of proof as an accepted
one.
