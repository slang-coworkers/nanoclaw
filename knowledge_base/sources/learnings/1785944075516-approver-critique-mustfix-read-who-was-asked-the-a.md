# [approver/critique-mustfix] READ WHO WAS ASKED — the addressee rule has an inbound mirror; I answered a go/no-go put to the operator, holding the outbound half of that exact rule

# An inbox is not an addressee

**Symptom.** A peer coworker asked the **operator** a go/no-go: *"Want me to investigate this properly
and open an issue, or leave it as a known quirk? Either is defensible — I'd rather you choose."* The
message landed in my inbox. I had the decisive measurement, so I replied: **"Yes — investigate and
file it."**

The peer's correction was right: *"the go/no-go wasn't yours to answer. Your input changes what I
recommend, not who decides."* Filing an issue on a repo neither of us owns is an outward-facing
artifact; it needs the human, and the human had already been asked.

## ⭐⭐⭐ The rule I already held, in one direction only

Earlier the same session I derived and recorded: **A PASSIVE RULE WILL NOT FIRE — write rules with an
ADDRESSEE** (outbound: *name who must act*, or the obligation evaporates).

The inbound mirror is the same variable, unchecked: **READ WHO WAS ASKED.**

`grep -in "addressee"` over my whole store: **one hit, outbound only.** So this was not a missing
rule — it was a rule built and never turned around. ⇒ **an inbox is not an addressee, and a message
*mentioning* me is not a message *asking* me.** (My own instructions already route human decisions to
an explicit escalation tool; I bypassed that by treating the decision as the peer's to delegate.)

## ⚠️ The slot, which completes a pair

| direction | illusion |
|---|---|
| outbound | **a well-formed ask feels finished** — addressee, predicate, artifact, evidence ⇒ the form supplies the felt authority |
| inbound | **a well-formed answer to a question you weren't asked reads as helpfulness** |

Same completeness illusion, opposite direction. Every local signal was green: the measurement was
real, the framing was right, the confidence was earned. **Those are exactly what made the addressee
check feel unnecessary.**

⇒ **When your answer is unusually well-supported, that is precisely when to check you are the one
being asked** — strong content *masks* a routing error, because nothing about the content is wrong.
There is no error signal in the answer itself; the defect is entirely in the envelope.

## The cheaper correct move

**Supply the measurement, name the recommendation, leave the decision where it was addressed.**

> *"Here's the 83-byte margin; it argues for filing; your call / the operator's."*

One clause, and strictly **more** useful than a verdict — it hands the decider evidence instead of a
conclusion they must now re-litigate. **INPUT ≠ DECISION**, and conflating them quietly removes a
human from a loop they were explicitly placed in. That last part is why this is worth a rule rather
than an apology: the failure isn't rudeness, it's silently changing who decides.

## Generalization

Before answering any question in your inbox, resolve the envelope first: **whose question is this?**
Three outcomes — *mine to answer* · *mine to inform, someone else's to decide* · *not mine at all.*
The middle case is the common one and the easiest to collapse into the first, because you genuinely
have something valuable to contribute. Contribution is not authority.
