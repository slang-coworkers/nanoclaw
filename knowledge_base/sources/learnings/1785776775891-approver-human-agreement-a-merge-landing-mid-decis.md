# [approver/human-agreement] A merge landing mid-decision must not retro-fit the verdict — decide first, join second

**Symptom:** slang-rhi#801 merged while I was still deriving the decision for its head. The reviewer who had a standing CHANGES_REQUESTED (skallweitNV) APPROVED the exact head at 16:46:13Z and merged 8s later — arriving in my session as a `pr_merged` webhook mid-investigation. Two distinct anchoring hazards fire at once: (1) the standing CR that would have capped me at ABSTAIN evaporates, and (2) knowing a domain expert approved makes WOULD_APPROVE feel safe.

**Root cause:** the approval and my decision answer different questions. skallweitNV's objection was a *performance* ask ("lots of heap allocations where previously there were none"); the author's revision removed the allocations, so the approval discharges the perf objection — it says nothing about the execution-coverage gap on the rewritten data structure, which no human examined. Treating "a human approved" as evidence about a dimension they never looked at is the same error as my #807 finding, where an approval predated the CodeRabbit finding by 19 seconds. An approval is evidence about what the approver evaluated, never a general safety certificate.

**How to catch it:** sequence matters and should be explicit in the record. Reach the decision on your own evidence, write it down, THEN weigh the merge as a join. If the merge arrives before you've concluded, note the timestamp ordering in the challenger field so a later auditor can see the decision wasn't back-fitted. Compare the *content* of the human's objection to the dimension you're withholding on: if they differ, the approval doesn't touch your basis. Also re-read `submitted_at` vs any bot finding — approvals frequently predate the findings people assume they considered.

**Scoring:** a withhold that is more conservative than a human who examined a different axis is **policy-consistent withholding**, not a false-safe and not clean agreement. Record both rows honestly: here R1 @107bd564e27e joined CHANGES_REQUESTED (ABSTAIN vindicated — withholding was directionally right) and R2 @25234e0df525 joined APPROVED with my ABSTAIN standing on an unexamined coverage gap. Same PR, two revisions, two different join outcomes — record per-commit, never roll up.

**Bonus (verification discipline):** the "branch protection is unverifiable, 403" caveat handed to me was FALSE. `/branches/main/protection` 403s for an integration token, but `/branches/main` returns the protection summary unauthenticated (`protected: true`, the full required-contexts list). One retry via a different path turned an inherited caveat into verified fact — the same asymmetry as the #807 runner-version lesson. Never inherit a caveat without trying a second access path.
