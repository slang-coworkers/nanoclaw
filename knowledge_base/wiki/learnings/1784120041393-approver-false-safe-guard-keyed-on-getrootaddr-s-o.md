# [approver/false-safe] Guard keyed on getRootAddr's op-set misses legalization-inserted BitCast/GetOffsetPtr — probe the peel-set against ALL later passes, not the test shapes

**PR:** shader-slang/slang#11152 @ 0e7c1e156e02 — decided WOULD_APPROVE (CLEAN), mode=live. Author closed it unmerged and superseded it with their own #12119 ("Fix #10188: **shape-independent** __ldg exclusion, supersedes #11152"). No human review ever existed (all bot COMMENTED) — closed-unmerged ⇒ CHANGES_REQUESTED-equivalent. **This is a false-safe: I would have auto-approved a fix with a real, unhandled correctness hole.**

**Symptom:** #11152 guarded the CUDA immutable→`__ldg` rewrite with `root->getOp() != kIROp_GetOptiXSbtDataPtr && isPointerToImmutableLocation(root)`, where `root = getRootAddr(load->getPtr())`. My challenger asked the RIGHT question (false-negative direction: can an SBT load reach `__ldg` by having an access-chain op `getRootAddr` doesn't peel?) but reached the WRONG answer — I compared `getRootAddr`'s peel-set against the guard's comment and against the two TEST-CASE shapes (scalar uniform, `FieldAddress` struct), concluded "code peels a superset of the comment ⇒ safe," and cleared it.

**Root cause I missed:** `getRootAddr` (slang-ir-util.cpp:936) peels ONLY `{GetElementPtr, FieldAddress, NodeOutputRecordGetElementPtr}`. It does NOT walk past `BitCast`/`GetOffsetPtr`/`Reinterpret`. Buffer element-type legalization (`lowerBufferElementTypeToStorageType`, e.g. for a `bool` or 16-bit SBT struct member) inserts exactly those ops between the load and the `GetOptiXSbtDataPtr` root. For such a member, `getRootAddr` stops at the `BitCast`, `root->getOp()` is NOT `GetOptiXSbtDataPtr`, the guard passes, `isPointerToImmutableLocation` returns true → `__ldg` re-emitted → the exact stale-read bug #10188 targets, still latent. The successor #12119 fixes it correctly at the producer layer: excludes SBT inside `isPointerToImmutableLocation` itself via a dedicated `isAddressIntoOptiXShaderBindingTable` walker that peels `GetOffsetPtr`/`BitCast`/`Reinterpret` too — shape-independent.

**How to catch it (transferable):** When a guard keys off the ROOT op of an access chain via a peel-walker (`getRootAddr` et al.), its completeness depends on the peel-walker's op-set being a SUPERSET of every pointer-forwarding op that ANY downstream pass can splice between the load and the root. Verifying against the guard's own comment, or against the shapes the PR's tests happen to exercise, is insufficient and is the trap here. The probe must be: enumerate what later legalization/lowering inserts on the chain — sub-word/bool member legalization inserts `BitCast`+`GetOffsetPtr`; reinterpret/type-punning inserts `Reinterpret`; buffer lowering inserts more — and ask "does the peel-walker traverse each?" If the fix keys on `getRootAddr` (a fixed, narrow peel-set) but the semantic property (is-this-the-SBT / is-this-immutable) must hold shape-independently, that mismatch is the RED FLAG: the fix belongs at the producer (`isPointerToImmutableLocation`) with its own complete walker, not at one consumer keyed on `getRootAddr`. My Step-0 recall even surfaced `[approver/challenger-miss] helpers keyed on one access-chain shape need the whole-consumer-set probe` — I held the right prior and still cleared it because I probed shapes, not the legalization-inserted op-set.

**Fix for the procedure:** For any guard of the form `getRootAddr(x)->getOp() == SOME_ROOT`, treat the peel-set gap as a first-class challenger check: (1) read the peel-walker's actual case list; (2) list pointer-forwarding ops NOT in it (`BitCast`, `GetOffsetPtr`, `Reinterpret`, `GetOffsetPtr`); (3) ask whether legalization can insert any of them for the input types in scope (bool/16-bit/packed members are the usual trigger). Any "yes" without a test covering that shape ⇒ OPEN_GAP / ABSTAIN, not clear. Also: a consumer-side guard for a property that should hold shape-independently is a producer-fix smell (matches slang CLAUDE.md "consumer-side patching" red flag) — lean ABSTAIN and let a human confirm the layer.
