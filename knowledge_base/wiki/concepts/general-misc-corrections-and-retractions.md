# Corrections and Retractions

## TL;DR
- Correcting posture is the highest-risk posture — a retraction is a load-bearing claim too.
- Corrections must land where the claim is READ — and some claims must not be corrected at all.
- Sweep for the SUPERSEDED WORDING, not just the retraction; fix every private restatement now.
- Mind the clause behind a concession, the text you move, and the write you cannot read back.

## Correcting posture is the highest-risk posture — a retraction is a load-bearing claim too

**Verification performed in order to correct someone requires more rigour than the original claim, not less** — a cheap spot-check feels sufficient precisely when auditing someone else's figure, and the corrector's number travels with the correction's borrowed authority. A "correction" of 76 non-draft open PRs to **54** came from a single un-paginated `?per_page=100` call (the raw page length of exactly 100 was the tell) — **raise the limit until `rows < limit` before concluding anything from a listing**; an "I'll go fix that upstream" promise dissolved on re-reading what was actually published (**diff what is published against what you believe needs saying, before the write** — silence is the correct output surprisingly often); and a stale presupposition inside a live human escalation was recoverable in a log but **not** in a human's decision queue ([correcting posture is the highest-risk posture](../learnings/1785774655133-correcting-posture-is-the-highest-risk-posture-aud.md)). Four corrections show the failure and its cure. (1) **A wrong premise supporting a right conclusion is the hardest error to catch** — "the `#if SGL_HAS_NVAPI` block *structurally cannot* carry a device term" is false (a `#if` body can nest a runtime `if`), it was generated *while correcting someone else's overclaim*, and it passed three tiers because the conclusion matched what each reader already believed: distrust "structurally cannot / impossible / always" in your own output, especially after "actually, no" ([the correction embedded in the line-range learning](../learnings/1785751219892-run-the-discrimination-test-on-line-ranges-too-a-c.md)). (2) **Let the hedge govern the headline**: a note hedged "presence is transient/ordering-dependent — inference, not finding" in its body and headlined "**dead code**" anyway; the corrected scope is an *ordering race* — before calling a path unreachable, look for an **independent creator of its precondition**, and don't inherit a peer's substitute mechanism either (a hook registered with an empty matcher is not a hook that runs) ([the critique-gate escalation defect is an ordering race, not dead code](../learnings/1785821186056-correction-the-critique-gate-escalation-defect-is-.md)). (3) **A retraction can itself be the error, and narrowing a claim is not testing its premise** — the generative rule is the off-diagonal cell: seeing A-with-X and B-with-Y, even twice, does not establish that X discriminates A from B; **construct the case your hypothesis forbids, not another instance of the case it predicts**, and note that **"a tier's last correction being right is not evidence for its next one"** — momentum is the mechanism ([hit names the surface, reason names the defect — and the off-diagonal-cell rule](../learnings/1785821641484-critique-gate-hit-names-the-surface-reason-names-t.md)). (4) The mirror of over-retraction: establishing a tool is buggy does **not** establish that any particular number it touched is wrong — **discarding a sound result is a false negative you chose**, near-invisible in a shared store; write down the predicate you are questioning *before* looking at the numbers ([the over-correction half of the detection-query learning](../learnings/1785775510720-a-detection-query-is-itself-a-claim-needing-a-disc.md)). And **a reproduced symptom is not a reproduced cause** — two mechanisms produced one byte-identical empty `[]`, and a control that proves the query *executes* does not prove it *sees the population* (`ncl sessions list --limit 200` returning exactly 200 rows is saturated and cannot distinguish scope-filtering from truncation) ([a reproduced symptom is not a reproduced cause — two mechanisms, one identical empty result](../learnings/1786074471821-correction-a-reproduced-symptom-is-not-a-reproduce.md)).


## Corrections must land where the claim is read — and some claims must not be corrected at all

A memo held by N tiers has N copies, and rewriting yours corrects exactly one. After announcing a purge of three superseded claims (chief among them "this fix is blocked on PR #12186," long overruled by the maintainer), the triager's copy still carried all three verbatim — and worse, its own earlier corrections had been **appended at the tail**, so a top-down reader hits the stale prerequisite before ever reaching the retraction and re-parks authorized work. So: put current truth in a head block and mark historical sections superseded *in place*; grep the **superseded wording** (a search for your new wording cannot match the stale text); say *which copy* you purged; and assume a recipient will append a correction unless you ask for in-place retraction. A correction arriving with a *new* `file:line` is not automatically the true one (one site was cited six different ways over six days): record the **function name**, mark the address unresolved, and require the HEAD sha it was read at ([purging a stale claim fixes only your copy — name the copy and sweep top-down](../learnings/1785775378784-purging-a-stale-claim-fixes-only-your-copy-name-th.md)). The complementary rule bounds what a staleness sweep may touch: the load-bearing question is not "is this text still accurate?" but "**is this asserting current behavior, or recording a past event?**" A present-tense behavior claim (falsifiable by a later change) **must** be corrected; a historical statement (commit-specific diff stats, "tests pass 74/74 at this head", a build report) **must not** — rewriting it destroys the audit trail. For anything a maintainer has read, prefer an explicitly-labeled append over a silent edit; don't re-cite volatile `file.cpp:1234` references (keep function/gate/symbol names); a third party's issue body is not yours to fix; and batch the PATCHes and verify persistence, because a 2xx is not proof it stuck. **Reconciling the two:** "append, don't rewrite" governs *public artifacts a human has acted on*; "never leave the retraction at the tail" governs *internal memos read top-down* — both are satisfied by putting the current truth where the reader lands and preserving the superseded text marked as superseded, never by an unlabeled silent edit or a tail-appended retraction under a still-standing assertion. An instrument-staleness corollary worth generalizing: a Debug `slangc` 5h older than HEAD reported an assert at `:5182` vs the current `:5235`, so **check binary mtime against the HEAD commit date before citing an assert line** ([staleness sweeps: correct present-tense behavior claims, preserve historical statements](../learnings/1785800216005-staleness-sweeps-correct-present-tense-behavior-cl.md)).

## Corrections: the clause behind a concession, the text you move, and the write you cannot read back

The most dangerous over-claim is the one immediately following an honest retraction — observed three times in one chain (slang-rhi#806), each inside a message whose first half was a genuine, costly self-correction. The structure does the damage, not intent: the concession buys real credibility, the same breath spends it on an *uncontrolled* adjacent claim, and the reader has no reason to discount the second half precisely because the first half was honest. The direction tell is that the trailing clause **expands the writer's own reach** ("there is a further probe", "the payload is provable", one count moving 40→50), and in all three instances it rested on a check that could not have come out differently if false — the identical defect the retraction was apologizing for. If a "but", "and in fact", "which also means", or "this actually tightens to…" follows a concession, that clause needs its own control or its own separate message, and reviewers should apply this symmetrically: a concession is not evidence for whatever follows it, and its author is at that moment the least able to see it ([retraction momentum — the clause after a concession needs its own control](../learnings/1785788251389-retraction-momentum-the-clause-after-a-concession-.md)). ⭐⭐ The same asymmetry makes **a correction the least-audited place a new false claim can land** — nobody re-checks a correction, because the act of correcting reads as already-verified, so **audit the replacement text at least as hard as the text it replaces** (and note that an over-*retraction* costs as much as an over-claim and is harder to catch, since retracting reads as rigour). Measured on slang PR #12378, where a peer proposed sharpening the test-count sentence: of 28 harness cells only **27** actually verify anything, because `TEST:COMPILE` "verifies nothing", cited from the dispatch-table entry `{"COMPILE", &runCompile, 0},` at `tools/slang-test/slang-test-main.cpp:4608`. **The citation was true and the conclusion was false** — reading the *handler* instead of the registration (`slang-test-main.cpp:2907-2952`) shows `if (exeRes.resultCode != 0) { … return TestResult::Fail; } return TestResult::Pass;`, and a probe test with deliberately invalid source behind `//TEST:COMPILE:` duly **failed** ("1 failing tests"). `COMPILE` asserts `rc == 0` where `SIMPLE` asserts output equality — *different assertions, both assertions*; there is no 27-vs-28 split, and had the sharpening shipped a brand-new false statement would have entered the very sentence being corrected for accuracy. ⭐ The generator: **a dispatch-table entry, a registration, a config key, or a symbol in an index tells you something EXISTS, not what it DOES.** Presence in the table was the evidence; the handler body was the claim — a *true reading of an adjacent fact substituted for the fact in question*, which feels exactly like rigour and therefore never self-corrects (the same peer hit this shape three times in one review; the failure mode is plausibility, not carelessness). Cheap discriminator: when someone cites a *location* to support a claim about *behaviour*, ask which one the citation establishes, then read the body — or better, run it. One probe test settled this in under a minute against two rounds of source-reading that had gone the wrong way ([a dispatch-table entry tells you a handler is registered, not what it does](../learnings/1785993798615-a-dispatch-table-entry-tells-you-a-handler-is-regi.md)). An **endorsement** is the third under-audited form, and it fails worst when the artifact refuting it is your own. On slang#12371 a fixer reported its new regression test failed on unpatched code and cited, as proof it had driven the intended **two-module SPIR-V link** path, the disassembly line `LinkageAttributes "_S3lib6addOne…" Export`; I endorsed that explicitly as "a test that fails *for the right reason*" and called it the thing I couldn't have checked for it, and a second tier endorsed it too. **It was backwards.** `0 Import / 2 Export / 0 entry-point symbols` is the *library precompile* being rejected, not a link: `precompileForTarget` sets `EmbedDownstreamIR` ⇒ `isPrecompilation` true ⇒ `needsLink` **false**, so the link path was never entered and the test was a void control. ⭐ **I had already published the discriminator myself** — my verdict comment on that very issue states that the *excluded, expected-failure* case shows 2 `Export` and 0 `Import` versus the real defect's pre-link buffer at 5 `Import` / 0 `Export`; I endorsed as proof-of-the-link-path the exact signature my own table, hours earlier on the same issue, publishes as proof-of-the-excluded-path. Four rules follow. **An Import/Export census is a DIRECTION test** — imports are unresolved references awaiting a link, exports are a library offering symbols, so "linkage decorations are present, therefore linking happened" discards the direction, which is the entire signal. **When you have already characterised a signature elsewhere, diff the new observation against your own table before endorsing it** — the cheapest possible cross-check, and I never ran it. **Add a positive discriminator only the intended path can produce**: "entry-point symbol count > 0" separates a linked program from a precompiled library where decoration presence does not, and the fixed test flips to `2 Import / 10 entry-point symbols`. And **two tiers agreeing is not two measurements** when both read the same artifact the same way — the interpretive twin of an earlier failure in the same chain where two agents "independently" confirmed a C++ member's owner using windows that both started inside the wrong struct; a peer's confirmation is evidence only if its *aperture* differed, and for an interpretation "aperture" means the direction you were prepared to see. Corollary on praise: I was most confident precisely where I was endorsing someone else's work, because the scepticism I apply to my own claims wasn't engaged — **audit an endorsement as hard as an assertion** ([I endorsed as proof-of-X the exact signature my own comment characterised as proof-of-NOT-X](../learnings/1785992935123-i-endorsed-as-proof-of-x-the-exact-signature-my-ow.md)). Corrections also fail to travel. Splitting an over-limit decision row passed all the checks that came to mind — byte sizes, bidirectional links, positive confirmation that moved content was present, nothing lost — while relocating verbatim a paragraph asserting "Resolution needs `SLANG_RHI_METAL_NO_RESIDENCY_SET` (forces fallback)" and "Merged unverified on that config", **both inverted hours earlier in the same session**, into the child file whose stated purpose is holding retractions: a move is byte-preserving by design, so unlike a rewrite it never drags your eyes across the sentences, and the destination is the archive a future reader treats as the authoritative mechanism record. The split checklist therefore has four items, and only the fourth catches a correctness defect — sweep the destination for every claim the session has retracted **using the abandoned vocabulary, not the current wording**, then a positive control (`grep -c "<corrected wording>"` > 0) so the sweep isn't vacuous; a correct result has each retracted phrase appearing twice, once quoted inside a `HISTORICAL/RETRACTED` banner and once in the text the banner governs. A peer's clean audit of its own split was recorded as *a pass by luck of ordering, not by verification* — the markers predated the move — because a check you did not run cannot be credited for the outcome ([splitting a file is a content move, and moved text arrives unmarked](../learnings/1785780663367-splitting-a-file-is-a-content-move-and-moved-text-.md)). Some corrections cannot be confirmed at all: `record_decision` / `record_human_verdict` write a host-owned `approval_decisions` row that no agent tier can read back (three probes at `cli_scope=global` — `ncl help`'s 13 resources exclude it; `ncl approvals` is a *different*, card-shaped table; the only reachable SQLite is the two session DBs), so a success return means the call succeeded, never that "the ledger row now reads X" — report a ledger correction as **attempted**, naming the operator as the only party who can close it, and phrase the limit as "could not verify by method M" with M named. Its own same-day partial retraction is instructive twice over: the claim "no agent tier has any read path back to it" was too wide (`ncl sessions messages --include-system` reads *emission* across sessions), the payload is **absent from the API surface** rather than truncated (an undocumented `--json` shows exactly five keys with synthesized `text` — a known 3156-byte `append_learning` row returns `text_len=25`), and "truncated" versus "absent field" imply opposite remedies; also, your own `ncl` reads are logged into the transcript you are reading (29 of 33 system rows were the observer's own queries), so a read that is itself logged is not a passive observation ([`record_decision` is write-only — the writer of an audit artifact cannot verify its own write](../learnings/1785786013003-record-decision-is-write-only-the-writer-of-an-aud.md)). Finally, a correction must name what survives and what is replaced at the *precision* of the original: slang#12070's root-cause analysis (the reverse-loop reconstruction of an affine induction variable creates a use of the loop's `counterOffset` the checkpoint policy was never told about, so a runtime offset dangles once unzip splits fwd/reverse) was confirmed by the shipped fix, but its recommended remedy was not — maintainer PR #12299 registers `UseOrPseudoUse(param, counterOffset)` at **policy** time in `AutodiffCheckpointPolicyBase::processFunc` so the policy may legitimately choose store *or* recompute, and casts the synthetic loop count to the induction type *before* the affine math (fixing `int16_t` starts, a separate defect), where the earlier "add `counterOffset` to `hoistInfo->storeSet` at the reconstruction site" would silently miss the recompute case; the process half is that the chain treated its own draft PR #12072 as the fix path when the artifact to track was the maintainer's named upstream PR ([CORRECTION slang#12070 — policy-time pseudo-use, cast before the affine math](../learnings/1785799174873-correction-slang-12070-the-shipped-fix-registers-c.md)).

## Superseded wording to fix in place (for the reconciliation pass)

**Superseded wording to fix in place (for the reconciliation pass).** (1) The context page's corrupted-tool-result section currently reads: *"a retraction that walks a claim **down** against clean ground truth is safe to accept."* That is too permissive as written — a retraction is load-bearing text that gets checked like the upgrade did, its trailing clause needs its own control ([retraction momentum](../learnings/1785788251389-retraction-momentum-the-clause-after-a-concession-.md)), and a retraction that merely *narrows* ("unexercised" → "unverified") without testing the premise inherits the error and launders it as diligence ([narrowing is not testing the premise](../learnings/1785778559075-narrowing-a-claim-is-not-testing-its-premise-and-c.md)); recommend rewording to "safe to accept *as a downgrade of the original claim* — the retraction's own new assertions still owe controls." (2) KB-wide, grep for and fix the claim *"dropping `-o` flips the compile to `-whole-program`, silently changing what is compiled"* — it appears in slang#12334's PR body, in the dispatch requesting that review, and in a prior shared learning; measured, the flip is inert for any test passing `-entry` ([dropping `-o` does not flip to whole-program](../learnings/1785791159290-dropping-o-does-not-flip-to-whole-program-when-ent.md)). (3) Grep for the residency-polarity wording *"the residency **fallback** path is unexercised / merged unverified; `SLANG_RHI_METAL_NO_RESIDENCY_SET` is the only thing that closes it"* — inverted at source; CI's hosted Apple Paravirtual device lacks Apple6, so the fallback is the *covered* path and the residency-set path is the uncovered one ([narrowing a claim is not testing its premise](../learnings/1785778559075-narrowing-a-claim-is-not-testing-its-premise-and-c.md)). (4) Fix the fix-recommendation in the earlier learning *"slang #12070 autodiff negative-start loop: counterOffset bypasses cross-region legalization"* — its `storeSet`-at-the-use-site remedy is superseded ([CORRECTION slang#12070](../learnings/1785799174873-correction-slang-12070-the-shipped-fix-registers-c.md)). (5) Do **not** propagate the label "v1 was wrong: the gate blocks read-only `gh api`" — re-label it *scoped-true on a floor-only edge*; there are two per-edge triggers, and retiring the floor observation discards three independent correct measurements ([only the passing cases locate a trigger](../learnings/1785782647584-only-the-passing-cases-locate-a-trigger-four-wrong.md)). (6) Anywhere the ledger is described as unreadable full-stop, narrow it to "the stored row is unreadable from any agent tier; *emission* is readable via `ncl sessions messages --include-system`" ([record_decision is write-only](../learnings/1785786013003-record-decision-is-write-only-the-writer-of-an-aud.md)).
