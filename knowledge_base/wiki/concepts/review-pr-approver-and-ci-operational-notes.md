# Approver and CI Operational Notes

Discipline for turning a harvested review into one auditable decision: pinning and re-verifying the head at record time, reading CI/check-suite status as a decision input, ordering the clause eval correctly, judging wrong-layer vs. correct-layer fixes, and the accumulated human-calibration record of when abstains/blocks/approves matched or missed the maintainer outcome. The review-*input* mechanics (harvest, Devin, delegated-reviewer docs) live in [reviewer pipeline operations](review-pr-reviewer-pipeline-operations.md).

## TL;DR
- **The head can move between analysis and record.** A force-push or a synchronize can silently stale your pinned SHA mid-decision; re-pin the live head at record time and byte-prove the review against it.
- **`mode` can go stale too.** Human reviews posted during a long Devin wait change `live`↔`live_late`; re-check `gh pr view --json reviews` at record time, not only at the start.
- **A merge/review JOIN is a claim, not evidence.** Verify it against live GitHub; refuse `record_human_verdict` when the merge SHA doesn't resolve.
- **On a CI-failed tasking event, re-pin to the settled head** — the failure is often already fixed by the time you decide; don't decide on the tasked commit.
- **Tell same-run re-report from a real new red by JOB IDS** — a second `[CI failed — new check_suite]` at an already-decided head is a re-classification trigger, not a new revision.
- **Devin-clean is not test-clean.** Never record WOULD_APPROVE while CI is still pending on a behavior-changing PR; new hard-error diagnostics can break pre-existing untouched tests, and on a Devin-only tier the CI gate is the only catch.
- **Static "0 bugs" is weak on CI-matrix / wheel changes** — only running the matrix finds build breaks; protected `.github/**` ABSTAIN is well-calibrated for this class.
- **Build the review doc before evaluating clauses** — running `eval-clauses.py` first leaves `commit_match` unevaluable.
- **Safe ≠ right.** A "drop the no-op before emit" patch may be safe yet wrong-layer; ask whether the producer should be fixed. A "safe by construction" claim is a hypothesis that can be WRONG.
- **A refuted 🔴 does not round up, and a predating human approval is not a license to round up a source-verified 🔴.**
- **Calibration is bidirectional.** Maintainers ship net-positive incremental hygiene fixes (over-cautious OPEN_GAP abstains on dev-tooling tend to align only weakly), and self-merge through a mechanically-red aggregate gate when the only red is confirmed infra-flake. BLOCKs on real latent gaps and PR-caused red CI have been vindicated by fix-then-merge.

## Head pinning and record-time re-verification

Everything staged during analysis is against a specific head, and that head is not stable. A force-push mid-decision silently stales the SHA you debounced to: on slang#12136 the full harvest+Devin+challenger+DECISION_REVIEW ran against a 15-min-quiet head that a force-push had already replaced, so the ledger payload was drafted against a stale SHA ([[approver/challenger-miss] Re-pin the head at record time — a force-push mid-decision silently staled my SHA](../learnings/1784184019511-approver-challenger-miss-re-pin-the-head-at-record.md)). A synchronize does the same: PR #12138 was pinned at a head, fully analyzed, and a synchronize superseded the pin mid-analysis ([[approver/clause-gap] Re-check live PR head at record time — synchronize can supersede your pin mid-analysis](../learnings/1784223025561-approver-clause-gap-re-check-live-pr-head-at-recor.md)). The rule is to re-pin and re-verify the live head at record time, not only at analysis start.

The same staleness applies to the human-review signal and the resulting `mode`. On slangpy#1002 R2 the reviews were empty at 21:42 when `mode=live` was staged, but a long Devin wait gives humans time to post — re-check `gh pr view --json reviews` at record time because mode can go stale ([[approver/clause-gap] Re-check human reviews at record time — mode can go stale during long Devin waits](../learnings/1784152510275-approver-clause-gap-re-check-human-reviews-at-reco.md)). And a merge/review JOIN handed to you ("MERGED by X, human verdict=APPROVED, call record_human_verdict") is a claim to verify against live GitHub, not evidence to stamp — refuse `record_human_verdict` when the merge SHA doesn't resolve ([[approver/human-disagreement] a merge/review JOIN event is a claim to verify against live GitHub, not evidence to stamp — refuse record_human_verdict when the merge SHA doesn't resolve](../learnings/1784114374872-approver-human-disagreement-a-merge-review-join-ev.md)). Finally, a CI-failed *tasking* event usually names a commit that has already been fixed by the time you decide — re-pin to the settled head, don't decide on the tasked commit ([[approver/clause-gap] A CI-failed tasking event is often already-fixed by the time you decide — re-pin to the settled head, don't decide on the tasked commit](../learnings/1784138155920-approver-clause-gap-a-ci-failed-tasking-event-is-o.md)).

## CI and check-suite status as a decision input

CI state is a first-class input to the verdict, and reading it correctly needs job-level detail. A second `[CI failed — new check_suite]` event at an already-decided head is a re-classification trigger, not a new revision; compare job IDs to tell a same-run re-report (a distinct suite ID re-rolling the same failure) from a real new red ([[approver/clause-gap] A '[CI failed — new check_suite]' event at an already-decided head is a re-classification trigger, not a new revision — check job IDs to tell same-run re-report from a real new red](../learnings/1784142064176-approver-clause-gap-a-ci-failed-new-check-suite-ev.md)). A mechanically-red required aggregate gate is not automatically a merge blocker: maintainers confirmed-self-merge THROUGH a red `check-ci` when the only red is a confirmed infra-flake (e.g. aarch64-only apt/`ports.ubuntu.com` network failures) ([[approver/human-disagreement] CONFIRMED: maintainers self-merge THROUGH a mechanically-red required aggregate gate when the only red is confirmed infra-flake — a red check-ci is not a merge blocker if it's aarch64-only apt/network](../learnings/1784146233401-approver-human-disagreement-confirmed-maintainers-.md)).

Conversely, a green-looking static review does not clear behavior-changing code while CI is pending — Devin-clean is not test-clean. On slang#12122 a WOULD_APPROVE was nearly recorded @9fe3de9e while CI was still pending ([[approver/false-safe] Never record WOULD_APPROVE while CI is still pending on a behavior-changing PR — Devin-clean is not test-clean](../learnings/1784150830548-approver-false-safe-never-record-would-approve-whi.md)). This is not hypothetical: a new hard-error diagnostic (E41303 on #11595) broke pre-existing untouched tests, and on a Devin-only tier the CI gate was the only catch ([[approver/challenger-miss-averted] New hard-error diagnostics break pre-existing untouched tests — CI gate is the only catch on Devin-only tier](../learnings/1784185856147-approver-challenger-miss-averted-new-hard-error-di.md)). CI-matrix / wheel changes are the sharpest case: a static "0 bugs" is weak because only running the matrix finds build breaks across added Python versions, toolchain bumps, or matrix combos ([[approver/human-disagreement] CI-matrix wheel changes: static '0 bugs' is weak — only running the matrix finds build breaks](../learnings/1784158778419-approver-human-disagreement-ci-matrix-wheel-change.md)). And when CI failure is genuinely PR-caused, BLOCK is right — a BLOCK on PR-caused red CI (#12130, GLSL float-remainder / SPIR-V OpFMod) was vindicated by the fix-then-merge trajectory ([[approver/human-agreement] BLOCK on PR-caused red CI vindicated by fix-then-merge trajectory (#12130)](../learnings/1784177389034-approver-human-agreement-block-on-pr-caused-red-ci.md)).

## Ordering the clause eval and review-doc synthesis

The clause evaluation depends on artifacts the review doc produces. On #12126 running `eval-clauses.py` *first* — before synthesizing the review doc — left `commit_match` unevaluable, because the field the clause reads is populated by synthesis. Synthesize the review doc first, then evaluate clauses ([[approver/critique-mustfix] eval-clauses before review-doc synthesis leaves commit_match unevaluable](../learnings/1784147694801-approver-critique-mustfix-eval-clauses-before-revi.md)).

## Codegen-inert re-review must use a true two-dot diff

For a long-lived PR with a "let codegen-inert synchronizes ride the recorded decision" rule (slang#12080), a new synchronize can *look* like more doc/test polish while a rebase hides real codegen changes. The codegen-inert check must use a TRUE two-dot content diff — a rebase makes a `gh` compare mask real codegen changes, so a three-dot or compare-view diff can falsely read as inert ([[approver/clause-gap] Codegen-inert check must use a TRUE two-dot content diff — a rebase makes gh-compare mask real codegen changes](../learnings/1784187594343-approver-clause-gap-codegen-inert-check-must-use-a.md)).

## Wrong-layer vs. correct-layer: producer fixes and diagnostic safety

A change that is *safe* is not necessarily *right*. slang#11323's pre-emit pass `eliminateCastToVoid` dropped a `kIROp_CastToVoid` no-op because no backend handles it — safe, but the right question is whether the producer of that op should be fixed instead (a wrong-layer rejection) ([[approver/challenger-miss] 'drop a no-op before emit' pass: safe ≠ right — ask if the producer should be fixed (wrong-layer rejection)](../learnings/1784109134516-approver-challenger-miss-drop-a-no-op-before-emit-.md)). The correct-layer redo is #12117, which lowers `(void)expr` to the canonical void value at the producer; a "produced-once/consumed-nowhere/emitted-nowhere" IR op is cleared by proving it is no longer MATERIALIZED, not merely dropped downstream ([[approver/confirmed-safe] correct-layer producer fix for a 'produced-once/consumed-nowhere/emitted-nowhere' IR op — clear it by proving the op is no longer MATERIALIZED, not just dropped](../learnings/1784113889947-approver-confirmed-safe-correct-layer-producer-fix.md)). That whole arc closes cleanly: the #11315 wrong-layer drop (#11323, abstained with CHANGES_REQUESTED) → correct-layer producer fix (#12117, approved, MERGED byte-identical + human APPROVED) — a producer-fix redo of a rejected symptom-patch is a strong clean-approve prior ([[approver/human-agreement] #11315 arc CLOSED: wrong-layer drop (#11323, I abstained, CHANGES_REQUESTED) → correct-layer producer fix (#12117, I approved, MERGED byte-identical + human APPROVED) — the producer-fix redo of a rejected symptom-patch is a strong clean-approve prior](../learnings/1784194721856-approver-human-agreement-11315-arc-closed-wrong-la.md)).

"Safe by construction" is a hypothesis that can fail. A note claimed slang#12122's new parse-time CLI-rejection diagnostic (E00046) was safe by construction because it defers to the emitter's own fold ([[approver/challenger-confirmed] New CLI-rejection diagnostics that defer to the emitter's own fold are safe by construction](../learnings/1784146073220-approver-challenger-confirmed-new-cli-rejection-di.md)) — and that conclusion was later retracted as WRONG in the same session ([[approver/false-safe] CORRECTION to earlier #12122 challenger-confirmed note — that 'safe by construction' claim was WRONG](../learnings/1784150857406-approver-false-safe-correction-to-earlier-12122-ch.md)). Keep both: the reasoning pattern and its correction are the lesson.

## The challenger must escalate a verified crash even from a 0-bug review

A production review that reports 0 bugs can still name a "main concern" in prose that is, on inspection, a verified crash. On slang#12136 an initial ABSTAIN_POLICY:CHALLENGER_CONCERN was recorded on the fallback tier (the production review had timed out IN_PROGRESS through the exit-22 WAIT window), but the 0-bugs primary review's own "main concern" gap was a real crash — the challenger must escalate it, and a late primary review supersedes a fallback ABSTAIN ([[approver/decision-flip] A 0-bugs primary review's 'main concern' gap can be a verified crash — the challenger must escalate it, and a late primary review supersedes a fallback ABSTAIN](../learnings/1784187296919-approver-decision-flip-a-0-bugs-primary-review-s-o.md)).

## Calibration: protected paths draw long human review cycles

Protected `.github/**` paths (CI/workflow tooling) reliably draw a long human review cycle even when the bot review is clean of bugs, so ABSTAIN_POLICY on that class is well-calibrated — PR #12086 (compile-perf reporting redesign touching two `.github/workflows/*.yml`) confirmed this ([[approver/human-disagreement] confirmed: protected-path (.github/**) ABSTAIN_POLICY on compile-perf/CI-tooling PRs is well-calibrated — these draw a long human review cycle even when the bot review is clean of bugs](../learnings/1784111097033-approver-human-disagreement-confirmed-protected-pa.md)). The abstain earns its keep on the exact failure static review misses: a human caught build-matrix failures on a wheels-matrix change that the automated signal did not, validating the protected-path abstain as calibration-consistent ([[approver/calibration] Protected .github/** abstain validated — human wheels-matrix catch](../learnings/1784158964544-approver-calibration-protected-github-abstain-vali.md)).

## Calibration: over-cautious OPEN_GAP abstains on dev-tooling

Maintainers ship net-positive incremental hygiene fixes even when a PR does not literally achieve its titular scope, so OPEN_GAP abstains on internal dev-tooling tend to be over-cautious. slang#12118 ("read suite files as explicit UTF-8 everywhere") missed 3 same-class bare `open()` reads yet was merged unchanged at the exact decision head ([[approver/human-disagreement] Titular-scope-incompleteness OPEN_GAP on internal dev-tooling tends to be over-cautious — maintainers ship net-positive incremental hygiene fixes](../learnings/1784141883615-approver-human-disagreement-titular-scope-incomple.md)). A demo/example README inconsistency (two stale Architecture-table rows on slang#12090) shipped as-is via author self-merge — a doc-inconsistency OPEN_GAP that the maintainer accepted ([[approver/human-disagreement] Demo/example doc-inconsistency OPEN_GAP shipped as-is via author self-merge](../learnings/1784111092025-approver-human-disagreement-demo-example-doc-incon.md)). Latent-bug gaps in dev-only release tooling are merged knowingly by maintainers, so ABSTAIN_POLICY aligns and this class rarely warrants BLOCK (slangpy#1065 "Merge 0.43.0" version-bump) ([[approver/human-disagreement] Latent-bug gaps in dev-only release tooling: maintainers merge them knowingly — ABSTAIN_POLICY aligns, and this class rarely warrants BLOCK](../learnings/1784150237934-approver-human-disagreement-latent-bug-gaps-in-dev.md)).

Withholding on SAFE is agreement, not a false-safe: slang#12064 (external-fork SPIR-V Flat-decoration fix) was decided ABSTAIN_POLICY/OPEN_GAP for a missing negative test and merged byte-identical at the decided head with the coverage gap unaddressed — a withhold-on-SAFE that matched the human outcome rather than a false-safe ([[approver/human-agreement] TERMINAL confirm slang#12064: ABSTAIN/OPEN_GAP merged byte-identical at decided head, coverage gap unaddressed — withhold-on-SAFE agreement, not false-safe](../learnings/1784178323358-approver-human-agreement-terminal-confirm-slang-12.md)). And a close is not automatically a defect signal: when the AUTHOR closes-unmerged for "obsoleted by future refactoring" (PR #12109, SpecializationWorkList-via-scratchData, WOULD_APPROVE @00bdbc62), distinguish strategic-obsolete from defect-rejection before scoring a WOULD_APPROVE against the close ([[approver/human-agreement] closed-unmerged by the AUTHOR for 'obsoleted by future refactoring' is NOT a false-safe — distinguish strategic-obsolete from defect-rejection before scoring a WOULD_APPROVE against a close](../learnings/1784268042791-approver-human-agreement-closed-unmerged-by-the-au.md)).

## Calibration: BLOCK and clean-approve vindications

BLOCKs on real, latent defects have been vindicated by the author fixing them before merge. On slang#11979 (shader-coverage-backends) a BLOCK on a latent-but-real payload-validation gap was vindicated — the author added the exact guard before merge ([[approver/human-agreement] BLOCK on a latent-but-real payload-validation gap was vindicated — author added the exact guard before merge](../learnings/1784141964975-approver-human-agreement-block-on-a-latent-but-rea.md)). On slang#12106 a memoization BLOCK→fix→APPROVE chain was vindicated end-to-end — the fix commit title named the exact cache the BLOCK implicated (csyonghe APPROVED at that commit) ([[approver/human-agreement] Full merge trajectory vindicated a memoization BLOCK→fix→APPROVE chain — the fix commit title named the exact cache the BLOCK implicated](../learnings/1784172597183-approver-human-agreement-full-merge-trajectory-vin.md)).

Clean approves confirm too. slang#12052's module-scoped `SharedSemanticsContext` seeding (extension-visibility fix) merged byte-identical to the approved diff (jkwak-work, APPROVED) ([[approver/human-agreement] CONFIRMED-SAFE: module-scoped SharedSemanticsContext seeding (extension-visibility fix) merged byte-identical to the approved diff](../learnings/1784167332666-approver-human-agreement-confirmed-safe-module-sco.md)). A bot-fixer Devin-only WOULD_APPROVE (PR #12128, production `claude-pr-review.yml` skips bot fixer branches) was vindicated when the challenger closed the Devin-coverage gap by direct inspection ([[approver/human-agreement] Bot-fixer Devin-only WOULD_APPROVE vindicated when challenger closes the Devin-coverage gap by direct inspection](../learnings/1784173400438-approver-human-agreement-bot-fixer-devin-only-woul.md)). The counter-rule for rounding up: never let a human approval that *predates* the production review finding round up a source-verified 🔴 — on PR #11471, `reviewDecision=APPROVED` (jkwak-work "Looks good to me." on 2026-07-13, so `mode=live_late`) did not license rounding up the later 🔴 ([[approver/human-disagreement] A human approval that predates the production review finding is not a reason to round up a source-verified 🔴](../learnings/1784166450120-approver-human-disagreement-a-human-approval-that-.md)).
