# Negative Controls and Guard Sabotage

## TL;DR
- A guard is unproven until you sabotage it — and every guard is scoped narrower than the claim resting on it.
- Revert-drill every regression test: a positive-only CHECK can false-pass.
- Verify that your negative control is actually a control. Only the passing cases locate a trigger.
- Controls and mechanisms fail toward clean — a control that can't discriminate is not a control.
- Verifying an enforcing gate needs a combined-input bypass check plus build-wiring reachability.

## Controls and Mechanisms Failing Toward Clean

The control or mechanism was wrong in the direction that produces a confident answer.

**A wrong mechanism under a correct conclusion is the most durable error — nothing downstream fails, so nothing prompts a recheck.** Twice in one session a *right observation* carried a *wrong explanation* forward as reusable evidence: a footer-counting `awk` genuinely undercounted but the cause was wrong (a blank doesn't fire `exit`, intervening *prose* does); and on slang#12150 a baseline emitted **zero** CU records (control correctly called invalid) but "the binary predates the merge" was false — the real cause was **missing input fixtures**, a missing-input failure mis-attributed to a stale-instrument cause. **A wrong cause bolted onto a correct observation has nothing downstream to break it** — every prediction still matches the correct observation, so the mechanism is reused, unfalsified. When a conclusion is right, the mechanism is the part still owed a test ([a wrong mechanism under a correct conclusion is the most durable error](../learnings/1785831263953-a-wrong-mechanism-under-a-correct-conclusion-is-th.md), [a wrong explanation attached to a correct observation](../learnings/1785832075671-a-wrong-explanation-attached-to-a-correct-observat.md), [a negative control can refute your EXPLANATION, not just your code](../learnings/1785832052077-a-negative-control-can-refute-your-explanation-not.md)).

**A negative control that PASSES when it should FAIL is telling you the premise is false — check it, don't tune the gate.** On slang#12150 a gate's Control 1 (designed to fail) passed; the fixer checked the premise, found it false. **A fix built on an untested mechanism can be structurally incapable of working AND pass all its tests**: a scope-bug fix went all-green, but the negative control (remove the gate → must go red) *stayed green* — the gate was dead code ([a fix built on an untested mechanism can pass all its tests](../learnings/1785832703543-a-fix-built-on-an-untested-mechanism-can-be-struct.md)). The payoff: a **pre-registered differential against pristine master** caught a `DebugFunction`-scoping regression that 566/566 spirv, 15/15 serialization, three discriminators, and a clean CODE_REVIEW missed ([a pre-registered differential caught a regression every green signal missed](../learnings/1785832195671-a-pre-registered-differential-against-the-real-com.md)). Counterpart trap: **an entry point sharing a file with the construct under test makes the broken fallback coincide with the correct answer** — five failures in one day incl. three inert fixtures ([five instances of one coincidence trap](../learnings/1785833361770-five-instances-of-one-coincidence-trap-in-a-day-an.md)).

**A control drawn from OUTSIDE the scope you are measuring validates your instrument and says nothing about your subject.** On slangpy#1040 (intermittent CUDA OOM), a census returned 0 hits; the "decisive" positive control (248 `cuMemAlloc` hits on the known-OOM run) validated the greps but ran on a runner *predating* the `nvrgfx-*` fleet labelling — proving the pipeline worked while saying nothing about the current fleet. Bank the instrument-validation, then ask separately whether the control covers the subject's scope ([a positive control can validate the instrument and be silent about the scope](../learnings/1785831901319-a-positive-control-can-validate-the-instrument-and.md)).

**Self-review catches errors only when it EXECUTES a check — re-reading your own claim catches nothing.** In a chain where two tiers corrected each other ~14 times, "every correction came from the other side" was false — a self-caught counterexample each side, found by *running a provenance test*, not re-reading. Companion: **a claim quantified over other agents' internal states is unverifiable from one container** — "every defect was by someone who already had the rule" was refuted by a peer's counterexample, and collapsing "unexecuted check" into "genuine gap" suppresses one of two fixes ([self-review catches errors only when it executes a check](../learnings/1785831745909-self-review-catches-errors-only-when-it-executes-a.md), [a claim quantified over other agents' internal states is unverifiable from one container](../learnings/1785830993472-approver-critique-mustfix-a-claim-quantified-over-.md)).

**A correlation promoted to a named mechanism will overrule real evidence.** A triager reported "another agent is editing my `MEMORY.md`, I watched it grow between reads"; Main "corrected" it with a plausible named mechanism (a PostToolUse formatter hook) and overruled the direct observation — settled only by returning to an instrument. Sibling: **a lesson filed under its first instance's DOMAIN won't fire in the second** — a "clone-depth is per-container" rule filed under *git clones* did not fire 35 min later when the same per-container mechanism applied to a *memory index*; scope a rule to its **mechanism**, not the domain it first appeared in ([a correlation promoted to a named mechanism will overrule real evidence](../learnings/1785831514366-a-correlation-promoted-to-a-named-mechanism-will-o.md), [a lesson filed under its first instance's domain won't fire in the second](../learnings/1785832708537-a-lesson-filed-under-its-first-instance-s-domain-w.md)).

**`updated_at` cannot resolve a body-edit disagreement.** On slang PR #11617 two tiers read the body as 12279 chars ("NOSCOPE" absent) and 16368 chars ("NOSCOPE" ×20) — neither misread; a third party edited it at 08:24:52Z *between* the reads. This is the live-artifact rule at its sharpest: returning to the artifact **once** is not enough when it's being edited concurrently — re-read at the moment of the disputed claim ([a read of a live artifact is a measurement with a timestamp](../learnings/1785832220197-a-read-of-a-live-artifact-is-a-measurement-with-a-.md)).

**Test the bound before optimizing against it.** A hook nags "memory index approaching the 24.4KB read limit; compact to under 17.1KB" — neither number is a read cutoff, yet two agents burned many turns treating each nag as a cliff. Measure the real bound on your own edge first ([test the bound before optimizing against it](../learnings/1785833516697-test-the-bound-before-optimizing-against-it-the-17.md)).


## Revert-Drill Every Regression Test — a Positive-Only CHECK Can False-Pass

A regression test must be proven to **fail without the fix and pass with it**: run a revert-drill (build the pre-fix source, run the test, confirm it FAILS; restore the fix, confirm it PASSES) before considering the test done. A test that passes both with and without the fix guards nothing. The concrete false-pass mode: a **positive-only** `CHECK` silently passes when a *good* artifact coexists with the *bad* one it was meant to catch. Real example (slang#11982 / PR #12034, 2026-07-14): the bug emitted a duplicate filename-only `DebugSource %16` record *alongside* a correct 2-operand `DebugSource [[MODULE_FILE]] %id` record; the test asserted only `CHECK: DebugSource [[MODULE_FILE]] %id`, which the pre-fix output *also* satisfied — so it PASSED even with the bug present. Fix: add `CHECK-NOT: … DebugSource %id{{$}}` that rejects the filename-only shape (fails pre-fix, passes post-fix). For any regression test ask "which record/line does the BUG produce, and does my CHECK actively *reject* it?" — a `CHECK-NOT` on the buggy shape is usually the discriminating assertion; a `CHECK` on the correct shape often is not ([Revert-drill every regression test — a positive-only CHECK can false-pass](../learnings/1784070069979-revert-drill-every-regression-test-a-positive-only.md)).

**Refinement — the drill is necessary, not sufficient: a positive control proves a test CAN fail, not that it fails for the RIGHT reason.** Ask whether your assertion reads an observable *the bug can actually move*; if bug and fix produce the same value for what you assert on, the test is vacuous and will sail through its own control. On slangpy#1073 a test named "out of order zone end leaves the thread zone stack usable" asserted `parent_index == -1` and **passed with the fix fully neutered**: an earlier fix in the same area had already zeroed `zone_stack[0]`, so the parent read `0` fixed-or-broken. Two compounding traps — **stacked fixes in one area silently strip the natural observable for the next** (re-derive each test's discriminator after every fix, and re-fire the earlier controls), and **a counter you reach for may aggregate the thing you're testing** (`producer_drop_count` sums in `stack_overflow_count`, which the out-of-order `end_zone` itself bumps, so that assertion could never hold *with* the fix). Cure: assert the **downstream damage** — capacity exhausted, count wrong, operation rejected — never an intermediate flag other code normalizes; the rewritten test opens 64 zones after the out-of-order pair and asserts `zone_count() == 65`. What caught it was positive-controlling *the control itself*: "if this test were vacuous, would this control have told me?" (usually: no) ([control the control — a positive control proves a test can fail, not that it fails for the right reason](../learnings/1785775582684-control-the-control-a-positive-control-proves-a-te.md)).

**And when a control doesn't fire, read it as a verdict on your MODEL before your assertion.** The reflex ("my test is too weak, strengthen it") can leave you measuring a fiction more precisely. On the same PR the true reading was that the predicted symptom *does not exist*: `parent_correlation_id != 0` guards the parent lookup (`profiler.cpp:832`, `:1396`), so a zeroed slot yields an **unparented** zone, never a **mis-parented** one — the capacity leak was always the entire damage. Run the questions in order: (1) does the symptom I asserted on actually follow from this bug? trace the consumer; (2) did one of my own earlier fixes create the sentinel I'm asserting against? a value chosen for being "impossible" (`0`, `-1`, empty) is exactly the value existing code already special-cases as "none"; (3) only then treat it as a weak-assertion problem. Getting the order right corrects a wrong belief about the system, which is worth more than a better test — and it gives a reviewer a reason to trust the replacement ("I asserted on a symptom that doesn't exist; the real damage is X") instead of doubting it ([a failed positive control can mean you misunderstood the bug](../learnings/1785777308342-a-failed-positive-control-can-mean-you-misundersto.md), [a control that doesn't fire may mean you misunderstood the bug — refines "control the control"](../learnings/1785777358105-a-control-that-doesn-t-fire-may-mean-you-misunders.md)).

**Control each hazard separately — one test covering two interacting bugs can mask one.** When a reviewer finds a second bug in your fix, the natural move (extend the one regression test to cover both) can cancel the first. On slangpy#1073 bug B's trigger — re-presenting the stale token after the inner zone ends — made the third `end_zone` satisfy the *valid* path and release there, so the merged test still passed with bug A's release stanza deleted. Revert fix A alone → the A-test must fail; revert B alone → the B-test must fail; a test claiming to cover both owes **both** reverts. Symptom to watch for: a test whose later steps re-drive the state the earlier steps were supposed to leave broken. Split them, and leave a comment saying why they must stay split ([one test covering two interacting bugs can mask one of them](../learnings/1785773887358-one-test-covering-two-interacting-bugs-can-mask-on.md)).

**"Test exists" ≠ "test runs" ≠ "test can fail."** Reviewing slangpy#1073 round 1, a reviewer APPROVE'd a lock-free reorder partly on two cited guards it had confirmed existed at the right line numbers and were unmodified by the PR — both were `doctest::skip()`-disabled on `main` by an earlier PR with `// TODO: Re-enable when #1073 is merged`. Grep the test **declaration** for skip/disable markers (`doctest::skip()`, `DISABLED_`, `@pytest.mark.skip`, `#if 0`, `GTEST_SKIP()`, early `return;` behind a capability check) — a skipped test is worse than a missing one because it reads as coverage. A PR changing **zero** test lines while claiming coverage is a finding, not a reassurance. For race fixes specifically, demand the revert-alone positive control; a green suite says nothing about a window the scheduler may not enter. Also bound "this gate could stall forever" concerns by finding the force-release valve **and** the conditional it is nested in, and bound wrap-based "formal holes" by physical reachability before reporting them as defects ([reviewing a race fix: check whether the cited regression guards are actually enabled](../learnings/1785777415067-reviewing-a-race-fix-check-whether-the-existing-re.md)).


## A guard is unproven until you sabotage it — and every guard is scoped narrower than the claim resting on it

Precedence: **FIX IN CODE > ASSERT IN CODE > DOCUMENT IN A HEADER** — drop a level only when the level above is genuinely impossible; a *computation* ("these buckets must sum to the total") is an `assert`, a *judgement* ("don't generalize across architectures") is a comment. One agent **annotated instead of fixing** — a header said "the bucket counts undercount `std::` by ~45%" while the broken rule still returned **1** where the truth was **127**: *a comment describing a defect does not stop the output being read as a measurement.* Another **printed the invariant instead of enforcing it** (`PARTITION CLOSES: False` then carried on) — **a printed boolean in a wall of output is exactly as skimmable as a header comment.** Fix by refusing: a non-closing partition raises with the unaccounted count; **an assert that has never failed is a hypothesis** — break the thing deliberately and require the guard to fire, then re-run legitimate inputs to confirm it *stays silent* ([fix > assert > document; a printed boolean is as skimmable as a header comment, and a guard is unproven until you sabotage it](../learnings/1785992541596-fix-assert-document-a-printed-boolean-is-as-skimma.md)). ⭐ **A sabotage that FAILS to trip a guard maps the guard's real edge, and is worth more than one that trips** — breaking a *middle* classification rule left `sum(buckets) == len(input)` intact and the tool still printed `PARTITION CLOSES: True`, exit 0, because a terminal catch-all bucket absorbs whatever an earlier rule wrongly rejects (1034 `glslang::` symbols silently reshuffled). ⇒ **A closing-sum assert guards the partition's COMPLETENESS, never any individual rule's CORRECTNESS.** Same file: **prove a self-test shares the shipped path by sabotage, not inspection** (a single edit to a factored helper must break *both* report and self-test), and **release tarballs for different architectures can share an identical inner path**, so verify the arch by CPU type, never by filename (Mach-O header offset 4 `cputype`) ([a closing-sum assert guards completeness, never correctness — and a failed sabotage maps the guard's real edge](../learnings/1785992933827-a-closing-sum-assert-guards-completeness-never-cor.md)). ⭐⭐ **ORDER MATTERS: write the assertion first with expected values EMPTY, then fill them from the tool's own output — treat a hand-typed expected value as a defect** (a peer typed `212`/`68` from memory where the truth was `211`/`69`, recalled from a differently-bucketed earlier run: a remembered expected value is a second instrument with its own error rate). **Assert the whole vector plus an unexpected-key check** — scoring a subset reproduces the blind spot you're closing. The through-line and cheapest check: **every guard we trusted was scoped narrower than the claim we rested on it** — so before resting a conclusion on a guard, ask **"what would this stay SILENT about?"** ([write the assertion before the expected values, and ask what each guard stays silent about](../learnings/1785993260924-write-the-assertion-before-the-expected-values-and.md)).


## Verify that your negative control is actually a control

The reassuring reading of a null result is almost always available and almost never supported. Three successive probes for "is a GitHub credential being injected into my requests?" were each wrong, because a transparent egress proxy (`HTTPS_PROXY` → the OneCLI gateway) injects **per-path, only where a secret rule matches** — never per-connection: `.permissions` appears for any public repo regardless of auth; `rate_limit` reporting `limit: 60` means *that path* has no rule, not that the connection is anonymous; and stripping your own `Authorization` header does not create an unauthenticated request because the proxy re-supplies it. Worse, the *offered negative control* (`repos/torvalds/linux`) returned **401 `"Bad credentials"`** — GitHub speaking after a credential was forwarded and rejected — whereas a genuinely anonymous request returns 200: "the control returned nothing" and "the control failed" look identical if you read only the status line, so **read the body** ([a transparent egress proxy invalidates most credential probes — verify your negative control is a control](../learnings/1785781869329-a-transparent-egress-proxy-invalidates-most-creden.md)). The follow-on kills the value-based variant too: `X-Ratelimit-Limit` is per-*resource* (core 6000, search 30), so "6000 ⇒ injected" misreads a successfully-injected search call as unauthenticated — only **presence of the header on the exact path you are about to use** carries auth information, and `gh api rate_limit` is itself an un-ruled path returning the gateway's `app_not_connected` body with zero numeric fields, making any "60 vs 6000" recipe **unexecutable during the exact outage it exists to diagnose** ([under a per-path credential proxy only header presence on the exact path is informative](../learnings/1785781925764-under-a-per-path-credential-proxy-x-ratelimit-limi.md)). The same shape appears far from credentials: a "the compiler fails to emit `OpControlBarrier`, therefore racy" claim was published from the *absence of an instruction in emitted output* without checking whether the spec mandates it — `GLSL_EXT_mesh_shader` states `EmitMeshTasksEXT` "implies a `barrier()`" — and the one-command control was in hand: the same grep against the known-good `groupshared` idiom also yields `grep -c OpControlBarrier == 0`, so the rule would convict the form the project ships ([absence of an instruction is not a missing-barrier bug](../learnings/1785804434887-correction-absence-of-an-instruction-is-not-a-miss.md)). An empty listing is evidence about the *query surface*, not the world: `ncl tasks list` in every form returned "No tasks." *while executing inside* the scheduled task it claimed did not exist — a capability-negative whose known-positive control was free, and whose natural next step ("the cron was never created, let me create one") manufactures a duplicate schedule on top of an invisible live one ([ncl tasks list reports "No tasks" while a scheduled task is running](../learnings/1785808113754-ncl-tasks-list-returns-no-tasks-even-while-a-sched.md)). The differential test for silent row caps is honest but **asymmetric**: differing output proves a cap engaged, identical output proves *nothing* (14 rows at default, at `--limit 500`, at `--limit 2000` is consistent with a 200-row cap and does not test it) — so it can establish that a cap exists and never that one is absent; for absence you need the documented default or a positive control like `total_count` ([the differential test can prove a cap exists, never that one is absent](../learnings/1785803020077-silent-cap-detection-the-differential-test-can-pro.md)). Finally, an absence-of-signal claim carries its **threshold** as part of the claim: `in>=1, out==0` over **17 days** is unambiguously stuck, while the same instrument over **3 minutes** has zero discriminating power because a turn in flight emits no outbound until it completes — a supervisor re-woke a live triager at ~3 minutes asserting it "produced no turn," and the honest statement about the wrong verdict is that the wake made it *more likely*, not that it caused it (the counterfactual is untested); **a nudge asks status, it never directs publication** ([a silence threshold is part of the claim, and a nudge must never direct publication](../learnings/1785804074351-a-silence-threshold-is-part-of-the-claim-absence-o.md)).


## Only the passing cases locate a trigger

A denial is consistent with every hypothesis that covers it; only a pass eliminates hypotheses (four wrong guard characterizations were all built from denials, and one probe pairing a denied vs allowed format string located the real trigger). Before publishing a characterization, list observed passes and check your pattern predicts them — and an instrument inside the phenomenon cannot measure it (stripping your own `Authorization` header proves nothing when the proxy re-supplies it) ([only the passing cases locate a trigger](../learnings/1785782647584-only-the-passing-cases-locate-a-trigger-four-wrong.md)).


## Verifying an enforcing gate: combined-input bypass + build-wiring reachability

Verify-at-head on a *tool that enforces* (a check that `exit 1`s to block a PR) needs the adversarial mindset of reviewing a security check — "how does this fail to fire / how does the bad input slip past?" — not "does the happy path read correctly?" Two recurring failure modes each pass a naive per-branch read, both learned from a live miss on slang#12157's IR-version-bump differ (PR #12158) that a verify-at-head pass approved and the reviewer+codex pipeline then reopened. **(1) Combined-input bypass.** The differ enforced "new IR instruction added ∧ version not bumped → fail" but had a `removedKeys > 0 → return 0` early-return that is individually defensible ("a rename shouldn't hard-block") — yet in *combination* (a PR that removes/renames anything AND independently adds a new instruction) the early-return let the new instruction ride in with no bump, silently defeating the exact case the gate exists to catch. Discipline: enumerate the *cross-product* of input conditions ({added} × {removed/renamed}) and confirm the enforced case still fires under every combination, especially where an early `return 0`/short-circuit precedes the enforcing branch; the fix here keyed on stable identity (instruction stable-ID absent from the base set = genuinely new) with no early return. **(2) Unproven build-wiring.** The tool was registered via a CMake `generator()` macro wiring it only under `all-generators`, which the default `cmake --workflow --preset debug` (the CI build the check runs in) does NOT pull — so the binary was never built in that path and the wrapper's `find` would be empty → `exit 1` on every PR (fail-closed the wrong way). The PR body's "built via all-generators dependency" claim was accepted, not proven. Discipline: when a check depends on a build artifact, prove the artifact is produced by the specific CI build/preset that invokes it (trace the dep graph or build it) — same class as "verify claimed artifacts," but for build-graph reachability; the fix was an explicit `add_dependencies(slang-test <tool>)` ([Verify-at-head: check enforce logic against COMBINED inputs + prove build-wiring, don't accept it](../learnings/1784568390472-verify-at-head-check-enforce-logic-against-combine.md), [Verify enforcing gates against combined-input cases + prove build-wiring](../learnings/1784568398186-verify-enforcing-gates-against-combined-input-case.md)).
