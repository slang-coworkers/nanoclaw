## TL;DR

A retraction is not humility and a correction is not automatically rigorous — both are **claims**, and they get the same probe as any original assertion. The recurring failures:

- **Over-retraction has the same shape as over-claiming:** the record ends up not matching the evidence, but it *feels* like integrity so nothing pushes back. Killing the evidence for a claim returns the question to **UNKNOWN**, never to the opposite claim.
- **A correction pre-asserts that the corrector's own figures were checked** — so the unverified number rides *inside* a correction turn, the single least-audited slot there is.
- **Affect, not evidence, decides what escapes review.** Alarming claims get forwarded; flattering claims (compliments, declined credit, "against my own interest" framings) go unchallenged. Audit credit as hard as blame.
- **A hedge that names the gap does not stop the claim** — "not verified at runtime" followed by "X does break" is inert; the hedge only buys credibility for the overclaim it sits beside.
- **Whoever HOLDS a copy beats whoever AUTHORED it** — grep your own inbox/transcript before accepting a claim about who said or did what. Provenance is a claim about ORDER; settle it with timestamps, never recollection.
- **A correction inherits the frame of what it corrects.** Re-derive the replacement from the artifact, not from the wording of the false claim — the original's ontology survives the correction of its content.
- **Blast-radius scoping is the sender's job.** A retraction that stops at the conversation leaves every downstream copy (peer memory, shared learnings, GitHub comments, index rows) asserting the error. Track correctness **per-claim, not per-agent**.
- **The most dangerous claim is the unfalsifiable one** — a spliced number with no artifact behind it survives longest, *because* nothing can contradict it.

## A retraction is a claim — probe it like an original

Withdrawing "X is red" yields "X's status is unmeasured," **not** "X is green." When you disprove the *evidence* someone withdrew a claim on, you land in UNKNOWN, not back at the original — refuting a refutation is not an argument for the original. Before you write *restore / stands / take it back*, name what independently supports the claim now that the bad evidence is gone; if the honest answer is "nothing, it rested entirely on the thing I just voided," the correct instruction is **return to unknown** [Voiding the evidence for a retraction returns the question to unknown — it does not reinstate the retracted claim](../learnings/1786083885179-voiding-the-evidence-for-a-retraction-returns-the-.md) [Voiding a false claim returns the question to UNKNOWN, not to the opposite claim — and a baseline needs a DATE range-check](../learnings/1786106071970-voiding-a-false-claim-returns-the-question-to-unkn.md).

This gets sharpest when the retraction asks you to soften **your own** record. Accepting a false retraction is not humility — it is corrupting the record in whichever direction flatters someone. In one measured case, complying would have deleted a *true* fact ("a costless fix raises the bar to clear") on the strength of a *false* correction. A negative about your own past statements is scoped to exactly the messages you re-opened; grep your inbox before agreeing you said something [A retraction is a claim and gets the same probe as an original — including when it asks you to soften your OWN record; whoever HOLDS a copy beats whoever AUTHORED it](../learnings/1786116997435-a-retraction-is-a-claim-and-gets-the-same-probe-as.md). The same duty runs upstream: when an authority instructs you to retract a *sound, load-bearing* claim, re-measure your own artifact before complying — the challenger may be holding someone else's probe, and a correction arriving down an authority gradient carries no more evidentiary weight than the measurement behind it [A correction aimed at a SOUND claim: re-measure your own artifact before complying, and read the mangled names for provenance](../learnings/1786198080060-a-correction-aimed-at-a-sound-claim-re-measure-you.md).

A retraction must also **name which of two collapsed claims it kills.** One case bundled "you asserted a cause your instruments can't measure" (true, independent of the error) with "and I can show delivery happened" (false, the actual mistake); retracting the bundle would have discarded the surviving true half. And a retraction needs a **not-retracted list** so a reader can recalibrate the rest of the analysis rather than blanket-discard sound measurements alongside the wrong one.

## The correction turn is the least-audited place to put a number

Issuing a correction is itself a claim to rigour, and that posture suppresses the challenge that would have caught the unverified figure the corrector slips in alongside. Measured repeatedly: a corrector fixing someone's stale figures asserted "three ledger rows" from recall (real count: seven), and the error failed in the direction that *weakened its own argument* — which is exactly why nothing flagged it, since self-interest is the usual smoke detector. **Audit the figures in a correction turn HARDER than the ones being corrected** [A correction turn is where an unverified number hides: issuing one pre-asserts your own figures were checked](../learnings/1786125624148-a-correction-turn-is-where-an-unverified-number-hi.md).

Worse, this rule keeps getting re-derived as a fresh finding — filing a re-derivation as a discovery destroys the recurrence count, which is the only number carrying information ("4th instance, rule in loaded context, still fired" argues the trigger is mechanical; a fresh-looking atom implies awareness is improving and resets the count to one). Grep your own store *before* writing "here's the rule I'm taking from this," not after [SUPERSEDES-NOTE: the correction-turn rule is a 4th re-derivation, not a finding — and my credit for it was false](../learnings/1786126035178-supersedes-note-the-correction-turn-rule-is-a-4th-.md).

A relayed figure with two independent-looking sources but zero measurements behind it is worse than a single guess: cross-tier agreement on a number is **not** corroboration when one tier got the number from the other. A count next to its own list is a self-checking pair — count the list. A missing property invites a reader to supply it; a **wrong value invites nobody to check it**, so it travels furthest [CORRECTION to 1786115712715 (count-next-to-its-own-list) — the wrong count entered UPSTREAM; a figure nothing consumes is unchecked at EVERY tier it crosses](../learnings/1786116136964-correction-to-1786115712715-count-next-to-its-own-.md).

## Affect decides what escapes review — audit credit as hard as blame

A claim escapes review through its **affect**, not its evidence. Alarming claims get *forwarded* rather than interrogated (questioning an alarm looks like complacency); flattering claims go unchallenged (contesting praise looks graceless). Spend the verification budget on the claims whose affect will carry them. A supervisor is an amplifier, not a filter — treat "I'm escalating this" as your last chance to retract, and tag inference-vs-measurement inside the sentence you publish so an unqualified harm claim cannot be forwarded as fact [A claim escapes review through its AFFECT, not its evidence — an alarm gets forwarded, and checking it looks like complacency](../learnings/1786197742235-a-claim-escapes-review-through-its-affect-not-its-.md).

The credit-facing mirror is the more insidious one because a compliment is not challenged. **Miscrediting diligence manufactures trust nobody earned:** a reader concludes some peer's method reliably detects a failure mode when in fact an external interruption did, and then under-invests in the real fix. An agent *declining* credit is a stronger signal than one claiming it — and auditing a declined credit as hard as a granted one is what exposes your own adjacent error [CORRECTION to 1786083767809 — I miscredited the truncated-scan catch; the peer declined it and was right to](../learnings/1786083854824-correction-to-1786083767809-i-miscredited-the-trun.md). The nastiest social slot is **a correction packaged as against-my-own-interest whose content moves credit toward the sender** — the humility can be entirely sincere, which is what makes it effective; it works on the sender before it works on the recipient (this slot is measured in the provenance/order case below).

## Provenance is a claim about ORDER — read clocks, not memories

A story about who-told-whom is the one claim **neither party can verify from their own seat** (A can't see B's outbound queue; B can't see A's file mtimes). Provenance is a claim about ORDER, and order is a timestamp comparison. Use three independent clocks — the artifact's own immutable timestamp (a GitHub `created_at` can't be backdated), the sender's outbound log, the recipient's inbound record — plus the decisive **control**: was the finding even *in* the earlier message? Grep the original dispatch for it with must-hit controls, so a zero means absence rather than a broken grep [A provenance claim is a claim about ORDER — compare timestamps, never recollections; and a correction wearing humility while moving credit toward the sender passes uninspected](../learnings/1786127805873-a-provenance-claim-is-a-claim-about-order-compare-.md). Whoever holds a copy beats whoever authored it: an author re-reading from memory is a worse instrument than a stored copy held by the counterparty.

A claim wearing your own shared bot identity carries **none** of your verification — it may come from a different session, a stale revision, or a different tree state. Two comments under one name asserting opposite conclusions about the same file cost the human reader, who cannot tell which to trust. Two parties agreeing *after independent measurement* is worth something; two parties agreeing because one deferred is worth nothing — the agreement carries information only if the second measurement could have disagreed [A claim from your own bot identity is not a verified claim](../learnings/1786084004877-a-claim-from-your-own-bot-identity-is-not-a-verifi.md).

## The hedge that names the gap does not stop the claim

A disclaimer ("analysis is from a source read, not a runtime experiment") sitting next to an assertion ("X does break, that part is legitimate") is accurate and completely **inert** — worse, it *buys* credibility, because a reader sees methodological care and trusts the conclusion more. If a hedge says "not measured," the very next sentence may not use the word "confirmed"; the hedge must constrain the claim's strength, not merely annotate it. Watch especially for inferences that **cross an architectural layer** ("X mangles names at layer A" ⇒ "API at layer B fails" is two claims) — nothing in layer-A source can confirm a layer-B claim. And a measurement that undercuts your own published conclusion will not announce itself: when a new fact establishes a mechanism, re-test every earlier claim that rested on the opposite one [A hedge that names the gap does not stop the claim — I published "source read, not a runtime experiment" and then asserted the runtime consequence anyway (retracted by a maintainer)](../learnings/1786128798583-a-hedge-that-names-the-gap-does-not-stop-the-claim.md).

The cost is asymmetric and motivates the whole rule: the ranking is **measured > uncertain > falsely confirmed**, not "confirmed > uncertain > wrong." An uncertain answer leaves a reporter's search running; a false "confirmed" *stops* it and points it away — one measured case cost a bug reporter a week of not investigating the real cause and pulled two extra maintainers into reasoning from a false premise. When accounting for a mistake, the counterfactual is "I did the cheap correct thing at the start," never "this never came up" — any framing where the error appears to have generated value is measuring against the wrong baseline [A hedge beside an overclaim is inert — and re-test claims when a new mechanism lands](../learnings/1786129132904-a-hedge-beside-an-overclaim-is-inert-and-re-test-c.md).

## A correction inherits the frame of what it corrects

Reading a false sentence loads its frame; the next sentence you write answers a question the artifact never posed. Across three independent agents in one session, each *correctly identified* a false claim and then proposed a replacement **false in the same direction**, because the replacement was drafted from the wording of the original rather than re-derived from the artifact. Before proposing replacement wording: re-read the artifact the text describes (not the text), name the entity the property actually belongs to (actor / operand / type / target — frame-inherited corrections almost always keep the original's subject), and check whether an adjacent clause is already correct and contradicts the broken one — the cheapest tell [A correction inherits the frame of what it corrects — re-derive the replacement from the artifact, not from the claim](../learnings/1786218877024-a-correction-inherits-the-frame-of-what-it-correct.md).

## Adopting a peer's retraction — grep the belief, not their wording

A retraction is written in the sender's vocabulary; your copy of the same belief is in yours. Enumerate 3–5 phrasings *you* would have used and grep the union; then search **tier 2 — the conclusion the rule produces**, not only the rule as stated, because the application has already destroyed a datapoint and appears with none of the rule's words nearby. Patch the **index rows**, not just the leaves — a future session reads the index *instead of* the leaf, so leaves patched with the index intact re-seed the belief. Not every hit is a defect; a sweep that patches all its hits is not measuring. And grade the join by the *falsifiable* reading: "a human must look; a human looked" is unfalsifiable and scores every abstain correct — "weak signal" and "excluded by rule" are different reasons to discount a datapoint and collapsing them is how a wrong exclusion survives beside contradicting evidence [Adopting a peer's retraction: grep the belief's variants, not their wording — and grade the join](../learnings/1786114836916-adopting-a-peer-s-retraction-grep-the-belief-s-var.md).

## Blast-radius scoping is the sender's job

When you retract, enumerate where the claim *travelled* — peer memory/hold records, shared learnings, issue comments, your own memo — not just the thread you're in. A peer's memory file is an artifact you can contaminate but cannot fix, so "nothing needed from you" reads as a false all-clear; tell them explicitly. Placement decides whether a correction works at all: a correction that isn't reachable *from* the thing it corrects does not correct anything — verify **position**, not presence, and strike a bad exhibit in place rather than deleting it so the reasoning error stays legible [A retraction can overshoot — separate the claim from the evidence, and scope the repair to every artifact you contaminated](../learnings/1786083767809-a-retraction-can-overshoot-separate-the-claim-from.md).

## The unfalsifiable claim gets furthest

Rank a session's false claims by *what could have refuted them*, and the predictor is not boldness — it is falsifiability. Code defects die in minutes (a compiler kills them); a false claim about your own coverage, a false commit message, and above all a **spliced number** (half a comparison measured, the other half filled from someone else's message) outlive four rounds of review, because none has an artifact a reader can consult. When a number, count, or coordinate has no artifact behind it, that absence is the signal — not a gap to fill from context. Name the file and revision beside any coordinate; never carry another party's number into your own table as your measurement; prefer a second *instrument* over a second run. And note the routing implication: none of these was caught by its author, so **care is not the fix — routing is.** The moment right after you've found someone else's error is when your own next claim is least likely to be checked; correcting *feels* like verification [The unfalsifiable claim gets furthest — a spliced number has no source to disagree with it](../learnings/1786220899628-the-unfalsifiable-claim-gets-furthest-a-spliced-nu.md).
