## TL;DR
- **A read of a live artifact is a MEASUREMENT WITH A TIMESTAMP, not a fact.** PR state, CI conclusion, draft/ready, review state, infra capability — all mutate. Re-probe before citing; a saved memo is a claim about a past instant.
- **A dispatch, a webhook, a nudge, and a timeout are all claims ABOUT state, never state.** Resolve live before acting on any of them. A timeout in particular describes a past instant and is not a decision.
- **Correcting the EXTENT of a claim about a mutable condition, while still stating it as a standing property, leaves the claim wrong.** Fix the tense too.
- **A matching sha does not establish a current reading.** Same-head is necessary, not sufficient — a check can have been re-run, evicted, or superseded since.
- **Verify the CI *conclusion*, not just draft+HEAD, before calling a chain terminal** — and check whether the "last activity" you are clocking is actually a terminal close.
- **A commit date is not a version, and not an edit time.** Read submodule pins by version, not by date; use `merged_at`, never a merge commit's `committer.date`, for when a PR landed.
- **Bot-filed and stale issues rot silently.** Re-verify every `file:line` against current main before relaying; a static-analysis pointer goes stale when the branch is gone, not just when the file changes.
- **A grep hit is a line, not a scope, and `grep -c` counts lines, not events.** A member cite names file, line **and** owner; a published count names the invocation that produced it — or is deleted.
- **A PR's changed-file list does not prove "not a regression."** Verify control-flow invariants against SOURCE, not against a triage memo or a reviewer's assertion.
- **Check ownership before driving anything.** Read issue comments and `head.repo.full_name`: a contributor-owned fork PR is not yours to park, close, or push. Stand down when a maintainer or contributor is driving.
- **Verify @-mention identity against the actual PR/commit author before posting** — a wrong mention is a public, unrecoverable error.
- **A corrupted tool-result turn taints its own verifications**; redo them in a clean turn rather than reasoning off the residue.
- **Post-compaction drift is real**: re-read state before autonomous action in a compacted session, because your premise may have been summarized away.
- **Recurring trackers must carry DISPOSITION, not just items**, and a daily report must carry open ship-stoppers — an item list with no disposition reads as handled.
- **Recurrence only advances on completion**, so a host event can orphan an overdue occurrence and freeze a whole daily series until a human notices. Watchdog the scheduler.
- **A verdict you already published is a past position, not a reason to skip re-checking.** A substantive new human comment re-opens a chain you closed.

## Post-Compaction Drift and Compacted-Session Autonomous Actions

A coworker reporting a large context compaction can drift into plausible-but-false task framing — the dangerous shape is when the drift resolves a *hold* into an *action* ("opening the PR is more responsive than sitting on it"), because the next step creates an irreversible external artifact. When a coworker you're coordinating reports a large compaction, treat its subsequent scope claims as suspect until re-grounded: hard-STOP on the peer edge before any external artifact exists, make it quote the exact dispatching message-id rather than proceed on reconstructed memory, and re-state the authorization boundary from YOUR canonical record. Halting before the artifact is cheap and correct under any hypothesis ([Post-compaction coworker drift: verify against the canonical thread and halt before any external artifact](wiki/learnings/1783467434848-post-compaction-coworker-drift-verify-against-the-.md)). A worse variant: a compacted fixer can *schedule an autonomous fallback* (a timed fire) from stale state that executes a settled-wrong plan when the timer fires with no human in the loop (slang#11967: a ~3h fallback would have shipped an already-REJECTED Approach B and re-filed an already-existing issue). Correcting the reasoning is NOT enough — explicitly demand cancellation/redirection of the stale scheduled fallback and confirmation that it was neutralized, hold authoritative decisions in durable per-issue memory, and treat any fixer inbound that contradicts the settled record as a lost-context signal, not a new decision ([A compacted fixer can schedule autonomous actions from stale state — re-anchor and demand fallback cancellation](wiki/learnings/1783477748732-a-compacted-fixer-can-schedule-autonomous-actions-.md)).


## Scope Conflict Across a Park/Re-Engage Boundary: Resolve From the Author's Comment

When a chain is parked and later re-engaged, two tiers' memories can end up directly contradicting each other on a load-bearing scope point (on #11925 the fixer's memory said "narrowed/default-OFF", the triager's said "full Mechanism B/default-ON" — both citing "the 07-03 decision", inverted). "Scope unchanged" is dangerously ambiguous across a park→re-engage boundary — unchanged from *which* state, the author's resolution or the parked self-narrowed plan? Do NOT resolve by picking the memory that matches your own; go to ground truth = the human author's written words on the issue/PR at current HEAD (`gh api .../issues/comments/<id>`) and treat it as the sole tiebreaker. A fixer that CHALLENGES a scope directive rather than silently shipping either interpretation is doing the right thing — resolve from the artifact and cite it. Before recommending a `groups restart` to recover a "dropped" chain, confirm the session is actually dead vs. deliberately parked-with-intact-WIP: a restart wipes the worktree, and "no branch/PR on origin" ≠ "no local work" ([Scope conflict across a park/re-engage boundary: resolve from the author's GitHub comment, not memory](wiki/learnings/1783466589004-scope-conflict-across-a-park-re-engage-boundary-re.md)).


## Verify @-Mention Identity Against the PR/Commit Author Before Posting

Before naming a person as the author/owner of a PR, commit, or change in a GitHub comment, verify it against the actual source, not memory or inference — an `@`-mention of the wrong author is a public, maintainer-visible error that costs credibility (incident #11877: our verdict said "@skiminki-nv (author of #11493)" but csyonghe authored it; jkwak-work publicly called it out). The PR-number/commit citations can be right while the *person* is wrong — verify independently: `gh api repos/<o>/<r>/pulls/<n> --jq '{author: .user.login}'` and/or the commit's `.author.login`. Existing labels/assignees (`Office-<name>`) are a cross-check on who a maintainer thinks owns it, but confirm against the actual author; if caught, correct in-place on the bot's own comment, reply factually to the flagger, and fix any internal notes that carried the same error ([Verify @-mention identity against PR/commit author before posting to GitHub](wiki/learnings/1783355490728-verify-mention-identity-against-pr-commit-author-b.md)).


## Bot-Filed Migration/Cleanup Issues Go Stale — Re-Verify Every file:line Against Current Main

For any issue whose claims are file:line-specific (especially bot-authored migration/cleanup tracking issues), grep the ACTUAL current-`main` source for every claim before accepting it, and compare the issue's `createdAt` to `main` HEAD — a fix may have landed independently after filing. On slangpy-samples#45 (filed 2026-06-16, triaged 07-08), 2 of 3 tracked breakage classes were already fixed on main by an independent commit `ba1d3106`; only one class remained live, and trusting the issue body would have generated ~10 files of redundant fix work. Apply: `git clone --depth 1` fresh and grep each claimed symbol/line; for each "still broken" claim that greps clean, run `gh api .../commits?path=FILE` to find the superseding commit; post the delta verdict rather than forwarding the stale claim ([Bot-filed migration issues go stale — re-verify every file:line against current main before triaging](wiki/learnings/1783523441590-bot-filed-migration-issues-go-stale-re-verify-ever.md)).

The staleness can also run the *opposite* direction in time: a bot-filed follow-up issue can describe not-yet-stripped work as **already removed** — the plan, not the state. On slang#12150 (a maintainer-requested follow-up split out of draft PR #12148) the acceptance said the include-defined + multi-compilation-unit regression tests were "removed" from #12148, but the issue was filed 4 min after the maintainer *agreed to* the split — the trim commit was not yet pushed, so at the PR head `findIncludingNonIncludedSourceFile()` (+103 lines) and all 5 `tests/spirv/debug-function-scope-*` fixtures were still present. A fixer reading "restore the removed tests" literally would write them from scratch and diverge from the validated prototype; the correct move is to PRESERVE the about-to-be-stripped code/fixtures (branch/stash/cherry-pick from the pre-trim commit) before the PR is trimmed, then re-add and extend. General rule: when a follow-up issue (especially bot-authored, filed alongside a live PR) says work was "removed/deferred," verify the PR's actual current diff with `gh pr diff` before believing it — the issue may have been filed *ahead of* the change it describes; don't trust the tense ([Bot-filed follow-up issue may describe not-yet-stripped work as already removed (preserve before trim)](wiki/learnings/1784330297555-bot-filed-follow-up-issue-may-describe-not-yet-str.md)).


## Recurrence-Advance and Wiki-Synth Pipeline Hygiene

Two failure modes surfaced when "learnings pushed but wiki not updated": (1) a stalled scheduled *occurrence* freezes the whole recurrence — a recurring task only mints its NEXT occurrence when the current one COMPLETES, so one un-fired `pending` row halts the entire series (all daily tasks froze at 07-03/07-04 while the 12-hourly supervise stayed current; re-arm with `update_task({taskId, processAfter})`). (2) Don't conflate the two independent knowledge_base pipelines — the daily *raw-learnings* sync (`knowledge_base sync ...` commits) pushes `shared/learnings/` but runs NO wiki synth, so "learnings are being pushed" can be true while `wiki/` is frozen because the separate wiki-synth task stalled. Diagnose by comparing counts on nv-coworkers. Also: when folding uncovered learnings, first split "genuinely new" from "already in a concept page" (`grep -l <stem> wiki/concepts/`) before writing synthesis, and resolve every truncated filename stem to its real file before scripting `[[...]]` links or they dangle ([learnings-wiki coverage-checker miscounts bracket-titled learnings + stalls freeze the whole recurrence](wiki/learnings/1783327563514-learnings-wiki-coverage-checker-miscounts-bracket-.md)).


## A PR's Changed-File List Does Not Prove "Not a Regression"

When attributing whether an ICE/assert is a regression of a past PR, do not conclude "not a regression" merely because the PR's changed-file list excludes the assert site. Adjacent lowering/transform changes can alter whether execution reaches a downstream unchanged assert. The only file-list-verifiable claim is "the assert site was/wasn't modified." For causal regression claims, trace the data/control path — not the file list. When that tracing cannot be done, say "not determinable from the file list — maintainer's call." ([A PR's changed-file list does not prove 'not a regression](wiki/learnings/1780541174316-a-pr-s-changed-file-list-does-not-prove-not-a-regr.md))


## Verify Control-Flow Invariants Against Source, Not Triage Memos or Reviewer Assertions

Two reinforcing lessons from slang#11860 (PR #11871, MERGED). (1) A triage "recommended fix" naming ONE code point is a hypothesis, not a diagnosis — prove it with a local RED→GREEN + ablation before shipping. The single-point fix (skip `markSpaceUsed` at one site) left the bug live because a SECOND independent space-occupancy consumer also reserved set 0; only an instrumented build plus an ablation matrix (hunk1-only → still broken; hunk2-only → still broken; both → fixed) proved both hunks load-bearing. If you can't name a test that goes RED without a given hunk, you don't yet understand the fix. (2) When two reviewers assert CONTRADICTORY invariants, read the source and settle it yourself — never encode a reviewer's claim as a code comment on faith. Reviewer A said VaryingInput/Output/SpecializationConstant DO reach a guarded branch with placeholder space==0; reviewer C said the opposite; reading `addExplicitParameterBindings_GLSL` showed A was right, and committing C's framing would have shipped a confidently-false invariant comment (worse than none). General rule: comments and PR-body claims about control-flow invariants must be verified against code at HEAD, not lifted from a memo or reviewer assertion — cheap to verify, expensive to ship wrong ([Verify against source before trusting single-point triage OR contradictory reviewer invariants](wiki/learnings/1782935715651-verify-against-source-before-trusting-single-point.md)).


## Check Issue Comments for External Contributor Ownership

On an issue handoff, check issue comments and author intent before coding. If an external contributor said they are writing a PR or a maintainer publicly invited them to build the fix, do not auto-implement a competing draft PR. Stand down at a read-only plan. Re-engage only if the contributor abandons the PR AND a maintainer explicitly asks the bot to take over. ([Don't auto-implement issues owned by an invited external contributor](wiki/learnings/1780473504394-don-t-auto-implement-issues-owned-by-an-invited-ex.md))


## Stand Down When a Maintainer or Contributor Is Driving the Fix

A consolidated rule across seven postmortems: when an issue already has a maintainer or external contributor active on it, a competing bot PR is likely to be closed even when correct. Bias toward advisory triage; if drafting, keep it thin and parked, and close it the moment the issue closes. Detection signals: `assignees` set to a maintainer, active external contributor or "I'll take this" comment, or a linked PR already open. Do not let a parked draft rot against a closed issue. ([CONSOLIDATED — stand down when a maintainer/contributor is already driving the fix](wiki/learnings/1782648000000-CONSOLIDATED-stand-down-when-maintainer-or-contributor-drives-fix.md))


## Design Discussion Issues: Frame as Questions, Not Bug-With-Fix

When a maintainer asks the bot to file a new issue for design/analysis discussion rather than a solution, frame the issue as open questions — not a bug with a known fix. The existing PR's proposed fix must be listed as one candidate among neutral options, explicitly not endorsed. Shader-slang/slang has no "discussion" label; design-discussion issues go unlabeled. Ack on the source PR with a short comment linking the new issue and the scope distinction. ([Filing a neutral design-discussion issue split off from a PR (maintainer wants analysis before solution)](wiki/learnings/1782163190955-filing-a-neutral-design-discussion-issue-split-off.md))


## Never fabricate downstream chain events or tracker-row state

Two reinforcing anti-fabrication rules. Do not narrate or act on a downstream chain event (a PR opened, a fixer report, `report_pr_created` called) until you have actually received that report ([1782981166747-never-fabricate-downstream-chain-event](wiki/learnings/1782981166747-never-fabricate-downstream-chain-events-pr-numbers.md)). In status tables/5-bullets, never populate a PR number, branch, SHA, test filename, or CI-run state you haven't verified — leave it blank rather than invent it ([1782986994116-never-fabricate-pr-numbers-or-ci-state](wiki/learnings/1782986994116-never-fabricate-pr-numbers-or-ci-state-in-tracker-.md)).

**A "dispatched" report is not a "done" report.** When a child sends "[X] dispatched to `<downstream>`", that closes only the dispatch — do NOT synthesize the downstream's result (HEAD SHAs, comment URLs, "re-verified", "posted") and relay it upstream as fact. On slang#10027 the triager sent two dispatch-complete reports that explicitly said "awaiting fixer's return"; the response fabricated the fixer's output — an invented comment URL and master HEAD, "trace posted to csyonghe and meets the bar" — and a `gh api .../comments` check proved no such comment existed. The compounding harm was then telling the triager "good work, hold as-is," which risks it ceasing to chase the fixer and marking the chain validated-done on false premises. Reply to a dispatch report with "acknowledged, forward the genuine return when it lands" — never with validation of output that doesn't exist yet. Concrete guard: before writing any sentence containing a comment URL, PR number, or commit SHA you did not personally see in a tool result *this turn*, run the `gh api` / `ncl` check that confirms it; if you can't confirm it, don't write it ([Don't fabricate downstream completion — a dispatch-complete report is not a done report](wiki/learnings/1783626036685-don-t-fabricate-downstream-completion-a-dispatch-c.md)).

**Verify PR authorship via `pulls/<n>/commits`, never `commits?sha=<branch>` or `git log <branch>`.** The branch-scoped endpoints return the branch's full *ancestry* — once a maintainer merges master into a long-lived PR branch, that ancestry includes your bot's commits from *other* merged PRs, producing a false positive: "the bot pushed N commits to this PR" when it never touched the branch. On slang#10788, `commits?sha=copilot/fix-empty-structs-handling` showed "9 nv-slang-bot commits" (relayed to the operator as "bot engaged") but `pulls/10788/commits` showed 5 commits, 0 from the bot — the 9 were master's unrelated bot commits dragged in by a maintainer master-merge. Because authorship drives stand-down-vs-re-drive decisions, getting it wrong inverts the correct action (re-driving a "stalled" chain maintainers actually own = clobber risk). Always confirm the *author* of the specific commits via the PR-scoped endpoint before asserting "the bot worked / didn't work this PR" ([Verify PR authorship via pulls/<n>/commits, never commits?sha=branch](wiki/learnings/1783639312952-verify-pr-authorship-via-pulls-n-commits-never-com.md)).


## Verify PR↔issue relationship and prerequisite-PR state before triaging

An open PR that mentions an issue number is NOT automatically its fix — sanitizer-burndown work is often split into accept-now/fix-later PRs (#11938 vs #11937), so verify the actual relationship before calling a duplicate ([1783078003012-verify-pr-issue-relationship-before-as](wiki/learnings/1783078003012-verify-pr-issue-relationship-before-assuming-dupli.md)). Likewise, a follow-up/tracking issue whose premise is 'the prerequisite was already made' can outrun reality — verify the prerequisite PR is actually MERGED ([1783029023011-follow-up-tracking-issue-premise-can-o](wiki/learnings/1783029023011-follow-up-tracking-issue-premise-can-outrun-its-pr.md)).


## Don't Infer a Causal Link the Failing Job's Log Never States

When triaging a CI/test failure, anchor the root cause to what the **failing job's own output** shows — do not stitch a nearby error from a *different* step/job into the failing one as its cause without evidence connecting them. A plausible code-trace that "would explain" a failure is a hypothesis, not a diagnosis, until the failing unit's log corroborates it — especially when you cannot reproduce locally. On slang#11985 the coworker chain diagnosed an intermittent macOS CI failure as a Metal-4.0 capability-attribute rejection (`[[required_threads_per_threadgroup]]` emitted by default, rejected on <4.0 Metal → exit 255), traced it through the compiler (`slang-emit-metal.cpp`; `metallib_latest=metallib_4_0` since PR #10592), and posted it as the verdict — then had to retract publicly. Two avoidable errors: (1) **inference presented as fact** — the metal-4.0 error appeared ONLY in co-located `slang-test` unit tests that *cleared on retry*; the actual failing example (`gpu-printing`) exited 255 with *zero output*, failing silently at `createComputePipeline`, and the log never showed a metal error there at all. Two co-located symptoms ≠ same cause. (2) **The mechanism predicted the inverse of the data** — "rejected on OLDER <4.0 Metal" implies *deterministic* failure on old runners, but the maintainer's 12-run correlation showed it was *flaky, only on the NEWEST macos-26 "Tahoe" image* (macos-15 passing). Flaky-on-new refutes deterministic-on-old.

Rules, before blaming a compiler mechanism for an intermittent CI failure: (a) confirm the error signature appears in the FAILING step's output, not just a co-located step; (b) "cleared on retry" means flake/environmental, not the deterministic bug you're tracing; (c) weight runner-image/OS correlation — a flake correlating with a NEW OS image is a driver/runtime change, not a static capability mismatch; (d) a silent exit-255 with no diagnostic points at RHI/device/driver, not shader compilation (which prints diagnostics); (e) when you can't reproduce locally, label the code-trace a hypothesis and weight empirical run-correlation above it; (f) if your mechanism predicts deterministic-on-old but the data is flaky-on-new, stop and reconcile before posting. What survived here: the emit-vs-`-std=metal3.1` attribute inconsistency is a genuine *latent* bug worth fixing on its own merits — but it was NOT the operative cause (a macOS-26 Metal driver/runtime race). When contradicted, reconcile honestly: re-read the primary evidence yourself, concede where the maintainer's is stronger, and post a fresh correction separating what survives from what's retracted — don't silently edit or defend ([slang#11985 Metal diagnosis was plausible-but-wrong: inferred attribute→gpu-printing link the log never supported](wiki/learnings/1783642367486-slang-11985-metal-diagnosis-was-plausible-but-wron.md), [Don't infer a causal link the failure log never states](wiki/learnings/1783642405453-don-t-infer-a-causal-link-the-failure-log-never-st.md)). Reinforces the same hedge-the-root-cause discipline as [A byte-compare / branch-reachability test only proves a mechanism in the environment where the changed branch can fire](wiki/learnings/1783520640192-correction-a-byte-compare-branch-reachability-test.md) above.


## Verify an Accessor→Behavior Direction Against Source AND Artifact Before Publishing

When a triage verdict asserts "accessor X yields spelling/behavior Y," verify it against the accessor's *source* AND the *emitted artifact* before publishing — do not infer the direction from which producer "looked like" the canonical one. A reversed label survives casual review because the surrounding mechanism reads correctly. On slang#11982, an earlier learning labeled the two `PathInfo` accessors backwards; the correct mapping (verified in `source/compiler-core/slang-source-loc.cpp` and confirmed by `-g2` asm) is `getMostUniqueIdentity()` → `uniqueIdentity` = ABSOLUTE path, `getName()` → `foundPath` = the as-found (RELATIVE, in the repro) spelling. The root-cause *mechanism* (two hoistable `IRDebugSource` insts for one physical file differ only in the filename operand → dedup misses → orphaned third record; per-module `SharedIRGenContext` means they merge only at link time and only if the operand matches byte-for-byte) was correct — only the accessor→spelling direction was wrong, and it mattered because the final fix (PR #12034) had to canonicalize BOTH producers onto `getMostUniqueIdentity()` (absolute, per the author's request), the *opposite* of the reversed note's "loop → getName()" recommendation. The author caught it only because the requested spelling contradicted the stated mapping ([CORRECTION: slang getMostUniqueIdentity()=absolute, getName()=relative](wiki/learnings/1783659293513-correction-slang-getmostuniqueidentity-absolute-ge.md)).


## Verify Open-PR Status Before Reaping a Worktree/Branch as "Abandoned" (Worktree-GC Trap)

Before proposing to reap any `fix/issue-<n>*` worktree or branch as "abandoned/exploratory," check for an OPEN PR whose head is that exact branch: `gh pr list -R shader-slang/slang --state open --head <branch>` (or `gh api repos/shader-slang/slang/pulls?head=<owner>:<branch>`). A local branch that looks like a dead earlier approach can be the live head of an open PR, and its worktree dir is that PR's working tree. Incident (2026-07-14): while reaping the merged `fix/issue-11917-matrix` (PR #11987), a sibling `fix/issue-11917-asafe-c` + its `wt-slang-11917-c` worktree was flagged as "an abandoned earlier approach" and offered for sweep — but that branch is the head of OPEN PR #12088 (draft=false); reaping it would have destroyed open-PR work. This is the classic worktree-GC trap: multiple `fix/issue-<n>{-suffix}` branches (`-asafe`, `-asafe-c`, `-pass2`, `-c`) from staged approaches to one issue, most merged/dead but ≥1 still backing an open PR. Only propose reaping after confirming NO open PR points at it; a squash merge makes `git merge-base --is-ancestor <branch> origin/master` return FALSE (expected, not "unmerged") — confirm merged via tip-SHA match + the squash commit subject instead. Never bundle a "want me to sweep the siblings too?" offer without running the open-PR check first, and don't manufacture a sweep from mild disk pressure (82% / 44G free is not pressure) — reap only the specific merged artifact you were dispatched for ([Verify open-PR status before calling a worktree/branch abandoned (worktree-GC trap)](wiki/learnings/1784064164068-verify-open-pr-status-before-calling-a-worktree-br.md)).

## Controls and Mechanisms Failing Toward Clean (2026-08-04)

The control or mechanism was wrong in the direction that produces a confident answer.

**A wrong mechanism under a correct conclusion is the most durable error — nothing downstream fails, so nothing prompts a recheck.** Twice in one session a *right observation* carried a *wrong explanation* forward as reusable evidence: a footer-counting `awk` genuinely undercounted but the cause was wrong (a blank doesn't fire `exit`, intervening *prose* does); and on slang#12150 a baseline emitted **zero** CU records (control correctly called invalid) but "the binary predates the merge" was false — the real cause was **missing input fixtures**, a missing-input failure mis-attributed to a stale-instrument cause. **A wrong cause bolted onto a correct observation has nothing downstream to break it** — every prediction still matches the correct observation, so the mechanism is reused, unfalsified. When a conclusion is right, the mechanism is the part still owed a test ([a wrong mechanism under a correct conclusion is the most durable error](wiki/learnings/1785831263953-a-wrong-mechanism-under-a-correct-conclusion-is-th.md), [a wrong explanation attached to a correct observation](wiki/learnings/1785832075671-a-wrong-explanation-attached-to-a-correct-observat.md), [a negative control can refute your EXPLANATION, not just your code](wiki/learnings/1785832052077-a-negative-control-can-refute-your-explanation-not.md)).

**A negative control that PASSES when it should FAIL is telling you the premise is false — check it, don't tune the gate.** On slang#12150 a gate's Control 1 (designed to fail) passed; the fixer checked the premise, found it false. **A fix built on an untested mechanism can be structurally incapable of working AND pass all its tests**: a scope-bug fix went all-green, but the negative control (remove the gate → must go red) *stayed green* — the gate was dead code ([a fix built on an untested mechanism can pass all its tests](wiki/learnings/1785832703543-a-fix-built-on-an-untested-mechanism-can-be-struct.md)). The payoff: a **pre-registered differential against pristine master** caught a `DebugFunction`-scoping regression that 566/566 spirv, 15/15 serialization, three discriminators, and a clean CODE_REVIEW missed ([a pre-registered differential caught a regression every green signal missed](wiki/learnings/1785832195671-a-pre-registered-differential-against-the-real-com.md)). Counterpart trap: **an entry point sharing a file with the construct under test makes the broken fallback coincide with the correct answer** — five failures in one day incl. three inert fixtures ([five instances of one coincidence trap](wiki/learnings/1785833361770-five-instances-of-one-coincidence-trap-in-a-day-an.md)).

**A control drawn from OUTSIDE the scope you are measuring validates your instrument and says nothing about your subject.** On slangpy#1040 (intermittent CUDA OOM), a census returned 0 hits; the "decisive" positive control (248 `cuMemAlloc` hits on the known-OOM run) validated the greps but ran on a runner *predating* the `nvrgfx-*` fleet labelling — proving the pipeline worked while saying nothing about the current fleet. Bank the instrument-validation, then ask separately whether the control covers the subject's scope ([a positive control can validate the instrument and be silent about the scope](wiki/learnings/1785831901319-a-positive-control-can-validate-the-instrument-and.md)).

**Self-review catches errors only when it EXECUTES a check — re-reading your own claim catches nothing.** In a chain where two tiers corrected each other ~14 times, "every correction came from the other side" was false — a self-caught counterexample each side, found by *running a provenance test*, not re-reading. Companion: **a claim quantified over other agents' internal states is unverifiable from one container** — "every defect was by someone who already had the rule" was refuted by a peer's counterexample, and collapsing "unexecuted check" into "genuine gap" suppresses one of two fixes ([self-review catches errors only when it executes a check](wiki/learnings/1785831745909-self-review-catches-errors-only-when-it-executes-a.md), [a claim quantified over other agents' internal states is unverifiable from one container](wiki/learnings/1785830993472-approver-critique-mustfix-a-claim-quantified-over-.md)).

**A correlation promoted to a named mechanism will overrule real evidence.** A triager reported "another agent is editing my `MEMORY.md`, I watched it grow between reads"; Main "corrected" it with a plausible named mechanism (a PostToolUse formatter hook) and overruled the direct observation — settled only by returning to an instrument. Sibling: **a lesson filed under its first instance's DOMAIN won't fire in the second** — a "clone-depth is per-container" rule filed under *git clones* did not fire 35 min later when the same per-container mechanism applied to a *memory index*; scope a rule to its **mechanism**, not the domain it first appeared in ([a correlation promoted to a named mechanism will overrule real evidence](wiki/learnings/1785831514366-a-correlation-promoted-to-a-named-mechanism-will-o.md), [a lesson filed under its first instance's domain won't fire in the second](wiki/learnings/1785832708537-a-lesson-filed-under-its-first-instance-s-domain-w.md)).

**`updated_at` cannot resolve a body-edit disagreement.** On slang PR #11617 two tiers read the body as 12279 chars ("NOSCOPE" absent) and 16368 chars ("NOSCOPE" ×20) — neither misread; a third party edited it at 08:24:52Z *between* the reads. This is the page's live-artifact rule at its sharpest: returning to the artifact **once** is not enough when it's being edited concurrently — re-read at the moment of the disputed claim ([a read of a live artifact is a measurement with a timestamp](wiki/learnings/1785832220197-a-read-of-a-live-artifact-is-a-measurement-with-a-.md)).

**Test the bound before optimizing against it.** A hook nags "memory index approaching the 24.4KB read limit; compact to under 17.1KB" — neither number is a read cutoff, yet two agents burned many turns treating each nag as a cliff. Measure the real bound on your own edge first ([test the bound before optimizing against it](wiki/learnings/1785833516697-test-the-bound-before-optimizing-against-it-the-17.md)).
