## TL;DR
- **A read of a live artifact is a MEASUREMENT WITH A TIMESTAMP, not a fact.** PR state, CI conclusion, draft/ready, review state, infra capability — all mutate. Re-probe before citing; a saved memo is a claim about a past instant.
- **A dispatch, a webhook, a nudge, and a timeout are all claims ABOUT state, never state.** Resolve live before acting on any of them. A timeout in particular describes a past instant and is not a decision.
- **Correcting the EXTENT of a claim about a mutable condition, while still stating it as a standing property, leaves the claim wrong.** Fix the tense too.
- **A matching sha does not establish a current reading.** Same-head is necessary, not sufficient — a check can have been re-run, evicted, or superseded since.
- **Verify the CI *conclusion*, not just draft+HEAD, before calling a chain terminal** — and check whether the "last activity" you are clocking is actually a terminal close.
- **A commit date is not a version, and not an edit time.** Read submodule pins by version, not by date; use `merged_at`, never a merge commit's `committer.date`, for when a PR landed.
- **Bot-filed and stale issues rot silently.** Re-verify every `file:line` against current main before relaying; a static-analysis pointer goes stale when the branch is gone, not just when the file changes.
- **A grep hit is a line, not a scope, and `grep -c` counts lines, not events.** A member cite names file, line **and** owner; a published count names the invocation that produced it — or is deleted.
- **A PR's changed-file list does not prove "not a regression."** Verify control-flow invariants against SOURCE, not against a triage memo or a reviewer's assertion.
- **Check ownership before driving anything.** Read issue comments and `head.repo.full_name`: a contributor-owned fork PR is not yours to park, close, or push. Stand down when a maintainer or contributor is driving.
- **Verify @-mention identity against the actual PR/commit author before posting** — a wrong mention is a public, unrecoverable error.
- **A corrupted tool-result turn taints its own verifications**; redo them in a clean turn rather than reasoning off the residue.
- **Post-compaction drift is real**: re-read state before autonomous action in a compacted session, because your premise may have been summarized away.
- **Recurring trackers must carry DISPOSITION, not just items**, and a daily report must carry open ship-stoppers — an item list with no disposition reads as handled.
- **Recurrence only advances on completion**, so a host event can orphan an overdue occurrence and freeze a whole daily series until a human notices. Watchdog the scheduler.
- **A verdict you already published is a past position, not a reason to skip re-checking.** A substantive new human comment re-opens a chain you closed.

## Controls, discrimination, and proxy verification — part 2 — part 2/3A bare count is an unverifiable claim, because it authenticates a *command over a scope* and omitting either leaves the gap. At slang master `5b3f7a24`, "833 occurrences under `docs/`" is ambiguous four ways — `grep -r` lines vs `grep -rl` files × `docs/` vs `docs/generated/tests` gives 788 / 833 / 786 / 828, all defensible, all different — so cite command + scope + commit; and `/workspace/agent/slang-r0/` is a **stale non-git snapshot** carrying its own `CLAUDE.md`, so a repo-wide `grep -r` landing there inflates every figure with no error and no staleness signal (3323 vs 3274 files under `docs/generated/tests`; 833 vs 893 `/dev/null` lines) — run `git log -1` first, and if it errors you are in the wrong tree. The arithmetic sanity check is worth more than the reconciliation: occurrence-lines can never be *fewer* than the files containing them, so "833 occurrences" against "932 files" is impossible, and that impossibility is the tell that two trees or scopes are in play — flag an unreproducible number rather than bridging it, since the underlying finding usually survives on magnitude-independent facts ([repo-wide grep counts: cite the command, beware the stale slang-r0 snapshot](wiki/learnings/1785791518674-repo-wide-grep-counts-cite-the-command-and-beware-.md)). Three agents reporting three different counts for one slang#12333 defect were **all correct** and measuring different units — 786 files / 828 lines of `/dev/null` mentions, 770/771 `//TEST:` directive lines, **755** executed `.slang` tests (the only figure answering "how many tests could silently pass") — with the arithmetic tying exactly (828−771 = 57 prose lines; 771 lines over 770 files because one file held two directives; 770−755 = 15 `.md` prompt files) and `comm -23` proving strict subset: reconcile the metrics before assuming anyone is wrong. The trap in the same case is the subtle one: a `search/code` negative control (`"ZZZNONEXISTENTZZZ"` → 0) proves the instrument is *live*, not that its output unit is your claim's unit — a code-search index cannot express "directive lines," so 932/906 were real match counts in the wrong unit, and passing (a) is exactly what makes it tempting to skip (b); a rate-limited, truncated paginated sweep is likewise a **floor** (`≥786`), and a floor agreeing with a point value is weaker corroboration than two point values agreeing ([a discriminating control proves your instrument works — not that it answers your question](wiki/learnings/1785792742163-a-discriminating-control-proves-your-instrument-wo.md)). Two structural traps close the cluster. **When two numbers disagree, first ask whether one of them is a constant**: a tool emitting `prCount: 29` beside a 20-item list drove multiple runs of hypotheses about "what filter drops exactly 9," and three observations settled it — 29/20, 28/20, 27/20 against true populations 77/75/74, i.e. the list is hard-clamped at 20 and the count drifts in lockstep with reality, so `gap = count − 20` has no independent meaning and every mechanism proposed for "the 9" was unfalsifiable by construction; get three data points before theorizing about a gap, and treat a suspiciously round unmoving value (20/30/50/100) as a clamp or page size until proven otherwise ([when two numbers disagree, first ask whether one of them is a constant](wiki/learnings/1785796046919-when-two-numbers-disagree-first-ask-whether-one-of.md)). And an undercounted enumeration is **coverage loss, not cost**: a sweep over 20 PRs when the repo holds 233 open / 76 non-draft asserts green over 56 PRs it never opened, so never report "no failures" over a set you did not enumerate — state the denominator, reconcile on **raw** page length (page 2 held 100 raw but only 20 non-draft, so a filtered-count stop rule terminates early because filtering happens after truncation, and `/pulls` has no `total_count` positive control), assert `non_draft + draft == raw_total` per page, and note the root cause never had to be found for the fix to be right; the same file measures why `curl https://api.github.com/rate_limit` is *worse* than `gh` as a probe — HTTP 200 with plausible anonymous numbers (`core: 60`, `graphql: 0`, `search: 10`) from the same container where `repos/.../pulls/<n>` returns `X-Ratelimit-Limit: 6000`, because injection is per-path and the body contradicts itself ([enumeration undercount is coverage loss, not cost — and `rate_limit` is broken via curl too](wiki/learnings/1785783404146-enumeration-undercount-is-coverage-loss-not-cost-a.md)). The same rule binds any `N:` you *write*: **a stated count is a claim, the list is the fact** — folding 282 learnings into 19 concept pages via 7 parallel subagents, each told to append footer rows to `**Source learnings (N):**` and bump N, every agent self-reported success and several explicitly claimed "footer counts match row counts"; a global recount found **19 pages with stated-N drift, several with no edits that day** (`review-pr-practices` 273→274, `slang-backends-spirv` 162→160, `slang-misc-build-infrastructure-and-tooling` 60→55), i.e. the drift is **chronic across daily folds**, not an artifact of one run — so never trust a writer's report about arithmetic it performed on itself, and never increment; always recompute from the deduped stem set and rewrite both the footer and frontmatter `source_count`. Two instrument lessons ride along, and the *order* they surfaced in is the point. The first checker reported 5 MISMATCHes, which was plausible — but its `flag&&!/^- \[/&&NF{exit}` clause stopped at the first non-row line, and real footers carry wrapped entries and interleaved blanks, so it counted a **prefix** of the list and manufactured mismatches on pages that were fine: **the instrument's bug produced exactly the finding being looked for**, and it was caught only by dumping the raw footer region instead of acting on the number ⇒ **suspect an instrument whose first act confirms your prior**, and validate it on a case whose answer you know before believing its verdict. That instinct generalises into the sharpest available class definition: the bug worth building detectors for is not "wrong number" but **"wrong number that confirms the current hypothesis"**, because only the contradicting kind has a downstream check — see *Detector asymmetry* below [Build detectors for "wrong number that CONFIRMS the hypothesis" — and treat a flattering diagnosis like a flattering number](wiki/learnings/1786075675816-build-detectors-for-wrong-number-that-confirms-the.md). The sound replacement carries an **internal control** so a parse failure announces itself instead of masquerading as a content finding — count rows to EOF *and* count unique citation stems, then require they agree:

```bash
rows=$(awk '/^\*\*Source learnings \(/{flag=1;next} flag&&/^- \[/{c++} END{print c+0}' "$f")
uniq=$(awk '/^\*\*Source learnings \(/{flag=1;next} flag&&/^- \[/{print}' "$f" \
        | grep -oP 'wiki/learnings/\K[^)]+' | sort -u | wc -l)
```

`rows == uniq` on every page says the parsed rows are real citation rows and none are duplicated; if the two diverge, the parse is suspect rather than the page. And the coverage lesson: **N independent self-reports do not compose into coverage** — each agent saw only its own slice, so a cross-cutting defect (drift on pages nobody touched) was invisible to all 7 and visible only to a global recount ([verifying N subagents' count claims — the claim and your checker can both be wrong, and the checker's bug looks like a finding](wiki/learnings/1785824696781-verifying-n-subagents-count-claims-the-claim-and-y.md)).

The reconciliation discipline above assumes the total is the thing in dispute; the sharper failure is a total that is **right** while its parts are wrong. On slang PR #12378 my PR body said the change added **27** test cells and my `[Fix Report]` to two peers said **21**; the harness itself says **28** (`slang-test` prints `100% of tests passed (28/28)`). I corrected to "19 DIAGNOSTIC_TEST cells + 9 TEST:SIMPLE cells = 28" — **the total was now right and the decomposition was still wrong**, because my counting pattern `^//TEST:` does not distinguish `TEST:SIMPLE` (a test cell) from `TEST:COMPILE` (a setup step building a module the import test consumes); the true split is 19 `DIAGNOSTIC_TEST:SIMPLE` + 8 `TEST:SIMPLE` + 1 `TEST:COMPILE` = 28. An independent reviewer caught it; my own re-check had "verified the total against the harness" and stopped there. ⭐ **A total that matches is not evidence the parts are right — errors can cancel**, so break every count down by category and confirm the parts sum (the same partition control that catches a wrong diff stat, applied to any aggregate). ⭐ **Two of your own artifacts disagreeing IS the detector** — this surfaced only because a peer report and a PR body cited different numbers; had both said 27, nothing would have surfaced, so when two of your outputs disagree don't just pick the winner, ask what the disagreement reveals about how both were produced. And **a grep pattern that matches a family will silently count siblings** (`^//TEST:` catches `SIMPLE`, `COMPILE`, `COMPARE_COMPUTE`, `INTERPRET`…): if your claim is about one member, count that member and print the per-kind breakdown so a mismatch is visible. Instrument trap en route, of a piece with the `curl rate_limit` case above: `grep -c` inside `$(( ))` failed with `syntax error in expression (error token is "0")` because `grep -c` emits a trailing newline, and the loop printed **0 for every file** — a total instrument failure that reads exactly like a legitimate "no matches anywhere". ⭐ **A uniform zero across every item is evidence about your instrument, not your data**; recount in `python3`, where the value cannot be malformed ([a correct total can hide a wrong decomposition](wiki/learnings/1785993168045-a-correct-total-can-hide-a-wrong-decomposition-bre.md)).

### Corrections: the clause behind a concession, the text you move, and the write you cannot read back

The most dangerous over-claim is the one immediately following an honest retraction — observed three times in one chain (slang-rhi#806), each inside a message whose first half was a genuine, costly self-correction. The structure does the damage, not intent: the concession buys real credibility, the same breath spends it on an *uncontrolled* adjacent claim, and the reader has no reason to discount the second half precisely because the first half was honest. The direction tell is that the trailing clause **expands the writer's own reach** ("there is a further probe", "the payload is provable", one count moving 40→50), and in all three instances it rested on a check that could not have come out differently if false — the identical defect the retraction was apologizing for. If a "but", "and in fact", "which also means", or "this actually tightens to…" follows a concession, that clause needs its own control or its own separate message, and reviewers should apply this symmetrically: a concession is not evidence for whatever follows it, and its author is at that moment the least able to see it ([retraction momentum — the clause after a concession needs its own control](wiki/learnings/1785788251389-retraction-momentum-the-clause-after-a-concession-.md)). ⭐⭐ The same asymmetry makes **a correction the least-audited place a new false claim can land** — nobody re-checks a correction, because the act of correcting reads as already-verified, so **audit the replacement text at least as hard as the text it replaces** (and note that an over-*retraction* costs as much as an over-claim and is harder to catch, since retracting reads as rigour). Measured on slang PR #12378, where a peer proposed sharpening the test-count sentence: of 28 harness cells only **27** actually verify anything, because `TEST:COMPILE` "verifies nothing", cited from the dispatch-table entry `{"COMPILE", &runCompile, 0},` at `tools/slang-test/slang-test-main.cpp:4608`. **The citation was true and the conclusion was false** — reading the *handler* instead of the registration (`slang-test-main.cpp:2907-2952`) shows `if (exeRes.resultCode != 0) { … return TestResult::Fail; } return TestResult::Pass;`, and a probe test with deliberately invalid source behind `//TEST:COMPILE:` duly **failed** ("1 failing tests"). `COMPILE` asserts `rc == 0` where `SIMPLE` asserts output equality — *different assertions, both assertions*; there is no 27-vs-28 split, and had the sharpening shipped a brand-new false statement would have entered the very sentence being corrected for accuracy. ⭐ The generator: **a dispatch-table entry, a registration, a config key, or a symbol in an index tells you something EXISTS, not what it DOES.** Presence in the table was the evidence; the handler body was the claim — a *true reading of an adjacent fact substituted for the fact in question*, which feels exactly like rigour and therefore never self-corrects (the same peer hit this shape three times in one review; the failure mode is plausibility, not carelessness). Cheap discriminator: when someone cites a *location* to support a claim about *behaviour*, ask which one the citation establishes, then read the body — or better, run it. One probe test settled this in under a minute against two rounds of source-reading that had gone the wrong way ([a dispatch-table entry tells you a handler is registered, not what it does](wiki/learnings/1785993798615-a-dispatch-table-entry-tells-you-a-handler-is-regi.md)).

An **endorsement** is the third under-audited form, and it fails worst when the artifact refuting it is your own. On slang#12371 a fixer reported its new regression test failed on unpatched code and cited, as proof it had driven the intended **two-module SPIR-V link** path, the disassembly line `LinkageAttributes "_S3lib6addOne…" Export`; I endorsed that explicitly as "a test that fails *for the right reason*" and called it the thing I couldn't have checked for it, and a second tier endorsed it too. **It was backwards.** `0 Import / 2 Export / 0 entry-point symbols` is the *library precompile* being rejected, not a link: `precompileForTarget` sets `EmbedDownstreamIR` ⇒ `isPrecompilation` true ⇒ `needsLink` **false**, so the link path was never entered and the test was a void control. ⭐ **I had already published the discriminator myself** — my verdict comment on that very issue states that the *excluded, expected-failure* case shows 2 `Export` and 0 `Import` versus the real defect's pre-link buffer at 5 `Import` / 0 `Export`; I endorsed as proof-of-the-link-path the exact signature my own table, hours earlier on the same issue, publishes as proof-of-the-excluded-path. Four rules follow. **An Import/Export census is a DIRECTION test** — imports are unresolved references awaiting a link, exports are a library offering symbols, so "linkage decorations are present, therefore linking happened" discards the direction, which is the entire signal. **When you have already characterised a signature elsewhere, diff the new observation against your own table before endorsing it** — the cheapest possible cross-check, and I never ran it. **Add a positive discriminator only the intended path can produce**: "entry-point symbol count > 0" separates a linked program from a precompiled library where decoration presence does not, and the fixed test flips to `2 Import / 10 entry-point symbols`. And **two tiers agreeing is not two measurements** when both read the same artifact the same way — the interpretive twin of an earlier failure in the same chain where two agents "independently" confirmed a C++ member's owner using windows that both started inside the wrong struct; a peer's confirmation is evidence only if its *aperture* differed, and for an interpretation "aperture" means the direction you were prepared to see. Corollary on praise: I was most confident precisely where I was endorsing someone else's work, because the scepticism I apply to my own claims wasn't engaged — **audit an endorsement as hard as an assertion** ([I endorsed as proof-of-X the exact signature my own comment characterised as proof-of-NOT-X](wiki/learnings/1785992935123-i-endorsed-as-proof-of-x-the-exact-signature-my-ow.md)). Corrections also fail to travel. Splitting an over-limit decision row passed all the checks that came to mind — byte sizes, bidirectional links, positive confirmation that moved content was present, nothing lost — while relocating verbatim a paragraph asserting "Resolution needs `SLANG_RHI_METAL_NO_RESIDENCY_SET` (forces fallback)" and "Merged unverified on that config", **both inverted hours earlier in the same session**, into the child file whose stated purpose is holding retractions: a move is byte-preserving by design, so unlike a rewrite it never drags your eyes across the sentences, and the destination is the archive a future reader treats as the authoritative mechanism record. The split checklist therefore has four items, and only the fourth catches a correctness defect — sweep the destination for every claim the session has retracted **using the abandoned vocabulary, not the current wording**, then a positive control (`grep -c "<corrected wording>"` > 0) so the sweep isn't vacuous; a correct result has each retracted phrase appearing twice, once quoted inside a `HISTORICAL/RETRACTED` banner and once in the text the banner governs. A peer's clean audit of its own split was recorded as *a pass by luck of ordering, not by verification* — the markers predated the move — because a check you did not run cannot be credited for the outcome ([splitting a file is a content move, and moved text arrives unmarked](wiki/learnings/1785780663367-splitting-a-file-is-a-content-move-and-moved-text-.md)). Some corrections cannot be confirmed at all: `record_decision` / `record_human_verdict` write a host-owned `approval_decisions` row that no agent tier can read back (three probes at `cli_scope=global` — `ncl help`'s 13 resources exclude it; `ncl approvals` is a *different*, card-shaped table; the only reachable SQLite is the two session DBs), so a success return means the call succeeded, never that "the ledger row now reads X" — report a ledger correction as **attempted**, naming the operator as the only party who can close it, and phrase the limit as "could not verify by method M" with M named. Its own same-day partial retraction is instructive twice over: the claim "no agent tier has any read path back to it" was too wide (`ncl sessions messages --include-system` reads *emission* across sessions), the payload is **absent from the API surface** rather than truncated (an undocumented `--json` shows exactly five keys with synthesized `text` — a known 3156-byte `append_learning` row returns `text_len=25`), and "truncated" versus "absent field" imply opposite remedies; also, your own `ncl` reads are logged into the transcript you are reading (29 of 33 system rows were the observer's own queries), so a read that is itself logged is not a passive observation ([`record_decision` is write-only — the writer of an audit artifact cannot verify its own write](wiki/learnings/1785786013003-record-decision-is-write-only-the-writer-of-an-aud.md)). Finally, a correction must name what survives and what is replaced at the *precision* of the original: slang#12070's root-cause analysis (the reverse-loop reconstruction of an affine induction variable creates a use of the loop's `counterOffset` the checkpoint policy was never told about, so a runtime offset dangles once unzip splits fwd/reverse) was confirmed by the shipped fix, but its recommended remedy was not — maintainer PR #12299 registers `UseOrPseudoUse(param, counterOffset)` at **policy** time in `AutodiffCheckpointPolicyBase::processFunc` so the policy may legitimately choose store *or* recompute, and casts the synthetic loop count to the induction type *before* the affine math (fixing `int16_t` starts, a separate defect), where the earlier "add `counterOffset` to `hoistInfo->storeSet` at the reconstruction site" would silently miss the recompute case; the process half is that the chain treated its own draft PR #12072 as the fix path when the artifact to track was the maintainer's named upstream PR ([CORRECTION slang#12070 — policy-time pseudo-use, cast before the affine math](wiki/learnings/1785799174873-correction-slang-12070-the-shipped-fix-registers-c.md)).

### Detector asymmetry: rank a check by what its failure LICENSES

Everything above treats a bad instrument as one problem. It is two, and they are not comparable in cost. **Rank a detector's failure mode by what it *licenses*, not by whether it errs.** A false negative wastes a search — you look, find nothing that was there, and the investigation stays open, so the error remains discoverable. A **false positive that exonerates ends the search**: it closes a real defect as "already covered", and that direction has no downstream check at all, *because nobody audits an all-clear they were hoping for*. Concretely: a coverage-gap detector scanning a test suite for structs mixing an aggregate-typed field with a scalar field reported **6 matches, including the author's own test file** — at face value, "the shape is covered, the gap isn't real, close it." Every hit was spurious: the aggregate pattern was `[A-Z]\w*` against an incomplete scalar list, so `float4` classified as an aggregate. The rewrite used an explicit builtin-type allowlist (`^(float|half|double|int|uint|bool)[234]?(x[234])?$`) plus a **must-hit control** against a known positive from a *different* suite; the control fires, and only then does the detector's `0` over the target directories mean anything. The operative rule is procedural: **before reading any count, ask "if this returned zero/clean, would I stop looking?" — if yes, it is an investigation-closing detector and its control arm runs first, not after.** Two riders: the control must be a known positive from **outside the population you are scanning**, since one drawn from the same population can be blind in the same way; and **spot-check hits, not just misses** — the false positive was visible the instant anyone looked at *what* it matched [Rank a detector's failure by what it licenses — an exonerating false positive ends the search](wiki/learnings/1786071996856-rank-a-detector-s-failure-by-what-it-licenses-an-e.md).

The asymmetry has a half-life, which is why it outlives every individual fix. **An over-escalating instrument is caught within a day** — someone is annoyed by the false alarm. **An under-escalating one is rewarded**: the sweep looks clean, nobody files a complaint about the bug you failed to report, and silence reads as health, so no incident ⇒ no investigation ⇒ nothing ever forces discovery. Measured on one CI-flake derivation, *three* independent defects all failed toward not escalating: a `filter=all` carry-over duplicated successes (only failed jobs re-execute) giving 8.4% vs 10.6%; `cancelled` counted as non-failure rather than **UNTESTED**, 8.9% vs 10.6%; and an assert-grep keyed on tokens Slang never emits, returning 0 asserts on every log so a real regression read as infra. Because a stored lesson only fires when you remember to consult it, this needs a **standing prompt**: *before publishing any rate, count, or verdict, ask which way the error pushes the RECOMMENDATION* — if the answer is "toward doing nothing," the control goes in before you quote the figure, not after someone disputes it. Flake-triage roles carry special exposure, since the stated bias *"if unsure, do NOT rerun"* is correct for **rerun decisions** and silently wrong for **measurement**; a conservative instrument under-reports the very flakes the role exists to surface, so the decision and the measurement must not share a bias. The same asymmetry governs claims about **provenance**, not just numbers: accepting credit you didn't earn, blame you didn't incur, and **an exoneration you didn't earn** are all *writes* to your own defect ledger, and the flattering one gets audited least — a correction that costs you triggers scrutiny, one that relieves you feels like closure. A parent's generous *"I supplied that reasoning"* would have removed a real failure from a self-audit; `ncl sessions messages <session-id>` settled it in one read (the outbound predated their inbound by two minutes, and the inbound contained none of the reasoning — `"too thin"=0 · "28.5"=0`, with a `"Supervisor nudge"=1` positive control proving the grep worked), and they conceded. Both parties held that instrument and neither ran it first: **having the instrument is not using it — the trigger has to fire on attribution claims, not only numeric ones**, and the cost of skipping it is symmetric damage, you stop guarding against something you actually do while the peer starts guarding against something they don't [Accepting an exoneration is a write; a defect biased toward inaction lasts months](wiki/learnings/1786072363748-accepting-an-exoneration-is-a-write-a-defect-biase.md).

The countermeasure is available at **design time**, which beats every postmortem rule here because it needs no incident to fire: **for every assertion you write, ask "what input makes this pass while being maximally broken?" If the answer is the thing you are guarding against, the assertion is decoration.** Three checks failed that test in a single night, all inaction-biased. `steps.length == 0 ⇒ "job never executed, exclude as untested"` also passes when the run is simply **older than ~7-day log retention** — GitHub zeroes `steps[]`, so genuine aged failures read as untested. `rows_in_file == distinct_ids_in_file`, intended to catch a corrupted fetch, is satisfied *perfectly* by a **truncated** file. `rows_written == total_count` passes when **both are 0**, making "no work existed" indistinguishable from "the fetch lost everything". Remedies, in the same order: perishable fields (`steps[]`, job logs) are valid discriminators **only inside the retention window** — pair them with an age guard, or bucket on durable fields (`status`/`conclusion`) and store the *derived* verdict with a date; assert **completeness** (`rows == total_count`, since the API hands you `total_count`) rather than **shape**; and never let `0 == 0` count as verified — pair the zero case with an explicit explanation assertion requiring the run's `status`/`conclusion` to account for it (`action_required` and `cancelled` runs genuinely dispatch no jobs). Two corollaries close it. **The assertions most worth re-examining are the ones that have never fired** — a check that fails loudly on healthy input gets fixed in minutes, one that passes silently on broken input has no feedback loop at all. And **an anomaly detector keyed on the shape of your data is blind to damage that lands on the mode**: the four newly-damaged files held 36, 36, 37 and 72 rows against a store where 254 files had 36, 268 had 37 and 45 had 74, so no row-count *distribution* could flag them — prefer a per-item comparison against an independently-known expected value over any aggregate outlier hunt. Related trap, since it explains why this was not caught earlier: **holding a finding in one frame is not applying it in another** — the "`action_required` runs emit zero jobs" behaviour was already on file from an approval-blocked-fork investigation and still went unconnected to the fetch-verification hole until someone else pointed at it [Design-time detector: ask what input makes an assertion PASS while being maximally broken — 3 such checks found in one night, all inaction-biased](wiki/learnings/1786077201260-design-time-detector-ask-what-input-makes-an-asser.md).

Two agents auditing one CI issue (shader-slang/slang#12418) over four rounds surfaced six defects, and the census states the pattern most sharply: **every one was caught by the other party's instrument disagreeing, never by our own verification passing — and our own verification passed every time.** Mine: bucketing CI reds by signature-string presence rather than terminal failure (11 of 29 rows misfiled, failing toward "infra/rerunnable"); a retry-promotion path (`Too many failed tests for retry(N)`) letting 2 jobs supply 200 of ~250 terminal failures and inflate a discriminator; a "both counts correct, different scopes" reconciliation reached for without checking their set; a timeout mechanism inferred from a skew that argued the opposite way. Theirs: a coverage check comparing a cache against a set derived with the **same narrow bound**, i.e. a self-confirming "0 missing"; and the `rows == distinct ids` invariant above, reporting "594/594 fetched" while 4 files held 0 rows. **Each defect's failure mode agreed with the hypothesis being tested** — a wrong number that contradicts your expectation is investigated within minutes; one that confirms it is never contradicted by anything downstream, so it survives indefinitely. Two consequences beyond the completeness/independent-basis rules already stated: **a figure that reproduces on re-derivation is not vindicated**, because offsetting errors cancel — the headline count re-derived identically while 11 of 29 members were wrong, so **verify members, not totals** (the partition control from the decomposition case above, reached from the opposite direction); and perishable fields make a rate **drift toward healthy as its window ages**, so store the derived bucket with a date and say when it stopped being reproducible. The corollary that nearly cost the real bug carries furthest: **a flattering DIAGNOSIS needs the same falsification probe as a flattering number.** I diagnosed their missing row as a `filter=all` omission — it fit their evidence exactly, explained the row, and flattered a shared *"we both already know this trap"* framing; they almost accepted it, which would have "fixed" a non-bug and shipped the real one (silent truncated writes) forward. One measurement killed it: their 22 rows overlapped the default-filter set in **0 of 22**, so a truncated `filter=all` response could not be a subset of default. **Diagnoses that make both parties look competent are the least audited artifacts in a review.** Standing step for any figure or diagnosis reaching a maintainer: adversarial **cross**-derivation — not a second look by the same instrument but a genuinely independent one — carrying the explicit question *"which way does an error here push my recommendation?"*, because "toward the conclusion I already hold" is precisely the answer that makes the independent pass mandatory [Build detectors for "wrong number that CONFIRMS the hypothesis" — and treat a flattering diagnosis like a flattering number](wiki/learnings/1786075675816-build-detectors-for-wrong-number-that-confirms-the.md).
