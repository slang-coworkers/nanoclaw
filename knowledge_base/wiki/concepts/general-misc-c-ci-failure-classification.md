## TL;DR

Classifying a CI red correctly means separating *what actually happened* from *what the envelope says*. The recurring errors: attributing a platform outage to your diff, reading a merge-queue resting state as a stall, inventing phantom reds through a wrong dedup, and trusting a monitor that goes blind exactly when the thing it watches is happening.

- **When N unrelated workflows fail at the same minute, check `githubstatus.com` before diagnosing your diff.** One `curl` retires many separate investigations. Simultaneity is evidence of a common cause.
- **`cancelled`/`steps=0`/`skipped` ≠ `failed`.** A cancelled or never-started job is *untested*, not broken. Read the JOB conclusion, not the run-level `ci_failed` envelope.
- **`mergeable_state=blocked` and multi-hour master gaps are BASELINE in a merge-queue repo.** To prove a check blocks merges, find a merge that happened with X red.
- **`checks_timed_out` ≠ `failed_checks`** — nothing ran, so a requeue re-enters the same stalled queue.
- **Dedup on FAILING-only check-runs fails open** (a later success is invisible); dedup keyed on job-name-alone or missing `workflow_id` for successes fails closed (phantom reds).
- **Bucket a failure ratio by the recovery boundary** — a whole-window ratio blends two regimes and reads as measured.
- **During an outage, prove rerun futility with the QUEUE (median wait), not the status page** — cheap GitHub-hosted checks recover first, self-hosted GPU legs last.
- **A monitor running on the resource it measures is self-suppressing;** alarm on queue AGE, not depth.
- **`started_at` is written at scheduling, not execution** — gate a resume on `status == "in_progress"`.

## Suspect the platform before your diff

Four `github.ci_failed` webhooks on one SHA, each a different check-suite, three looking like unrelated one-offs and the fourth (`Verify PR Labels`) plausibly the bot's own responsibility — root cause was none of them: GitHub Actions was in a **major outage** opened at 15:22:49Z, every affected run postdating it. The tell read too late: three workflows all reported job conclusion **`cancelled`** at the same minute, and `cancelled` is not `failed` — nobody cancelled them and a diff cannot cancel a job. **When several independent workflows fail the same way in the same minute, suspect the PLATFORM before your change** (`curl -sf githubstatus.com/api/v2/summary.json`); read the JOB conclusion, never the run-level `ci_failed` envelope; and don't rerun/rebase/"fix" against outage-era runs — they are artifacts. Webhook delivery is itself delayed during an incident, so arrival order ≠ event order. See [When N unrelated workflows fail at the same minute, check githubstatus.com before diagnosing your diff](../learnings/1786041200195-when-n-unrelated-workflows-fail-at-the-same-minute.md) and the status-page-first + self-blind-monitor combination in [A monitor on the shared pool goes blind exactly when it matters — check the platform status page first](../learnings/1786055621825-a-monitor-on-the-shared-pool-goes-blind-exactly-wh.md).

The `run:`-echo trap (grepping a marker that is also a workflow-source literal — folded under [GitHub CLI/API instrument traps](general-misc-c-github-cli-api-traps.md)) is a specific case of misreading a log: an `echo "Error: downloaded clang-format is not a valid binary"` line was the *fallback arm* of a version-check block, echoed as source, and read as an infra flake when the real failure was a genuine clang-format diff — a rerun could never have gone green. Strip echoed lines before classifying, and confirm a match is a *consequence* (a following `##[error]`, a diff) not a *declaration*. A `test-compile-regression` job emitting **1732 PASS and 1732 FAIL, exactly 1:1**, pins the defect to the environment (a broken validator) — grep the *emitted bytes*, not a retyped compact form.

## The outage kill signatures, and proving rerun futility

Jobs dying at `Getting action download info` with `Failed to resolve action download info. Error: Service Unavailable` is a **control-plane outage**, not a per-PR flake — do not rerun while it is live (the rerun dies at the same step and burns the daily cap). Verify liveness with two instruments (the *incident* JSON, not just the component enum, whose `updated_at` lags), and prove futility non-speculatively with a **sibling retry already fired during the outage** that re-failed. Onset is a *regime change, not a rate*: bucket a workflow's runs chronologically (71/71 green then 5/5 failed from 15:43Z) — a 5/76 ratio would read as noise. Two twins-14s-apart give the cleanest transient proof: same workflow/event/sha, one succeeded and one failed at action resolution ⇒ transient by construction. Same job name + different EVENT can give opposite results (`reuse-compliance-check` failed on `push` while succeeding on `pull_request`). Attribution: a rising attempt count is not evidence you acted — check `triggering_actor` *per attempt* (`nv-slang-bot[bot]` is shared; the **timestamp** discriminates). See [Suppress reruns during a live GitHub Actions control-plane outage — prove futility with an in-outage retry](../learnings/1786034105669-suppress-reruns-during-a-live-github-actions-contr.md).

By ~90 minutes later that futility proof is **stale**, and the naive re-probes disagree: the status page said `major_outage` (component lagged ~2h) while a green-sample probe showed recovered runs — but the greens were **survivor bias**, the few cheap jobs that squeezed through. The measurement that decides it is the **queue**: 103 attempts queued, median wait 51 min, oldest 75 min, and nothing terminal completing except lightweight checks. A green sibling proves that job ran, not that the fleet recovered; cheap GitHub-hosted checks recover first, self-hosted GPU legs last. Absence of fresh failures during an outage is the absence of *completions*, not health — bucket `status` before `conclusion`. See [Prove rerun futility with the QUEUE, not the status page — measure median wait](../learnings/1786040521348-prove-rerun-futility-with-the-queue-not-the-status.md). The Slang outage kill signature: `filter` job cancelled with `steps=0` → downstream `skipped` → aggregator `abandoned`; infra-killed jobs return `steps: []` *and* 404 on logs, so a "name the first failed step" procedure returns nothing (the real reason lives only at `.../check-runs/<id>/annotations`, e.g. "the hosted runner lost communication").

## Merge-queue: baseline states and eviction reasons

`mergeable_state=blocked` and a multi-hour master gap are **baseline** in a merge-queue repo, and neither proves a stall — killed by three controls: 4 of 5 sampled unrelated PRs were *also* `blocked` (the default resting state); the master-commit gap distribution had median 2.89h / max 40h with 10 of 29 gaps over the "suspicious" 3.9h; and the decisive one — **a PR merged with `Check Submodule Pointers: failure` on its own queue entry** (4 of 7 did), which settles "is it required?" as no amount of correlation can. To prove check X blocks merges, find a merge that happened with X red. See [`mergeable_state=blocked` and a multi-hour master gap are BASELINE in a merge-queue repo — neither proves a stall](../learnings/1786006814776-mergeable-state-blocked-and-a-multi-hour-master-ga.md).

A `failed_checks` eviction **consumes** the auto-merge that was on — so post-eviction `autoMergeRequest == null` means it was *cleared by the eviction*, not never set, and nothing will auto-requeue. Print all three fields (`autoMergeRequest`, `mergeQueueEntry`, the timeline events) and let them choose the disposition; never infer from the elapsed gap. A "benign-now mislabel is a scheduled failure": a bar labelled "auto-recovery" invites the next sweep to keep waiting, while "manual re-add owed" forces an act — record the corrected disposition in the durable per-PR ledger, not just a status message. See [Merge-queue "auto-recovery window" is only valid while auto-merge survives the eviction](../learnings/1786004759300-merge-queue-auto-recovery-window-is-only-valid-whi.md).

`checks_timed_out` needs the **opposite** reading from `failed_checks`: `failed_checks` → something ran and failed → classify it; `checks_timed_out` → **nothing ran** → no failure to classify, and a requeue re-enters the same stalled queue. During the outage PR #12348 was evicted `checks_timed_out` with *zero failing checks* while its merge-group run sat `queued` with zero jobs started for 4h44m. Pull `reason` from the timeline, then check whether the merge-group run ever started jobs. A subtlety: a *new* real eviction landing 70 minutes after a prior sweep's write is not a reversal of that sweep's (correct) phantom finding — **diff the event timestamp against the prior write time** before treating a new-vs-stored disagreement as a defect. See [Merge-queue checks_timed_out: an outage evicts PRs with zero failing checks](../learnings/1786054584563-merge-queue-checks-timed-out-an-outage-evicts-prs-.md).

## Phantom reds: dedup that fails open, and dedup that fails closed

Two opposite dedup defects:

- **Failing-only dedup fails OPEN.** Filtering check-runs to the failing ones then deduping newest-wins hides a *later success* under the same key — a check that fails and then succeeds 16 seconds later survives as "live red." Group **every** `status == "completed"` check-run under `(workflow_id, event, name)`, sort by `completed_at`, then ask whether the newest is a failure. (33 reds → 30 after the fix; three pure phantoms.) The filter reads as an optimization and the bug is silent and directional — it only invents work. Tie-break: byte-identical `completed_at` with different conclusions ⇒ flag AMBIGUOUS. [Deduping only FAILING check-runs fails open — a later success must suppress the red](../learnings/1786040488771-deduping-only-failing-check-runs-fails-open-a-late.md)
- **Missing `workflow_id` for successes fails CLOSED.** Resolving `workflow_id` only for runs containing a failure gives every sibling success `wf="UNKNOWN"`, a *different* group key, so the failure survives dedup. Resolve `workflow_id` per **sha** (`actions/runs?head_sha=<full-40>`), not per run (looping `actions/runs/<id>` over 1384 ids blew a 10-min timeout). Emit a sentinel (`UNRESOLVED-<id>`) for unmapped runs and print the count. A fabricated red costs classification time and *looks like diligence*; catch it by checking whether the thing the check complains about is actually true at HEAD (a "missing label" phantom had the label present all along). [Phantom-red dedup needs workflow_id for ALL runs on the sha, not just failing ones](../learnings/1786026601586-phantom-red-dedup-needs-workflow-id-for-all-runs-o.md)

## Bucket a ratio by the state change, not the sweep window

A drain/reimage recommendation rested on `test-compile-regression 4/11` and `test-falcor 4/21` — both arithmetically correct, both useless, because the window straddled a host recovery at 21:28Z (pre: 4 fail/1 cancelled; post: 7 success, zero crossings). A ratio across a state change reports a *blend* of two regimes and reads as measured. Before quoting a failure ratio, sort the rows and look for a run of same-outcome rows at one end; a remediation ask needs a *current-state* window, not a sweep-length one. Cross-PR spread discriminates code-vs-not-code but says nothing about whether the state persists. The second half fell to a per-failure cause check: all 4 `test-falcor` failures were one tracked flake (`test_GBufferRTTexGrads_d3d12` + `3221225477`), and the supposedly-clean control host produced the same signature 20 minutes later — a control host is only clean until it isn't. No outcome check catches this class; the window silently encodes a state that no longer exists. See [A whole-window failure ratio averages across a state change — bucket by the recovery boundary, not the sweep window](../learnings/1785983982985-a-whole-window-failure-ratio-averages-across-a-sta.md).

## Self-blind monitors and the fields that lie

**A monitor running on the contended resource it measures is self-suppressing.** `ci-health.yml` reverted to the hosted pool it reports on, producing a 6h37m publish gap (prior max 90 min) and 69% of wall-clock unobserved over 7 days — biased 5–8× toward blindness during congestion, so any "it was quiet this week" claim is near-worthless. Alarm on **queue AGE, not depth**: depth 70 was only the 95th percentile (all-time max 998, two zombie runs queued 70+ days), while median queued age was 267 min. `success` does not mean *timely* (a 1.8-min job waited 155 min in queue). Same story from the "starved vs never-minted" angle: same stale-feed symptom, two mechanisms — present in `queued` ⇒ starved (move off the pool); absent from both queues ⇒ never minted (the event throttle reached cron), and no pool change helps. **A remedy for mechanism A is not evidence about symptom S when mechanism B produces the same S** — verify the mechanism a fix targets is the one currently firing before crediting it. A frozen feed makes a threshold alarm self-repeating on identical input; gate the vote on frame age. See [A monitor on the shared pool goes blind exactly when it matters — check the platform status page first](../learnings/1786055621825-a-monitor-on-the-shared-pool-goes-blind-exactly-wh.md) and [Starved vs never-minted: same stale-feed symptom, different mechanism (and the false fix-credit it nearly caused)](../learnings/1786056886197-starved-vs-never-minted-same-stale-feed-symptom-di.md).

**`started_at` is written at scheduling, not execution** — a job stuck in `queued` still has it populated, so a resume trigger keyed on `started_at` fires into the outage it was meant to avoid. Gate on `status == "in_progress"`, which cannot be forged that way. This belongs to a family of GitHub API fields that read as one thing and are another: `user.type != "Bot"` (board-sync posts as `User`), `runs/<id>/jobs` conclusion (latest attempt only — a failed attempt 1 is invisible; use `attempts/<n>/jobs`, tell is `run_attempt > 1`), and a check-**suite** `conclusion=failure` sitting above zero failing check-runs. **Before gating on any field, ask what WRITES it and when — not what it is named.** See [A field named like a state is not a test for that state](../learnings/1786047663183-a-field-named-like-a-state-is-not-a-test-for-that-.md).
