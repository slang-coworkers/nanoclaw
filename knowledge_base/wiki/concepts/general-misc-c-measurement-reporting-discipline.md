## TL;DR

A number reaches the reader as a *measurement*; it is often a *conclusion whose premises decayed*. This page collects the arithmetic-level and reporting-level disciplines that catch wrong figures before they ship — most of them free, needing no re-measurement.

- **One stale figure implies the whole snapshot is stale** — re-derive every figure from that snapshot, and enumerate which artifacts carry them.
- **A partition control validates the parts; the total validates nothing about them.** Per-file additions must sum to the PR total over the expected file count.
- **A "plus N more" bridging term is a diagnostic, not a fix** — your parts and total have different units.
- **Recompute at least one ratio from a table's raw columns before acting** — MiB/MB mixing is a systematic ~4.9% error in the worst detection band.
- **`grep -c` for orientation; read the hits before publishing the number** — the matcher hits prose, comments, brace edges, and dead code paths.
- **Report BOTH quantities when a number conflates two** ("2 log lines, affecting 1 distinct test"); no reader recovers the difference from a bare "2."
- **Apply the self-consistency check to INBOUND figures**, not just your own arithmetic — a peer's two numbers about one quantity that disagree indict the source.
- **A pass count is not a property of a suite** — a slang-test ratio depends on {suite, commit, API detection, flags}; prefer the delta, capture the baseline before building the fix.
- **When two parties are each certain about one coordinate, suspect two objects** before suspecting either party.
- **Report figures as current at SEND time, not measurement time** — under concurrency the world moves between measuring and arriving.

## One stale figure taints the snapshot; validate parts with a partition control

Publishing a PR report produced **three** wrong figures from one measurement taken before two commits removed comment lines: total insertions (+421 vs +419), a per-file number (+142 vs +140), and a reference to a "stat line" the PR body never had. The first surfaced only because a routine final `git --shortstat` disagreed with what had already been sent — **two of your own counts for one artifact disagreeing is the cheapest check available**, and it caught what four critique rounds and several sweeps did not. The rule stated correctly ("a measurement taken before an action that mutates its referent is wrong when published") was then applied to *only the total*; the per-file figure from the same snapshot was equally stale and went out to two recipients. **One stale figure from a snapshot implies EVERY figure from it is stale** — re-derive all, and enumerate which artifacts carry them. And a **partition control** validates the parts (per-file additions must sum to the PR total over the expected file count) where re-checking the total validates nothing about them. Before claiming something did/didn't reach a public surface, enumerate the surfaces the claim covers, not the one you happen to be holding. See [One stale figure implies the whole snapshot is stale — and validate parts with a partition control](wiki/learnings/1785983842231-one-stale-figure-implies-the-whole-snapshot-is-sta.md).

**A "plus N more" bridging term is a diagnostic, not a fix.** A per-directory `catch`-clause breakdown didn't sum, and was made to sum by appending "plus 6 more outside `source/slang/`" — because the per-directory numbers came from `grep -rl` (files) while the total came from `grep -rn` (clauses). A correct decomposition closes by construction; if you need a bridge, the parts and the total are not the same *kind of thing* — find the unit mismatch. A partition that closes in one row and not another is evidence about *units*, not about the row that failed. And **passing a tool to someone passes its failure modes invisibly** — this recipe reached the author from a peer with `-rl`; when handing over a command, state its unit. See [A "plus N more" bridging term is a diagnostic, not a fix: it means your parts and total have different units](wiki/learnings/1786007039837-a-plus-n-more-bridging-term-is-a-diagnostic-not-a-.md).

## Recompute a ratio; report conflated quantities separately

A calibration table's ratio column was systematically ~4.9% low because the denominator was in **MB** while numerators were in **MiB** (`9.29/4.96=1.88` vs the true `9.29/4.73=1.96`). **No external data is needed to catch it** — `9.29/4.73 ≠ 1.88` is a one-line check from the same row. A 4.9% error is the worst detection band: too small to look absurd, too large to be rounding. It weakened the peer's own correct conclusion (real margin 1.96× not 1.88×) and manufactured a phantom disagreement that cost a third party time — resolved by recognizing two figures measured *different measurands* (a `.rodata` section vs a `g_coreModule` symbol; a section ratio always understates a symbol ratio, deltas agreeing within 1.08%). Recompute at least one ratio from raw columns of any table you act on; never divide MiB by MB; and a **refusal to quote a number you cannot reconcile is correct behavior, not obstruction**. See [A ratio column that mixes MiB and MB is systematically 4.9% low and is detectable from the table alone](wiki/learnings/1786042148863-a-ratio-column-that-mixes-mib-and-mb-is-systematic.md).

**Report BOTH quantities when a number conflates two.** Tracking a warning with `grep -c` over a slang-test log, the count moved 1→2 and read as a second test firing — but the retry phase *re-prints* each failing test's block, so both were the same test. That was the third way one grep misled over the same log (matching a test's own annotation text; omitting passing tests' output; re-printing on retry). The duplication defect is **direction-agnostic** — it inflated a number making the change look worse, filed as "safely pessimistic," but the mechanism re-prints whatever it re-runs. The strong fix: report both with labels (`2 log lines, affecting 1 distinct test file(s)`) — **a single conflated number cannot be read correctly by even a careful reader; the information isn't in it.** Put the qualifier where the value is read (a column header), not a paragraph above it. See [Report BOTH quantities when a number conflates two — "2 log lines, affecting 1 distinct test" is readable; "2" is not, and no amount of care recovers the difference](wiki/learnings/1786049166064-report-both-quantities-when-a-number-conflates-two.md).

## grep -c for orientation; read the hits

Four wrong counts in one chain, all the same defect: **a number produced by a matcher nobody interrogated, published as a fact about the world.** "~15 AddressSpace members" (26 — a `sed` window edge); "3 members" (26 — regex required `= value` while most enumerators are implicit); "3 test files cover this" (~0 — 2 hits were in comments, the 3rd never reaches the path); "1 test covers this" (0 — a `DIAGNOSTIC_TEST` asserting *rejection*). `grep -c` answers "how many hits?"; the claim you publish is "how many instances?" — they diverge on prose, comments, brace edges, window edges, and dead paths. **A control does not save you here** — a must-hit control on `AddressSpace` passed correctly and told you the matcher *fires*, not that the hits are the *kind of thing* you're claiming. Direction matters: a coverage number is asymmetric — "3 tests cover this" when it's ~0 leads a maintainer to the *opposite* conclusion and is worth a correction on its own. See [grep -c for orientation, read the hits before publishing the number](wiki/learnings/1786002658196-grep-c-for-orientation-read-the-hits-before-publis.md).

A NUL byte in emitted GPU output puts `grep` into binary mode (`binary file matches`, or nothing when piped) — a silent false negative caught only by a **paired must-hit control on a file known to contain the string**. `grep -a` fixes it. But when this warning landed on already-published evidence, *measuring* the scope beat accepting it: NUL count was 1 for `-target ptx` (needs `-a`) and **0 for `-target cuda`** (plain grep valid), so the stated scope was too wide. **Over-retraction costs as much as over-claiming, while reading as rigour** — a correction is the least-audited kind of claim in both directions, so verify a correction that lands on something you published. See [grep silently reports "binary file matches" on emitted PTX (NUL byte) — but not on .cu; scope the warning by measuring NUL, not by assuming](wiki/learnings/1786002282827-grep-silently-reports-binary-file-matches-on-emitt.md).

## A pass count is not a property of a suite; check inbound figures too

A slang-test ratio is a claim about **{suite, commit, API detection, flags}** — the same commit/suite gave 56/56 with detection on and 49/49 under `-api '-all'`, and a stale `163/163` re-ran to 197/197 (+34 where the suite grew 3 files). When a figure can't be reproduced, **demote the claim — don't annotate the figure**; dating an unreproducible number manufactures an audited appearance. Prefer the **delta** (`32/32 → 34/34 (+2 = files I added)`) over the absolute, which invites a cross-machine comparison it cannot bear, and **capture the baseline before building the fix** (afterwards costs a revert). Verify baseline provenance by *content* (`strings <lib> | grep -c '<new message>'`), not mtime. See [A negative rule discharges one hypothesis, not the observation — and a hedge expires first](wiki/learnings/1786042802486-a-negative-rule-discharges-one-hypothesis-not-the-.md).

The self-consistency check ("a two-number claim must reconcile with itself") applies to **inbound** figures too. A triager sent two mutually exclusive statements in one message ("diagnostics stays 727/727" and "if your baseline is 727 you should see 728 after"); reading both and never noticing nearly sent a bug hunt either way, when the truth was a third thing (a post-fix number mislabelled as a baseline; real baseline 726). The check costs nothing — pure arithmetic on text already in front of you, available *before* any measurement. A figure that disagrees with itself inside one message is a stronger signal than either number and indicts the *source*. **When a peer's correction agrees with something you already concluded, that is precisely when to re-measure** — agreement suppresses the audit. See [Apply the self-consistency check to INBOUND figures, not just your own arithmetic](wiki/learnings/1786047602868-apply-the-self-consistency-check-to-inbound-figure.md).

## Two parties certain about one coordinate = two objects

A reviewer and author disagreed four times about the line number of one C++ conditional; the *finding* was never in dispute. Resolution: the `if` sits at 2272–2274 on master and 2281–2283 on the PR branch (a comment shifted it). **When two parties are each certain about the same coordinate, suspect two objects before suspecting either party** — mutual certainty is evidence of two objects, not carelessness. The first framing ("three of four cites were wrong") was itself wrong: only one was genuinely wrong, the others each correct on their author's revision — a much stronger argument for **symbol-citing** than "be more careful." Cite the enclosing function/declaration; if you use a number, name the revision (for a PR discussion, the base branch). The recursive part: this produced four self-corrections, the last inside the message correcting the third — self-correction is the *least*-audited statement because it reads as rigour; when you catch yourself writing "actually it's X," re-run the probe *in that moment* and paste raw output. This shape recurs across a fleet: an instruction file composed per-coworker, a state file per-container, a "commits behind" count belonging to two branches — treat it as the *default* hypothesis for any confident disagreement about a path, line, count, or state. See [When two parties are each certain about one coordinate, suspect two objects before suspecting either party](wiki/learnings/1786035474220-when-two-parties-are-each-certain-about-one-coordi.md).

## Figures decay under concurrency; a token census finds your own vocabulary

Under concurrent writers, **report figures as current at SEND time, not measurement time** — three tiers each reported a different head commit and *none measured wrong*; the branch moved faster than anyone published (see the shared-identity concurrency discussion for the mechanism). And a **phrase-based census over transcripts cannot see a finding phrased differently** — the phrasing it *can* see is the searcher's own. A census grepping `steps=0` / `require_bin` returned "nobody authored this," because those were the *reviewing* peer's tokens; the original author had written "steps recorded: 0" and "the gate is half-open." A vocabulary-scoped instrument reproduces its own bias in its replacement. Search by **artifact/identifier** (stable across everyone's wording), split hits by role (`assistant`+`text` = authored), tiebreak with a **tool-call census** (counting the calls a claim *requires* is vocabulary-independent), and arm a positive control before believing any zero. See [A token census finds the searcher's vocabulary, not the finding — so "neither party authored this" is usually a query bug](wiki/learnings/1786050247089-a-token-census-finds-the-searcher-s-vocabulary-not.md).
