## TL;DR

- A merge is evidence about **adoption**, not about a finding's **truth**. Score finding-correctness (on evidence, immune to what shipped) and outcome/adoption in separate columns; a merge only touches the first column when a human *verifiably engaged* the finding and rejected it on its merits.
- "Not acted on" requires the **merge-queue timeline**, not thread state — unresolved bot threads, a self-merge, and a stale APPROVE are all two-way compatible and carry no bits about engagement.
- A blocking finding that names a **mechanism at file:line** is a hand-off, not an obstacle; when the author's next commit fixes *that mechanism*, it is the strongest confirmation the call was right. Reversing your own verdict on a new revision is the procedure working — a resolved 🔴 must not round forward any more than a clean R1 rounds forward to excuse a dirty R2.
- Verify a fixed BLOCK against the artifact that **carries the invariant**, not the prior revision's probe — a correct fix can leave the old probe's answer unchanged.
- The worst observability state is not "our finding is invisible" but "the visible claim points the other way." Check the *direction* of the public footprint, and route a mismatch to whoever legitimately owns posting.
- A **waived** policy predicate must not record "pass" — sharing a status value with verified passes inflates the evidence count and biases toward approval; give it `not_applicable`/`unevaluable`.
- A **retraction is a claim** and needs the same test as the thing it retracts — an over-corrected retraction installs a new false claim, and its self-punishing direction stops anyone auditing it.
- A **provenance label** ("their finding, not verified by me") protects your credibility, not the decision; if an unverified claim is load-bearing, verify it or name it as the thing to check.
- A correction is not done until you re-grep the retracted wording across **every artifact** (including machine-readable blocks); and a leaf written while an approve still stands inherits that approve's blind spot.
- Retracting a conclusion means losing the right to cite its inputs as corroboration — the population *is* the claim.

## A merge scores adoption, not correctness

The keystone is [A merge is evidence about ADOPTION, not about a finding's TRUTH](../learnings/1786387600782-approver-human-disagreement-retraction-split-a-mer.md): scoring a true finding as a LOSS because a self-merge happened 71 minutes later "does not measure correctness; it measures adoption," and a store trained that way learns to suppress correct findings that arrive near a merge — the ones with least time to be adopted. Keep two columns, never collapsed: finding-correctness (TRUE/FALSE on evidence alone) and outcome/adoption (ACTED ON / NOT with the reason). A merge touches the first only when a human *verifiably* engaged and rejected on merits — check thread `isResolved`/`resolvedBy`, whether any approval postdates the finding, and who merged. The follow-up [Not acted on needs the MERGE-QUEUE timeline, not thread state](../learnings/1786389600072-approver-critique-mustfix-not-acted-on-needs-the-m.md) is the correction *to that same PR*: an `AddedToMergeQueueEvent` 18 min before the decision (and 76 min after the last finding, with all threads visible) meant engagement was UNKNOWN, not "demonstrably absent" — every negative-engagement signal was two-way compatible. Ask of each signal *could it have come out otherwise?*; state the actionable-window claim at the strength evidence supports; and fix a flagged claim as a **class**, re-grepped across every artifact (a reviewer's line numbers are a sample, not the set). The meta-lesson: all three errors were over-reading evidence in the direction that flattered the finding.

## Mechanism-named blocks, and reversing your own verdict

[A BLOCK that names a mechanism at file:line got fixed in 4 hours — and reversing to approve is correct, not inconsistent](../learnings/1786399044605-approver-calibration-slang-rhi-826-a-block-that-na.md) draws two calibration lessons: a block naming a mechanism ("144 Vulkan device-creation failures after this teardown call at file:line, preceded by these loader warnings") is actionable where "CI is red" only triggers a re-run, and the author's targeted 43-line fix landing *on that mechanism* is the strongest confirmation the call was right. And reversing your own verdict on a new revision is the revision-chain rule working in both directions — "rounding a resolved 🔴 forward is exactly as wrong as rounding an unresolved one down." What makes an R2 approve safe rather than relieved: verify the *failure signature* disappeared (not just that CI went green), read the lifetime from source (a "retain longer" fix has exactly three failure modes — double-free, leak, destruction-order — check all three), and know what green cannot see (the leak detector was compiled out, so zero leak lines is not evidence). The related trap in [A fixed BLOCK can be verified by the WRONG artifact](../learnings/1786400603165-approver-challenger-miss-a-fixed-block-can-be-veri.md): re-running the prior revision's probe (still exactly two trigger sites) read as "BLOCK stands," but the fix moved the eager/lazy boundary, not the triggers — the crash was structurally impossible at R4. When re-gating a revision that fixes your own BLOCK, re-derive which artifact carries the invariant; the tell is a probe result identical across a fix that demonstrably worked.

## Observability direction, and waived predicates

[The worst observability state is not "our finding is invisible" — it is "the visible claim points the other way"](../learnings/1786406423355-approver-false-safe-the-worst-observability-state-.md): on a public-header PR, the only public footprint was a bot review asserting the *exact inference the approver had retracted* ("source- and ABI-compatible"), so a maintainer would see an authoritative wrong claim and no counter-evidence. Ask not "did we leave a footprint?" but "what does the footprint that exists say?" — a visible claim contradicting your finding is *adversarial*, not merely empty, and should be routed (not merely recorded) to whoever owns posting. A quieter corpus-scale defect is [A waived policy predicate must not record "pass"](../learnings/1786409832561-approver-clause-gap-a-waived-policy-predicate-must.md): `eval-clauses.py` recorded a never-run `ci_green_on_sha` as `pass` under `require_ci_green: false`, so 257 of 269 decisions summed a vacuous pass into their evidence count — failing toward *more* approval. A predicate that was never evaluated must never share a status value with one that passed; read a clause's evidence string, not just its status, and measure such an audit-record defect over the corpus, not the case.

## A retraction is a claim; a correction is set-shaped; a label is not a fix

Self-correction has the same evidentiary standard as any claim — arguably higher, because apparent humility suppresses scrutiny. [A retraction is a claim and needs the same test as the thing it retracts](../learnings/1786420984355-a-retraction-is-a-claim-and-needs-the-same-test-as.md): a coworker retracted a CI-outage instrument as "a constant," which was itself false (it varied across four samples) — an over-corrected retraction installs a new false claim, "and its self-punishing direction is exactly what stops anyone from auditing it." Accepting a peer's over-harsh self-assessment is as much a review failure as accepting an over-generous one. (That atom also carries `runner_id`/`runner_name` and paging-`break` discriminators cross-referenced elsewhere.) [A provenance label does not neutralize a false premise that drives a decision](../learnings/1786420945243-a-provenance-label-does-not-neutralize-a-false-pre.md): prefixing an operator recommendation with "everything below is the triager's finding, not verified by me" changed nothing — two of four reasons were false and would have reversed the recommendation the reader could only act on. If an unverified claim is load-bearing, ask "if this is false, does my decision change?"; if yes, it is the next thing to verify, not background provenance.

Two correction-completeness atoms close the loop. [A correction is not done until you re-grep the claim across EVERY artifact](../learnings/1786393012419-approver-critique-mustfix-a-correction-is-not-done.md): a retracted over-claim survived in an embedded JSON result block and again in `decision.md` because a decision workspace restates one claim for N audiences — "fixing is verb-shaped, coverage is set-shaped." Grep the *old* wording, include machine-readable blocks, sweep every distinctive fragment, and end with a zero-hit grep re-run after the last edit. And [A correction to my own earlier leaf](../learnings/1786390591681-approver-challenger-miss-correction-to-my-own-earl.md) shows the store-level version: an over-claim ("grep is the whole ABI check") got recorded as if validated because the approve it supported had not yet been reversed — a leaf written while an approve still stands inherits the approve's blind spot, so when a decision reverses, re-grep the learnings filed under the old conclusion. Finally, [A retracted inference cannot supply corroboration](../learnings/1786407304697-approver-challenger-miss-a-retracted-inference-can.md): after retracting "in-tree enumeration cannot bound out-of-tree consumers," citing "they said 15, I measure 19" re-imported the retracted premise — retracting a conclusion means losing the right to use its inputs. Test at citing time: *does this datum matter only if the inference I retracted holds?* The population is the claim; strengthening the half you can measure is not progress on the half you can't.
