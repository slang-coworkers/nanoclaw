# State Verification Discipline (part 3) (part 2)

> **This page is part 2 of 2** of the State Verification Discipline (part 3) synthesis (split 2026-08-07 to stay under the 40 KB read cap). Siblings: [part 1](general-misc-state-verification-discipline-3.md). The TL;DR below is shared across all parts.

## TL;DR
- **A read of a live artifact is a MEASUREMENT WITH A TIMESTAMP, not a fact.** PR state, CI conclusion, draft/ready, review state, infra capability — all mutate. Re-probe before citing; a saved memo is a claim about a past instant.
- **A dispatch, a webhook, a nudge, and a timeout are all claims ABOUT state, never state.** Resolve live before acting on any of them. A timeout in particular describes a past instant and is not a decision.
- **Correcting the EXTENT of a claim about a mutable condition, while still stating it as a standing property, leaves the claim wrong.** Fix the tense too.
- **A matching sha does not establish a current reading.** Same-head is necessary, not sufficient — a check can have been re-run, evicted, or superseded since.
- **Verify the CI *conclusion*, not just draft+HEAD, before calling a chain terminal** — and check whether the "last activity" you are clocking is actually a terminal close.
- **A commit date is not a version, and not an edit time.** Read submodule pins by version, not by date; use `merged_at`, never a merge commit's `committer.date`, for when a PR landed.
- **Bot-filed and stale issues rot silently.** Re-verify every `file:line` against current main before relaying; a static-analysis pointer goes stale when the branch is gone, not just when the file changes.
- **A grep hit is a line, not a scope, and `grep -c` counts lines, not events.** A member cite names file, line **and** owner; a published count names the invocation that produced it — or is deleted.
- **A PR's changed-file list does not prove "not a regression."** Verify control-flow invariants against SOURCE, not against a triage memo or a reviewer's assertion.
- **Check ownership before driving anything.** Read issue comments and `head.repo.full_name`: a contributor-owned fork PR is not yours to park, close, or push. Stand down when a maintainer or contributor is driving.
- **Verify @-mention identity against the actual PR/commit author before posting** — a wrong mention is a public, unrecoverable error.
- **A corrupted tool-result turn taints its own verifications**; redo them in a clean turn rather than reasoning off the residue.
- **Post-compaction drift is real**: re-read state before autonomous action in a compacted session, because your premise may have been summarized away.
- **Recurring trackers must carry DISPOSITION, not just items**, and a daily report must carry open ship-stoppers — an item list with no disposition reads as handled.
- **Recurrence only advances on completion**, so a host event can orphan an overdue occurrence and freeze a whole daily series until a human notices. Watchdog the scheduler.
- **A verdict you already published is a past position, not a reason to skip re-checking.** A substantive new human comment re-opens a chain you closed.
- **A correct conclusion is not evidence of correct reasoning.** When a mechanism is load-bearing for someone else's decision, verify the mechanism directly — no outcome, test, review, or CI signal can ever audit a wrong mechanism riding a right conclusion.
- **A consequence of a claim cannot corroborate the claim.** A second statement downstream of the same premise adds apparent authority and zero evidential weight; a real challenge owes a *different instrument*.
- **"Impossible" must be a search's conclusion, never its premise.** Telling an implementer a test is unsatisfiable makes them stop looking, and the construction they skip is usually the answer to the open question — and *is* the regression test.
- **Order the questions: is the implier always PRODUCED? — before — can it be ELIMINATED before the governing scan?** A sibling producer defeats a proxy gate at production, earlier than any elimination story anticipates. Prefer structural evidence over contingent; a contingent argument silently rots when a pass changes.
- **A claim about state at instant T must be MEASURED at instant T.** Locate the probe point relative to every pass that produces or consumes what you count; a probe downstream of the consumer is structurally incapable of refuting the implication. A dump reads as data and ends debate, which is what makes a wrong-instant measurement worse than a wrong argument.
- **A conclusion that outlives the death of its own justification is an alarm, not a vindication.** Hold it UNSUPPORTED, keep the conservative code, and refuse to manufacture mechanism N+1. "Unsupported" is a stable resting state.
- **State the observable and stop.** If you cannot name the observation that distinguishes your mechanism from its alternatives, you do not have the mechanism — and correcting one unfounded mechanism with another is the predictable next move.
- **Correcting toward LESS safety needs more evidence than correcting toward more.** Retract the reasoning immediately; relax the behaviour only once the replacement proof is complete. A retraction is itself a claim owing evidence.
- **Loosening a gate to kill a false positive requires a positive control proving it still catches the true case.** A checker that greps a syntax will fire on documentation of that syntax; require structural specificity, never a filename blacklist. An impossible count means an instrument or corpus defect — resolve it, never bridge it.

## Fidelity of Transcription — Between a Source and Your Claim About It (2026-08-07 fold)

Sections above verify *state*. This one covers the failure on top: you verified the right thing, then **lost it in transit** — source→prose, instrument→message, log→conclusion, measurement→published rule. Shared signature: **the result reads fluently**, so nothing in the artifact prompts a re-check. A dropped disjunct still parses; an abbreviated label still tabulates; an echoed script line still looks like a log line.

**Prose about someone else's control flow must quote the source, not paraphrase it — and a paragraph wrong twice will be wrong a third time in a NEW way.** One PR paragraph explaining why CI showed no test results failed three times running, each fix opening the next: **stale mutable totals** ("81 check-runs, 74 skipped") read at a head a force-push superseded; a **self-falsified claim** ("no workflow ran") that dispatching CI myself refuted; a **false universal** introduced by the generalisation that fixed it ("each `ci.yml` dispatch on a draft yields to the priority gate" — `extras/ci/wait-for-priority.py` yields only if a contender exists, and `--max-yield-hours 12` anti-starvation lets a long-waiting run proceed anyway); then a **dropped disjunct** while paraphrasing that script — "an older bot run is still **queued**" where the source says "queued **or in progress**", also losing "for the same workflow." Rules by generality: **generalising to escape staleness is not free** — swapping a measured total for an invariant is right, but a universal cannot be confirmed by the single observation that prompted it, so you trade a stale fact for an unverified one; **a quoted line with a `file:line` cannot lose "or in progress"**, while every paraphrase is a fresh chance to drop one; **state the invariant, not the reading** ("non-skipped `test-*` check-runs = 0" survived all four rounds and was the only merge-relevant claim — everything corrected was scaffolding: counts that mutate, mechanisms restated, colours that change per dispatch); **count the rewrites — at three, stop rewriting and start quoting.** All four were caught by an adversarial reviewer reading the actual scripts, never by re-reading the prose: rereading is what produced the text, so it cannot audit it ([Quote a mechanism's source instead of paraphrasing it — one paragraph was wrong three distinct ways (stale totals, false universal, dropped disjunct)](../learnings/1786080934191-quote-a-mechanism-s-source-instead-of-paraphrasing.md)).

**Paste instrument output; don't retype it — abbreviation is the move that destroys falsifiability, because it feels like tidying.** A relayed table showed `control  0  0`; I argued the row impossible (`control` appears in 150 of 378 files, so a corpus holding `transient` in 60 leaves cannot hold `control` in zero). Sound arithmetic, wrong conclusion, and the cause moved twice — my "their query is broken" wrong, then their own "the control searched a fabricated string, so `0` = passing; my *labelling* was the defect" also wrong. Settled: the tool printed `quokka-control  index=0  LEAVES=0 (control)` — fabricated string, correctly labelled — and the **relay** hand-retyped the label down to `control`, deleting the one token that made the row checkable. **The loss was invisible from both ends**: the sender knew what they meant, the reader read what arrived, neither view contained the discrepancy, so no party could detect it alone. Corollaries: **an impossible-looking number in a peer's relayed output has three live causes — broken instrument, bad label, lossy relay — and only the third is invisible from both ends**, so prefer causes needing no fault in their tooling until you have seen the command; **never diagnose a mechanism inside an instrument you cannot read, from a rendering of its output** — the honest verdict was available and skipped (*"this row is uninterpretable without knowing the searched string; which was it?"*); **an offered exoneration is a claim like any other** (declining theirs was right — the labelling wasn't the defect either, so accepting files a wrong cause for a real problem). Reach for this **first**: **establish which party can see which object before arguing about the object** — I am authoritative over what arrived on my edge, they over which string they searched ([Paste instrument output, don't retype it — hand-abbreviating a label made a passing control look like a broken query, invisibly from both ends](../learnings/1786082028729-paste-instrument-output-don-t-retype-it-hand-abbre.md)).

**A log contains the program's TEXT as well as its output — identify the decision line; don't grep the topic.** Three times in one task a correct-looking grep over a CI/test log returned source instead of verdict, and stopping at the first match would have inverted every conclusion. GitHub Actions echoes each `run:` block, so `grep -i yield` over a run log returns `echo "yielded=false"` and `--max-yield-hours 12` (both source) *above* the verdict `Yielding to human/merge CI #30024 (pull_request, in_progress)`. A retry-worker `grep -i lookback` returned five lines of the workflow's own comment and zero decisions — the real decision was `CI is still active (4 run(s)); not rerunning bot CI.`, so the matches read as "the worker never considered my run." And `slang-test`'s failure output prints the expected annotation *plus* a suggested replacement, so grepping the annotation finds it pass or fail. Source text usually appears **first**, before the deciding code has run. Guards: exclude echoed source (`run:` blocks are wrapped in `^[A log contains the program's text as well as its output](../learnings/1786071161776-a-log-contains-the-program-s-text-as-well-as-its-o.md)).

**A rule published without its domain is the same defect one level up — and the domain is usually invisible in the measurement that produced the rule.** I recorded "two memory stores drift ⇒ write both / merge both ways." For a peer's store that is **destructive**, and the drift measurement cannot tell you which case you have: **three store shapes give an identical signal (divergent md5s, disjoint content) with opposite remedies.** **Mirror pair** (same schema, same index header, no "not live" marker) → write both. **Disjoint namespace** (different schema, an index declaring *"THIS STORE IS NOT THE LIVE ONE"*, whole unique namespaces, shared basenames being *different documents about the same topic* rather than drifted copies) → **never `cp`**; a copy either way destroys a namespace. **Partial overlap** (same schema and header, large unique sets on *both* sides) → **merge per-file, additively, never `cp` a whole store**. Mine was partial overlap (366 vs 343 files → 206 home-only, 183 workspace-only, 160 shared), and the near-miss is the lesson: **I merged before establishing the shape**, safe only because I happened to merge per-file — on a disjoint pair the same action destroys a namespace. **The method saved me, not the reasoning.** Underneath: **a memory store can be two git repos on different mounts**, so a hash resolves in one and is `fatal: Not a valid object name` in the other and two readers *correctly* disagree — discriminate on your own edge (`git -C <p> rev-parse --show-toplevel`, `cat-file -t <hash>`, `findmnt -no SOURCE,TARGET --target <p>`), **compare mounts, not paths**, never cite a hash without its tree. And **the real damage of drift is disjoint safety-critical halves, not divergent hashes** — one store held a "nudge already posted — DO NOT POST ANOTHER" guard the other lacked while the second held findings the first lacked, each one `git checkout` from destroying the other's half. Measure per-substring in **python** (`needle in text`), not `grep`, and take attribution from `originSessionId` in frontmatter — never a path, never dirty-file presence, since dozens of sessions share one clone ([CORRECTION: "two memory stores ⇒ write both" needs the reachability contract first — three store shapes, identical drift signal, opposite remedies](../learnings/1786075851829-correction-two-memory-stores-write-both-needs-the-.md)).

## Mechanism vs. Fact — When the Conclusion Is Right and the Reasoning Is Not (2026-08-09 fold)

The section above covers losing a verified fact in transit. This one covers the inverse: **the fact was never verified, and nothing downstream will ever say so.** Most of it comes from one chain (shader-slang/slang#11917 batch-2, DRAFT PR #12336) where four successive mechanisms for one conclusion all died and the shipped code was identical under every version; the closing paragraph carries the same shape into a self-checking gate.

**A wrong mechanism riding a right conclusion draws no pushback from outcomes — which is precisely what makes it the most durable error class.** A safety note said: enumerate all four tag insts in the scan arm, *because* `GetTagForSuperSet`/`SubSet`/`MappedSet` are not covered by the tagged-union implication, so gating on `GetTagOfElementInSet` alone would be a stale-FALSE **miscompile**. The premise was TRUE (those three are not synthesized by `lowerTaggedUnionTypes`); the inference was INVALID ("not synthesized by that pass" does not entail "not reached by the implication"); the conclusion — keep all four — was still correct, just a defense-in-depth superset rather than load-bearing. Because following the note yields correct code, **no outcome, test, review, or CI signal can ever push back**, so it propagated by relay: issued by one tier, amplified verbatim in two dispatches by another, stored in memory labeled *"safety-critical"*, and stated publicly on the issue — **each relay adding apparent authority without adding verification**, since "safety-critical" is a claim about mechanism, not outcome, and a repeated label reads as settled. Hence: **a correct conclusion is not evidence of correct reasoning**; when a claim's mechanism is load-bearing for *someone else's* decision (what to enumerate, what to skip, what counts as a miscompile versus belt-and-braces), verify the mechanism directly. The signal that caught it was the implementer reporting it **could not prove the safety-critical part** and stalling — not a tooling problem or a weak test, but evidence the premise was false: **an unsatisfiable test demand is evidence about the claim, not about the test**, so re-derive the mechanism before assuming someone needs a better test (here the ratchet ran *in reverse*, the deepest tier catching the tier that issued the note). Two asymmetries govern the repair. **Correcting toward *less* safety needs more evidence than correcting toward more** — discovering "this guard isn't load-bearing" is not licence to drop it, so keep the conservative behaviour while the mechanism is unsettled, retract the *reasoning* immediately, relax the *behaviour* only once the replacement proof is complete. And correct in public **once**, after the open fact resolves, because a correction is itself a relay and a corrected correction spends more credibility than the original error — but fix every private restatement *now*, sweeping headings, tables, index rows and stored notes for the **superseded wording** rather than the retraction, and replacing in place, since an appended retraction leaves the false claim standing where readers land first (that sweep found **two** surviving restatements in memory, both still labeled "safety-critical", after the retraction was known) ([a wrong mechanism riding a right conclusion draws no pushback from outcomes](../learnings/1785826139100-a-wrong-mechanism-riding-a-right-conclusion-draws-.md)).

**A consequence of a claim is not evidence for it, "impossible" must be a search's conclusion rather than its premise — and order the questions "is the implier always PRODUCED?" before "can it be ELIMINATED?"** On the same chain, when a downstream fixer reported a blocked must-fix — *"can't write a test that isolates the direct scan arm"* — the reply was that co-emission *"also explains the blocked test: no isolating test can exist,"* with instructions to document the impossibility instead of building the test. That explanation is not additional evidence; it is **the same claim wearing a different hat**, inheriting the same unresolved dependency, since "no isolating test can exist" holds *only if* a consuming inst can never reach the governing scan without its implier — precisely the open question. Both statements flip together on one fact, so neither can audit the other. **Rule 1: a consequence of X cannot corroborate X** — when a second statement feels like it confirms your finding, check whether it is downstream of the same premise; if it is, it adds zero evidential weight and only apparent authority, the same shape as "agreement isn't corroboration when the peer's source is you." A real challenge owes a **different instrument**. **Rule 2: "impossible" must be the conclusion of an exhausted search, never its premise** — telling an implementer "this is unsatisfiable, document it instead" makes them stop looking and skip the exact construction that would answer the open question, and it is self-sealing: nobody looks, so the claim never gets contradicted, so it hardens. The reframe that fixed it: **the search for the test and the answer to the open question were the SAME TASK** — *if* you can construct a module that reaches the governing scan with the consumer present and the implier eliminated, that construction simultaneously proves the guard load-bearing **and is** the regression test, so the objective is "try to build it," not "prove it can't be built." Both reasoning rules stand; the **worked example was false and needs correcting in place, because a wrong worked-example is what readers copy.** Verified in source at HEAD `0864e60e6`: co-emission is **not universal**, so the original strong claim was RIGHT and the arm is genuinely load-bearing — in `getLoweredType` (`slang-ir-typeflow-specialize.cpp`), `as<IRTaggedUnionType>(info)` @:5606 and `as<IRElementOfSetType>(info)` @:5611 are **sibling `if`s on the same value** and the element-of-set branch returns `makeTagType(...)`, a tag type with **no** tagged-union type in the chain; `specializeLookupWitnessMethod` gates on `IRElementOfSetType` @:5774-5776 and contains **zero** `TaggedUnion` references across its whole body (:5748-5834), yet emits `GetTagForMappedSet` @:5823. `ElementOfSetType` is an **independent producer**, structurally parallel to the tagged-union path, so the premise "every tag-type value traces back to a tagged-union opcode" was false. The transferable sharpening is an **ordering of questions**: (1) **is the implier ALWAYS PRODUCED?** — enumerate *all* producer paths of the thing you rely on as a proxy, because a sibling branch producing your trigger without your proxy defeats the gate immediately; (2) **only then, can the implier be ELIMINATED before the governing scan?** (DCE/SCCP/simplification). Skipping straight to (2) is the trap: **co-emission at production ≠ co-presence at the governing scan** is right — two insts emitted together can be *separated* later, so "always emitted together" never licenses "always seen together by a later scan" — but here co-emission failed *at production itself*, earlier than that framing anticipates. And **structural beats contingent evidence**: an argument requiring a contingent fact (some pass happens to eliminate the implier) silently rots when an optimizer changes, whereas a producer family that never had an implier cannot rot — when both are available, publish the structural one. Process notes that held: the retraction rested on a subagent trace, so when a peer reported the reversal it was re-derived in source rather than flipped on a relay; it took three re-traces (original claim → retraction → reversal), each a *different instrument* on the same question, which is exactly what a real challenge requires ([a consequence of a claim is not evidence for it](../learnings/1785826510128-a-consequence-of-a-claim-is-not-evidence-for-it-re.md), [CORRECTION: the co-emission example was FALSE — ask "is the implier always PRODUCED?" first](../learnings/1785826705506-correction-to-a-consequence-of-a-claim-is-not-evid.md)).

**A wrong-INSTANT measurement is more dangerous than a wrong argument, because a dump launders a reasoning error as data.** The implementer probed `-dump-ir-before lowerTagInsts` and got exactly the hoped-for isolating shape — tag ops present, tagged-union opcodes **zero**, across three files. Wrong instant. Pipeline order in `slang-emit.cpp`: governing scan **:1519** → in-window pass `unpinWitnessTables` **:1576** → `lowerTaggedUnionTypes` **:1607**, the *consumer* that removes the implier → `lowerTagInsts` **:1617**, where the probe was taken. A probe after the consumer is **structurally incapable** of returning any other answer: `tagged-union = 0` was guaranteed by *where the dump was taken*, not by anything about the module. Re-probed in-window at `:1576`: tagged unions present in **every** shape, no isolating shape across ~80 files swept. Why this beats a bad argument at doing damage — a bad argument reads as reasoning and reasoning gets challenged, while **a dump reads as data and data ends debate**: this one had a real command, real output, exact numbers, and it agreed with the hypothesis, so nothing about it looked like an inference and nothing invited the only question that mattered — *at what instant was this taken?* It was one step from publication as an "isolating regression test," which would have laundered a false mechanism into apparently-empirical fact (credit: the implementer caught it in its own favour-result and retracted unprompted). Rules: **a claim about state at instant T must be measured AT instant T** — before trusting any dump, locate the probe point relative to every pass that produces or consumes the thing you are counting, and **choose a probe point by its pipeline position relative to producer and consumer, never by the name of the pass you care about**; then ask **"could this probe have returned a different answer?"** — if no, it carries no information regardless of how clean it looks, which is the positive-control question aimed at *placement* rather than at the instrument. **Primed instruments fail in both directions with the same error:** a refute-framed agent "proved" *all* paths from **1** of 40+ sites; a confirm-framed agent "proved" operand provenance by noting a *gate* exists, but the gate constrained a different value (`info`) than the operand (`inst->getWitnessTable()`) — it authenticated a **location** and reported it as **scope**. Both are locally-true-generalized-past-its-scope, so **framing controls which answer is cheap to reach, not whether the reasoning is sound**; a totality question needs an **enumeration**, not a sample, and **a citation authenticates the location, never the scope**. The alarm pattern: **when a conclusion survives the death of its own justification, repeatedly, that is evidence it is being reverse-justified, not evidence it is robust** — the fix is not finding the right mechanism faster but holding the conclusion **UNSUPPORTED** while keeping the safe code and refusing to manufacture mechanism N+1, because "unsupported" is a stable resting state that does not decay into "probably fine." Outcome: all four insts retained, justified purely as a **conservative superset**, with **no mechanism published** — the honest, publishable, unsatisfying position. Note where the value actually came from: the two findings with real code impact came from ordinary gate-vs-handled-set checking, not the mechanism debate — a pass doing a *second unconditional job* a narrow gate would skip, and a flag declared and gated on but **never set by any scan arm**, so the pass silently never ran and a diagnostic was lost. **A byte-identical revert-drill proves a gate does not BREAK things; it can never prove the gate WORKS** — a gate on a never-set flag skips nothing, so emission is trivially identical and the drill is green; gate tests need a control proving the gate FIRED, and byte-identity drills cannot catch a lost diagnostic. (That worked example needed its provenance corrected **twice**: the `AssumeAddress` dead flag was real, but as a **transient in-development state of the batch-2 draft, caught by its author before publication** — it never reached `master` or any remote ref. First written as a *shipped* defect, then over-corrected toward *never happened*. Positive control at `master`: `RequiredLoweringPassSet` (`slang-code-gen.h:52-88`) has 34 flags, all with setters, no `assumeAddress`; PR #12336's own body supplies the other half — *"An earlier draft of this change added an `assumeAddress` flag… and never set it."* ⇒ **"it happened" and "it shipped" are different claims, and over-correcting a false "shipped" into a false "never" is the same error with the sign flipped**; a prospective "gate on X when it lands" needs a mechanism, "X shipped" needs an artifact.) Two concrete measurement traps from the same episode: **`grep -c` counts MATCHING LINES, not occurrences** — "16 sites", used to attack someone else's totality claim, was really **42 lines / 43 occurrences**, an objection about undercounting that was itself undercounted; use `grep -o PATTERN file | wc -l`. And **a bare 12-hex hash that looks like a commit may be a BLOB**: a peer's "different SHA, so possible revision drift" dissolved once `git cat-file -t <hash>` returned `blob` and `git rev-parse <HEAD>:<path>` reproduced it — byte-identical content, invented divergence ⇒ **check an object's TYPE before treating a hash mismatch as a revision mismatch.** The generalization this closes: the chain kept regenerating *one* error — producer-vs-governing-scan — in successively subtler media, first as reasoning ("not synthesized by that pass"), then as an invalid inference ("co-emitted, therefore covered"), then as a scope error ("all paths, from one of 42 sites"), and finally **as a measurement**. **When a family of errors keeps recurring, check whether your instruments encode it, not just your arguments** ([a wrong-instant measurement launders a reasoning error as data](../learnings/1785827639255-a-wrong-instant-measurement-is-more-dangerous-than.md), [the probe that cannot fail is the one that looks most convincing](../learnings/1785827665319-a-wrong-instant-measurement-is-more-dangerous-than.md)).

**A plausible mechanism substituting for an observed fact produced four wrong claims in one task — the tell is that you can state the mechanism but not the observation that distinguishes it from its alternatives.** Across #11917 batch-2, four wrong claims from three tiers shared that shape, and **none were caught by tests**; all were caught by an independent reviewer or by re-measuring. (1) *"Co-emission: every tag inst is emitted alongside a tagged-union opcode, so the implication covers it"* — checked at *emission* time while the gate reads at *scan* time; different instants. (2) *"`ElementOfSetType` is an independent producer"* — structurally plausible, with source citations, but the dump at the governing scan contradicted it. (3) *"Added by CODEOWNERS auto-assignment"* — reviewers appeared on a PR nobody had been nominated on, so the mechanism that *would* explain it was named; a timeline query showed a specific account had acted. (4) *"A human added them"* — the corrected version of (3), still wrong, because an account name establishes the **actor**, not whether the action was manual or automated. Note the 3→4 progression: **an unfounded mechanism was corrected with another unfounded mechanism** — the pull toward supplying a mechanism survives being corrected once. "Reviewers appeared" is compatible with CODEOWNERS, a human, a bot, or an automation acting as a user; if you cannot say which you *observed*, you do not have the mechanism. **Fix: state the observable and stop** — *"the timeline attributes the action to account X"* is complete and cheap, while *"X manually added them"* costs a correction round and is not needed for any decision. Related: verify the *binary* before trusting any probe (a stale `.o` older than your last edit; or, if you inserted a struct member mid-struct during an in-flight incremental build, a layout-split binary that still links, still runs, and produces garbage). Two further asymmetries: **an instruction's confidence is not evidence** — a "must-fix" arriving from two tiers above was promoted because the label *sounded* like a finding, and it had no evidence behind it either time, so keep the verification step non-negotiable regardless of how confident the instruction is; the drill beating the publication is the only reason nothing false shipped. And **report searches as searches** — "swept 60 modules, found none" ≠ "no such module exists"; both negative findings shipped with their bound stated, and reviewers accepted them *precisely because* of that. Meta-observation worth keeping: **writing a section about honesty does not make the claims inside it calibrated** — in the same paragraph explaining a verification trap, "nothing in a conventional test pass would have caught it" was overclaimed and contradicted by the author's own passing test three sections down. Run the adversarial pass over your careful prose too, not just your code ([a plausible mechanism substituting for an observed fact — four wrong claims, one shape](../learnings/1785830249844-a-plausible-mechanism-substituting-for-an-observed.md)).

**A fail-closed gate needs its own negative control, because loosening a check to kill a false positive is exactly how you create a false negative.** A daily knowledge_base sync built fail-closed — every transform followed by a check that would FAIL if the transform hadn't happened — aborted on 2 files carrying the markdown-link prefix and 2 carrying the obsidian-link prefix, while the transform had in fact worked perfectly: all four hits were **prose about the link syntax**, ellipsis placeholders rather than paths. The class is worth naming: **a checker that greps for a SYNTAX will fire on documentation OF that syntax**, and any KB that records lessons about its own tooling accumulates the exact strings its tooling greps for, so this gets *more* likely over time, not less. It was a false positive that **blocks forever** — it would have failed every future sync — and both tempting fixes (delete the mention, or `grep -v` a filename) either destroy content or open a real blind spot. The repair is structural specificity, requiring a real path rather than the bare prefix, **and then a positive control on the loosened check**: append a real link to `index.md`, confirm the grep returns ≥1, restore, confirm it returns to 0 — measured 1 then 0, so it catches real links and ignores ellipsis prose. Without that control there is no way to distinguish "the fix worked" from "I blinded the check". The same root produced `finalize()` reporting `dangling 1` and `coverage 2209/2208` — more citations than learnings, which is impossible — from a markdown link whose target was a **metavariable** under the learnings directory sitting inside prose explaining the converter; rewriting it as non-link prose gave `dangling 0`, `coverage 2208/2208`. **An impossible count means an instrument or corpus defect — resolve it, never bridge it.** That note then re-committed its own trap by spelling the offending link out in full, so the link-target regex matched it and `dangling 1` stood for weeks ⇒ **a note documenting a syntax trap is itself a corpus member: write the example so it cannot be parsed as the thing it describes** (name the shape in prose, don't reproduce it). And a fail-closed script that aborts is doing its job — investigate the abort before weakening the gate; here the abort was wrong but the *design* was right, costing 2 minutes against publishing broken links ([a fail-closed sync check needs a negative control too](../learnings/1785826224691-a-fail-closed-sync-check-needs-a-negative-control-.md)).
