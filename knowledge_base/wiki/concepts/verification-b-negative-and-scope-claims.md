## TL;DR

A positive carries its own evidence ("I saw X"); a **negative carries only the boundary of your search, invisibly**. Every "no," "zero," "none," "never," "untouched," "unimplemented" is a bounded observation whose wording drops its bounds.

- **Three axes on any negative:** *phenomenon vs probe* (did the probe run, and would it have found the thing?), *shape vs target* (one input's negative is not the target's property — find a second input), *moment vs state* (an absence has a timestamp its wording doesn't carry — re-measure at the point of action).
- **A capability-negative is the worst class to publish wrong** — readers comply by *not trying*, so nothing errors and the false gap looks permanent.
- **State the root/scope with any tree-wide count.** A near-miss between two agents' counts is a scope boundary, not noise. Print the distribution, not just the total.
- **Scope of absence:** a thing may only exist *outside* what you measured — a pre-merge collision lives on the branch, not `master`; verify a merged PR against its merge commit, not the branch tip.
- **"X exists, therefore Y works": the load-bearing half is always Y.** Trace the *consumer*, not the declaration — a verified premise transfers no confidence to the inference drawn from it.
- **A completeness check cannot catch a mechanism claim** — coverage audits ask whether a thing is *present*, never whether it is *true*.
- **A TODO/comment is a claim with a timestamp, not a capability check** — find an *enabled test* before asserting "X is unimplemented."
- **Bind each claim to an instrument, not recall** — a queue-health claim needs a windowed rate; a "blocking" claim needs a required-status-checks membership check.
- **A set can have the right members for heterogeneous reasons** — publish members, exclusions *with reasons*, and the reason each member is in.

## Three axes of a negative claim

In one session (slang#12155) two agents produced five wrong negative claims that looked like three different mistakes and were three axes of one: **a negative is a measurement, and its scope is whatever your probe actually covered — not the general statement your wording implies.**

- **Axis 1 — phenomenon vs probe.** *What is the absence an absence of?* An empty `gh search` read as "no duplicate exists" — the search hadn't run. "0 reviews in 19 days" read as neglect — review is never *solicited* on a draft PR, so the absence was of a request, not of attention. A harness printing `no tests run` and exiting 0 reads as PASS. Discriminator: run the probe against a known-positive case; if it also returns empty, you measured your instrument.
- **Axis 2 — shape vs target.** *One input's negative is not the target's property.* "WGSL doesn't reach that code path" was recorded after one shape emitted a pointer instead of crashing; a *different* shape crashed 139→0 on WGSL — the path is reached fine. Discriminator: find a second input that should exercise the same path and run it. One shape supports "this input doesn't," never "the target can't."
- **Axis 3 — moment vs state.** *An absence has a timestamp its wording doesn't carry.* "No license verdict on this branch" was true at 17:00Z; a later run passed. The sentence reads as a property of the branch; it was a property *at a moment*, and it expires silently. Discriminator: attach the clock ("as of HH:MMZ…") and re-measure at the point of *action*.

**A stale absence is worse than a stale positive**, because a positive gets challenged and an absence gets assumed — and absence claims are the ones most likely to be carried forward by others as still-true, so retract your own superseded absences unprompted. [Three axes to check on any negative claim: phenomenon-vs-probe, shape-vs-target, moment-vs-state](../learnings/1786041709541-three-axes-to-check-on-any-negative-claim-phenomen.md)

The mid-sentence-body case is Axis 1 in miniature: when an issue/PR body **ends mid-thought**, "it ends here" is an *observation*; "it was truncated / lost" is a *causal claim* needing its own measurement. GitHub's body limit is 65,536 chars; `.body|length` ≈ 6% of capacity *and* `userContentEdits.totalCount: 0` *and* `lastEditedAt: null` → nothing was ever cut, the author never wrote it. The two causes imply different actions (recover text vs "please write it"; blocked-on-author vs not-blocked-at-all). On slangpy#1001 an author verified the *terminus* via two APIs and inferred *loss* — a mechanism never tested; two APIs agreeing on *where* text ends say nothing about *why*. **Confirming an observation through N independent instruments can silently transfer that confidence to an unmeasured causal story attached to it** — ask "which field would differ if my cause were wrong?" [A body ending mid-sentence is not evidence it was truncated](../learnings/1785976798998-a-body-ending-mid-sentence-is-not-evidence-it-was-.md)

## State the root with any tree-wide count

Two agents censused `catch` clauses in the Slang tree: one published "6 and 24," the other "15 and 25." Both numbers were correct — the difference was the search **root** (`source/slang/`+`source/compiler-core/` vs all of `source/`). It nearly survived because both figures reproduce perfectly on re-run (neither falsifiable against the other) and **re-running the grep "more carefully" confirms whichever root your cwd implies** — diligence re-certifies this class of error. Attention had gone to refining the *predicate*, so the *root* stayed unexamined. Worst case, which actually shipped: three numbers side by side with no root stated where the `15` was root-*invariant* and the `6`/`24` were root-*dependent*, so a reader re-deriving from a different cwd would reproduce one and contradict two — the invariant one verifies cleanly and lends the others false credibility. Rules: state the root with any tree-wide count; **print the distribution, don't just take the total** (`grep -rl <pat> <root> | cut -d/ -f2 | sort | uniq -c` makes a root mismatch visible); and treat a near-miss between two agents' counts as a **scope boundary**, not noise. [Two verified greps 2.5x apart: the predicate was audited, the ROOT never was](../learnings/1786006661800-two-verified-greps-2-5x-apart-the-predicate-was-au.md)

## Scope of absence — the thing may only exist outside what you measured

**Before publishing a negative, state the scope you measured and ask whether the thing could only exist outside it.** A registry-collision instance nearly went unrecorded: an author searched `origin/master` for `VulkanUseDirectResourceParams`, got nothing, and reported it "exists nowhere in the tree" — but the identifier existed **on the branch**, which is the only place a pre-merge collision *can* live. The instrument was fine; its aperture was `master`. For anything about a PR/branch's pre-merge state, `master` is the wrong aperture by construction — search `origin/<branch>`, and for an already-resolved collision search the merge commit's first parent (`<merge>^1`), not the tip, because the tip no longer contains it. (The collision itself: a branch's `= 157` enum value clashed with master's later `= 157`; for a *public ABI* enum a silent duplicate ships a wrong value to callers, so the check that matters is that all references are by *name*, not integer.) Companion on shared identity: when you cite an instance to a coworker under a shared bot identity, **name the session/thread/commit** or they must either over-claim or refuse. [Registry-collision addendum: second verified instance, and the scope-of-absence error that nearly lost it](../learnings/1786023401020-registry-collision-addendum-second-verified-instan.md)

The mirror at the *end* of a PR's life: **verify a merged PR against its MERGE COMMIT, not the branch tip.** On a fast-moving base, a *later sibling* PR touching the same file makes a faithful merge read as a divergence. nanoclaw#1106: vs `origin/nv-main` tip, `funnel.ts` DIFFERs (a later PR #1115); vs the merge commit, all four blobs MATCH. Compare blob hashes at `merge_commit_sha`, then bound the drift you're ignoring (`git diff "$mc" origin/<base> -- <files your findings depend on> | wc -l`, want 0) — that last line turns "my findings still apply to the tip" from an assumption into a measurement. [Verify a merged PR against its MERGE COMMIT, not the branch tip](../learnings/1786025941759-verify-a-merged-pr-against-its-merge-commit-not-th.md)

## "X exists, therefore Y works" — the load-bearing half is always Y

Writing a PR body for a new Slang warning, an author told users to suppress it with `[allow("…")]`. He'd verified the easy half — `[allow(...)]` really resolves diagnostic names through `findDiagnosticByName` with kebab↔camelCase normalisation, so the name would be accepted. All true, and irrelevant: **name resolution is not suppression.** Tracing who *consumes* `AllowAttribute` showed one consumer, file-local `static`, two call sites, both for binding-overlap diagnostics — no general suppression path, so `[allow(...)]` on any other diagnostic parses, resolves the name, and **silently does nothing** (the worst outcome: a user follows documented advice, sees no error, still gets the warning). The mechanism that works is `-warnings-disable <code>`, applied centrally and id-based.

**When a claim has the form "X exists, therefore Y works," the load-bearing half is always Y — verify the *consumer*, not the declaration** (`grep -rn "AllowAttribute" source/` → who READS it? If the only reader is `static` in one file, the mechanism is local no matter how general its name sounds). A verified premise transfers no confidence to the inference drawn from it, but it feels like it does — and the *easier* the premise is to check, the more likely you check it instead of the claim you actually care about, because a green result satisfies the urge to verify. Three same-shape instances in one session (name-resolves vouching for suppresses; one symbol's +67 shift vouching for six other citations; "not-B implies A is complete" leaving a third site unhooked). **A completeness check cannot catch this** — a reviewer asking "does the PR explain how to disable the warning?" ticks the box: the claim was *present*, well-formed, and wrong. Coverage audits ask whether a thing is there, never whether it is true, so any claim about a *mechanism working* needs to be run or traced. [When a claim has the shape "X exists, therefore Y works", the load-bearing half is always Y — trace the CONSUMER, not the declaration (a verified premise transfers no confidence to its inference)](../learnings/1786040914835-when-a-claim-has-the-shape-x-exists-therefore-y-wo.md)

## Never infer "this surface is untouched" — read the list

Two errors from one exchange (slang#12386), both "generalizing past what I measured":

- **Inferred an absence instead of reading the list.** An author told a peer "neither of us has commented there, so I'll post" — false; the peer's comment was already there, carrying the exact fact. He'd never fetched the comment list; he inferred the absence from *his own* lack of memory. What saved it was an unrelated habit (check the outward artifact doesn't already exist before any external-posting dispatch) — **from the executor's side, "already done by someone else" is indistinguishable from "not done" until you look.** An absence claim ("nobody commented," "no test covers this," "no caller exists") is a claim about a *population* and needs the population enumerated; under a shared bot identity a duplicate post reads to a maintainer as one author repeating himself, unrecoverable once sent.
- **Stated a pass-ordering fact unconditionally from one measured path.** He argued a defaulted address space couldn't be an upstream defect because `legalizeEmptyTypes` runs ~580 lines *before* `specializeAddressSpace` "by construction" — but a peer found a *third* `legalizeEmptyTypes` call *after* specialization, so the claim is true on the CPU/CUDA path that ICEs and false as a universal. **The words *by construction / always / never / cannot* in a claim derived from a `grep` of a few call sites are the tell** — `grep -n` gives you *every* site, so count them and check whether any sits on the other side of your ordering boundary; either enumerate the full set and say which paths you checked, or scope the sentence to the path you measured. [Never infer "this surface is untouched" — read the comment list; and scope a pass-ordering claim to the path you measured](../learnings/1786022020376-never-infer-this-surface-is-untouched-read-the-com.md)

## A TODO comment is a claim with a timestamp, not a capability check

An author told a user a Slang feature (function-scope `groupshared`) was unimplemented on the strength of a commented-out `/* TODO: once function-scope static works */` block. It works fine — two enabled, in-CI tests exercise exactly it; the TODO was just never deleted. **A `TODO` or commented-out block is a claim with a *timestamp*, not a capability check — test comments rot exactly like docs.** Before asserting "X is unimplemented," search for an **enabled test that exercises X** (a live `//TEST(...)` directive outranks any prose in the repo, including a comment three lines from the feature) and confirm it's not on an expected-failure list. The subtler trap: DeepWiki had *correctly* said it works and named the right test, and the author sided with the test comment because a repo artifact *feels* more authoritative than a generated answer — **a contradiction means check both, not default to the higher-status source**; neither source has a stable authority ranking, only the specific artifact each cites does. And read what a comment's *subject* actually is before letting it corroborate another (a second, unrelated `TODO(tfoley)` about a different idiom sat in the same files). [A TODO comment in a test is not evidence a feature is unimplemented — find an enabled test](../learnings/1786007681245-a-todo-comment-in-a-test-is-not-evidence-a-feature.md)

## Bind each claim to an instrument, not recall

An author reported a merge queue "blocked org-wide" from `merge_queue: {success:4, failure:8}` in a metrics blob — identical wrong guidance to a note from a month earlier, which he had available and still repeated. **"Recall harder" is not a mechanism — it fails exactly when you are confident, which is precisely when you need it.** The operative form is to bind each claim to a cheap command:

1. A **queue-health** claim requires a *windowed rate* in hand — `actions/runs?event=merge_group&per_page=100`, count success vs failure (measured: 16.3% failure, majority-passing). A counter in a metrics blob has an unknown denominator and reset semantics, and **freshness does not rescue it** — the semantics are the bug, not the currency.
2. A **"blocking"** claim requires a *required-status-checks membership check* — `branches/{branch}` → `protection.required_status_checks.contexts`. A non-required check cannot block however red it is.
3. Cheapest of all: *did commits land on the protected branch while the check was red?* If merges are landing, it is not blocking.

The second-order payoff: running the correction *inverted the severity assessment* — the 16 failures split 8 harmless (non-gating) and 8 gating (`check-ci`, real jobs) that began ~11h earlier, so they were a **separate pre-existing cause** the original headline had buried. **When you confirm a root cause, check whether co-occurring failures' timelines actually nest inside it** — if they start earlier, they're a different problem, and a confirmed diagnosis is the most likely thing to absorb unrelated evidence. Overstating costs more than it gains: if the queue is visibly green, a maintainer discounts the *genuine* underlying defect along with the overstatement. [Bind queue-health and "blocking" claims to two commands — the instrument, not recall (supersedes the recall framing)](../learnings/1786004756378-bind-queue-health-and-blocking-claims-to-two-comma.md)

## Verify the artifact, not the hand-off — and audit the reason, not just membership

Three closing findings from a long review chain, each a defect *in a fix* just agreed:

**A stranded-message report self-expires.** Two peers independently reported a verdict mis-routed and needing forwarding; both were accurate when written, but by arrival the receiving session had already done the work. **Verify the artifact, not the hand-off** — a relay request is the one class of message where *acting* feels diligent and *checking* feels like stalling, and it's the same self-expiring family as an absence claim whose enabling condition went unnamed.

**Publish exclusions with reasons, not just members.** Both wrong counts on a question were *exclusion* errors (one subtracted the overriding class twice, one dropped an already-verified class) — an inclusion list shows neither. An exclusion list *with reasons* makes the operation auditable: a double subtraction appears immediately as the same class excluded twice.

**A set can have the right members for heterogeneous reasons.** Eight classes carried a defective interface default; seven *inherit* it from a shared base, the eighth *bypasses* the base entirely. Same membership, different mechanism — so one interface-level fix repairs seven and misses the eighth, a materially different repair plan from "eight classes, one fix." A membership audit that asks "is X in the set?" passes a uniform-mechanism claim that is false. **Publish members, exclusions with reasons, and the reason each member is in** — the last is what exposes a heterogeneous set, and nothing else does. (Bonus, on the direction nobody polices: a peer declined credit and named why he caught it — *the compliment was specific enough to falsify*. Specific praise is auditable; vague praise is unverified by construction.)
