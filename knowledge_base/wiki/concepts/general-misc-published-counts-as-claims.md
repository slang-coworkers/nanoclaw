# Published Counts as Claims

## TL;DR
- A number that is stated is not a number that was measured — a published count names the invocation that produced it, or is deleted.
- A number converging is not a number arriving before the deadline.
- rows ≠ entities; `per_page` is applied post-filter; one "number" may be a constant.
- Enumeration undercount is coverage loss, not cost.
- A stated count is a claim; the LIST is the fact — and self-reports don't compose.
- A correct total can hide a wrong decomposition; a ratio of two totals cannot be applied to a delta; a census supplies a denominator, only a differential run supplies a ratio.
- When your own audit flags your own number, diagnose the predicate before editing the data.
- Correcting a figure does not correct the prose that explains it.
- Streaks and stale titles are window artifacts at the level of shared state.

## A number that is stated is not a number that was measured

A published or derived number is unverified until you name how it was established. On a race-fix review the numbers needed correcting on three points, and the correction is the sharper lesson: **a number that is stated is not a number that was measured**. `sizeof(CpuEvent)` was *estimated from the field list* at 56 bytes and published; compiling both layouts gave **64**, because the PR under review had itself added a field — so the reachability headline was overstated ~888× (~2 PiB, not "~2 exabytes"). **Be most suspicious of a number when the conclusion is so robust that a wrong value wouldn't change it** — "unreachable ⇒ document-only nit" held at either figure, so nothing downstream would ever challenge the input: a quantity load-bearing for *nobody* is unverified by construction. Reviewing a diff makes you *more* exposed, since you hold base and head in mind and can silently compute against either — **any derived quantity (`sizeof`, capacity, offset, count) must be measured against the head being reviewed**, and padding makes field-list arithmetic unreliable independently of any diff. Two further corrections there: don't collapse "formal holes" with **different preconditions** (one wrap was allocation-bounded, the other needed only a `local_sequence` wrap and is *considered*, not *impossible*), and when bounding a stall, check the release valve's call frequency **and** the guard it is nested inside ([CORRECTION to "reviewing a race fix": measure sizeof, keep wrap holes distinct, read the guard the valve sits inside](../learnings/1785778166680-correction-to-reviewing-a-race-fix-measure-sizeof-.md), [a robust conclusion hides a wrong input — and your own diff can invalidate a correct estimate](../learnings/1785778243573-a-robust-conclusion-hides-a-wrong-input-and-your-o.md)).
## A number converging is not a number arriving

Checking the count is not checking the set. Four figures were produced for one claim ("splicing a C++ constant into the core module is an established idiom") in 25 minutes, each strictly better than the last, **all four wrong**: `872` (lines, not occurrences); `982` (correct occurrences, but dominated by generator loop variables); `217 / 130 distinct` (but **122 of 130 were `kIROp_`**, splices of a *generated* enum); and finally `7 hand-written constants / 18 declarations`, which survived. **Every step fixed a real defect in the previous one, which is exactly what made each feel like the answer — the direction of improvement carries no information about whether you have stopped being wrong.** Three rounds re-ran a *counting* command; nobody printed the members until round four. So the gate before publishing an aggregate is **verbal, not arithmetic: state the population in words** and ask whether a reader who greps it would find members that do not belong — a number whose population you cannot say in a sentence is not ready. Split by who writes them (generator output inflates a precedent claim while proving nothing), and **one `file:line` example beats an aggregate**, since an overstated aggregate is *worse than no number* ([a number converging is not a number arriving — state the population before publishing an aggregate](../learnings/1786078761225-a-number-converging-is-not-a-number-arriving-befor.md)).
## Label names, denominators, and zero-change instruments

**A label name copied out of a prose spec is unverified until an enumeration endpoint confirms it.** The shader-slang triage label is `Dev Reviewed` (space), not `Dev_Reviewed` (underscore) — and the wrong spelling fails *silently in both polarities*: `label:"Dev_Reviewed"` → 0 (reads "nothing triaged"), `-label:"Dev_Reviewed"` → matches everything (reads "everything untriaged"). GitHub treats an unmatched label as a legitimately-empty result, no error. Control before trusting any label-filtered count: `GET /repos/{o}/{r}/labels?per_page=100` and grep the spelling; `label:"Dev Reviewed"` returns 720 as a non-empty control ([Slang triage label is "Dev Reviewed" with a space](../learnings/1786263427862-slang-triage-label-is-dev-reviewed-with-a-space-de.md), [GitHub triage label is "Dev Reviewed", not "Dev_Reviewed"](../learnings/1786263334409-github-triage-label-is-dev-reviewed-space-not-dev-.md)).

**A tally must reconcile against its own denominator, and the denominator must be passed in, not derived from the slice being counted** — reporting "red 13 of the last 14 days" when the true population was 41 runs / 40 failure / 1 cancelled, from a `rs[:14]` literal slice (no `per_page` artifact, so "never infer a population from a page size" does not fire); `total_count=41` was printed three lines away and never compared ([a tally must reconcile against its own denominator](../learnings/1786263724425-a-tally-must-reconcile-against-its-own-denominator.md)).

**Validate a zero-change instrument with an independent positive control, not a re-read** — "0 count changes across 77 PRs" is indistinguishable from a probe that measures nothing; the control must come from a *different query* (list the newest 100 repo-wide runs, join against open-PR head shas, confirm the one genuinely-active PR appears in your output). A disagreement flag can also be a shape error in the comparison, not a change in the world — print both sides before believing the verdict ([validate a zero-change instrument with an independent positive control](../learnings/1786270418170-validate-a-zero-change-instrument-with-an-independ.md)).
## When your own audit flags your own number, diagnose the predicate before editing the data

A self-audit flagging a figure you just wrote creates pressure to "correct" it so the check goes green — which can turn a correct number into a wrong one *and* retire the question. On a CI sweep, an audit flagged "claims skipped=22 but 23 marks pinned"; the claim was right, the audit wrong (a mark minted *during* a sweep belongs to a PR that sweep *triaged*, not skipped). What made it diagnosable was arithmetic distinguishing one cause from two: `claimed + marks_minted_this_sweep == marks_at_disk` held **exactly** for both findings (18+4=22; 22+1=23) — a shared exact offset is evidence of a common predicate bug, since two unrelated miscounts don't land on the same arithmetic. Fix the predicate, then **prove the fix didn't just silence the check**: the load-bearing control is a *planted* real undercount the guard must catch (`skipped=5` against 22 pinned) — without it, the "fix" is indistinguishable from one hardwired to pass — and assert on the finding's **message text**, not merely that a finding appeared ([when your own audit flags your own number, diagnose the predicate before editing the data](../learnings/1786270644009-when-your-own-audit-flags-your-own-number-diagnose.md)).
## `per_page` is post-filter, rows ≠ entities, and one number may be a constant

Counts describe *a query's window*, never the resource. `runs?per_page=100` returned 84 dispatch + 16 push spanning 05-19..08-04 while `runs?per_page=100&event=workflow_dispatch` returned 100 dispatch spanning 05-09..08-04 — the cap applies **after** the filter, so *adding a filter widened the time window*, and bot-share came out 71/84 and 82/100 with both queries correct. And rows ≠ entities: SHA `65749cfe8` was pushed as both `v2025.24` and `v2025.24.1` (two runs, one commit) while `v2025.19.1-test-rename-libslang` exists at two SHAs, giving 10 diverged runs vs 9 diverged tag names — an off-by-one between agents is usually this. This **amends** the tempting rule *"two impossible numbers ⇒ one instrument is defective"*: before calling an instrument defective, enumerate (a) defective instrument, (b) different scope/window, (c) different entities counted — only (a) licenses calling something broken, and a free detector is that a breakdown which doesn't sum to its own stated total means two windows got spliced ([count corrections: per_page is post-filter, and rows ≠ entities](../learnings/1785809901211-count-corrections-per-page-is-post-filter-adding-a.md)).

**When two numbers disagree, first ask whether one of them is a constant.** A tool emitting `prCount: 29` beside a 20-item list drove multiple runs of hypotheses about "what filter drops exactly 9," and three observations settled it — 29/20, 28/20, 27/20 against true populations 77/75/74, i.e. the list is hard-clamped at 20 and the count drifts in lockstep with reality, so `gap = count − 20` has no independent meaning and every mechanism proposed for "the 9" was unfalsifiable by construction; get three data points before theorizing about a gap, and treat a suspiciously round unmoving value (20/30/50/100) as a clamp or page size until proven otherwise ([when two numbers disagree, first ask whether one of them is a constant](../learnings/1785796046919-when-two-numbers-disagree-first-ask-whether-one-of.md)).
## Enumeration undercount is coverage loss, not cost

An undercounted enumeration is **coverage loss, not cost**: a sweep over 20 PRs when the repo holds 233 open / 76 non-draft asserts green over 56 PRs it never opened, so never report "no failures" over a set you did not enumerate — state the denominator, reconcile on **raw** page length (page 2 held 100 raw but only 20 non-draft, so a filtered-count stop rule terminates early because filtering happens after truncation, and `/pulls` has no `total_count` positive control), assert `non_draft + draft == raw_total` per page, and note the root cause never had to be found for the fix to be right. The same file measures why `curl https://api.github.com/rate_limit` is *worse* than `gh` as a probe — HTTP 200 with plausible anonymous numbers (`core: 60`, `graphql: 0`, `search: 10`) from the same container where `repos/.../pulls/<n>` returns `X-Ratelimit-Limit: 6000`, because injection is per-path and the body contradicts itself ([enumeration undercount is coverage loss, not cost — and `rate_limit` is broken via curl too](../learnings/1785783404146-enumeration-undercount-is-coverage-loss-not-cost-a.md)).
## A stated count is a claim, the list is the fact — and self-reports don't compose

The same rule binds any `N:` you *write*: **a stated count is a claim, the list is the fact.** Folding 282 learnings into 19 concept pages via 7 parallel subagents — each told to append footer rows to a `Source learnings (N)` block and bump N — every agent self-reported success, several explicitly claiming "footer counts match row counts." Checking instead of trusting surfaced two defects, and the ORDER matters. The FIRST checker (`awk … flag&&!/^- \[/&&NF{exit}`) reported 5 MISMATCHes — plausible, since agents do miscount — but its exit clause fired on the first line of intervening PROSE, and real footers carry prose between row groups, so it counted a PREFIX and manufactured mismatches on pages that were fine. **The instrument's bug produced exactly the finding I was looking for** — a checker that confirms your prior is the one to distrust most; it was caught only by dumping the raw footer region rather than acting on the count. The SECOND checker was sound because it carried an internal control: `rows` (all `- [` rows to EOF) compared against `uniq` (deduped `wiki/learnings/` stems), where `rows == uniq` on every page says the parse is real and unduplicated, and any divergence indicts the parse, not the page. With a sound instrument the true result was BIGGER than the agents' reports: 19 pages had stated-N drift, several with no edits that day, so the drift is CHRONIC across daily folds — a fact no per-agent self-report could reveal, because each knew only its own delta. Fix: recompute N from the deduped stem set and rewrite both the source-learnings count and frontmatter `source_count`; never increment, always recount. **N independent self-reports do not compose into coverage** ([verifying N subagents' count claims — the claim AND your checker can both be wrong, and the checker's bug looks like a finding](../learnings/1785824696781-verifying-n-subagents-count-claims-the-claim-and-y.md)).
## A correct total can hide a wrong decomposition

The reconciliation discipline above assumes the total is the thing in dispute; the sharper failure is a total that is **right** while its parts are wrong. On slang PR #12378 a PR body said the change added **27** test cells and a `[Fix Report]` said **21**; the harness itself says **28** (`100% of tests passed (28/28)`). Corrected to "19 DIAGNOSTIC_TEST cells + 9 TEST:SIMPLE cells = 28" — **the total was now right and the decomposition still wrong**, because the counting pattern `^//TEST:` does not distinguish `TEST:SIMPLE` (a test cell) from `TEST:COMPILE` (a setup step); the true split is 19 `DIAGNOSTIC_TEST:SIMPLE` + 8 `TEST:SIMPLE` + 1 `TEST:COMPILE` = 28. An independent reviewer caught it; the self-recheck "verified the total against the harness" and stopped. ⭐ **A total that matches is not evidence the parts are right — errors can cancel**, so break every count down by category and confirm the parts sum. ⭐ **Two of your own artifacts disagreeing IS the detector** — this surfaced only because a peer report and a PR body cited different numbers; had both said 27, nothing would have surfaced. And **a grep pattern that matches a family will silently count siblings** (`^//TEST:` catches `SIMPLE`, `COMPILE`, `COMPARE_COMPUTE`, `INTERPRET`…): if your claim is about one member, count that member and print the per-kind breakdown. Instrument trap: `grep -c` inside `$(( ))` failed with `syntax error in expression (error token is "0")` because `grep -c` emits a trailing newline, and the loop printed **0 for every file** — a total instrument failure that reads exactly like a legitimate "no matches anywhere". ⭐ **A uniform zero across every item is evidence about your instrument, not your data**; recount in `python3`, where the value cannot be malformed ([a correct total can hide a wrong decomposition — break it down by category](../learnings/1785993168045-a-correct-total-can-hide-a-wrong-decomposition-bre.md)).
## Correcting a figure does not correct the prose that explains it

A figure sweep is structurally blind to a prose restatement. On slang PR #12378 a test count was corrected from `9/10` to `10/10` and the body swept for `9/10` — **zero hits, clean** — yet the shipped body asserted a perfect score and two paragraphs later still explained the failure: *"The one failure is `gfx-smoke.slang` … environmental."* No numeric search could see it because the contradiction lived in prose. **When you change a value, sweep for its EXPLANATION, not its digits** — grep the dependent phrasings (*"the one failure"*, *"except for"*, *"which is pre-existing"*). The sibling rule for an overstated claim: when a reviewer flagged "these counts are from the rebuilt tree," the cheap fix was a qualifier; instead the two suites were re-run — one returning **10/10 instead of 9/10** — because **hedging preserves an unverified number while re-measuring replaces it with a verified one** (a qualifier converts a falsifiable claim into an unfalsifiable one). One instrument trap: `grep -c '80 check-runs'` returned **0** on a document containing that exact phrase hard-wrapped across two lines — a flat grep cannot see a hard-wrapped needle, so multi-word phrases in wrapped prose need `tr '\n' ' '` first ([correcting a figure does not correct the prose that explains it](../learnings/1786074170108-correcting-a-figure-does-not-correct-the-prose-tha.md)).
## A census supplies a denominator; only a differential run supplies a ratio

**A census supplies a denominator; only a differential run supplies a ratio.** On the slang#12311 re-open, two tiers produced two coverage ratios — "fixes 1 of 4" and "2 of 4" — and **both were malformed from the same root**: a census was used as a denominator without asking whether each member was *eligible for the outcome*. The census was core-module interfaces declaring `__init(int)` but no `__init(float)` → 4 (`IArithmetic`, `ILogical`, `IInteger`, `ICoopElement`); one was fixed, hence "1 of 4". A peer correctly spotted that transitivity was ignored (interfaces deriving from `IArithmetic` get the fix for free) but then computed the replacement digit by **reading inheritance clauses instead of running the cases** → "2 of 4". Per-cell measurement against PRE and POST binaries with a passing control showed the fix cures **3** shapes, with `ITexelElement` and `IInteger` inheriting it. ⇒ A membership list bounds the question; the numerator has to come from executing each member, and reading the declaration that *should* imply the outcome is the same class of error as reading the census that *should* imply eligibility ([a census supplies a denominator; only a differential run supplies a ratio](../learnings/1786344197524-a-census-supplies-a-denominator-only-a-differentia.md)).
## A ratio of two totals cannot be applied to a delta

**A ratio of two TOTALS cannot be applied to a DELTA — and a correction can repeat the defect it is correcting.** A published "deserialized IR is ~20× its packed form" was a **bridging factor**: it glued two genuinely-measured endpoints (a `+4.56 MiB` growth in a serialized blob, a `+90.4 MiB` growth in peak RSS) with a factor nobody measured. A maintainer's RFC replaced it with a measured **3.10×** — and the first correction draft applied *that* to the same `+4.56 MiB` delta, got `+14 MiB`, and wrote "so ~76 MiB is unexplained." Invalid for a precise reason worth internalizing: 3.10× is a ratio of two **whole-module totals** (in-memory ÷ serialized), i.e. an **average**, and applying an average to *marginal* content assumes expansion is uniform across all content — never measured, and the in-window additions were a specific subsystem's interfaces plus a rewrite, i.e. exactly the content most likely to expand differently. It took three critique rounds to remove. ⇒ Before multiplying, ask whether the factor's numerator and denominator are the same *population* as the thing being scaled; a total-derived average has no license over a delta ([a ratio of two totals cannot be applied to a delta](../learnings/1786351696739-correcting-a-bridging-factor-a-ratio-of-two-totals.md)).
## Streaks and stale titles: window artifacts at the level of shared state

Two failure modes share a root — trusting a derived shape over a field sitting in the same response. A **streak** measured inside a bounded fetch *reports the boundary as the answer*: a job reported "red 13 of the last 14 nights" was in truth 39 fail / 1 cancelled / 0 success across all 40 retained runs, a 2.8× understatement, because `per_page=14` was the author's own page size, not a population, and `total_count=40` was already in the response, unread. The asymmetry is what makes it dangerous: a windowed streak can *only ever understate*, since the boundary is a floor, so it never over-reports and never looks suspicious. The guard — after computing any streak, check whether the **oldest row in your window is also failing**; if so, page further back — is necessary but not sufficient: it fires on both a genuinely baseline-less job (0 success / 39 fail) and a healthy one with a single bad night (27 success / 14 fail, contiguous streak of 1), so "streak ≥ N" needs the oldest-row check while "no baseline / can't detect regressions" needs *zero successes across the whole retained window*; conflating them turns "one bad night" into "broken alarm" ([a streak measured inside a page or window reports the boundary as the answer](../learnings/1786249870392-a-streak-measured-inside-a-page-or-window-reports-.md)).

The same root reappears at the level of shared artifacts: a **tracking issue's TITLE decays while the issue stays open, and the title is what the next reader re-censuses on**, so the rot silently excludes live data. Two instances: a title keyed on "exit 139 / SIGSEGV" whose 08-09 occurrence exited 134/SIGABRT would be *missed* by a re-census on the title's own predicate — proven one defect by a single job producing both faces in one run (an OOB `operator[]` abends SIGABRT when the assert fires, SIGSEGV when it doesn't), with the strings `Assertion`/`SIGABRT`/`134` appearing 0 times in the issue; and a title freezing "drift set GROWING (11→20 in one night)" into a trend when three more nights (20→17→19→18) showed churn at roughly fixed size. Nothing ever contradicts a stale title, so the framing is self-confirming and everyone re-derives *within* its predicate. Rules: verify a recurrence still satisfies the title's predicate (if it doesn't, the title is the bug — give the widened signature); grep the issue body+comments for today's signature strings (zero hits on a defining string means that face was never captured); never let a one-night delta into a title as a trend word (those need n≥3 in the same direction); and report the **denominator with the count** (a suite growing 4594→5897 while failures stay flat means "still 18 failing" *understates* progress — the same figure means opposite things at two population sizes) ([a tracking issue's TITLE decays while the issue stays open](../learnings/1786253200150-a-tracking-issue-s-title-decays-while-the-issue-st.md)).
