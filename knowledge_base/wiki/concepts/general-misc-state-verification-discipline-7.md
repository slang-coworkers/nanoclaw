## TL;DR
- **A read of a live artifact is a MEASUREMENT WITH A TIMESTAMP, not a fact.** PR state, CI conclusion, draft/ready, review state, infra capability — all mutate. Re-probe before citing; a saved memo is a claim about a past instant.
- **A dispatch, a webhook, a nudge, and a timeout are all claims ABOUT state, never state.** Resolve live before acting on any of them. A timeout in particular describes a past instant and is not a decision.
- **Correcting the EXTENT of a claim about a mutable condition, while still stating it as a standing property, leaves the claim wrong.** Fix the tense too.
- **A matching sha does not establish a current reading.** Same-head is necessary, not sufficient — a check can have been re-run, evicted, or superseded since.
- **Verify the CI *conclusion*, not just draft+HEAD, before calling a chain terminal** — and check whether the "last activity" you are clocking is actually a terminal close.
- **A commit date is not a version, and not an edit time.** Read submodule pins by version, not by date; use `merged_at`, never a merge commit's `committer.date`, for when a PR landed.
- **Bot-filed and stale issues rot silently.** Re-verify every `file:line` against current main before relaying; a static-analysis pointer goes stale when the branch is gone, not just when the file changes.
- **A grep hit is a line, not a scope, and `grep -c` counts lines, not events.** A member cite names file, line **and** owner; a published count names the invocation that produced it — or is deleted.
- **A PR's changed-file list does not prove "not a regression."** Verify control-flow invariants against SOURCE, not against a triage memo or a reviewer's assertion.
- **Check ownership before driving anything.** Read issue comments and `head.repo.full_name`: a contributor-owned fork PR is not yours to park, close, or push. Stand down when a maintainer or contributor is driving.
- **Verify @-mention identity against the actual PR/commit author before posting** — a wrong mention is a public, unrecoverable error.
- **A corrupted tool-result turn taints its own verifications**; redo them in a clean turn rather than reasoning off the residue.
- **Post-compaction drift is real**: re-read state before autonomous action in a compacted session, because your premise may have been summarized away.
- **Recurring trackers must carry DISPOSITION, not just items**, and a daily report must carry open ship-stoppers — an item list with no disposition reads as handled.
- **Recurrence only advances on completion**, so a host event can orphan an overdue occurrence and freeze a whole daily series until a human notices. Watchdog the scheduler.
- **A verdict you already published is a past position, not a reason to skip re-checking.** A substantive new human comment re-opens a chain you closed.
- **A number converging is not a number arriving.** Before publishing an aggregate, state its population in words and inspect the SET's members, not the count — every fix to a wrong figure is what makes the next wrong figure feel like the answer.
- **A population assumption must be violable loudly.** Any tool deriving its work-set from a hardcoded list, separator, glob, or prefix needs a coverage assertion that refuses to write; otherwise it reports success on the subset it happened to see.
- **Correcting a figure does not correct the prose that explains it.** Sweep for the explanation ("the one failure", "except for", "which is pre-existing"), not the digits — and re-measure instead of hedging, because a qualifier preserves an unverified number as an unfalsifiable one.
- **A stale status line is worse than a missing one.** When state changes materially, re-read what the most-trusted summary surface *asserts*, not whether it mentions the thing.
- **Ask of every check: could it have come out differently if the claim were false?** Enumerate what must DIFFER in a fixture, not what must exist — a fallback that coincides with the correct answer, a baseline that emits nothing, and a probe downstream of the consumer all pass regardless.
- **A negative control must differ by exactly one variable — the fix — and by the comparator the claim's own wording names.** "No worse than master" means pristine master; "this test discriminates" means this tree minus the fix. A tidy one-variable control against the wrong comparator is more dangerous than a sloppy one.
- **A failed control indicts the apparatus, not the claim.** Never convert an apparatus failure into a verdict — not "false", not even "untestable" — before trying a different construction. When a control fails, check that it fails for the reason your test targets.
- **A common-mode defect is invisible to per-item review — ask one question over the SET.** N truthful self-reports sum to a false coverage claim; a cross-cutting audit must include items nobody edited, and agreement among reports sharing a method is one observation, not N.

## Controls, discrimination, and proxy verification — part 1/3

### The proxy shape: a check that could not have come out differently

The single recurring defect behind four independent errors on slang#12192 was not laziness but *shape*: every check ran, passed, and produced output, and none of them could have produced a different result if the claim were false — a `grep` finding `SLANG_UNEXPECTED` in a switch proved presence, never reachability (buffer handles route out earlier); a byte-identical SPIR-V diff at `-g1/-g2/-g3` could not fail because `OpLine` emits only from statement-granularity marker insts, so a value-inst `sourceLoc` change cannot move the output; a second-hand line number stood in for its enclosing function (`:2035/:2272` sit in two *other* functions, one of which early-returns for the type at issue); and one aggregate `total > 0` non-vacuity guard stood in for per-site coverage, hiding an assertion site whose required instruction the test input never produces ([four errors, one shape — we verified a proxy instead of the thing](../learnings/1785775797519-four-errors-one-shape-we-verified-a-proxy-instead-.md)). The installable reflex is one question — *if the claim were false, would this specific check have told me?* — and if you cannot describe the failing output, you hold a proxy. Three corollaries land the same blow from other angles. First, the *same* green signal carries opposite weight depending on the claim: for "my fix works" green is a smoke test (inert, skipped, stale-binary, or asserting an observable the bug cannot move), while for "this refactor is behavior-preserving" green is close to the whole proof — but only after you have enumerated where preservation could break (on slang#12281's −88/+55 collapse: the early-out moved from inside the driver to before it, so `IREmptyTypeLegalizationContext`'s empty body and the inlined chain's fall-through-vs-early-return had to be checked first, *then* 8/8 meant something) ([green is weak for "my fix works", strong for "I changed nothing"](../learnings/1785792616063-a-green-run-is-weak-evidence-for-my-fix-works-and-.md)). Second, when prose and a test disagree, the test is the artifact reality already voted on: slang#12148's PR-description glossary asserted "one `DebugSource` per source file… the **same** deduplicated inst" while the PR's own test comment (written by the same author) documented the precompiled-module case where a filename-only record sits *beside* the full-path record — self-consistent prose can never catch this, only prose-versus-tests can, and universally-quantified words ("one per", "always", "each", "never") are the highest-yield targets ([when prose and a test disagree, the test is the artifact that was forced to be true](../learnings/1785800192069-when-prose-and-a-test-disagree-the-test-is-the-art.md)). Third, generalized breadth of re-checking never converges on the deciding input: **name the field whose change would invalidate the conclusion, in advance**, then re-read that field at the moment of action — on #12281 the fixer recorded that its "no approval yet" finding expired the instant one landed, naming `reviewDecision` and the approval's `commit_id`, so when an approval arrived ~90 minutes later the webhook was immediately interpretable (merging master would dismiss a five-round approval ⇒ correct action = nothing); the same discipline pre-commits an escalation rule before the second data point can be rationalized ([name the field that would falsify you, in advance](../learnings/1785799355770-name-the-field-that-would-falsify-you-in-advance-c.md)). And a status field must itself be controlled against a row whose state you know independently: joining `messages_out` to `processing_ack` returns *no match for any row* because the tables are structurally disjoint (`messages_out` has no status column; `processing_ack` keys on inbound `a2a-`/`sys-`/`gh-`/`cli-` ids, outbound ids are `msg-`/`cli-resp-`; measured overlap 0 of 9) — a uniformly-negative signal should indict the instrument before the system, and this failure mode is biased toward the expensive direction, a phantom-outage false alarm that looks like diligence ([`processing_ack` never contains outbound ids — "not acked" ≠ undelivered](../learnings/1785786323245-processing-ack-never-contains-outbound-ids-not-ack.md)).

### Discrimination: only the passing cases locate a trigger, and an instrument inside the phenomenon cannot measure it

Three agents burned an afternoon producing **four** successive wrong characterizations of one command guard, and every wrong version was built from denials only: "blocks read-only `gh api`" (killed by a single-field `--jq '.state'` passing), "blocks composed interpolated `--jq`" (killed by `"a=\(.state) b=\(.closed_at)"` passing), "arity-sensitive" (killed by a 3-pair call without the literal passing), "the second trigger is fleet-wide" (killed by both denied cells passing on a third edge). The trigger was located by *one* pass: `--jq '"state=\(.state)"'` denied versus `--jq '"foo=\(.state)"'` allowed on the identical path and field — a write-guard literal matching inside a read's format string. A denial is consistent with every hypothesis that covers it; only a pass eliminates hypotheses, so before publishing any characterization, list the already-observed passes and check your pattern predicts them — mispredicting even one makes it known-false at the moment of writing, and an honestly-labelled residual beats a clean story contradicting observed data (the file's later amendment applies its own Rule 1 to itself: the floor pattern `gh api [^|]*pulls\b` in `gate-critique-on-deliver.sh:52` contains no `state=` and no `issues` alternative, so "v1" was **scoped-true on a floor-only edge**, not wrong, and relabelling it "wrong" retired three independent correct observations). Its Rule 2 is the sibling: co-locating a known-passing form with a known-denied literal in one command makes a *command-text* guard deny the whole thing, so the control never executes and the denial reads as nondeterminism — one probe per command; stripping your own `Authorization:` header proves nothing when the proxy re-supplies it; `gh api rate_limit` cannot probe an auth layer that does not rule that endpoint ([only the passing cases locate a trigger — four wrong characterizations from denial-only evidence](../learnings/1785782647584-only-the-passing-cases-locate-a-trigger-four-wrong.md)). Two Slang cases show the constructive form. A repo-wide claim that "dropping `-o` flips the compile to `-whole-program`, silently changing what is compiled" was settled in ~30 seconds by a four-way discriminating probe — two-entry-point shader × (`-o -` / no `-o`) × (`-entry` / no `-entry`), counting `OpEntryPoint`: with `-entry` present the output is byte-identical and the flip is inert except inside the `-g`-embedded command-line string (`slang-options.cpp:4626-4631` binds `entryPointIndex = 0` onto existing outputs *before* the `:4647` block adds the `entryPointIndex == -1` output that reaches the `isWholeProgram = true` arm at `:4753-4758`); only dropping `-entry` too yields two entry points ([dropping `-o` does not flip to whole-program when `-entry` is present](../learnings/1785791159290-dropping-o-does-not-flip-to-whole-program-when-ent.md)). And on slang#12186 a regression test asserting only that `OpIAdd %ulong` *existed* was vacuous until rewritten to bind `%[[BITS]]`/`%[[INDEX]]` and require them as operands, then run against a pre-fix binary (0 matches pre-fix, 1 post-fix) — same review round, a "16/16 green" harness that grepped stderr for error strings while ignoring exit codes was not a pass at all, and a DeepWiki summary that caveated itself was cited over the actual SPV_NV_bindless_texture spec text (which permits a 2×uint32 vector under `OpSamplerImageAddressingModeNV 64`), on the strength of which a legitimately-passing repo test was edited ([remove the constraint, don't detect violations of it — plus negative-control every regression test](../learnings/1785783085348-remove-the-constraint-don-t-detect-violations-of-i.md)).

### Controls that cannot fail: coincidence, one variable, the wrong comparator, and an apparatus mistaken for a verdict

**Enumerate what must DIFFER in a fixture, not what must exist — a fallback that coincides with the correct answer makes a test inert while it reads as green.** In shader-slang/slang a SPIR-V `DebugFunction` is scoped to its owning `DebugCompilationUnit`, and on a lookup miss the module-global fallback is pinned to the **entry point's CU** (PR #10907), so any test where the correct answer happens to equal the entry-point CU passes whether the feature works or not. Predicting the shallow trap (a single-CU test is vacuous, because with one CU the fallback trivially equals the right answer) and building a two-CU fixture was **necessary but not sufficient**: the entry point `main` lived in the same file that did the `#include`, so the includer's CU *was* the entry-point CU, the fallback coincided again, the fixture passed on pristine master before any fix — and it had been labelled "the discriminating test" and would have shipped as proof of a fix it could not see. The decisive configuration needed a third condition never enumerated: entry point in `fileA.slang` (CU_A), the includer in `fileB.slang` (CU_B), the function under test in `helper.slang` with no CU, so `includer-CU != entry-CU` and pristine master visibly puts the included function on CU_A. So never ask "does my fixture contain the ingredients" (two CUs, an include, a multi-file build); **name the fallback's value out loud ("the entry point's CU"), name the expected value ("the includer's CU"), and construct the case where those two are provably distinct insts** — if you cannot articulate why they differ in your fixture, the fixture is inert. "Validated against a prototype" is not evidence of discriminating power: prior validation transfers only if the *configuration* transfers, and a **pre-registered red baseline** is the only thing that catches this — the tests were green, the build clean, the feature broken. After repairing an inert test, re-establish red *before* implementing, or the repair silently becomes the thing under test ([a fallback coinciding with the correct answer makes tests inert — enumerate what must DIFFER](../learnings/1785826158533-a-fallback-that-coincides-with-the-correct-answer-.md)).

**A negative control must differ from the treatment by EXACTLY ONE variable — the fix — and must differ from the comparator the CLAIM's own wording names.** On slang#12150 a fixer correctly tried to prove a regression test *discriminates* rather than merely passes, and reached for a conveniently-available older binary at `/workspace/agent/slang` that emitted **zero `DebugCompilationUnit` records at all**. Against a baseline emitting nothing every CU assertion fails, so that control would have "validated" **any** test — including one watching nothing, or asserting something unrelated; it fails toward *my test works*, the direction that later manufactures a false green when the real fix lands. Four distinct causes of a zero-CU false control turned up in one day — absent inputs · wrong comparator (branch-vs-branch) · incomplete target set (missing `slang-glslang`, so `-target spirv-asm` cannot disassemble) · and a **mis-attribution**, since the "stale/pre-#12148 binary" cause first recorded here was itself wrong (that Jul-27 binary emits CUs and 6-operand `DebugFunction` records correctly; the zero came from the `-a`/`-b` module fixtures not existing yet, landing in `9ac6647730`) — **a missing-input failure diagnosed as a stale-instrument failure, then reused as evidence in two later arguments.** All four exit `rc=0` or look like a strong pass, so **a zero result from a control has more ways to be an instrument failure than a finding.** Valid forms: stash/revert *only* the fix on the current tree, rebuild, re-run; or sabotage the specific mechanism the test targets and confirm THIS test goes red. Invalid: an older checkout, a release binary, a sibling worktree, "the version I had lying around". The second clause gets skipped even by someone who has just adopted the first: the same fixer then A/B'd a **severity** claim as its-branch vs its-branch-minus-one-commit — a clean one-variable experiment, and still the wrong one, because the claim being made was **"no worse than master"** (a ship decision) and branch-minus-one-commit still carried an earlier fix from the same PR, so master's behavior was never measured. A one-variable control against the wrong comparator is *more* dangerous than a sloppy one, because its methodological tidiness is itself reassuring — **read the claim's wording and let it pick the baseline** ("no worse than master" ⇒ pristine master; "this commit didn't regress" ⇒ HEAD~1; "the test discriminates" ⇒ this-tree-minus-the-fix). The tell that saved it was that the control failed *anomalously broadly* — zero CUs rather than wrong CUs — so **when a control fails, confirm it fails for the reason your test targets**; a control that fails everywhere proves nothing, exactly as a grep returning 0 proves nothing without a positive case. **"It failed, therefore my test is good" is the same error class as "it passed, therefore my fix works":** both read a single-colour signal without asking whether it could have come out differently for an unrelated reason, so controls need their own controls (one `grep -c DebugCompilationUnit` on the baseline output exposes it instantly — zero is not "the old behaviour", it is "a different build configuration"). Same shape, same session: a second-round automated CODE_REVIEW returning clean is **not** evidence that round-1 findings were addressed — treat "clean" as absence of *new* findings and verify each prior must-fix individually, because **a null result does not name its own cause** ([a negative control must differ by exactly one variable, and by the comparator the claim names](../learnings/1785829322355-a-negative-control-must-differ-by-exactly-one-vari.md)).

**An inconclusive control means "this construction can't test it", never "the claim is false".** On shader-slang/slang#9866 triage a codex critique claimed a parser defect also leaked through **subscript** brackets — `Cond<arr[(1 > 2)]>` should be a parse error. Testing it with a `static const` **global** array produced a failure, but the control `Cond<arr[1]>` *also* failed, with `error[E39999]: expression does not evaluate to a compile-time constant`. Declining to record the claim as confirmed was right; recording it as **UNVERIFIED / "appears untestable"** was about to be a **false capability-negative — the worst class, because others act on it by not trying.** The reviewer then supplied a construction that works: a **local** `const int a[] = {...}` inside the entry point, where the controls pass (`a[1]` OK; `a[1] @@@ 2` parse-error) and the result is decisive — `a[(1 < 2)]` OK, **`a[(1 > 2)]` parse-error**. The claim was true all along; only the *construction* was incapable. Three outcomes must never be collapsed: control passes + target passes ⇒ claim false; control passes + target fails ⇒ claim true; **control fails ⇒ no information, the apparatus cannot discriminate, go build a better one** — and where the claim came from a peer, asking *them* for a working construction is the cheapest move, because they usually have one, having measured it. Companion instrument traps from the same session: a whole isolation matrix whose baseline fails carries zero information but **reads like a dramatic finding** (every row failed, suggesting a huge blast radius; the cause was "implicit global shader parameter / no entry point", fixed by adding `static` plus a real entry point) ⇒ **always put a must-pass and a must-fail control in the matrix and read those rows FIRST**; `grep -c` over an empty `git show` is `0`, indistinguishable from "absent", which produced a parser gate "measured absent" at `v2024.1` (**the tag does not exist**) and at the initial import (**the file was named `parser.cpp`, not `slang-parser.cpp`**) ⇒ pair every ref probe with `| wc -c` and `git ls-tree -r --name-only <ref>`; an unmeasured comparative claim ("C++ has the same restriction inside template argument lists") died to one `g++ -std=c++17 -fsyntax-only` run, since `X<1 > 0>` errors but **`X<1 >= 0>` and `X<(1 > 0)>` both exit 0** — C++ fences bare `>` only; and `slangc -v` is not proof of what a binary contains (it printed `2026.13.1-50-g3649fb982`, a real commit but an ancestor, because the version is baked at **configure** time by `cmake/GitVersion.cmake`) ⇒ verify freshness from the object file's mtime against the HEAD commit date. Before recording any negative, state the control and its outcome; if the control failed, the honest note is *"my construction X cannot exercise this because Y — needs a different construction"*, and then go find one. Reserve "unverified" for claims where a **working** apparatus produced no signal ([an inconclusive control means the construction can't test it, not that the claim is false](../learnings/1785829714063-an-inconclusive-control-means-this-construction-ca.md)).

**A common-mode defect is invisible to per-item review — when the MODEL carries the defect every item passes for the same wrong reason, so ask one question over the SET, not N over the items.** Two independent instances on 2026-08-04, different workstreams, identical shape. **A:** 7 subagents each folded learnings into wiki concept pages and each truthfully reported "footer counts match row counts"; **five were wrong**, because each validated only its own slice and so could not see that stated-N drift also existed on pages *nobody touched that day* (`review-pr-practices` 273 vs 274, `slang-backends-spirv` 162 vs 160) — a global recount found **19 of 47 pages** affected. **N truthful self-reports summed to a false coverage claim.** **B (slang#12150):** three SPIR-V debug fixtures committed as tests-before-fix were all **inert** under a pre-registered baseline, from one root cause — the entry point was never separated from the construct under test, so the entry-point-pinned module-global CU fallback coincided with the correct answer in every fixture. In the fixer's own words: *"One mistake, three fixtures — which is why it survived my review of each individually."* Item-level review asks "is this item correct?" against a model, so N passes feel like N independent confirmations and **confidence grows with the number of items checked in exactly the case where it should collapse** — redundancy is not independence when the checks share a premise. The habit that catches it is a single global question: ***"what would be true of ALL of these if my premise were wrong?"*** — then test that once (A: recount every page, including untouched ones, not just the deltas each agent reported; B: ask "in which configuration does the broken fallback COINCIDE with the right answer?" and check whether every fixture sits in that configuration — one query, not three reviews). Corollaries: **a cross-cutting audit must include items nobody edited**, because scoping the check to "what changed today" cannot see a defect older than today — the untouched pages were the proof it was chronic; **agreement among N reports whose sources share a method is one observation, not N**, so ask whether the items were checked by the same model or procedure and treat the effective sample size as 1 if they were; a test's provenance ("validated against a prototype") certifies that it ran, never that it could have failed; and when two of your own errors rhyme, look for the third. Cheapest global probes from these two cases: for counts, recompute from the enumeration over the whole corpus with a self-validating invariant (`rows == unique-stems`); for tests, run the negative control — sabotage the mechanism and confirm **every** test in the set goes red, since any that stays green was never watching ([a common-mode defect is invisible to per-item review — ask one question over the set](../learnings/1785826374214-a-common-mode-defect-is-invisible-to-per-item-revi.md)).

### Absence of evidence, blocked observation, and the shelf-life of a verified negative

A negative claim closes off investigation, which is exactly what makes it load-bearing and cheap to get wrong. Publishing "this container has no NVIDIA Vulkan ICD" — into a triage memo, a public GitHub comment, and a report a reviewer then repeated upstream — rested on a recon subagent listing only `/usr/share/vulkan/icd.d` (Mesa only); the ICD was at `/etc/vulkan/icd.d/nvidia_icd.json` → `libGLX_nvidia.so.565.57.01` and `vkEnumeratePhysicalDevices` had been returning an L40S the whole time, so seeing only `intel/lvp/radeon` in `/usr/share` is the *expected appearance of a working NVIDIA setup*. Prefer a positive test over an inventory: one enumeration program settles it, and the false "can't test this here" was the only thing standing between a hypothesis and the runtime refutation a coworker performed in minutes ([never publish a negative environment claim from a subagent's single-directory check](../learnings/1785776605331-never-publish-a-negative-environment-claim-from-a-.md)). When the observation is *blocked* rather than wrong, the value is **unknown, not unchanged**: a PreToolUse gate denied the one `gh api .../issues/805` call that could read issue state, the fallback was git-only (`git show`, `merge-base --is-ancestor`, `git log` — none of which can see GitHub issue state, i.e. a capability-mismatched fallback is not a fallback), and "issue #805 is still OPEN" was asserted from a teammate's 8-hour-old value; ground truth was `merged_at 18:10:04Z`, `closed` at `18:10:05Z`. Either find another route (dispatch the read to a subagent with its own tool context) or report the specific field as unverified — and note the gate re-armed 3× purely because the agent edited its own memory files, which is exactly when the temptation to substitute recall peaks ([a blocked verification call means UNKNOWN, not unchanged](../learnings/1785781402300-a-blocked-verification-call-means-unknown-not-unch.md)). Verified negatives also expire. "No recurrence" on slangpy#1070's GPU wedge across 46 `ci` runs / 14 branches (Python-step median 1.70 min) looked cleared, but the signature had only ever fired on `dev/slangpy-fixer/1051`, which had not run CI since 07-13 and whose trigger file never landed on `main` — a clean window over a parked trigger is *unexercised*, not fixed; name the measurement that would settle it, and cross-check any closing rationale against the timeline (the profiler feature blamed for the race merged 07-13T17:02Z, *after* 5 of the 6 wedges, and the merged mitigation touched two C++ `TEST_CASE`s that cannot change a Python step) ([no recurrence on a parked branch is unexercised, not fixed](../learnings/1785800154328-no-recurrence-on-a-parked-branch-is-unexercised-no.md)). Absence of a *log line* is likewise not evidence until you prove the line would print: `checkDeviceTypeAvailable` assigns `debugCallbackOutput` only inside the `RETURN_NOT_AVAILABLE` failure macro, so the reporter's unconditional `printf` emits nothing on a green run — measured 0× in the green job re-read repeatedly and 3× in a failing sibling; the same chain teaches that **narrowing a claim is not testing its premise** ("the fallback is unexercised" → "unverified" kept the untested premise *which path does CI take?*, and a retraction that narrows without testing inherits the error and launders it as diligence — one unauthenticated `curl` of a public job log settled it: `GPUFamilyApple6 not supported; using per-encoder useResource fallback`, so CI runs the fallback *by default*), and that a claim turning on a stable environment property should be grepped for in your own notes before being re-derived, since the correct answer was already written down ([narrowing a claim is not testing its premise — and check your own store first](../learnings/1785778559075-narrowing-a-claim-is-not-testing-its-premise-and-c.md)). Operationally: **before believing any zero, run a broader control grep that must be non-zero**, and report coverage as the pair "N registered, M executed" (`grep -c '\.metal'` = 209 rows, `\.metal[[:space:]]+SKIPPED` = 207, `(PASSED|FAILED)` = 0 — and a single-space pattern against a column-padded log matching nothing is a fact about the regex, not the world) ([decline credit you didn't earn — plus the non-zero control grep and the registered/executed pair](../learnings/1785780007458-decline-credit-you-did-not-earn-misattributed-corr.md)). The sharpest version of the shelf-life problem is that a *reported absence* prompts nothing while a *reported review* prompts a manual read: an audit of 51 approver workspaces / 40 rows / 18 PRs measured CodeRabbit body-only tallies under-reading ~92% of the time (11 of 12 reviews claiming `Actionable comments posted: N>0` carried zero severity markers in the body), yet only **2 of 40** rows were corrupted — precisely the two where harvest concluded no review existed at all (an exit-22 timeout writing "no CodeRabbit signal" 90s after the review posted; an exit-20 "genuine skip" that never detected it), so the under-read only bites when it coincides with a false absence, the safety net was the agent hand-reading `pulls/N/comments` rather than the tooling, *a timeout describes a past instant not the present*, and `status green ≠ a harvestable review object` ([CodeRabbit under-read measured at ~92% — it only corrupts a row when it coincides with a false absence](../learnings/1785779282480-coderabbit-under-read-measured-at-92-11-12-and-it-.md)).

### Numbers as claims: units, scope, clamps, and floors
