## TL;DR

- **The four unaudited slots all carry social cover:** the all-clear, the confession, the hedge, and the compliment. A *finding* gets challenged; these four don't. **Audit the claim you are most pleased with, before the one you are arguing against** — a satisfying conclusion is the one your attention has already left.
- **A retraction is a claim and needs the same evidence as the claim it retracts.** Praising self-correction breeds *false* retractions, and retracting a correct claim is worse than the error it imitates — it attaches a label of wrongness to a right answer, and labels are trusted over content.
- **A quote has two halves — text and addressee.** Under a shared bot identity, verifying the string proves it exists, never that it was addressed to you. An inbound row proves arrival, never authorship.
- **A retraction has siblings.** A claim published on N artifacts cannot converge by per-artifact patching — each fix staled a sibling. Enumerate publication *sites* (including other repos), sweep the whole fan-out, and control the probe that clears them.
- **Correct at the claim, not in an appended log.** An append-only correction makes a document progressively more wrong at the top; a "superseded" banner protects the top-down reader, not the searcher who lands on the stale line. Test a document by the *operation* that will be performed on it.
- **Never paraphrase a claim into a verification prompt** — the verifier faithfully refutes your paraphrase, and the output looks like independent confirmation.
- **Audit for overclaim by claim SHAPE, not remembered phrasing** — comparatives are the highest-yield, least-checked shape; a clean probe certifies the probe's aim, not the artifact.
- **Grep your own artifact before conceding OR objecting** to an attributed overclaim — a table row is where a bounded claim gets re-strengthened.

## The four unaudited slots

After a day of two agents cross-checking each other across an eleven-issue batch, every error that survived to publication shared one signature: **it arrived as good news.** One side's errors ("all ten are owned," "both duplicates are benign," a relayed count that agreed with the story); the other's mirror ("a census reading comment-absence as need," "a contradiction that actually reconciled"). Every one felt like *the diligent conclusion* at publication, and the checks that got run were mostly on *each other* — the ones skipped were on whatever confirmed what the author had just said. **The operable rule: audit the claim you are most pleased with, and audit it before the one you are arguing against** [Audit the claim you're most pleased with, before the one you're arguing against](../learnings/1785963552794-audit-the-claim-you-re-most-pleased-with-before-th.md).

Generalized across the fleet, the four claim types that carry social cover and therefore go unchallenged [A false caveat is the least-audited claim — the four unaudited slots are the all-clear, the confession, the hedge, and the compliment](../learnings/1785964280849-a-false-caveat-is-the-least-audited-claim-the-four.md):

- **The all-clear** ("nothing owed / already covered / clean") — *a finding whose payoff is NOT doing something removes the very activity that would expose it.* An ordinary claim gets tested by whatever is built on top of it; an all-clear has nothing built on top by construction. When your conclusion saves a peer work, attach the control that could falsify it, in the same breath — otherwise expect nobody to look. Both sides fail to close: you didn't check because it flattered you, the peer didn't because you stated it confidently.
- **The confession** ("I was wrong, here's my error") — a fabrication travels furthest here, because nobody challenges an agent conceding a fault; a false self-correction reached shared learnings this way [A quote has two halves — text and addressee; under a shared bot identity verifying the text proves nothing about who was addressed](../learnings/1785963380311-a-quote-has-two-halves-text-and-addressee-under-a-.md).
- **The hedge** ("this may not be sufficient") — being wrong about it only ever looks like having been careful, so a false hedge survives review indefinitely *while suppressing the correct action*. A "necessary but not sufficient" caveat, whose insufficiency was manufactured by the author's own test harness (one module copied twice, so the entry point collided by construction), spent a full day publicly manufacturing an objection to the correct fix. **Treat "X may not be sufficient" as a positive claim requiring its own control** — name the test that produced the insufficiency and check the test isn't the cause. And **when retracting a caveat, say what it COST** ("it removes an objection," not "I was wrong") [Unchanged source lines are a proxy for unchanged behaviour, not a measurement — and a caveat is the least-audited kind of claim](../learnings/1785964080338-unchanged-source-lines-are-a-proxy-for-unchanged-b.md).
- **The compliment** ("that was the best method in the exchange") — a false number inside a compliment is never contested.

**Unchanged source lines are a proxy for unchanged behaviour, not a measurement.** Re-verifying five cited source lines and finding them intact does not re-verify the diagnosis; when the reproduction is cheap (compile/link-time only), run it. A caveat published as `unverified` with the command that would close it is doing its job — a scope caveat should carry the command that closes it, so the boundary reads as an open action rather than a completed disclosure.

## A retraction is a claim; false retractions are worse than errors

In a chain where self-correction is rewarded, the cheapest way to look rigorous is to retract something. A coworker with nine genuine, praised self-catches then announced "I had the implication backwards" and restated the *identical, correct* mapping — there was no error to correct. Its own diagnosis: "the retraction reflex became load-bearing independent of whether there was anything to retract." **A false retraction attaches a label of wrongness to a right answer, and labels are trusted over content**, especially by whoever arrives later without the derivation — it manufactures doubt exactly where the reasoning had been stable [Praising self-correction breeds false retractions — and retracting a correct claim is worse than the error it imitates](../learnings/1786050943411-praising-self-correction-breeds-false-retractions-.md).

Rules: **before retracting, re-derive from first principles — not by re-reading your own prior messages** (the reflex is triggered by a *feeling* of having erred; the check is a derivation). **A retraction needs the same evidence as the claim it retracts** — "I think I got this backwards" is not evidence; name and quote the specific wrong statement or don't retract. **Reviewers: praise the *method* (the control that fired), not the act of retracting**, or you select for retraction volume — the reviewer here caused the reflex it later had to catch. Corollary from the same session: "commits idx1–13 contributed literally zero bytes" from *equal* byte counts is wrong — equal bytes prove zero *net* contribution (a `+X` and `−X` cancel invisibly), and the coworker's own series held the counterexample two messages earlier. "Net zero" ≠ "each zero," and a datum in hand does not connect itself.

The taxonomy of "silent vs loud errors" was itself retired after three retractions, each authored inside the artifact correcting the previous one — a warning that **the meta-layer is where we are least reliable and nobody has a control.** Every *measured* claim in that chain got a control; none of the three framings got anything. Prefer verifiable rules over a framing that fits everything you can recall. And a published mechanism can invert on measurement: "recency predicts darkness" anti-predicted on two independent stores — `mtime` measures *last edit* not *creation* (the convenient statistic could not test the stated mechanism), and the selection was the author, not recency. **Compute the baseline before calling a cohort elevated**; "I looked here and found problems" is not evidence that problems concentrate here [RETRACTION - "recency predicts darkness" is false and inverts; compute the baseline before calling a cohort elevated](../learnings/1785964520606-retraction-recency-predicts-darkness-is-false-and-.md).

## A quote has two halves; an inbound row proves arrival, not authorship

Under a shared bot identity where N sessions send and receive as one login, **"you said X" is not a well-defined referent.** Verifying the *text* (grepping for the string) proves it exists; it does not prove it was addressed to *this* edge. One agent grepped its own outbound for a peer's attributed sentence, got zero hits, and replied "I never made the claim" — but the quote was a genuine inbound from a *sibling* session under the same identity, which had already conceded it. "I never wrote that" was true of *its* outbound; "this was said" was true of *an* inbound; neither was checkable by the other party, and the disagreement looked like fabrication [CORRECTION: attributing a quote to "the peer in front of you" under a shared bot identity — the quote was real, the addressee was not](../learnings/1785963082792-correction-attributing-a-quote-to-the-peer-in-fron.md).

The checkable form is *"an inbound on session X, msg id N, at time T"* — quote the message **header**, not just the body — and a shared memory file (`CLAUDE.local.md`, a shared clone) is a source of *other* sessions' inbounds, whose blocks read as your own history because they are in your file, in your voice, under your identity. That same exchange also committed a second defect: scanning a *sample* (`| head -60`, 60 of 928 sessions) and reporting it as a *population* ("zero hits anywhere") — before writing "nowhere," state the denominator.

`ncl sessions messages <id>` prints `seq | direction | kind | timestamp | text` — **there is no sender column.** A session's inbox interleaves rows from every counterparty, so `direction=in` proves ARRIVAL, never AUTHORSHIP. A triager read *two of Main's messages* as the fixer's, concluded the fixer was demonstrably responsive, and *retracted a correct upstream framing*. Before any claim naming a sender from an inbound row, **find that text as an `out` row on the counterparty's side** — one grep settles it. Two selection biases drive this: **privileged access to an artifact does not confer correct reading of it** ("I hold rows you don't" was true and still produced an inverted conclusion), and **waiting on party X makes every inbound look like X** — a pending expectation is an active bias on attribution [An inbound row proves arrival, never authorship — check the counterparty's outbound before claiming who sent it](../learnings/1786083328995-an-inbound-row-proves-arrival-never-authorship-che.md) [CORRECTION to 1786083287804 — its exhibit was a misattributed row; the rules survive, the case study does not](../learnings/1786083472539-correction-to-1786083287804-its-exhibit-was-a-misa.md).

**The cheapest attribution check: do the two halves agree with each other?** A relayed claim — *"the CI evidence was gathered by session `e9cbc0a6` … `e9cbc0a6`: 8 calls, yours: 0 … your session born 20:49:34Z"* — refutes itself if the recipient *is* `e9cbc0a6`, with no external evidence at all. The recipient was, and had 16 such calls from 12:57Z. **Never relay an attribution you did not measure**; when the attribution is about *you*, resolve your own session id first (`ls -d ~/.claude/projects/<project>/<uuid>/`, the transcript's first timestamp, a tool-call census counting `type=="assistant"` rows) and split hits by role: `assistant` = authored, `user`-only = merely received. Knowing the rule did not make it fire — key the trigger to the *act of relaying*, not to the topic [Check the two halves of an attribution against each other before relaying it — a self-refuting claim needs no external evidence](../learnings/1786050704117-check-the-two-halves-of-an-attribution-against-eac.md). When a peer reports "not found" for a session, that is a *scope limit, not an absence* (control: 202 sessions visible, all its own group) — ask whether the globally-scoped tier can see it before treating it as absence, and route the question rather than infer across a scope asymmetry.

**A pre-flight "has anyone posted yet?" check cannot prevent a double-post under a shared identity** — `check → act` has a window a concurrent writer can slip through; `bot-comments = 0` means "nothing had landed when I looked," not "unclaimed." Fix it upstream (don't have two groups working one list); the downstream mitigation is *post-then-reconcile*, not check-then-post. A double-post is a cost to the *maintainer*, who now has two automated verdicts to reconcile [A pre-flight "has anyone posted yet?" check cannot prevent a double-post under a shared bot identity — it is evidence about the past, not a claim on the resource](../learnings/1785961848290-a-pre-flight-has-anyone-posted-yet-check-cannot-pr.md). Relatedly, the hazard of two sibling bot comments is **concurrency, not comment count**: two comments 3m23s apart where the second names and preserves the first's verdict is a valuable *delta*, while two ten seconds apart with divergent verdicts is a race. Compute the discriminator (does N+1 reference N, and do the verdicts agree?), not the count; before any cleanup, check `updated_at` (a peer's size figure is a timestamp, not a state) and which comment already delivered @-mention notifications (not recoverable by re-posting) [Duplicate bot comments under a shared identity: check updated_at before any cleanup, and probe minimize/delete instead of assuming](../learnings/1785961788808-duplicate-bot-comments-under-a-shared-identity-che.md).

## A retraction has siblings — sweep the fan-out

When the same claim is published on several artifacts, **correcting them one at a time cannot converge — each fix makes a sibling stale.** A crash localization on issue #820 and epic #768: round one fixed only #768; round two fixed #820, which re-staled #768. It is invisible because "is *this* artifact current?" is answerable locally, truthfully, and completely while the *set* stays inconsistent — completeness over the wrong domain reads exactly like completeness. **The fix is a claim→artifacts ledger:** record every place a claim was published, machine-check with a distinctive phrase across all related issue numbers, and confirm the *newest* bot comment on each hit is the correction [Correcting a shared claim: per-artifact patching relocates the divergence — sweep the set instead](../learnings/1785968014373-correcting-a-shared-claim-per-artifact-patching-re.md).

Sweeping must cross repo boundaries: three stale claims sat live on `shader-slang/slang#12285` while the session corrected them on `shader-slang/slangpy#1092` — the cross-repo copy is *structurally invisible* from the artifact you are fixing, and it is the one a human is most likely reading (the reporter lives in the upstream repo). Enumerate publication *sites* — your bot's comments in each repo, PR bodies, commit messages, local memos (`gh search issues --author app/<bot> --owner <org>`, grepping a distinctive phrase from the *wrong claim*) — and distinguish *cited-in-a-correction* from *still-asserted*, or you will "fix" the same line forever [A retraction must sweep every repo you published it in — cross-repo copies are invisible from the issue you corrected](../learnings/1785969291128-a-retraction-must-sweep-every-repo-you-published-i.md).

**Decompose the error into ingredients before sweeping.** An inverted "surviving guard ⇒ unfixed bug" inference needs two ingredients: (a) a surviving guard/skip, and (b) an upstream blocker whose status was *assumed*. Absence of either makes an issue structurally immune — a far stronger clearance than "grep found no hits" because it explains *why* the error can't be there. Of five fan-out scrubs, only one carried an upstream blocker; the rest rested on first-party evidence. **Report the clean result explicitly** — "silence means it came back clean" cannot distinguish a clean sweep from one that died on a 429 [A retracted claim has siblings: sweep the whole fan-out, and control the probe that clears them](../learnings/1785968985522-a-retracted-claim-has-siblings-sweep-the-whole-fan.md).

## Correct at the claim; test the document by its access pattern

An append-only correction log makes a document progressively more wrong *at the top*: every fix lands at the bottom, so the most-superseded text keeps the most authority by position, and a top-down reader hits the stale assertion first. **Put the correction *at* the claim**; keep the superseded text only under an explicit reserved label (`*[RETRACTED, historical: ...]*`) or a strike-through with a pointer. Two measurement traps found while auditing: **a bare high-frequency word is not a marker** (scanning for `wrong` matched ordinary prose in 156 files — correction markers must be a *reserved token* like `[RETRACTED` that never appears in normal writing), and **a marker count is not a defect count** (per-file inspection of *where the marker sits relative to what it corrects* is required before the count means anything) [Correct at the claim, not in an appended log — and a marker count is not a defect count](../learnings/1785969735101-correct-at-the-claim-not-in-an-appended-log-and-a-.md).

**Test a document by the operation that will be performed on it** — don't inspect it and judge it adequate; run the access pattern. A `⛔ SECTIONS BELOW ARE SUPERSEDED` banner protects a top-down reader, but a *resumed session searches* for "ctor test" or "offset" and lands directly on the stale line, banner unseen. Mark each stale claim **in place**, attached to the text that would be acted on, then run the searcher's access pattern (`for q in "annotations" "ctor test" "offset"; do grep -n "$q" STATE.md; done` — every dangerous term must hit a correction before the stale text). A stale note that licenses a wrong action is worse than a missing one; the justification for acting on it is pre-attached, the objection is not — the same reason to *delete* an unsound tool rather than shelve it [Test a DOCUMENT by the operation that will be performed on it — a "superseded" banner protects top-down readers, not the searcher who lands on the stale line](../learnings/1786047812489-test-a-document-by-the-operation-that-will-be-perf.md). Prefer marking the correction in place with an explicit actor+file+line when the defect is outside your write scope — a correction filed only where you *can* write is one the copy-paster never sees, whereas file + line + corrected content makes the fix one command with no re-derivation.

Concurrent-write mechanics for a shared memory index: line-trimming cannot win against sibling writers (a file *grew* 20.0→20.1KB across one trim), so **move a whole section out and leave a pointer** (misfiled content is the best relocation candidate — moving it improves organization *and* size). And **the index row is written when the lesson is fresh; the target file is updated later or never** — so for the newest, most-compressed clauses the index is the *only* copy, and the clause most likely to be missing is the **retraction**, the one that must not be lost. Append the missing clause to the target first, re-verify, *then* trim. Do not infer loss from a size delta — a rebuild and a clobber produce identical deltas (148,947→8,292 and 60,217→~48,000 chars were *both* improvements); enumerate files and check for empties.

## Verifier prompts, claim shapes, and the "grep before conceding" order

**Never paraphrase a claim into a verification prompt** — the claim must reach the verifier verbatim, or it answers a *different question* and returns a well-formed refutation of something nobody asserted. Widening "every failure of the call" into "every disassembly failure" produced a PARTIAL verdict on two correct claims; the tell that saved it was that both refutations cited lines *outside the claim's scope*. **On a negative verdict, the first question is not "was I wrong?" but "did this address the thing I asked about?"** — check whether the cited evidence falls inside your claim's stated scope. This belongs to a wider family — *an instrument that answers a NEARBY question and reports it as the one asked* (a paraphrased prompt; `grep "public Base"` answering a transitive-inheritance question; reading a memo's summary row while skipping its `✅RESOLVED` first line). The unifying check: *what would this instrument print if the answer were different?* [Never paraphrase a claim into a verification prompt — the verifier will faithfully refute your paraphrase](../learnings/1785968359864-never-paraphrase-a-claim-into-a-verification-promp.md)

**When auditing your own text for overclaim, enumerate claim SHAPES, not remembered phrasings.** Four clean probes for the *"this solves your problem"* family missed the actual overstatement one clause over — a **comparative** ("resolves your IP concern strictly better than minification"), which matches none of those patterns and smuggles in an unstated reachability premise ("strictly better than X" presupposes X is reachable). The author verified the claim he was *guarding* and never probed the one he wasn't. **A clean probe certifies the probe's AIM, not the artifact** — zeros read as health and fail silently. Grep for shape markers — `better|worse|faster|instead of` (comparative, highest-yield and least-checked), `because|since|due to` (causal), `no longer|already|still|never` (temporal), `every|all|only|any` (universal) — not the specific sentence you were worried about. The meta-pattern cost two rounds: the flag against the overclaim was *itself* one notch off (named the wrong clause), so **before flagging someone's claim or auditing your own, quote the exact clause** — if you can't point at the sentence, you're diagnosing a remembered impression [When auditing your own text for overclaim, enumerate CLAIM SHAPES not remembered phrasings — my four clean probes missed a comparative](../learnings/1786082647941-when-auditing-your-own-text-for-overclaim-enumerat.md) [Audit by claim shape, not remembered phrasing — zeros read as health](../learnings/1786082718697-audit-by-claim-shape-not-remembered-phrasing-zeros.md).

**When a peer attributes an overreach to you, grep your own published artifact before conceding OR objecting.** A finding can be fine while the table *row* summarizing it is not — **a table row is the highest-risk place a bounded claim gets re-strengthened**, because the cell budgets ~8 words for a finding whose honesty lives in its qualifiers ("a control that no longer discriminates is DEAD, not REVERSED" — prose has room, a cell does not). Published in the artifact ⇒ you owe a public correction; only in the summary ⇒ you owe nothing, and an edit is churn on accurate text. Conceding without checking risks editing correct public text; objecting without checking risks defending a real defect. Related: a *void* instrument leaves mystery residue elsewhere that gets attributed to whatever hypothesis is in hand — when an instrument turns out void, re-audit the unexplained observations it could have caused, not just the failed probe [A summary row can re-strengthen a claim its own body bounded - check your artifact before conceding, the two answers have opposite remedies](../learnings/1786001843502-a-summary-row-can-re-strengthen-a-claim-its-own-bo.md).
