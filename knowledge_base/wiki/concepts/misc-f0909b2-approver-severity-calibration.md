## TL;DR

- An approver finding is **two separately-falsifiable claims**: (1) *the defect is real* (mechanism reproduces) and (2) *material enough not to ship as-is* (severity placement). Exhaustively verifying (1) produces **zero evidence** about (2) — but it *feels* like it does, and the accumulated rigor transfers unearned onto the placement.
- The calibration test that generalizes: **"Does this hurt anyone if every human ignores it?"** Reserve BLOCK for harm that needs no human to act — wrong codegen, ABI break, data loss, a red gate. A defect whose damage requires someone to follow printed advice (warn-only lint, test tooling with no CI gate) is `ABSTAIN_POLICY:CHALLENGER_CONCERN`, not BLOCK.
- Score human joins on the **falsifiable** reading. "A human must look; a human looked" scores every abstain correct no matter what — worthless. The falsifiable claim an ABSTAIN_POLICY actually makes is "material enough not to merge as-is", which a clean human APPROVE at your head (or head+1, verified) can **refute**. Log the refutation as a loss with the split stated; don't round it up to "well, the bug was real."
- An **ABSTAIN is not the safe default** — it *performs conscientiousness* and asserts "a human must look", and a wrong one wastes attention and corrupts calibration exactly as a wrong approve does. Verify an OPEN_GAP's load-bearing premises against source with the **same rigor** as an approval. "Silent" / "untested" / "no test covers" are claims requiring a read, not intuitions.
- **"Untested" ≠ "unsafe."** A coverage gap is OPEN_GAP only if a wrong answer in the uncovered cell is *reachable on a supported path* AND *produces a bad outcome*. Compute the arithmetic of the uncovered cell before grading it material — a false abstain sends a human to write a test that can only pass by construction.
- A **calibration prior about your aggregate behavior** (e.g. "I run over-conservative") is a reason to re-examine evidence, never a thumb on a specific verdict's scale — and n=1 (a single self-merge) must not swing the next same-shape call.
- Distinguish **closable gaps** (someone can run the available check cheaply ⇒ legit OPEN_GAP) from **structural unreachability** (no check reachable from a PR ⇒ flag + let it merge if the failure is loud, prompt, and bounded).

## Detection and severity fail independently

The clearest statement of the batch's central calibration lesson comes from slang#12455, where a BLOCK's detection was verified across five rounds — two independent edges, byte-level region diffs across three heads, a control recomputing each digest — and the defect confirmed live on merged master, yet a maintainer approved and the author merged [[detection and severity are separately falsifiable]](../learnings/1786454003222-detection-and-severity-are-separately-falsifiable-.md). Claim 1 ("the mechanism is real") survived; claim 2 ("material enough not to ship") was refuted in one click, and had never been tested by anyone. The trap: **every round of confirming the mechanism raised confidence in a verdict that did not depend on the mechanism being confirmed.** The companion human-disagreement write-up on the same PR reaches the same place — the change was a warn-only diagnostics-catalog lint in generated-test tooling with no CI gate whose only harm (a provenance digest overwritten) required someone to *follow* printed remediation, so it belonged at CHALLENGER_CONCERN, not BLOCK [[slang#12455 merged with my BLOCK's defect live — severity placement was wrong for warn-only tooling]](../learnings/1786442149465-approver-human-disagreement-slang-12455-merged-wit.md). Both frame this as a **policy-level signal** (the procedure mandates BLOCK on any 🔴 in the review doc, and the review reported 🔴), worth raising upstream rather than a rule to quietly bend — the fix belongs in *what counts as 🔴 in test-only tooling*.

## Naming a soft spot is not discharging it — but "untested" is not "unsafe" either

Two same-session slang-rhi#828 learnings capture opposite errors and together give the sharp rule. First: an approver reached WOULD_APPROVE *while explicitly writing* "this is my soft spot: the 12-byte-block × multi-subresource cell is uncovered" — DECISION_REVIEW returned must-fix on exactly that cell [[naming a soft spot is not discharging it]](../learnings/1786461679840-approver-challenger-miss-naming-a-soft-spot-is-not.md). The tell: when a write-up contains "my soft spot / the one uncovered corner / narrower than stated," that sentence is a trigger to abstain, not a caveat to append to an approval — and "covered intersection of covered dimensions" is a rationalization whenever the intersection *is* the change's raison d'être (A-covered + B-covered ≠ (A×B)-covered). But that atom's *conclusion* — that the ABSTAIN was right — was itself **superseded** by a later same-PR learning: the posited failure was never checked for *reachability*, and it isn't reachable [[SUPERSEDES the #828 soft-spot learning: "untested" is not "unsafe"]](../learnings/1786463614614-approver-clause-gap-supersedes-the-828-soft-spot-l.md). On Vulkan (the only backend using 12-byte offset alignment) RGB32 `rowPitch = width × 12` so every `sizeInBytes` is a multiple of 12 and `calcAligned(sizeInBytes, 12)` is the identity — zero padding, no arithmetic for an off-by-one to inhabit. The full swing was WOULD_APPROVE → false-ABSTAIN → WOULD_APPROVE, and the middle abstain *felt* like rigor (heeding a critique, erring to caution) but "caution not grounded in a reachable failure is not conservatism." The unifying rule: **apply the conservative-lean bar's first clause — clears if the trigger is unreachable on a supported path — to the trigger YOU posit, not just the PR's claims**, and compute the uncovered cell's arithmetic before grading it. (Meta: a learning filed mid-decision can capture the wrong conclusion; date/pass-stamp reasoning while a verdict is still swinging and be ready to supersede.)

## An abstain performs conscientiousness — verify its premises

slangpy#1100 moved WOULD_APPROVE → ABSTAIN_POLICY:OPEN_GAP → WOULD_APPROVE across three DECISION_REVIEW rounds; the abstain rested on two premises ("silent", "untested") that both failed verification against source — the consequence was checkable via `NativeHandle::is_valid()`, and the underlying RHI result was tested upstream (a line the author had literally printed earlier that session) [[an abstain performs conscientiousness — verify its premises as hard as an approval's]](../learnings/1786463095347-approver-human-disagreement-step-3-gap-severity-an.md). Two biases pulled the same way and only one was guarded: the *approve* was framed as "the flattering call" while an ABSTAIN *also* flatters (deferring to a reviewer who just caught you is the path of least resistance), and over-correcting a genuinely under-argued clear into an abstain on *fresh unverified premises* just trades one unsubstantiated claim for another. Watch the **reversal direction**: approve→abstain→approve under reviewer pressure signals you may be landing where the last reviewer pointed rather than on verified fact — re-derive from artifacts, not from the critique. Check the *default*: if a feature's trigger defaults to leaving existing callers unchanged (here `pipeline_compilation_mode` defaults to `serial`), the behavior change is scoped to opt-in users — the intent, not a defect.

## Calibration priors and the n=1 trap; the closable-vs-structural discriminant

A calibration prior is dangerous inside a single verdict. On slang-rhi#827 an approver's measured over-conservative history *pushed it to soften a red finding it had written itself* — recording ABSTAIN_POLICY past its own `bugs=1` count, which Step 2 (a parse, not a judgment: any 🔴 ⇒ BLOCK) forbids [[I softened past a red count I wrote myself — my over-conservative prior pushed me to do it]](../learnings/1786444820735-approver-false-safe-i-softened-past-a-red-count-i-.md). The structural tell: two self-consistent positions existed (bugs=1 ⇒ BLOCK, or discount the source ⇒ rewrite to bugs=0 ⇒ OPEN_GAP) and it took neither — a decision that contradicts a counter you wrote yourself is incoherent regardless of which branch is right. The rule: **a calibration prior is a reason to re-examine evidence, never a thumb on the scale**; note the direction (correcting for over-conservatism moved to the *hardest* state, against both the prior and the eventual human outcome). The same atom carries a supporting lesson — a contamination rule learned from *time-varying* state (post-merge CI colour) silently widened to a *timeless* property (an ABI break the diff either has or doesn't); scope a rule when you file it.

The same n=1 guard governs the merge-vs-abstain shape on slang-rhi#830, where an OPEN_GAP on a missing Vulkan-RT nonzero-offset test was refuted by an author self-merge [[ABSTAIN(OPEN_GAP) vs author self-merge-as-is]](../learnings/1786478395503-approver-human-disagreement-slang-rhi-830-abstain-.md). The transferable discriminant when the sole blocker is a missing regression test: not "is the arithmetic statically consistent" (you can always convince yourself of that — the trap codex caught) but **"is the untested behavior a new code BRANCH, or is it device/driver behavior a GPU-less runner cannot execute?"** Vulkan RT SBT dispatch with a nonzero base offset is driver-dependent and not covered by a green build, so OPEN_GAP holds regardless of static consistency — but the merge is *weak* evidence (author self-merge is non-independent; merge/green ≠ execution proof), so keep OPEN_GAP for this shape and log the disagreement without rounding the next one up. The complementary discriminant from slang#12448: distinguish "unverified because nobody ran the available check" (closable ⇒ legit OPEN_GAP) from "unverified because no reachable check exists" (a structural property of the repo's CI topology — the PR's own acceptance test was a `workflow_call`-only nightly, uncheckable from a PR) [[human APPROVED with my OPEN_GAP open — the acceptance criterion was an unchecked box no PR CI can check]](../learnings/1786451888928-approver-human-disagreement-slang-12448-human-appr.md). For the structural case, ask whether being wrong announces itself loudly and soon (a red nightly) or hides silently — "no reachable check" + "loud, prompt, bounded failure" ⇒ flag it and let it merge; reserve the abstain for gaps that are closable or whose failure is silent.

## The subtlest false-safe: an approval joins to its OWN commit

A false-safe can masquerade as a calibration *hit*. On slang#12125 R3 (`WOULD_APPROVE @ef385f02`) was recorded as a hit because two humans later approved and never requested changes — but the approvals were submitted against **descendant** commits, and an interval commit `466d303547d6` fixed two substantive defects present in the code cleared at R3 [[an approval joins to its OWN commit_id, never the nearest decision row]](../learnings/1786479387688-approver-false-safe-r3-on-12125-an-approval-joins-.md). The human-verdict channel could never surface it (nobody requested changes), so joining on "did a human approve?" scores it a hit forever; only reading the interval commits revealed the miss. The join rule, both parts required: (a) `DISMISSED` ≠ retracted (read `dismissed_review.state`; a dismissal_commit_id that is a master-merge is stale-review-on-push); (b) the un-inverted approval still joins **only to its own commit_id** — if that commit ≠ your decided head, `gh api compare <your-head>...<approval-head>` and read every interval commit before scoring anything.

## Debug-info emit: test-tightness/doc 🟡 gaps clear, but record-content gaps don't

Two SPIR-V debug-info emit PRs merged UNCHANGED at the exact reviewed/decided head give a sharp gap-severity prior for this change class — with a hard caveat on its boundary. slang#12860 ("Canonicalize SPIR-V matrix debug types") shipped with the head bot's three 🟡 gaps all unaddressed: an unbounded FileCheck `CHECK-NOT` for the `%false` row-vector form, a missing explanatory comment on the new unconditional `ColumnMajor=true` shape, and a `normalizeMatrixDebugType` comment misattributing the emitted shape to normalization. slang#12858 ("Fix SPIR-V debug info for opaque types" — ABSTAIN_POLICY on fork-head across both revisions, then human-APPROVED by jkwak-work and merged) shipped its three findings unaddressed too — a numeric struct-size path tested only at size 0, a missing doc comment on `getDebugInfoNone()`, and an unexplained/asymmetric `"@"` linkage-name convention. The transferable calibration: for SPIR-V debug-type/debug-info emit changes, 🟡 gaps of the shape *"FileCheck negative-check not bounded"* or *"missing/misattributed explanatory comment on the emitted record shape"* are empirically maintainer-non-blocking test-robustness/doc nits that clear under the conservative-lean bar (no real-world trigger, no blast radius) — **provided the substantive correctness question is independently settled** (for #12860, the design decision that the debug type describes value/register shape while `OpMemberDecorate RowMajor/ColMajor` describes buffer storage was verified against the DXC/glslang comparison in issue #12757; for #12858, the numeric-size branch is a mechanical `IRInst*`→`SpvInst*` `ensureInst(...)` adaptation emitting the same operand it always did, exercised by every existing struct-with-members debug test). The false-safe probe held for #12858: the new test's end-anchored (`{{$}}`) `DebugTypeComposite` lines require both a new `@`-prefixed `OpString` linkage name and the `DebugInfoNone` size operand (both absent pre-fix), plus an empty-struct control that still emits `%uint_0` — end-anchored operand-count discrimination + a same-file control is a sufficient test shape here. **The boundary — do NOT over-generalize to "debug-info emit gaps are safe":** prior learnings (#12220 DebugEntryPoint silent-drop, #11982 divergent-operand dedup miss) show debug-info emit has a real false-safe surface that byte-valid + spirv-val-clean cannot see, so when a 🟡 questions the *content or dedup of the emitted debug record itself* (not the test's tightness or a comment's wording) it still routes to OPEN_GAP — verify dedup with `-dump-ir` and check for a second producer of the debug inst [[approver/challenger-miss] SPIR-V debug-type canonicalization: bot 🟡 test-robustness/doc gaps shipped unaddressed (merge-confirmed clears)](../learnings/1788485137044-approver-challenger-miss-spir-v-debug-type-canonic.md) [[approver/calibration-confirmed] Opaque-type DebugInfoNone SPIR-V fix merged unchanged — advisory doc/coverage nits were correctly non-blocking](../learnings/1788485501480-approver-calibration-confirmed-opaque-type-debugin.md).
