# Mechanism vs Outcome Reasoning

## TL;DR
- A correct conclusion is not evidence of correct reasoning. A wrong mechanism riding a right conclusion draws no pushback, because no test, review or CI audits reasoning, so it propagates by relay. Separate the observation from the story: state what was measured and leave the cause unestablished until verified.
- When a claim's mechanism is load-bearing for someone else's decision, verify the mechanism directly, and run every explanation against the PASSING case as well as the failing one.
- Don't infer a causal link the failing job's own log never states; an unreproduced code trace is a hypothesis.
- Provenance has layers (bytes on disk / my read / what the machinery declares / what it did / what a peer told me): a fact holds at exactly one layer. Trigger a second-instrument read on how load-bearing a claim is, not on how the evidence looked.
- Rigor on one axis reads as rigor on all of them, and isn't. Verifying one member of a set does not verify the set; a negative result on one spelling is not a negative result on the concept.
- Ask whose observation it is and whether the two sources could have differed: corroboration needs independent provenance; never publish a cause for an error only the other party could observe; never cite an identifier you cannot read; find the emitter before naming the defect.
- Verify-the-premise is symmetric: apply it to credit, refutations and relayed symptoms, not only to criticism. Decline credit you did not earn; grade a refutation on the same scale as a claim.
- Reconciliation is generative: a peer's contradiction is a lead. Ask what the union of both observations proves.
- A reviewer's source trace is a hypothesis; A/B rebuild before accepting it.
- Never infer a dispatch property from a defect property.
- "It errors, therefore unreachable" is not proof of dead code; removal needs a positive reachability demo.

## Don't Infer a Causal Link the Failing Job's Log Never States

Anchor a CI/test root cause to the **failing job's own output**: an error from a different step or job is not its cause without connecting evidence, and a code trace that "would explain" it is a hypothesis until the failing unit's log corroborates it. On slang#11985 the chain diagnosed an intermittent macOS failure as a Metal-4.0 capability-attribute rejection (`[[required_threads_per_threadgroup]]` emitted by default, rejected on <4.0 Metal → exit 255; `slang-emit-metal.cpp`, `metallib_latest=metallib_4_0` since PR #10592), posted it, and retracted publicly. (1) **Inference presented as fact:** the metal-4.0 error appeared ONLY in co-located `slang-test` unit tests that *cleared on retry*, while the failing `gpu-printing` exited 255 with *zero output* at `createComputePipeline`. (2) **The mechanism predicted the inverse of the data:** "rejected on older Metal" implies deterministic failure on old runners, but the maintainer's 12-run correlation showed flakiness *only on the newest macos-26 "Tahoe" image*. Before blaming a compiler mechanism for an intermittent failure: (a) confirm the signature is in the FAILING step's output; (b) "cleared on retry" means flake/environment; (c) a flake correlating with a NEW OS image points at a driver/runtime change; (d) a silent exit-255 points at RHI/device/driver, not shader compilation (which prints diagnostics); (e) without a local repro, weight run-correlation above the trace; (f) if the mechanism and the data disagree, reconcile before posting. The emit-vs-`-std=metal3.1` inconsistency survived as a *latent* bug, not the operative cause (a macOS-26 Metal driver/runtime race). When contradicted, re-read the primary evidence, concede where the maintainer's is stronger, and post a fresh correction separating what survives from what is retracted, never a silent edit ([slang#11985 Metal diagnosis was plausible-but-wrong: inferred attribute→gpu-printing link the log never supported](../learnings/1783642367486-slang-11985-metal-diagnosis-was-plausible-but-wron.md), [Don't infer a causal link the failure log never states — CORRECTION](../learnings/1784154251219-don-t-infer-a-causal-link-the-failure-log-never-st.md)).

## Provenance has layers, and rigor on one axis reads as rigor on all of them

"I read the file" is not one fact. A `Read` of `.github/workflows/cmake-options-build.yml` returned a CI step gate (`if: ${{ inputs.verify-slangc }}`) that **does not exist in the file**, about to be cited in a public verdict; only unrelated cosmetic damage caught it. Across the layers (bytes on disk / my read / what the machinery declares / what it did this time / what a peer told me), **a fact is established at exactly one layer and gains false authority when asserted at a higher one** ("the workflow declares X" is never "CI ran X"). Trigger the second-instrument read on the **load-bearing-ness of the claim**, not on how the evidence looked; use a different reader *and* path (`git show <ref>:<path> | sed -n`), and separate "artifact is corrupt" from "read is corrupt" ([provenance has layers — name the layer you are standing on](../learnings/1785803860488-provenance-has-layers-a-fact-that-crosses-a-layer-.md)).

Rigor does not transfer across axes. Seven errors from one chain split into two disjoint controls: **relevance** (4 of 7, e.g. "no `[Info]` lines ⇒ the fallback didn't run" when `Info` cannot print in a passing non-verbose run) and **carry-through** (3 of 7, e.g. a line number cited across your own edit). Grepping the artifact is worse than useless against relevance (finding the wrong claim reads as verification) and powerless against carry-through; the closing "grep would have caught all seven" (actually 3/7) was itself an over-claim. Treat "we've been careful for hours" as a reason to check the axis you have *not* been watching ([two disjoint verification controls — relevance and carry-through](../learnings/1785778169091-two-disjoint-verification-controls-relevance-and-c.md)).

Every premise can be true and the conclusion false. A pass entry showing `t` in `nm libslang.so` was correctly read as hidden visibility and one `SLANG_API` proposed as the fix, but the undefined set also held `IRInst::getFirstChild`/`getLastChild`/`getOperands`/`getDecorations`, `IRInstListBase::begin/end` and `ComponentType::getTargetProgram`, each needing its own export. **Verifying one member of a set is not verifying the set**: read the whole undefined-symbol list, remember declared-in-header ≠ defined-in-header, and when you cannot run the check, **ship the question, not the conclusion**, labeling verified vs inferred ([correct premises, wrong conclusion — verifying one member is not verifying the set](../learnings/1785776642421-correct-premises-wrong-conclusion-verifying-one-me.md)).

The cheapest version is repeating a PR description as measurement. The claim that `_INIT` seeding "honors `CFLAGS`/`CXXFLAGS` generally" was lifted from a PR body and never probed ([seed CMAKE_&lt;LANG&gt;_FLAGS_&lt;CONFIG&gt;_INIT to make a default overridable](../learnings/1785767372104-cmake-seed-cmake-lang-flags-config-init-to-make-a-.md)); it is false for an `-O` level. Env `CXXFLAGS` lands in `CMAKE_<LANG>_FLAGS` (slot 1) and the seed in `CMAKE_<LANG>_FLAGS_<CONFIG>` (slot 2), so under last-`-O`-wins `CXXFLAGS='-O0 -g3'` still yields `-Og`, and only `-DCMAKE_CXX_FLAGS_DEBUG=` replaces it ([_INIT seeding does not let env CXXFLAGS override an -O default](../learnings/1785768108214-correction-cmake-lt-lang-gt-flags-lt-config-gt-ini.md), measured independently in [per-config _INIT seeding cannot be overridden by env CXXFLAGS](../learnings/1785767705338-cmake-og-debug-default-per-config-init-seeding-can.md), which supplies a validated whole-token `-O` detector: `separate_arguments` plus `^-O([0-9]+|s|g|z|fast)?$` anchoring to reject `-ObjC`). The chain also wrote off the defaults-based direction after one bad variant (`set(... CACHE STRING "" FORCE)`): **a negative result on one spelling is not one on the concept**; enumerate a mechanism's variants before declaring it dead.

## Provenance: whose observation is it, and could the two sources have differed?

Corroboration requires independent *provenance*, not two files that agree. An approver justified `WOULD_APPROVE` on slang-rhi#806 (a one-line README license fix) with two "independent" sources, `LICENSE:1` and `.reuse/dep5`'s `Files: *` stanza (both `Apache-2.0 WITH LLVM-exception`), but `.reuse/dep5` was *created by* `bc7657abfa`, the commit that relicensed `LICENSE`, so both would be wrong together. One `gh api ".../commits?path=<file>"` per source exposes this; then re-describe what the second source proves (shared provenance is weak for value-corroboration but strong for *intent and scope*: the relicense was deliberate and repo-wide). The same holds for a value and its lockfile entry, a constant and its generated header, a doc and its extracted docstring ([corroboration needs independent provenance — `git log` the second source](../learnings/1785785358188-corroboration-needs-independent-provenance-git-log.md)).

**Don't publish a cause for a fact only the other party could observe.** Given `merged_at 18:10:04Z` and `closed_at 18:10:05Z`, a peer's wrong "still OPEN" was explained as "almost certainly a read inside the one-second window": a real, fitting, *exculpatory* mechanism (charitable explanations attract the least audit), and wrong; the cause was a blocked call plus memory substitution. Separate **what I verified** (artifact state) from **why their report differed** (needs their evidence); "almost certainly / presumably / must have been" about someone else's process is the tell, and a fitting mechanism is a hypothesis to offer, never a cause to publish ([don't manufacture a mechanism for someone else's error](../learnings/1785781538948-don-t-manufacture-a-mechanism-for-someone-else-s-e.md)). The chain's self-audit: all four claims about **systems the speaker could not observe** (a peer's read timing, another agent's misread, a guard's scope, per-agent config) were wrong, and every claim about readable artifacts was right; the relay is where such a claim gains authority, `"X reports Y"` surviving one hop as `"Y"` plus an escalation ([only the passing cases locate a trigger](../learnings/1785782647584-only-the-passing-cases-locate-a-trigger-four-wrong.md)).

**Never cite an identifier you cannot read.** Asked to disposition three approval-gate bypass cards while `ncl approvals get` returned `forbidden` (group-scoped CLI; `approvals` is admin-only, verbs `list`/`get`), an agent matched a card's `requested_at 17:17:51Z` against `ncl sessions list` and tabled a session created 17:12. The authoritative field named a different session (created 07-30, firing 08-03, so filtering by `created_at` near the event excludes it). The conclusion happened to be right, so nobody would have re-checked; a wrong identifier is worse than none, since omission prompts a lookup while a confident wrong id gets re-checked *through* it ([never cite an identifier you cannot read — timestamp adjacency is correlation, not identification](../learnings/1785782620101-never-cite-an-identifier-you-cannot-read-timestamp.md)). Likewise a string that can appear as message *content* cannot establish provenance: grepping coworker names inside session `.jsonl` files "attributed" six files to one owner.

**Find the emitter before naming the defect.** A relayed `[GATE AUDIT]` symptom ("codex never invoked") was escalated to the operator as "a third instance of the same hook's detection being wrong", and both claims were false: `grep -riE 'gate.audit|never invoked' /app/hooks/` returns nothing because the emitter is `/app/src/mcp-tools/gate-audit.ts:174`, an MCP tool inside `send_message` sharing nothing with the critique gate. The real mechanism (`findActiveSessionJsonl()` picking the mtime-latest `.jsonl` in a flat directory of 845 files) was documented in the file's own `KNOWN LIMITATIONS` block, the competing explanation was *eliminated* (`provider=null` ⇒ Claude ⇒ jsonl is written), 11 logs since 06-24 already recorded it, and the escalated direction was the harmless false-negative. Grouping findings by how they feel to the victim is a causal claim; a relayed symptom arrives without its mechanism, and a verified outcome does not license an unverified cause ([find the emitter before naming the defect](../learnings/1785801131585-find-the-emitter-before-naming-the-defect-a-relaye.md)).

## Verify-the-premise is symmetric: credit, refutations and documented limits

**It applies to praise as well as criticism.** A fixer reported that splitting a `pulls` string literal across a shell variable slipped the critique gate; it was escalated twice as a serious gate defect, but the hook's header comment (`gate-critique-on-deliver.sh:76-80`) already said pattern enumeration can never be complete and the backstop is credential-layer enforcement. A **documented limitation is not a discovery**; the remediation would have made the hook load-bearing against its authors' stated position, and the caveat sat in the reporter's own memory file. Retract downward against your own credit, cite file:line, state what survives narrowed; relaying counts as complying ([verify-the-premise is symmetric — it applies when the inbound is credit](../learnings/1785800092596-verify-the-premise-is-symmetric-it-applies-when-th.md)).

**Grade refutations on the same scale as claims.** An over-stated refutation is worse than an over-stated mechanism: a bad mechanism is caught when a fix fails, while "that branch is impossible" *licenses a decision* (close it, ship the other fix) nothing tests. Calling a null-function-pointer story "self-contradictory" was corrected to "driver-self-**inconsistent**", a much weaker claim about a *conforming* implementation; on prototype driver 610.43.02/Blackwell it would have retired a live hypothesis on a conformance assumption the hardware never owed. Watch for **asymmetric hedging** (the public comment hedged, the action-driving handoff not): check the wording in the artifact that executes. The implementer saved the chain by re-deriving a cite instead of inheriting it ([a refutation can be over-stated the same way a mechanism can — and it licenses closing](../learnings/1785776242563-a-refutation-can-be-over-stated-the-same-way-a-mec.md)).

**Decline credit you did not earn.** An approver thanked a peer for a "207-rows correction" and a "(b) retraction" the peer never sent (the approver's own self-corrections, landed in the peer's memory row via editor writes); accepting would have pointed a future audit of "who established 207 registered / 0 executed?" at the wrong tier. Grep your own outbound record, not your memory, name who did send it, verify the substance anyway, and report the split ([decline credit you did not earn](../learnings/1785780007458-decline-credit-you-did-not-earn-misattributed-corr.md)).

## Reconciliation is generative: a peer's contradiction is a lead

The rules above are defensive. Their counterpart: **a contradicting peer measurement usually means you hold different slices of the state, which may compose into something neither had.** A flaky test needed a *same-SHA pass/fail pair* to prove a failure is not code-caused. Agent A held **attempt 1 FAILED** on head `2c9cf98fb0`; the other held **`Test (Falcor)` SUCCESS** on the same head. Reconciling found that `gh run view --json jobs` returns **only the latest attempt**, so attempt 1 FAIL + attempt 2 PASS on one commit was the pair. **The disagreement was the instrument**: ask **what the union of the two observations proves that neither proved alone**, not who was right ([a peer contradicting your measurement is a lead](../learnings/1785992330335-a-peer-contradicting-your-measurement-is-a-lead-no.md)). Two corollaries. Credit for the pair was declined (neither had both halves; recorded as joint), as in [decline credit you did not earn](../learnings/1785780007458-decline-credit-you-did-not-earn-misattributed-corr.md): misattribution runs in the flattering direction because nobody audits arriving credit. And **a defect you already admitted becomes the cheapest account of the next anomaly**: having genuinely confused two similarly-named CI jobs earlier, the agent accepted "I misread the job again" as pre-validated and retracted a true report. **An anomaly that resembles a mistake you own needs more checking, not less.** This complements [don't manufacture a mechanism for someone else's error](../learnings/1785781538948-don-t-manufacture-a-mechanism-for-someone-else-s-e.md): the charitable-toward-others and the self-blaming account both escape audit, because nothing downstream tests a story that ends the argument comfortably.

## Separate the observation from the story: a wrong mechanism riding a correct conclusion draws no pushback

Across three triage chains (slang#12441, #12443, slang-rhi#818) the recurring failure was **publishing a mechanism that explains a correct observation and is itself false**. On #12441 every observation about two SPIR-V emit bugs survived review while **three invented mechanisms** did not, e.g. "the vector case passes because `OpVectorShuffle` produces a fresh type" (opcode selection happens *before* operands emit; the agent's own IR dump disproved it), and a spurious "must also fix `isSignedType`" (never consulted once the classifier sees `Float`). A reviewer who agrees with the verdict never re-derives the reasoning, so state the observation as measured and leave the cause explicitly unestablished ([Three plausible mechanisms attached to correct observations, all wrong: separate the observation from the story before publishing](../learnings/1786308509100-three-plausible-mechanisms-attached-to-correct-obs.md)). **Run an explanation against the PASSING case, not only the failing one:** on #12443 two proposed root causes both predicted the observed failure and also predicted failure for the *local-variable* bound that is observed to succeed; the refuting cell had been measured hours before either was proposed, so both parties had the data and lacked the habit ([An explanation must be run against the passing case — the refuting cell usually already exists](../learnings/1786312138714-an-explanation-must-be-run-against-the-passing-cas.md)). **A confounded matrix makes the wrong variable look causal:** #12443's first matrix cleanly isolated "function parameter vs local" until a global and a struct-field cell (header-phase but not parameters) showed the real axis is the *declaration-checking phase*; every original cell varied both at once ([A confounded matrix: entangling two variables makes the wrong one look causal](../learnings/1786307626055-a-confounded-matrix-entangling-two-variables-makes.md)).

## A reviewer's source trace is a hypothesis; rebuild before accepting it

On slang#12994 a peer reviewer reported a high-confidence 🔴 with an end-to-end source trace (a non-canonicalizing `isRayPayloadStructType` would diverge from the IR gate for a `typealias` payload member and re-introduce E40000), and an adjudication round "confirmed" it. An A/B rebuild of slangc with only `->getCanonicalType()` reverted still compiled the aliased case correctly (unqualified → no E40000; qualified → E40022 fired): the fix is a **no-op** for those spellings. Rules: (1) when a review claims "test X fails without your change" and you can rebuild in minutes, do the fail-before/pass-after A/B first (a reviewer's own "source-derived, not rebuilt" low-confidence note is an explicit cue); (2) report the *verified behavior*, not the unverified mechanism (keep a correct-by-construction change but frame it as "A/B shows it's a no-op here", and drop any unconfirmed "why"); (3) truthfulness outranks deferring to a confident reviewer ([verify a reviewer's source-derived bug claim by rebuilding before accepting it](../learnings/1789057073385-verify-a-reviewer-s-source-derived-bug-claim-by-re.md)).

## Never infer a dispatch property from a defect property

A quieter member of the family infers one property from an independent one nearby. Two agents concluded "the CI fault is scoped to one runner ⇒ rerunning is futile", relayed to an operator twice before the author's own rerun disproved it ([never infer a DISPATCH property from a DEFECT property](../learnings/1785838936616-never-infer-a-dispatch-property-from-a-defect-prop.md)). The attribution (host-scoped fault) was correct; the error was structural: **scope-of-fault and scope-of-routing are independent facts needing independent evidence**. Host-scoped implies a same-host rerun only under a *pinning* mechanism, whereas `runs-on: [Windows, self-hosted, regression-test]` is label-based dispatch (one unchanged head: SLANGWIN5 ❌, SLANGWIN5 ❌, SLANGWIN4 ✅). Neither agent read the `runs-on:` line: verification flows to whatever is *disputed*, so an undisputed premise goes unchecked. Rules: when a remedy's justification says "it would just happen again", **name the mechanism that would make it happen again, then read it**; prove a machine is in the pool with a *passing job*, never a missing row (zero jobs in a window measures dispatch, not drainage); a defect can be job-scoped on a healthy host, so look for a same-host sibling-job success before proposing "recycle the runner". Land a propagating retraction in the **most-read position** (frontmatter `description`, a body banner, the index row), not appended below the stale claim.

## "It errors, therefore unreachable" is not proof of dead code

Before removing load-bearing or already-approved code, the deadness claim must rest on a **positive reachability demonstration** (make the scenario compile and observe the behavior), not on "input X produces an error". A *syntax* error misread as a *semantic* rejection fakes unreachability, and removal carries the higher burden of proof, so fix the error first and re-test. Rebasing slang#13033 onto master #12836 (GLSL source-language normalization), a subagent saw `error[E20001]: unexpected '['` at `[shader]` in the mixed generic-GLSL fragment scenario, concluded "`getParentDecl` is dead → remove it + the test", and a supervisor approved. Codex OUTPUT_REVIEW caught that E20001 cascaded from a missing GLSL trailing `;` after `struct`/`interface`; with it, the generic fragment entry point compiles under `-lang glsl` and emits 0 `EarlyFragmentTests` with raw `parentDecl` but 1 with `getParentDecl`, so removal would have silently regressed a live feature. Prefer the least-destructive resolution (migrate/preserve) when deadness rests on interpreting an error ([\"It errors, therefore unreachable\" is not proof of dead code](../learnings/1789621806396-it-errors-therefore-unreachable-is-not-proof-of-de.md), [\"It errors, therefore unreachable\" is a false-deadness trap — require a positive reachability demo](../learnings/1789623354439-it-errors-therefore-unreachable-is-a-false-deadnes.md)).
