# Mechanism vs Outcome Reasoning

## TL;DR
- A wrong mechanism riding a right conclusion draws no pushback from outcomes — no test, review, or CI can audit reasoning, so it propagates by relay. A correct conclusion is not evidence of correct reasoning.
- When a claim's mechanism is load-bearing for someone else's decision, verify the mechanism directly.
- Don't infer a causal link the failing job's log never states.
- Separate the observation from the story: a wrong mechanism riding a correct conclusion draws no pushback.
- Provenance has layers — ask whose observation it is and whether the two sources could have differed; rigor on one axis reads as rigor on all of them.
- Reconciliation is generative: a peer's contradiction is a lead. Never infer a dispatch property from a defect property.

## Don't Infer a Causal Link the Failing Job's Log Never States

When triaging a CI/test failure, anchor the root cause to what the **failing job's own output** shows — do not stitch a nearby error from a *different* step/job into the failing one as its cause without evidence connecting them. A plausible code-trace that "would explain" a failure is a hypothesis, not a diagnosis, until the failing unit's log corroborates it — especially when you cannot reproduce locally. On slang#11985 the coworker chain diagnosed an intermittent macOS CI failure as a Metal-4.0 capability-attribute rejection (`[[required_threads_per_threadgroup]]` emitted by default, rejected on <4.0 Metal → exit 255), traced it through the compiler (`slang-emit-metal.cpp`; `metallib_latest=metallib_4_0` since PR #10592), and posted it as the verdict — then had to retract publicly. Two avoidable errors: (1) **inference presented as fact** — the metal-4.0 error appeared ONLY in co-located `slang-test` unit tests that *cleared on retry*; the actual failing example (`gpu-printing`) exited 255 with *zero output*, failing silently at `createComputePipeline`, and the log never showed a metal error there at all. Two co-located symptoms ≠ same cause. (2) **The mechanism predicted the inverse of the data** — "rejected on OLDER <4.0 Metal" implies *deterministic* failure on old runners, but the maintainer's 12-run correlation showed it was *flaky, only on the NEWEST macos-26 "Tahoe" image* (macos-15 passing). Flaky-on-new refutes deterministic-on-old. Rules, before blaming a compiler mechanism for an intermittent CI failure: (a) confirm the error signature appears in the FAILING step's output, not just a co-located step; (b) "cleared on retry" means flake/environmental, not the deterministic bug you're tracing; (c) weight runner-image/OS correlation — a flake correlating with a NEW OS image is a driver/runtime change, not a static capability mismatch; (d) a silent exit-255 with no diagnostic points at RHI/device/driver, not shader compilation (which prints diagnostics); (e) when you can't reproduce locally, label the code-trace a hypothesis and weight empirical run-correlation above it; (f) if your mechanism predicts deterministic-on-old but the data is flaky-on-new, stop and reconcile before posting. What survived here: the emit-vs-`-std=metal3.1` attribute inconsistency is a genuine *latent* bug worth fixing on its own merits — but it was NOT the operative cause (a macOS-26 Metal driver/runtime race). When contradicted, reconcile honestly: re-read the primary evidence yourself, concede where the maintainer's is stronger, and post a fresh correction separating what survives from what's retracted — don't silently edit or defend ([slang#11985 Metal diagnosis was plausible-but-wrong: inferred attribute→gpu-printing link the log never supported](../learnings/1783642367486-slang-11985-metal-diagnosis-was-plausible-but-wron.md), [Don't infer a causal link the failure log never states — CORRECTION](../learnings/1784154251219-don-t-infer-a-causal-link-the-failure-log-never-st.md)).


## Provenance has layers, and rigor on one axis reads as rigor on all of them

"I read the file" is not one fact. A `Read` of `.github/workflows/cmake-options-build.yml` returned a CI step gate (`if: ${{ inputs.verify-slangc }}`) that **does not exist in the file** — about to be cited as load-bearing evidence in a public verdict answering a maintainer. What caught it was cosmetic damage *independent* of the factual defect; had the fabrication arrived cleanly formatted, it would have been published. Hence the layer table — bytes on disk / my read of them / what the machinery declares / what it did this time / what a peer told me — where **a fact is established at exactly one layer and gains false authority the moment it is asserted at a higher one** ("the workflow declares X" is never "CI ran X"). The rule that does not depend on a tell: **trigger the second-instrument read on the load-bearing-ness of the claim, not on how the evidence looked**, use a different reader *and* path (`git show <ref>:<path> | sed -n`), and separate "artifact is corrupt" from "read is corrupt" ([provenance has layers — name the layer you are standing on](../learnings/1785803860488-provenance-has-layers-a-fact-that-crosses-a-layer-.md)). Rigor does not transfer across axes. Seven errors from one chain classified by *where* they failed give two disjoint controls, neither subsuming the other: **relevance** failures (4 of 7 — a circular citation gated on the very flag that made it describe the other path; "no `[Info]` lines ⇒ the fallback didn't run" when `Info` cannot print in a passing non-verbose run) versus **carry-through** failures (3 of 7 — a line number cited across your own edit, an unqualified referent the peer resolved to the wrong file). Grepping the artifact is *worse than useless* against relevance (it confirms the wrong claim is present, which reads as verification) and powerless against carry-through — so the closing over-claim ("grep would have caught all seven": actually 3/7) is itself the lesson: treat "we've been careful for hours" as a reason to check the axis you have *not* been watching ([two disjoint verification controls — relevance and carry-through](../learnings/1785778169091-two-disjoint-verification-controls-relevance-and-c.md)). The subtlest variant has every premise true and the conclusion still false: a pass entry showing `t` in `nm libslang.so` was correctly diagnosed as hidden visibility, so one `SLANG_API` was proposed as the cheap fix — but the undefined set also held `IRInst::getFirstChild`/`getLastChild`/`getOperands`/`getDecorations`, `IRInstListBase::begin/end` and `ComponentType::getTargetProgram`, each needing its own export. **Verifying one member of a set is not verifying the set**; read the whole undefined-symbol list, remember declared-in-header ≠ defined-in-header, and when you cannot run the check, **ship the question, not the conclusion**, labeling verified vs inferred ([correct premises, wrong conclusion — verifying one member is not verifying the set](../learnings/1785776642421-correct-premises-wrong-conclusion-verifying-one-me.md)). The cheapest version is repeating a PR description as measurement: the claim that `_INIT` seeding "honors `CFLAGS`/`CXXFLAGS` generally" was lifted from a PR body and never probed ([seed CMAKE_&lt;LANG&gt;_FLAGS_&lt;CONFIG&gt;_INIT to make a default overridable](../learnings/1785767372104-cmake-seed-cmake-lang-flags-config-init-to-make-a-.md)) and is false for an `-O` level — env `CXXFLAGS` lands in `CMAKE_<LANG>_FLAGS` (slot 1), the seed in `CMAKE_<LANG>_FLAGS_<CONFIG>` (slot 2), so under last-`-O`-wins `CXXFLAGS='-O0 -g3'` yields effective `-Og`, and only `-DCMAKE_CXX_FLAGS_DEBUG=` replaces it ([_INIT seeding does not let env CXXFLAGS override an -O default](../learnings/1785768108214-correction-cmake-lt-lang-gt-flags-lt-config-gt-ini.md), measured independently in [per-config _INIT seeding cannot be overridden by env CXXFLAGS](../learnings/1785767705338-cmake-og-debug-default-per-config-init-seeding-can.md) — which supplies a validated whole-token `-O` detector, `separate_arguments` plus `^-O([0-9]+|s|g|z|fast)?$` anchoring for `-ObjC` rejection). And the sibling reasoning error in that chain: the whole defaults-based *direction* was written off after testing one bad variant (`set(... CACHE STRING "" FORCE)`) — **a negative result on one spelling is not a negative result on the concept**; enumerate a mechanism's variants before declaring the direction dead.


## Provenance: whose observation is it, and could the two sources have differed?

Corroboration requires independent *provenance*, not merely two files that agree. An approver justified a `WOULD_APPROVE` on a slang-rhi#806 one-line README license correction against "two independent authoritative sources" — `LICENSE:1` and `.reuse/dep5`'s blanket `Files: *` stanza, both reading `Apache-2.0 WITH LLVM-exception` — but `.reuse/dep5` was *created by* `bc7657abfa`, the same commit that relicensed `LICENSE`, so had that PR been mistaken both would be wrong together and the agreement added zero redundancy; one `gh api ".../commits?path=<file>"` per source exposes it, and the honest move is to re-describe what the second source proves (shared provenance is weak for value-corroboration, strong for *intent and scope* — a common origin in a REUSE-compliance commit is direct evidence the relicense was deliberate and repo-wide with README the lone dissenter). Generalizes to a value and its lockfile entry, a constant and its generated header, a doc and the docstring extracted from the same source ([corroboration needs independent provenance — `git log` the second source](../learnings/1785785358188-corroboration-needs-independent-provenance-git-log.md)). The mirror-image trap is publishing a *cause* for a fact only the other party could observe: given `merged_at 18:10:04Z` and `closed_at 18:10:05Z`, a peer's wrong "still OPEN" was reported as "almost certainly a read inside the one-second window" — a mechanism that is genuinely real, fits the visible data, is *exculpatory* (charitable explanations attract the least audit), and was wrong; the actual cause was a blocked call plus memory substitution, and crediting the false cause let the more valuable lesson go unrecorded. Separate **what I verified** (artifact state) from **why their report differed** (needs evidence from their side); "almost certainly / presumably / must have been" about someone else's process is the tell, and a mechanism that fits the timestamps is a hypothesis to offer them, never a cause to publish ([don't manufacture a mechanism for someone else's error](../learnings/1785781538948-don-t-manufacture-a-mechanism-for-someone-else-s-e.md)). The same file's chain-level self-audit is the strongest single datum here: of four claims made about **systems the speaker could not observe** (a peer's read timing, another agent's misread, a guard's scope, per-agent config) all four were wrong, while every claim about readable artifacts was correct — and the relay is where an unverifiable claim acquires false authority, `"X reports Y"` surviving one hop as `"Y"` with an escalation attached ([only the passing cases locate a trigger](../learnings/1785782647584-only-the-passing-cases-locate-a-trigger-four-wrong.md)). Two concrete forms of synthesized provenance: never cite an identifier you cannot read — asked to disposition three approval-gate bypass cards with `ncl approvals get` returning `forbidden` (group-scoped CLI; `approvals` is admin-only, and its verb list is `list`/`get` only), an agent matched a card's `requested_at 17:17:51Z` against `ncl sessions list` and tabled a session created 17:12; the authoritative field named a different session on a different thread (created 07-30, firing 08-03, so filtering by `created_at` near the event actively excludes the right answer), and because the *conclusion* happened to be right nobody would have re-checked the citation — a wrong identifier is worse than none, since omission prompts a lookup while a confident wrong id gets re-checked *through* it ([never cite an identifier you cannot read — timestamp adjacency is correlation, not identification](../learnings/1785782620101-never-cite-an-identifier-you-cannot-read-timestamp.md)); and a string that can appear as message *content* cannot establish provenance, which is how grepping coworker names inside session `.jsonl` files "attributed" six files to one owner. That last case is the emitter lesson: a relayed `[GATE AUDIT]` symptom ("codex never invoked") was escalated to the operator as "a third instance of the same hook's detection being wrong," and both claims were false — `grep -riE 'gate.audit|never invoked' /app/hooks/` returns zero hits because the emitter is `/app/src/mcp-tools/gate-audit.ts:174`, an MCP tool inside `send_message` sharing nothing with the critique gate; the real mechanism (`findActiveSessionJsonl()` picking the mtime-latest `.jsonl` in one flat directory of 845 files) was documented in the file's own `KNOWN LIMITATIONS` block, the competing explanation was *eliminated* rather than assumed (`provider=null` ⇒ Claude ⇒ jsonl is written), 11 log files from 06-24 onward already recorded it, and the escalated direction was the harmless false-negative rather than the dangerous false-*pass*. Grouping findings by how they feel to the victim is a causal claim; a relayed symptom arrives without its mechanism, and a verified outcome does not license an unverified cause ([find the emitter before naming the defect](../learnings/1785801131585-find-the-emitter-before-naming-the-defect-a-relaye.md)). Two final asymmetries. **Verify-the-premise is symmetric** — we apply it reflexively to demands and criticism and skip it for praise: a fixer reported that splitting a `pulls` string literal across a shell variable slipped the critique gate, a parent escalated it twice as a serious gate defect, an orchestrator carried a remediation toward the operator, and then the hook's own header comment at `gate-critique-on-deliver.sh:76-80` turned out to say pattern enumeration can never be complete and the durable backstop is credential-layer enforcement — a **documented limitation is not a discovery**, the proposed remediation would have made the hook load-bearing against the authors' stated position, and the caveat had been sitting in the reporter's own memory file while it wrote the escalation up; retract downward against your own credit, cite file:line, state what survives narrowed, and note that relaying counts as complying ([verify-the-premise is symmetric — it applies when the inbound is credit](../learnings/1785800092596-verify-the-premise-is-symmetric-it-applies-when-th.md)). And **grade your refutations on the same scale as your claims**, because an over-stated refutation is worse than an over-stated mechanism: a bad mechanism gets caught when someone's fix fails, while "that branch is impossible" *licenses a decision* (close it, drop it from the rationale, ship the other fix) that nothing downstream tests — calling a null-function-pointer story "self-contradictory" was corrected to "driver-self-**inconsistent**", a much weaker claim about a *conforming* implementation, and on prototype driver 610.43.02/Blackwell exactly the population where feature-bit-vs-extension inconsistency appears, one notch of overstatement would have retired a live hypothesis on a conformance assumption the hardware never owed; watch for **asymmetric hedging** (the public comment was hedged, the handoff that drove action was not — check the wording in the artifact that executes), and note that the whole chain was saved only because the implementer refused to inherit a cite and re-derived it ([a refutation can be over-stated the same way a mechanism can — and it licenses closing](../learnings/1785776242563-a-refutation-can-be-over-stated-the-same-way-a-mec.md)). Provenance runs in the flattering direction too: **decline credit you did not earn** — an approver thanked a peer for a "207-rows correction" and a "(b) retraction" the peer never sent (both were the approver's own self-corrections that had landed in the peer's memory row via editor writes), and accepting the thanks would have pointed a future audit of "who established 207 registered / 0 executed, and against which artifact?" at the wrong tier; grep your own outbound record rather than your memory of the exchange, name who did send it, verify the substance independently anyway, and report the split ([decline credit you did not earn](../learnings/1785780007458-decline-credit-you-did-not-earn-misattributed-corr.md)).


## Reconciliation is generative: a peer's contradiction is a lead

Every rule above is defensive — catch the bad claim. The counterpart is that **a peer's measurement contradicting yours usually means you are each holding a different slice of the state, and the slices may compose into something neither of you had.** A flaky test needed the categorical proof that a failure is not code-caused: a *same-SHA pass/fail pair*. Agent A held **attempt 1 FAILED** on head `2c9cf98fb0`; the other agent swept the run's jobs and held **`Test (Falcor)` SUCCESS** on head `2c9cf98fb0`. They reconciled rather than adjudicated, and the resolution — `gh run view --json jobs` returns **only the latest attempt**, so the second probe was reading attempt 2 — revealed *attempt 1 FAIL + attempt 2 PASS on one commit*, i.e. the pair. **The disagreement was the instrument**: had they agreed, the pair would still be invisible, because "it failed" and "it didn't" each contain no same-SHA proof. So do not stop at *who was right*; ask **what the union of the two observations proves that neither proved alone** ([a peer contradicting your measurement is a lead](../learnings/1785992330335-a-peer-contradicting-your-measurement-is-a-lead-no.md)). Two corollaries fell out of the same exchange. The credit for the pair was offered and *declined* — neither party had both halves, so it was recorded as jointly derived and claimed by neither; misattribution runs in the **flattering** direction precisely because nobody audits arriving credit, and the discriminators used to reject blame (transcript term-frequency, who ran which command) apply unchanged to accepting praise, which is the same conclusion reached independently in [decline credit you did not earn](../learnings/1785780007458-decline-credit-you-did-not-earn-misattributed-corr.md). And **a defect you have already admitted and fixed becomes the cheapest account of the next anomaly, which is exactly what makes it a bad default**: earlier that session the agent had genuinely confused two similarly-named CI jobs and corrected it, so when a fresh contradiction appeared "I misread the job again" was sitting there *pre-validated*, was accepted, and a true report was retracted — the explanation skipped the scrutiny a new hypothesis would have received. **When an anomaly resembles a mistake you already own, that resemblance is a reason for more checking, not less.** This is the constructive complement to [don't manufacture a mechanism for someone else's error](../learnings/1785781538948-don-t-manufacture-a-mechanism-for-someone-else-s-e.md): the charitable-toward-others and the self-blaming account both attract too little audit, for the same reason — nothing downstream tests a story that ends the argument comfortably.


## Separate the observation from the story: a wrong mechanism riding a correct conclusion draws no pushback

Across three intense triage chains (slang#12441, #12443, slang-rhi#818) the recurring failure was not measuring wrong — it was **publishing a mechanism that explains a correct observation but is itself false**, which no outcome contradicts. On #12441 every *observation* about two SPIR-V emit bugs survived review while **three invented mechanisms** did not (a spurious "must also fix `isSignedType`" — never consulted once the classifier sees `Float`; a "vector case passes because `OpVectorShuffle` produces a fresh type" — opcode selection happens *before* operands emit, and the agent's own IR dump disproved it; a "consumer unwrap is right because the attribute is load-bearing" — read off a *different* type than the buggy one). A wrong mechanism riding a right decision draws no pushback from outcomes, because a reviewer who agrees with the verdict never re-derives the reasoning — so state the observation as measured and leave the cause explicitly unestablished when it is ([Three plausible mechanisms attached to correct observations, all wrong: separate the observation from the story before publishing](../learnings/1786308509100-three-plausible-mechanisms-attached-to-correct-obs.md)). The discipline that catches it: **an explanation must be run against the PASSING case, not only the failing one** — on #12443 two proposed root causes both predicted the observed failure yet also predicted failure in the *local-variable* bound that is observed to succeed; the refuting cell had been measured hours before either mechanism was proposed, so neither party lacked data, both lacked the habit ([An explanation must be run against the passing case — the refuting cell usually already exists](../learnings/1786312138714-an-explanation-must-be-run-against-the-passing-cas.md)). And **a confounded matrix makes the wrong variable look causal**: #12443's first matrix cleanly isolated "function parameter vs local" until a global and a struct-field cell (header-phase but not parameters) showed the real axis is the *declaration-checking phase*, not parameter-ness — every original cell varied both at once ([A confounded matrix: entangling two variables makes the wrong one look causal](../learnings/1786307626055-a-confounded-matrix-entangling-two-variables-makes.md)).

## A reviewer's source trace is a hypothesis; rebuild before accepting it

On slang#12994 a peer reviewer (and its correctness sub-reviewer) reported a 🔴 with high confidence and a detailed end-to-end source trace — a non-canonicalizing `isRayPayloadStructType` would diverge from the IR gate for a `typealias` payload member and re-introduce E40000 — and an adjudication round even "confirmed" it live and load-bearing. An A/B rebuild of slangc with only `->getCanonicalType()` reverted showed the aliased case *still* compiled correctly (unqualified → no E40000; qualified → E40022 fired): the fix is a **no-op** for those spellings, and the trace, however plausible, did not match runtime behavior. Rules: (1) a detailed source trace is a hypothesis, not proof — when a review claims "test X fails without your change" and you can rebuild in minutes, do the fail-before/pass-after A/B first (a reviewer's own "source-derived, not rebuilt" low-confidence note is an explicit cue to verify); (2) report the *verified behavior*, not the unverified mechanism (keep a correct-by-construction change but frame it as "A/B shows it's a no-op here", and drop any earlier "why" claim you didn't confirm); (3) truthfulness outranks deferring to a confident reviewer ([verify a reviewer's source-derived bug claim by rebuilding before accepting it](../learnings/1789057073385-verify-a-reviewer-s-source-derived-bug-claim-by-re.md)).

## Never infer a dispatch property from a defect property

A quieter member of the same family is inferring one *property* of a thing from a different, independent property that happens to be nearby. Two agents independently concluded "the CI fault is scoped to one runner ⇒ rerunning is futile, it could just reland on the same bad box" — a claim relayed to a human operator twice before the author's own rerun disproved it ([never infer a DISPATCH property from a DEFECT property](../learnings/1785838936616-never-infer-a-dispatch-property-from-a-defect-prop.md)). The attribution ("fault is host-scoped") was *correct*; the error was structural — **scope-of-fault and scope-of-routing are independent facts needing independent evidence**, and "the fault is host-scoped" implies "a rerun lands on the same host" only under a *pinning* mechanism, whereas `runs-on: [Windows, self-hosted, regression-test]` is label-based dispatch, the exact opposite of pinning (proof on one unchanged head: SLANGWIN5 ❌, SLANGWIN5 ❌, SLANGWIN4 ✅). The `runs-on:` line is the instrument for the routing fact and neither agent read it — the recurring shape being that verification effort flows to whatever is most recently *disputed*, so a premise nobody is arguing about (here the dispatch rule) never gets checked. Rules: when a remedy's justification contains "it would just happen again," **name the mechanism that would make it happen again — then go read it**; prove a machine is still in the pool with a *passing job*, never with a missing row (zero jobs of a type in a window measures dispatch, not drainage); and a defect can be job-scoped on a perfectly healthy host, which kills "recycle the runner" as the remedy — look for a same-host sibling-job success before naming a fix. And when a retraction must propagate, land it in the **most-read position** (frontmatter `description`, a body banner, the index row), not merely appended — appending leaves the stale claim exactly where a reader lands first.
