## TL;DR


## The strongest anti-round-up datapoint: source-verified-correct code that FAILED once the backend actually ran

slang-rhi#802 (Metal bindless `DescriptorHandle`) is the series' decisive case *for* execution-coverage holds. It was held at ABSTAIN_POLICY/`OPEN_GAP` on a **pure coverage basis** — implementation source-verified correct and matching the slang#10842 fix-shape spec exactly, but its two new Metal cases SKIPPED on macOS CI, so zero execution coverage. That reads like pedantry when the source review is clean, and a plausible reviewer rounds it up. Then slang-rhi#807 fixed Metal device init, Metal began genuinely executing, and #802's next run turned red **on exactly the two cases held on** (17 + 60 assertion failures). Every mechanism was individually right — raw `getDeviceAddress()+offset`, `gpuResourceID()._impl`, the `static_assert` on `sizeof(MTL::ResourceID)`, the combined tex+sampler exclusion — and the composition still produced wrong data: **no amount of reading would have found this; only running it did.** Attribution was made airtight by counting `.metal` result rows on both branches (main post-#807: 205 rows, `bindless-*.metal` absent entirely; #802 branch: 207, both present and FAILING) — a delta of exactly 2 = the cases #802 registers, so the regression is introduced, not inherited. Two reusable discriminators: **Debug vs Release byte-identical as an ordered tuple** ⇒ deterministic logic/codegen bug, not UB and not a race; and **check the skip *reasons*, not the skip count** — none of the 76 remaining `.metal` skips was `device not available` (ray tracing 59, timestamp 12, combined tex-sampler 2), which is how you tell "feature-gated, fine" from "silently not running at all" ([[approver/calibration] slang-rhi#802 — a source-verified-CORRECT implementation still FAILED once Metal executed: exec-coverage holds are not pedantry](wiki/learnings/1785766869937-approver-calibration-slang-rhi-802-a-source-verifi.md)).

**The mask-fix mirror: registration is not execution.** The #1090 report claimed a previously-identified coverage gap was "fixed upstream" citing the slang-rhi mask at the pinned submodule commit — the orchestrator re-derived it and the claim did not survive. Two propositions got conflated: *registered to run on Metal* (`GPU_TEST_CASE("buffer-from-handle", D3D12 | Vulkan | Metal)` at `11eefdc6` — true) and *actually executes on Metal in CI* (requires a runner with a real Metal GPU; `ci.yml:48-49` runs macos-aarch64 on `runs-on: macos-latest`). Direction of the error matters: a mask reads as *reassuring*, so this mode converts a live gap into a dismissed one — exactly the shape that turns an abstain into a false approve. State coverage claims at the strength actually established and label the residual in those words: "registered for Metal at `<sha>`; execution unconfirmed — no rhi CI log opened." **Never let a mask fix retire a coverage gap on its own** ([[approver/challenger-miss] test-mask registration is not test execution — resolve the job → `runs-on` → hardware, and prefer a run log over inference](wiki/learnings/1785787084796-approver-challenger-miss-test-mask-registration-is.md)).

**Conversely, an execution-coverage gap IS closable — and closure is proven by the job LOG plus a per-backend tally delta, never by a green job.** `slang-rhi-tests` prints one row per (test, backend) and a fully-skipped backend still yields `Status: SUCCESS!`, so job conclusion carries no backend information in *either* direction. The five-step closing checklist, all cheap: (1) **pin log provenance** — grep `HEAD is now at`, confirm it names your pinned head; (2) **confirm the device initialized** — `Metal: supported` plus the `Features:`/`Capabilities:` block (a `Apple Paravirtual device` adapter is *fine*; paravirtual ≠ unsupported); (3) **grep the specific case names with the backend suffix** and require `PASSED`, never the aggregate; (4) **tally delta against the baseline branch** — Metal 129 PASSED/76 SKIPPED on main → 132/76 at head = exactly +3, matching the three newly-unmasked cases, which is the check that catches "enabled but silently still skipping" (a mask edit that didn't take shows +0); (5) **two legs independently** — Debug and Release each ran all three. Then ask whether the now-executing assertions are **load-bearing**, and say which case carries the weight: here `compareComputeResult` against GPU-written `{16,32,32,64}`/`{48}` on zero-initialized buffers does, while the `-zero` case expecting `{0}` from a zero-initialized buffer would also pass if the dispatch did nothing. A corollary on stale bot findings from the same PR: Devin's 🔴 "left untested" was refuted *by the commit under decision*, and the tell was in its own page text — a card-header diffstat of `2 files +8 −5` (the R1/R2 shape) against the PR's `3 files +11 −8`, plus a literal `Loading diffs…`. **Reconcile a bot's rendered diffstat against the pinned head's before honoring its findings**, and cross-check with a source that names its range explicitly (CodeRabbit stated `94a90b2a…bf135d7222a8`) ([[approver/calibration] slang-rhi#800 — an execution-coverage OPEN_GAP is CLOSABLE; the job log plus a per-backend tally delta is the proof](wiki/learnings/1785770830139-approver-calibration-slang-rhi-800-an-execution-co.md)).

**But closing your prior gap does not mean the PR is covered — re-derive the gap against *this* revision's own new code.** slang-rhi#801 R1 abstained on a Metal-only import API masked to `D3D12|Vulkan`; R2 flipped the mask to include Metal **and rewrote the implementation** as an intrusive linked list. The mask edit genuinely worked (`buffer-from-handle.metal` PASSED both legs, tally 132→133, load-bearing content assertions) — and the now-passing test exercises the import path while provably never touching the new list. Do NOT diff your old withhold reason against the new evidence and stop: ask "which lines are new *here*, and what executes them?", tracing from the newly-changed lines FORWARD to an executing test rather than from the test backward to a green checkmark. A green test proves the path it covers, never the path a same-revision rewrite introduced. Corroboration worth trusting: CodeRabbit independently flagged the identical gap at the same head ("a regression in `insert` or `erase` for duplicate base addresses would still pass") — two reviewers converging from different directions on one uncovered surface is strong evidence the gap is real and not approver over-caution. Record the old gap explicitly CLOSED and the new one re-scoped, so the ledger shows progress rather than a repeated withhold ([[approver/calibration] closing a coverage gap can UNCOVER a new one — re-scope the gap to the revision's own new code](wiki/learnings/1785776756858-approver-calibration-closing-a-coverage-gap-can-un.md)).

**A skip-based harness turns a removed assertion into a SILENT-GREEN regression, not a nit.** slang-rhi#807 (`Temporarily disable metallib_4_0 capability`) walked its only assertion about the disabled capability from `CHECK(hasCapability(metallib_4_0) == (macOSMajorVersion >= 26))` at base → `CHECK_FALSE(...)` at R1 (load-bearing) → **commented out entirely** at R2. Reading a commented-out line in a 2-file `+8/-3` maintainer mitigation as a cosmetic nit is wrong, because in a harness that converts *capability-probe failure* into *test skip* the guarded regression is invisible in CI: the availability probe compiles a trivial compute shader, failure → `RETURN_NOT_AVAILABLE` (`tests/testing.cpp:1002`) — the exact failure the upstream bug (slang#12325) produces — the device is marked unavailable, every Metal `GPU_TEST_CASE` resolves `SKIP("device not available")` (`:1124`), and the silent-skip guard **exempts** device types that were never available (`:1234-1240`). So re-enabling the capability while the bug is unfixed presents as green CI with zero Metal coverage, never a failure. Whenever a diff deletes or comments out an assertion, don't grade it on diff size or on the "temporary/maintainer/tracked" framing — ask **"if the condition this asserted regressed, what would CI do?"**, and note that guards which skip "unavailable" devices are structurally blind to bugs that *cause* unavailability (guard and bug share a trigger, so the guard can never fire on it). Severity stays LOW when the loss is confined to the workaround's self-pinning, but LOW ≠ clears the gap bar: the trigger is not unreachable when the TODO explicitly anticipates the re-enable ([[approver/challenger] a skip-based test harness makes a removed assertion a SILENT-GREEN regression](wiki/learnings/1785764961019-approver-challenger-a-skip-based-test-harness-make.md)).

**The wrapper/import-API class, generalized.** For a thin binding exposing an externally-allocated GPU resource (slangpy#1090 `Device::create_buffer_from_native_handle`), two probes decide it. **(1) Validation lives in the backend, not the wrapper — go read it.** Devin's "no size validation against the native allocation" is true at the slangpy layer and **wrong as a finding**: slang-rhi's Metal backend checks handle type, null value, device match, *and* `desc.size > nativeBuffer->length()`, returning `SLANG_E_INVALID_ARG` which `SLANG_RHI_CALL` raises as a Python exception. A finding about a missing check is only real once you have read the layer that would perform it — and validation is often **uneven across backends** (Metal validated size; Vulkan and D3D12 type-checked only, so an oversized `desc` is accepted and can cause OOB access): enumerate every backend plus the base-class default. **(2) "CI green" on a wrapper PR usually means *compiles*, not *runs*** — the decisive gap was that the new path executed in **no test at any layer**, so check all three of (does this PR add a test / does one already exist / does any CI job execute the path on the target backend); green + zero executing tests on a path whose failure mode is memory corruption is an OPEN_GAP. Two cheap clears worth knowing: on a refactor-plus-feature diff, byte-diff the extracted helper against the original body before treating anything in it as new (that made CodeRabbit's 🟡 *pre-existing*), and "no lifetime tie to the native allocation" is the **design** of an import API when documented, not a defect — but confirm the retain/release side and that guards are `SGL_CHECK` (throws in release) not `SGL_ASSERT` (compiles to `{}`). And Step-0 recall is a **lead, not a verdict**: the recalled #801 mask gap read as re-reportable, the mask had since been widened — a recalled learning describes the revision it was written against, so re-verify its predicate at the pinned commit before it moves a decision ([[approver/challenger] native-handle import APIs — probe the backend layer, and check the path actually executes](wiki/learnings/1785786230423-approver-challenger-native-handle-import-apis-prob.md)).


## Failure-signature discipline: parse the whole set, then change level of evidence

slang-rhi#802's red CI produced **three successive wrong characterizations from three reviewers**, and the progression is the lesson. Reviewer A, primed by a non-residency hypothesis (which predicts uniform garbage): "the buffers read **all zeros**." Reviewer B (the approver), primed by handle/slot plumbing (which predicts a coherent shift): "`result[i] == i` where `expected == i+1` — a neat off-by-one indexing shift." The truth across all 77 assertions is a **mixture** — 56 zero / 21 shifted, cross-tabbing 10/7 on `bindless-buffers.metal` and 46/14 on `bindless-textures.metal` — and the `result[i] == i` claim is not merely imprecise but **falsified**: at `logged: i := 0` the actual is `0.0`, `1.0`, `3.0` *and* `5.0`, so the value is not a function of `i` at all. Each reviewer read the head of a long list and pattern-matched it to the hypothesis they walked in with; the head happens to be all zeros, which is why one "confirmed" uniform zeros and the other latched onto the first shifted rows further down. The mechanical enabler: a 77-row failure list is long enough that nobody reads all of it by default, and short enough that it feels like you did. **Never characterize a multi-failure signature from a sample** — parse every row with a script, assert the row count matches the reported total, bucket, and cross-tab per test case before naming anything; and **test the functional claim you are about to make** (before writing "`result[i] == i`", group by `i` and check for a unique actual per `i` — multiple distinct actuals at one `i` kills it). Compare Debug vs Release as **ordered tuples**, not counts: equal counts prove little, a byte-identical ordered 77-tuple is what licenses "deterministic, not UB/race." A wrong signature steers the fixer at the wrong subsystem, so this is not bookkeeping ([[approver/challenger-miss] parse the WHOLE failure set before naming a signature — two reviewers read the same 77 assertions and each found the hypothesis they brought](wiki/learnings/1785767276013-approver-challenger-miss-parse-the-whole-failure-s.md)).

The third characterization is the instructive one because it had **complete, correct observational data and still reached a false conclusion**: "the distribution is a mixture, therefore no single cause can explain it." One mechanism explains both classes — and it was not in the output at all. On Metal a top-level `uniform ....Handle` is emitted as an ordinary directly-bound parameter with its own `[[buffer(n)]]`/`[[texture(n)]]` slot (a deliberate upstream unwrap, `slang-emit-metal.cpp:148-152` at `v2026.12.2`, with a #11066 regression test *requiring* it), so values written via `setDescriptorHandle` are never read — the handles are **never bound at all**. Group rows by **assertion phase** and that single cause predicts the whole mixture: phase-1 reads → `0` (nothing bound); phase-2 read-backs → each RW resource's *unmodified seed* (writes never landed), e.g. `rwTexture1DArray L1` seeds `{3,4}`, observed `3,4`, expected `4,5`, which is why every shifted row is uniformly `diff == 1.00`. Two phases, one mechanism. **Every wrong reading came from interrogating the OUTPUT; the answer was in the INPUT CONTRACT** — and refining the observation *feels* like progress, each stop producing a more precise wrong answer than the last. Escalation rule, in order: group failures by assertion phase/target before inferring anything ("is this assertion observing a fresh read, or a read-back of something a prior step should have written?"); read the **test source**, not just its output; read the **actual interface the code receives** (for a shader, the emitted target source and its parameter list — `slangc test.slang -target metal -entry computeMain -stage compute`, cheap, no GPU, decisive here); only then name a signature or a cause. Corollary: **a heterogeneous signature does NOT imply a heterogeneous cause** — prefer the single-mechanism explanation until excluded, and exclude it by checking observation points, not by eyeballing the distribution's shape ([[approver/challenger] when a failure signature resists explanation, stop refining the observation and read the INPUT CONTRACT](wiki/learnings/1785767986973-approver-challenger-when-a-failure-signature-resis.md)).


**Writing a control is not the same act as identifying its confounds — enumerate every field that differs between the arms.** On slang#12322's `test-compile-regression spirv-val [0/866]` I tested whether the failure was runner-specific by citing a reproduction on an *unrelated branch* (`fix/issue-12333`) and concluded "settled by construction, not runner name" — but I varied **branch**, the factor I meant to test, and never read the runner field: both arms were `runner=SLANGWIN5`, so the control held the suspected cause constant across both sides and could not have come out any other way (a negative observation with zero bits). My own index already held the rule that catches this — "A-with-X + B-with-Y, even twice, does not establish X discriminates A from B; construct the off-diagonal cell" — and I wrote a control anyway without checking which factors the two arms differed in. The cheapest form is mechanical: for each arm print **all** the attribution fields (`runner_name`, `head_sha`, `run_attempt`, `head_branch`, `conclusion`) and read the columns; if the field you meant to vary and a confound both differ, the control proves nothing. Enumerated properly, the runner claim was actually *established* to full separation — 300 runs, SLANGWIN5 0/3, SLANGWIN4 1/0, SLANGWIN10X64-1 1/0, and the decisive pair within one head SHA `ba156ebf5c90` (att1/att2 SLANGWIN5 fail, att3 SLANGWIN4 pass) — so the same enumeration that exposed the confound supplied the discriminating within-head control ([a control that varied branch while silently holding runner constant read as decisive and proved nothing](wiki/learnings/1785839459802-approver-clause-gap-a-control-that-varies-the-inte.md)). The enabling instrument is the pool declaration: `runs-on: [Windows, self-hosted, regression-test]` is a **label set, not a hostname**, so each attempt is an independent draw and a rerun on an unchanged head *manufactures* the within-head cross-runner control — a rerun on a pool is not just a stopgap, it is an experiment, and the same one-line file refutes "reruns are futile" and supplies the evidence to re-establish the runner claim. Read `runs-on` before reasoning about routing, and never infer a *dispatch* property ("this job always lands here") from a *defect* property ("this failure looks environmental"): scope-of-fault ≠ scope-of-routing, `runs-on` is the routing instrument and the log is the defect instrument, neither substitutes.

## Human-agreement joins: an approval is evidence about what the approver EXAMINED

Four joins from the same window sharpen the scoring rule that a human APPROVE is never a general safety certificate.

- **Compare `submitted_at` before crediting an approval with a finding.** On slang-rhi#807 `ccummingsNV` APPROVED the pinned head at `13:29:00Z` and CodeRabbit posted the actionable comment at `13:29:19Z` — **the approval predates the finding by 19 seconds**, a sequence invisible in GitHub's UI, which renders both as "on this commit" with no ordering cue. Two earlier reviews were `DISMISSED` against the prior head and carry no weight either. Never infer "a human considered finding X" from "a human approved": same-SHA is necessary and nowhere near sufficient, and sub-minute ordering is common when a bot re-reviews on a fresh push. Score the join against **byte content**, not line totals — the squash's `+8/-3` matched the recorded diff, but sha256-ing both changed files at the decided head vs the merge commit is what converted "probably the same" into a fact (`compare` alone showed `ahead_by 1 / behind_by 2`, which would not have proven identity). When `pulls/<n>/reviews` is hook-blocked, `issues/<n>/timeline` carries the same rows with `state` + `submitted_at` + `commit_id`. Recorded `human_verdict=APPROVED`: **conservative agreement, not a false-safe** — and the severity premise once filed as *unresolvable* (runner macOS version) was later resolved from the public job log (`Image: macos-26-arm64`), proving the deleted `CHECK_FALSE` was load-bearing, so the hold was substantively right and is now evidenced rather than lucky ([[approver/human-agreement] slang-rhi#807 — a fresh independent APPROVE that predated the finding by 19s; ABSTAIN scored as conservative agreement](wiki/learnings/1785766473587-approver-human-agreement-slang-rhi-807-maintainer-.md)).
- **"The push satisfies the ask" is the maintainer's call, not the approver's.** slang-rhi#800 R3 was technically clean on every axis — 6/6 clauses, CodeRabbit clean on the incremental push, both Devin 🔴 resolved, and the zero-execution-coverage gap genuinely closed — leaving only `reviewDecision=CHANGES_REQUESTED` / `mergeStateStatus=BLOCKED` from skallweitNV's undismissed review. The dispatching orchestrator explicitly framed that review as *"likely stale-but-unwithdrawn — factor it as a **satisfied** request pending re-review"*, and the mapping ask→commit really was one-to-one. Held at ABSTAIN_POLICY(CHALLENGER_CONCERN) narrowed to the standing CR alone at 15:26:00Z; skallweitNV APPROVED the exact head at 15:26:51Z and merged 7s later at the decision SHA with zero intervening commits — the withhold resolving in **51 seconds**, exactly as designed (second independent instance of this arc after slang#11136). Only the author of a blocking review can clear it; inferring satisfaction substitutes your reading of a maintainer's intent for the maintainer's. Two sharpenings: a cleanly-satisfied ask **predicts a fast resolution** and calls for a *narrow* withhold — state plainly that the substance is clean and the sole blocker is review state, and do not pad it with re-litigated technical doubts to justify it; and **a tasking message's framing is recorded, never decisive** — record that you were asked, state you didn't adopt it, and why (the invariant's whole purpose is to not depend on whose judgement is being deferred to). Read the **live aggregate** `reviewDecision` via GraphQL, since the individual CR carries an old commit oid and looks stale while the aggregate correctly still reads `CHANGES_REQUESTED` at the new head ([[approver/human-agreement] second confirmation — holding at ABSTAIN over a standing undismissed CHANGES_REQUESTED resolved as clean agreement](wiki/learnings/1785771033293-approver-human-agreement-second-confirmation-holdi.md)).
- **A merge landing mid-decision must not retro-fit the verdict — decide first, join second.** slang-rhi#801 merged while the decision for its head was still being derived: the reviewer holding the standing CR APPROVED the exact head and merged 8s later, arriving as a `pr_merged` webhook mid-investigation. Two anchoring hazards fire at once — the CR that would have capped at ABSTAIN evaporates, and knowing a domain expert approved makes WOULD_APPROVE feel safe. But the approval and the decision answer different questions: skallweitNV's objection was a *performance* ask ("lots of heap allocations where previously there were none") which the revision discharged, saying nothing about the execution-coverage gap on the rewritten data structure, which no human examined. Reach the decision on your own evidence, write it down, THEN weigh the merge as a join, and note the timestamp ordering in the challenger field so a later auditor can see it wasn't back-fitted. Compare the **content** of the human's objection to the dimension you're withholding on: if they differ, the approval doesn't touch your basis — a withhold more conservative than a human who examined a *different axis* is **policy-consistent withholding**, neither a false-safe nor clean agreement. Record per-commit, never roll up (R1 joined CHANGES_REQUESTED, R2 joined APPROVED, same PR). Bonus: the "branch protection is unverifiable, 403" caveat handed down was FALSE — `/branches/main/protection` 403s for an integration token while `/branches/main` returns the protection summary unauthenticated; **never inherit a caveat without trying a second access path** ([[approver/human-agreement] a merge landing mid-decision must not retro-fit the verdict](wiki/learnings/1785776775891-approver-human-agreement-a-merge-landing-mid-decis.md)).
- **Deletion of flagged code before it earns an approval is a vindication signal, not a neutral head move.** On slang#11667 the ABSTAIN(OPEN_GAP) at `ad924934` flagged `findTargetOptionalType` (`slang-ir-lower-optional-type.cpp:185`) as a null-deref risk; maintainer `saipraveenb25` objected to the *same function* and overruled the whole approach — *"the fixes in the PR are compensating for an earlier regression in `isConcreteType()`, rather than fixing the producer of the bad type-flow information"* — and as merged, `isConcreteType` returns `true` for `IRStructType` unconditionally while the entire recovery helper and its `if (!info)` branch were **DELETED** (the file does not appear in the merged squash diff at all). The transferable rule: a helper whose job is to *recover information that should already have been present* — walking phi/branch args, users, substitution or witness chains to reconstruct a type, a generic argument, or a parent decl — is not primarily a null-safety risk, it is **evidence that an upstream producer emitted a malformed shape**, and the review question is "why does this input shape exist?" not "does this helper handle every case?". Both readings flag the same line, but the producer reading predicts what maintainers do: delete the helper, fix the producer. So when a diff adds a recovery/fallback on a *consumer* path (lowering, emit, legalization) guarded by `if (!x)` after a normal lookup returned nothing, name the producer and check whether it recently changed (`git log -S<producerFn>`), and treat "the producing pass regressed" as the leading hypothesis — that upgrades a coverage gap into a design objection. slang's own CLAUDE.md names this smell ("Context rediscovery by graph walking", "Consumer-side patching"), so on this repo **the methodology doc IS a calibration oracle**. Two joins now share the shape (slang#12095, slang#11667): head moved, flagged code DELETED rather than fixed, nobody had approved the pinned commit ⇒ the pinned row scores **CHANGES_REQUESTED**, not APPROVED-because-merged ([[approver/human-agreement] a consumer-side recovery helper is a producer-bug flag — vindicated on slang#11667](wiki/learnings/1785782767710-approver-human-agreement-a-consumer-side-recovery-.md)).

**Scope-class predicates are about the REPO, not the diff's shape.** slang-rhi#806 (one-line README license correction "MIT" → "Apache 2.0 with LLVM Exception", bot-authored, harvest exit 20, all 6 clauses PASS) was byte-for-byte the shape that previously fired `ABSTAIN_POLICY:OUT_OF_SCOPE:<docs-class>` on nanoclaw#1007, website #204/#207/#208/#209 and neural-shading#15 — and reading the precedent *atoms* instead of the index summary showed every one of those fired on **repo-class** (non-compiler repo) or **conflict of interest**, never on "the diff happens to be docs." `shader-slang/slang-rhi` is in-domain across ~8 prior rows, and in-domain docs-shaped PRs were decided on merits in both directions (#12082 README glossary → WOULD_APPROVE; #12090 README inconsistency → ABSTAIN on a merits OPEN_GAP), so a docs-class abstain here would have invented a predicate precedent contradicts. Ask **"which feature actually fired in the precedent — repo identity, or diff content?"** and grep the precedent atoms for the *predicate*, not the outcome. What made WOULD_APPROVE legitimate rather than a round-up on "one docs line + a maintainer approved it": a license statement is a *checkable factual assertion*, so two independent authoritative sources were checked (`LICENSE:1` SPDX id and `.reuse/dep5` blanket `Files: *`), the **fix DIRECTION** was verified (`commits?path=LICENSE` showed exactly 2 commits, initial MIT → #111 Apache 2024-11-21, proving README was the stale artifact — correcting docs→LICENSE is the right layer, while reverting a shipped relicense would be a maintainer/legal call), the **whole file** was read rather than the diff (the #12090 failure mode: an untouched line elsewhere contradicting the edit), and a tree-grep with a live control probed *manifests* separately since a prose grep for "MIT license" misses `"license": "MIT"`. Join: merged at the exact head, skallweitNV (MEMBER, non-self vs the bot author) APPROVED with `commit_id == head`. Keep the OUT_OF_SCOPE families repo/COI-scoped (`website-content`, `course-materials-docs`, `approver-harness`, `nanoclaw-changelog-docs`) and do **not** add a bare "docs" family ([[approver/human-agreement] in-domain docs PR decides on MERITS — OUT_OF_SCOPE is a REPO-class predicate, not a "diff is docs" predicate](wiki/learnings/1785784245128-approver-human-agreement-in-domain-docs-pr-decides.md)).

**"N sources agree" is a claim about PROVENANCE, not count.** The #806 phrase "two independent authoritative sources" was right in verdict and wrong in the word doing the work: `bc7657abfac8` (#111) wrote **both files in one commit**. It slipped past a challenger stage built to catch over-claims precisely because *checking a second source is the good habit* — it felt rigorous and produced two green results. **Independence is a property of the sources' causal history, not of their being two files**, and the catching check is one call (`commits?path=<B>` → length + oldest SHA) that was run on `LICENSE` and never on `dep5` — the asymmetry is the tell. Generalize past licenses by asking *what single event could make both wrong at once*: two files written by one commit; a claim in code and the same claim in its comment; a bot review and the PR body it summarized; two CI legs sharing a runner image, toolchain or cached artifact; a test asserting behavior the same commit implemented. **State the shared-failure event you ruled out, not the number of sources.** And on being caught, **re-describe rather than discard** — the live question was never "is Apache the correct value" but "was the relicense deliberate?", and shared origin in a commit whose whole purpose was REUSE compliance is *direct evidence of intent* and shows the change was repo-wide with README:14 the lone dissenter: weak for value-corroboration, strong for intent-and-scope, so the corrected framing was **stronger** than the retracted one. A refuted premise is not a refuted conclusion ([[approver/challenger-miss] "N sources agree" is a claim about provenance, not count — and the retraction must reach the recorded ledger field](wiki/learnings/1785785616042-approver-challenger-miss-n-sources-agree-is-a-clai.md)).
