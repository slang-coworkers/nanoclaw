# PR-Approver: Endpoint-Split Review Signal & Proxy-Reading Clauses

Two structural blind spots in the approver's Step-1 machinery: the review signal is scattered across three GitHub endpoints and the harvest queries one, and three of the mechanical clauses evaluate a *proxy* for the thing they name — in every case failing toward the answer that permits an approval. Sibling pages: [decision procedure](review-approver-decision-procedure.md), [Devin tier & critique-gate](review-approver-devin-tier-and-critique-gate.md), [harvest endpoints](review-approver-harvest-endpoints.md), [OUTPUT_REVIEW evidence discipline](review-approver-output-review-evidence.md).

## TL;DR

- The single largest approver defect class is a **correct tally over the wrong surface**, not a wrong verdict. GitHub scatters PR feedback across three disjoint endpoints — `pulls/N/reviews` (wrappers + `state`), `pulls/N/comments` (inline findings, where substance lives), `issues/N/comments` (maintainer directives, no review object) — and the harvest queries one.
- An empty-body `COMMENTED` review is a wrapper, not silence — look one level down at the inline comments. Text is not automatically actionable: an author answering a bot (`in_reply_to_id` at a bot comment) must not re-arm the procedure; a reviewer raising an objection must.
- Never characterize a page's contents from a summarized/lossy read — enumerate with stdlib `urllib` and count in code. Author and reviewer comments can land on any page.
- Whether findings show in a review body is CodeRabbit formatting luck, not harvester correctness. Bucket inline-comment provenance by `original_commit_id` (GitHub rewrites `commit_id` as head advances). A green bot commit-status does not imply a harvestable review object.
- When you find a parsing bug, sweep your own stored artifacts for the signature and partition hits by whether the field was load-bearing. An artifact-level defect is not a decision-level harm — open the decision input of record (`review-doc.md`) before crying false-safe; over-claiming in the alarming direction is also miscalibration.
- Treat "is there a standing blocker?" as a union over all three endpoints, filtered by `author_association` — a MEMBER/OWNER/COLLABORATOR plain issue comment outranks a bot `COMMENTED` review. Authority is `author_association`, never API shape; record which endpoint each directive came from.
- For a join, `issues/N/timeline` interleaves force-pushes, reviews, and merges in one timestamp ordering; on a force-pushed fork, confirm content by sha256 of file blobs, not ahead/behind.
- A predicate written in one endpoint's vocabulary silently excludes the others — prefer semantic terms ("actionable feedback from a non-bot") over schema terms ("review with state X"). After any predicate fix, evaluate against KNOWN CURRENT STATE: a never-fires predicate is bad, an always-fires one is worse. The missing discriminator is often the ADDRESSEE, not the endpoint.
- A deletion is not a disposition: approve/reject/expiry converge on the same absence. Naming an unreachable authoritative field is a complete answer, not a hedge — a wrong identifier is worse than none.
- Harvest exit-10 "stale" after a main-merge `synchronize` is a false skip if the reviewed blobs are unchanged — the findings are live. Provenance-label every secondary signal (Devin, CodeRabbit) before calling it head-current.
- Three clauses read proxies, each failing toward approval: `ci_green_on_sha` reads legacy combined status (blind to Actions check-runs) and `require_ci_green` defaults TRUE; `commit_match` reads `reviews[].commit_id` (retroactively rewritten to head) — compare `submitted_at` timestamps, not SHAs; `tier_eligible` is a threshold on a total — re-evaluate per head, never reason about the delta.
- Non-terminal / absent build signal on both CI surfaces ⇒ `unevaluable`, not `pass`. Count check-runs by predicate and reconcile the parts against `total_count`; green ≠ exercised on accelerator-gated paths (a built macOS leg runs no Metal GPU test).

## The review signal is endpoint-split — three surfaces, and the harvest reads one

GitHub scatters PR feedback across three disjoint endpoints, and the harvest queries one: **`pulls/N/reviews`** (review *wrappers*, the only place a `state` lives), **`pulls/N/comments`** (inline findings — usually the *substance*), and **`issues/N/comments`** (PR conversation, where **maintainer directives** arrive with no review object and no state).

**Split #1 — an empty-body `COMMENTED` review is a wrapper, not silence.** Submit an inline batch with no top-level prose and `/reviews` shows `body: ""` while `/comments` carries multi-paragraph text at the same timestamp. On slang#12080 two opposite wrong conclusions were each one step away — "empty body ⇒ nothing said ⇒ retract the nudge" (the author had written four substantial replies) and "a maintainer spoke ⇒ re-decide" (the text was the author answering *bot* findings). First ask "is there text on any surface", then classify by **audience**: `in_reply_to_id` at a bot comment marks a burst informational. *Empty body ≠ silence*; *text ≠ actionable* — author-answers-bot must not re-arm the procedure, reviewer-raises-objection must ([[approver/clause-gap] an empty-body COMMENTED review is a wrapper, not silence — the substance lives on the inline-comments endpoint](../learnings/1785761437816-approver-clause-gap-an-empty-body-commented-review.md)). ⚠️ That learning's supporting claim *"the relevant replies were on page 3, page 1 was entirely July bot traffic"* is **FALSE and retracted**: a deterministic count shows **250** inline comments over pages of 100/100/**50**, 37 by the author, spread across the early pages too. It came from a summarizing fetch, so its "paginate to page 3" advice was right by accident; the real defect was using a lossy reader for an enumeration question. Comments can land on **any** page — enumerate with stdlib `urllib` and count in code until a short page, and grep already-fetched material before reporting a negative ([[approver/clause-gap] CORRECTION — the empty-body-wrapper learning's page-1 claim is false; grep your own already-fetched material before reporting a negative](../learnings/1785761810820-approver-clause-gap-correction-to-empty-body-comme.md)).

**Split #2 — CodeRabbit findings live on `pulls/N/comments`; the harvest tallies `reviews[].body`.** This one under-reads on a **successful exit-0 harvest**. A CodeRabbit body is often status boilerplate only (`**Actionable comments posted: 11**` plus collapsed run-config) with **zero** severity markers, while the 11 findings — 2 🟠 Major / Functional Correctness on slang-rhi#803 R1 — sit on the unqueried endpoint. Seeing findings in the body is **formatting luck, not harvester correctness**; "no body markers ⇒ no findings" is never valid. GitHub **rewrites** an inline comment's `commit_id` as head advances (8 of 11 R1 findings later reported R3), so bucket by **`original_commit_id`**; and a **green CodeRabbit commit status does not imply a harvestable review object**. Not a bot-detection bug — the harvest uses an exact-match REST allowlist (`login in ("github-actions[bot]","coderabbitai[bot]")`). The suffix asymmetry still traps hand-written checks: REST `user.login` carries `[bot]` (`user.type=="Bot"`), GraphQL `author.login` is bare (`__typename=="Bot"`), and `gh pr view --json reviews` returns `login` only. The procedural failure behind the row: exit-22 correctly said `pending`, the review landed 3 min later, and the artifact was synthesized 7 min after that with no final re-probe — *a timeout describes a past instant*, so re-probe at the LAST moment before committing ([CodeRabbit findings live on pulls/N/comments, not reviews[].body — a successful exit-0 harvest can score 0 findings when 11 exist](../learnings/1785778143329-coderabbit-findings-live-on-pulls-n-comments-not-r.md)).

**Blast radius, and how to report it.** Across stored rows the signature (`Actionable comments posted: N>0`, zero body markers) fires on **11 of 18** CodeRabbit harvests that found a review — 17 findings never machine-parsed, every harvest exit 0. Partition hits by *load-bearingness*: where a clause FAILED the decision short-circuited at Step 1 and the under-read is cosmetic; where all clauses passed, the review signal **was** the decision input (6 rows, one a WOULD_APPROVE on slang-rhi#797 whose Devin had timed out). When you find a parsing bug, **sweep your own stored artifacts for the signature and partition by load-bearingness** ([[approver/infra-abstain] endpoint-split audit: 11 of 18 CodeRabbit harvests silently under-read — detection recipe + load-bearing partition](../learnings/1785779251344-approver-infra-abstain-endpoint-split-audit-11-of-.md)). On slangpy **11 of 12** `N>0` reviews carried zero body markers (~92%), yet only 2 of 40 rows were wrong, because **the agent reading `pulls/N/comments` by hand was the safety net, not the tooling** (100% of inline findings on the 7 affected rows are cited by `file:line`). The 2 bad rows are exactly where the harvest concluded **no review existed** (exit 22-timeout, exit 20), so nothing prompted a manual read — *the under-read becomes a wrong row only when it coincides with a false absence*. High manual compensation is a latent defect, not reassurance ([[approver/clause-gap] the CodeRabbit body-only tally under-reads ~92% of the time — manual reading, not tooling, was the safety net](../learnings/1785779185938-approver-clause-gap-the-coderabbit-body-only-tally.md)).

⚠️ **An artifact-level defect is not a decision-level harm.** The same audit first escalated slang-rhi#797 as a probable false-safe from `harvest.json` alone; the decision input (`review/review-doc.md`) quoted **both** findings verbatim with severity and an explicit call (*"🟠 Major … test-only … Not blocking"*), result `bugs:0, gaps:1, questions:1, APPROVE_WITH_NITS`; 9 of 9 auditable rows carry severity markers in the doc. Keep **is the artifact wrong?** apart from **was the decision wrong?** (answerable only from `review-doc.md` and recorded verdict fields). "The signal field is untrustworthy on N rows" is true; "N findings were missed" needs per-row confirmation — publishing it unverified is miscalibration in the *alarming* direction, the mirror of rounding up to approve. Finding a bug creates momentum toward finding victims; slow down there ([[approver/critique-mustfix] an artifact-level defect is not a decision-level harm — open the decision input of record before crying false-safe](../learnings/1785780014953-approver-critique-mustfix-an-artifact-level-defect.md)).

**Split #3 — maintainer directives arrive on `issues/N/comments` with no state, and outrank everything with one.** On slang-rhi#803 a scan kept reporting "7 reviews, all COMMENTED — no standing blocker" while skallweitNV (MEMBER) had written *"we want to keep slang-rhi free from using git submodules. Can you fetch TinyBVH through FetchContent…?"* — a change request invalidating the approach, invisible to `pulls/N/reviews` by construction. Treat the standing-blocker question as a **union over all three endpoints, filtered by `author_association`** (MEMBER/OWNER/COLLABORATOR issue comment outranks a bot `COMMENTED` review), paginate all three, and record the endpoint per directive so a later scan doesn't "retire" it by re-checking only reviews. One maintainer's comments differ in kind: a bandwidth note is a tripwire; "do X instead of Y" is a directive a later revision must satisfy ([[approver/clause-gap] maintainer directives live on issues/N/comments and carry no review state — union all three endpoints, authority from author_association](../learnings/1785780753487-approver-clause-gap-maintainer-directive-on-issues.md)).

**For a join, `issues/N/timeline` orders everything together.** Joining slang#11667 (`pr_merged`) against a row pinned at `ad924934`, `/reviews` showed only bot reviews at the pin plus a later human `approved` — reading as "no human objected; merge is APPROVED-equivalent." Both wrong: a plain issue comment had rejected the approach, and the head was force-pushed between it and the approval. `gh api repos/O/R/issues/N/timeline?per_page=100 --paginate` interleaves `committed`, `head_ref_force_pushed`, `reviewed`, `merged`, `closed` in one ordering, proving the maintainer objected AT the pinned head (scorable as CHANGES_REQUESTED). On a force-pushed fork `compare A...B` misleads (`ahead_by=108` from a rebase); confirm content by **sha256 of file blobs** via `contents/<path>?ref=<sha>`. Never infer "a human considered X" from "a human approved" — compare timestamps ([[approver/clause-gap] score a join with issues/N/timeline — it interleaves force-pushes with reviews, and maintainer vetoes arrive as plain issue comments](../learnings/1785782777239-approver-clause-gap-score-a-join-with-issues-n-tim.md)).

### A predicate in one endpoint's vocabulary silently excludes the others

The harvester bug and the resume-trigger bug are one defect: **the query's data model became the definition of the thing**, invisibly, because the query succeeded. A resume trigger recorded as *"a non-bot **review** with an actionable **state** lands"* could never fire on its own blocker, which lived only on `issues/N/comments`. For any gate/trigger/blocker predicate ask **which surfaces can carry this signal, and does my vocabulary reach all of them?** — prefer semantic terms over schema terms. Companion trap: **a deletion is not a disposition.** An approvals queue reading `[]` with `get` → "approval not found" does not mean "rejected" — rows delete on approve, reject, or expiry alike, so absence identifies the *set*, not the member (like `mergeable_state`, which says *that* something is unmet, never *which*). Enumerate every path producing the same absence; if more than one, mark the disposition **unknowable**. Naming an unreachable authoritative field ("cannot read; F is authoritative; the tier that can read it should map it") is a *complete* answer — **a wrong identifier is worse than none**, since the next reader re-verifies *through* it ([[approver/clause-gap] a deletion is not a disposition, and a predicate in one endpoint's vocabulary silently excludes the others](../learnings/1785819644171-approver-clause-gap-a-deletion-is-not-a-dispositio.md)).

⚠️ **Widening that never-fires predicate produced an always-fires one — the missing discriminator was the ADDRESSEE.** *"Actionable non-bot feedback in ANY endpoint"* is already satisfied by the very directive being waited on, so it would wake a fresh decision immediately, forever. That comment opens `@<contributor>`: a change request **to the PR author**, not an inbound to the approval decision. Ordered test for a decision-relevant inbound: (1) non-bot author via `author_association` (not a login suffix — `--json reviews` strips `[bot]`); (2) addressed to the reviewer/decision, not the contributor; (3) changes a load-bearing input (size total, gate/ABI premise, CI state, standing verdict). All three, or no resume. **After any predicate fix, evaluate it against KNOWN CURRENT STATE: if it fires now with nothing new, it is wrong.** Always-fires is the worse half — it burns a re-decision each check and trains readers to ignore the signal ([[approver/clause-gap] widening a never-fires predicate produced an always-fires one — the missing discriminator was the addressee](../learnings/1785821476819-approver-clause-gap-widening-a-never-fires-predica.md)).

**Harvest exit-10 "stale" is a false skip when the `synchronize` was a main-merge that didn't touch the reviewed files.** "Stale" is SHA inequality (`harvest.commit_id != pinned head`), but a merge of `main` often leaves the PR's reviewed files byte-identical, so the findings are fully live and the default (fall to Devin-only) discards the strongest signal — on slangpy#1146 the stale CodeRabbit review carried a 🟠 Major correctness finding, and Devin-clean would have yielded a false-safe WOULD_APPROVE. Before falling to Devin-only, diff the *reviewed files* between the review's `commit_id` and head (`gh api compare/<review_commit>...<head>` `.files`, or per-file blobs); if identical, synthesize the review doc from those findings, set `_approver_result.commit_id` = head so `commit_match` passes, document the SHA-lag-but-identical-blobs fact, and judge normally (here → ABSTAIN_POLICY/OPEN_GAP). Read the artifact, not the exit code ([harvest exit-10 "stale" is a false skip when the synchronize was a main-merge that didn't touch the reviewed files](../learnings/1789057105461-approver-clause-gap-harvest-exit-10-stale-is-a-fal.md)).

**Provenance-label every corroborating signal before citing it as head-current.** Only the PRIMARY `github-actions[bot]` review is commit-verified (harvest.json `commit_id` + `diff_hash`, checked by `commit_match`). Devin's reviewed commit is whatever `devin-fetch.sh` recorded (often `"unknown"`); CodeRabbit reviews its own last-seen commit, which lags a fresh `synchronize`. On slang#12887 DECISION_REVIEW returned must-fix twice for calling Devin "head-current" at `"unknown"` and citing CodeRabbit's "Merge Risk Low" from one commit behind — a lagging secondary inflates the evidence base ([provenance-label every corroborating review signal before citing as head-current](../learnings/1789093237239-approver-critique-mustfix-provenance-label-every-c.md)).

## Clauses read proxies — `ci_green_on_sha`, `commit_match`, `tier_eligible`

Three of the six mechanical clauses evaluate a *proxy* for what they name, each failing toward approval.

**`ci_green_on_sha` reads the legacy combined status, blind to GitHub Actions.** `eval-clauses.py:187` uses only `commits/{sha}/status`; `grep -cF check-runs eval-clauses.py` = **0** in both approvers. Actions posts check-runs, not statuses, so `state == "success"` means only "the legacy statuses passed." On slangpy#1144 it recorded pass (`license/cla` + `CodeRabbit`) while all 12 `build (...)` check-runs were `in_progress`; the host `APPROVER_CI_GATE` normally backstops this by waking the approver only on a settled head, so it bites when the gate is OFF ([[approver/clause-gap] ci_green_on_sha reads the Status API, blind to in-progress check-runs](../learnings/1788764743013-approver-clause-gap-ci-green-on-sha-reads-the-stat.md)). On slangpy#1090 the same `success` covered **6 of 12 builds `in_progress`**, including both macOS legs — the only Metal-capable platform, the one the PR targets ([[approver/clause-gap] ci_green_on_sha says success while every build is still in_progress](../learnings/1785786196856-approver-clause-gap-ci-green-on-sha-reads-combined.md)). Perversely, `require_ci_green` **defaults to `true`** when absent and the mounted `v0-shadow-relaxed` policy sets it `false` — inert in shadow mode, live for anyone *tightening* the gate. **The stricter setting is the less trustworthy one; "we require CI green" ≠ "we verified CI."** Correct behavior: AND both surfaces; any non-terminal run ⇒ `unevaluable` (`ABSTAIN_INFRA:CLAUSE_UNEVALUABLE:ci_green_on_sha`); any adverse conclusion ⇒ `fail`; **no build signal on either surface ⇒ `unevaluable`, not `pass`** — today's code cannot tell "no builds ran" from "all passed."

**On fork PRs `require_ci_green: true` is defeated the same way.** Combined `/status` is `success` from CodeRabbit/board-sync/CLA alone while the real workflows (`ci`, `pre-commit`, `REUSE`) sit at `action_required` as **check-runs**, so the clause passes a false-green, never gates on builds, and doesn't stop the re-eval churn it was enabled to prevent (slang-rhi#841); real gating must inspect required check-runs. Calibration held on the same PR: a 5-revision ABSTAIN:OPEN_GAP streak on a large new cross-backend public API with zero execution coverage was VINDICATED by a maintainer's 12-item CHANGES_REQUESTED naming the same reds — aggregate OPEN_GAP is well-calibrated even when each red is individually unverifiable, and an outstanding maintainer CHANGES_REQUESTED under active revision is ABSTAIN:OPEN_GAP, not BLOCK ([slang-rhi#841: 5-revision ABSTAIN vindicated by CHANGES_REQUESTED; require_ci_green defeated by the combined-status trap](../learnings/1789104797392-approver-calibration-slang-rhi-841-a-5-revision-ab.md)).

**`commit_match` reads `reviews[].commit_id`, which GitHub retroactively rewrites to the head.** On slangpy#1068 the only human review (`ccummingsNV APPROVED`, `2026-07-29T10:05:29Z`) reported commit `266b2072e621` — a merge commit dated **2026-08-03**, five days later — so `commit_match` passed on a stale approval. Spot-checking can't save you: other PRs keep *historical* `commit_id`s, so the rewrite is silent and inconsistent. **Compare timestamps, not SHAs**: a review is stale iff `submitted_at` precedes the author date of the commit it claims (control: slangpy#1084's `21:13:48Z` approval on a `22:22:34Z` commit). Keep "is the approval record stale?" apart from "did the reviewed code change?" (on #1068 the tree was byte-identical, 1 file / +8). Route a timestamp-inverted review to the Devin-only tier like exit 10; a human approval, fresh or stale, is a **JOIN signal**, never grounds to lift an independent abstain ([[approver/clause-gap] commit_match reads reviews[].commit_id, which GitHub retroactively rewrites to the current head](../learnings/1785786613151-approver-clause-gap-commit-match-reads-reviews-com.md)).

**`tier_eligible` is a threshold on a total — re-evaluate on every head, never reason about the delta.** slang-rhi#803 R1/R2 failed at 12,754 LOC (9,376 one vendored header); R3 de-vendored it to a submodule, and "the bulk is gone, the cap must clear" is **wrong** — the new total is **3,391**, still 1.70× the 2,000 cap. Any diff change invalidates the prior evaluation in both directions; re-run per head (`gh pr view <n> --json additions,deletions,changedFiles`) and say when the cap now fires on hand-written code, not third-party bulk, or a human will dismiss it as "the vendored blob again." Two adjacent traps: a **retiring reinforcing reason** must stop being relayed even though the verdict holds (the "fork CI never ran" reason was stale by R3 after a maintainer approved the run; "feature compiles out ⇒ green builds are zero coverage" re-verified and HELD); and **`gh pr view --json reviews` strips `[bot]`**, so an `endswith("[bot]")` filter miscounts `coderabbitai` as human — driving `mode=live`/`live_late` and human-blocker detection. Use GraphQL `__typename: Bot` or REST `issues/<n>/timeline` ([[approver/clause-gap] re-test the size cap against the NEW TOTAL after a vendored-blob removal, not the delta](../learnings/1785777122746-approver-clause-gap-re-test-the-size-cap-against-t.md)).

**Count check-runs by predicate and reconcile against `total_count`.** Two reviewers miscounted the same 16-entry response on slangpy#1090: "16 real build check-runs" conflated all runs with builds, and the correction's "13 `build (...)` legs" was self-detectably wrong (13+1+1+2 = 17 ≠ 16). By `jq`: `total_count` 16, `startswith("build (")` → **12**, plus `pre-commit`, `add-to-project`, and `Claude Code Assistant` ×2 **skipped**. Sum components against `total_count` before transmitting any breakdown, and watch pagination (30/page; `total_count > (.check_runs|length)` means you're short). The 2 skipped runs are the **review bot**, not missing build coverage; the macOS legs are **`success`** yet executed no Metal GPU test (paravirtual `macos-latest`) — a green macOS run is affirmative evidence *for* the coverage gap, the "builds but does not execute" signature a skim reads backwards ([[approver/clause-gap] count check-runs by predicate, not by eyeball — and green ≠ exercised on accelerator-gated paths](../learnings/1785787407011-approver-clause-gap-count-check-runs-by-predicate-.md)).
