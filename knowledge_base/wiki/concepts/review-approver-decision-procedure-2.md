## TL;DR


## The review signal is endpoint-split — three surfaces, and the harvest reads one

The single largest measured defect class in the approver pipeline is not a wrong verdict; it is a **correct tally over the wrong surface**. GitHub scatters PR feedback across three disjoint endpoints and the harvest queries one:

1. **`pulls/N/reviews`** — review *wrappers*, and the only place a `state` (`APPROVED` / `CHANGES_REQUESTED` / `COMMENTED`) lives.
2. **`pulls/N/comments`** — inline findings. This is where the *substance* usually lives.
3. **`issues/N/comments`** — PR conversation text, where **maintainer directives** arrive with no review object and no state at all.

**Endpoint split #1 — an empty-body `COMMENTED` review is a wrapper, not silence.** GitHub models a review as a wrapper containing inline comments; submit an inline batch with no top-level prose and `/reviews` shows `body: ""` while `/comments` carries multi-paragraph text at the same timestamp. On slang#12080 two opposite wrong conclusions were each one step away — "empty body ⇒ nothing was said ⇒ retract the nudge" (the author had written four substantial replies) and "a maintainer spoke ⇒ re-decide" (the text was the author answering *bot* findings). The discriminator is **"is there text on any surface"**, then classify by **audience**: `in_reply_to_id` pointing at a bot comment is the cheap tell that a burst is informational. Two rules, one per direction: *empty body ≠ silence* (an empty `COMMENTED` wrapper is the expected shape for an inline-only batch — look one level down, never conclude "nothing happened"), and *text ≠ actionable* (author-answers-bot must not re-arm the procedure; reviewer-raises-objection must) ([[approver/clause-gap] an empty-body COMMENTED review is a wrapper, not silence — the substance lives on the inline-comments endpoint](../learnings/1785761437816-approver-clause-gap-an-empty-body-commented-review.md)). ⚠️ That learning's supporting claim *"the inline comments spanned 3 pages, the relevant replies were on page 3, page 1 was entirely July bot traffic"* is **FALSE and retracted** — the author's replies are spread across the early pages too (deterministic count: **250** inline comments over 3 pages of 100/100/**50**, 37 by the author). It was derived from a summarizing fetch, so its "paginate to page 3" advice was right by accident; the real defect was never pagination depth but using a lossy reader for an enumeration question. Correct guidance: author and reviewer comments can land on **any** page; never characterize a page's contents from a summarized read, enumerate with stdlib `urllib` and count in code until a short page ([[approver/clause-gap] CORRECTION — the empty-body-wrapper learning's page-1 claim is false; grep your own already-fetched material before reporting a negative](../learnings/1785761810820-approver-clause-gap-correction-to-empty-body-comme.md)).

**Endpoint split #2 — CodeRabbit findings live on `pulls/N/comments`, and the harvest tallies `reviews[].body`.** This is the dangerous one because it under-reads on a **successful exit-0 harvest**. A CodeRabbit review body is frequently status boilerplate only (`**Actionable comments posted: 11**` plus a collapsed run-config), containing **zero** severity markers, while the 11 findings — 2 of them 🟠 Major / Functional Correctness on slang-rhi#803 R1 — sit on the unqueried endpoint. Whether you see findings in the body is **CodeRabbit formatting luck, not harvester correctness**, so "body had no markers ⇒ no findings" is never a valid inference. Two companion traps from the same investigation: GitHub **rewrites** an inline comment's `commit_id` as the head advances (8 of 11 findings originally posted at R1 later reported R3), so bucket provenance by **`original_commit_id`**; and a **green CodeRabbit commit status does NOT imply a harvestable review object** at that commit. One hypothesis worth not re-chasing: this is *not* a bot-detection bug — the harvest scripts filter with an exact-match allowlist (`login in ("github-actions[bot]","coderabbitai[bot]")`) over REST, with no `endswith` and no GraphQL in that path (the suffix asymmetry is still a real trap for hand-written checks: REST `user.login` carries `[bot]` and `user.type=="Bot"`, GraphQL `author.login` is bare with `__typename=="Bot"`, and `gh pr view --json reviews` returns **`login` only** — no in-band way to detect a bot at all). The procedural failure that actually caused the row: exit-22 correctly reported `pending`, the review landed 3 minutes later, and the artifact was synthesized 7 minutes after that **without one final cheap re-probe** — *a timeout is a statement about a past instant, not about the present*, so re-probe at the LAST moment before committing the artifact ([[approver/infra-abstain] CodeRabbit findings live on pulls/N/comments, not reviews[].body — a successful exit-0 harvest can still score 0 findings](../learnings/1785778078080-approver-infra-abstain-coderabbit-findings-live-on.md)).

**Measured blast radius, and the right way to report it.** Audited across stored rows, the signature (`Actionable comments posted: N>0` with zero body markers) fires on **11 of 18** CodeRabbit harvests that found a review — 17 findings never in the machine-parsed input, every harvest exit 0, no error to notice. Partition the hits by whether the field was *load-bearing*: where a clause FAILED the decision short-circuited at Step 1 and the under-read is cosmetic for the verdict; where all clauses passed the review signal **was** the decision input (6 such rows, one of them a WOULD_APPROVE on slang-rhi#797 whose Devin had timed out — the row least able to absorb an under-read is exactly where one happened). The generalizable move: when you find a parsing bug, **sweep your own stored artifacts for the signature and partition by load-bearingness** ([[approver/infra-abstain] endpoint-split audit: 11 of 18 CodeRabbit harvests silently under-read — detection recipe + load-bearing partition](../learnings/1785779251344-approver-infra-abstain-endpoint-split-audit-11-of-.md)). At slangpy scale the rate is worse: of **12** CodeRabbit reviews claiming `N>0`, **11 carried zero body markers** (~92%), and yet only 2 of 40 recorded rows were wrong — because **the agent reading `pulls/N/comments` by hand during synthesis was the safety net, not the tooling**. Across the 7 rows whose revision had inline findings, 100% of those findings are cited by `file:line` in the review doc. The 2 rows that failed are exactly the two where the harvest concluded **no review existed at all** (exit 22-timeout, exit 20), so nothing prompted a manual read and the doc affirmatively asserted "no CodeRabbit signal" — *the under-read only becomes a wrong row when it coincides with a false absence*. A high manual-compensation rate is not reassurance; it is a latent defect waiting for the day the prompt to compensate is absent ([[approver/clause-gap] the CodeRabbit body-only tally under-reads ~92% of the time — manual reading, not tooling, was the safety net](../learnings/1785779185938-approver-clause-gap-the-coderabbit-body-only-tally.md)).

⚠️ **But an artifact-level defect is not a decision-level harm — confirm against the decision input of record before crying false-safe.** The same audit initially escalated slang-rhi#797 as a probable false-safe from `harvest.json` alone. Opening the actual decision input (`review/review-doc.md`) showed **both** CodeRabbit findings quoted verbatim with per-finding severity and an explicit call (*"🟠 Major … test-robustness nit, test-only … Not blocking"*), and an embedded result of `bugs:0, gaps:1, questions:1, verdict: APPROVE_WITH_NITS` — not 0/0/0; 9 of 9 auditable rows carry severity markers in the doc. Keep two questions strictly separate: **is the artifact wrong?** (yes — worth fixing) and **was the decision wrong?** (answerable *only* by opening `review-doc.md` and the recorded verdict fields). "The recorded signal field is untrustworthy on N rows" is true and useful; "N findings were missed" is a stronger claim needing per-row confirmation, and publishing it unverified is an over-claim in the *alarming* direction — the mirror of rounding up to approve, and just as much a calibration failure. Finding a bug creates momentum toward finding its victims; that momentum is precisely when to slow down ([[approver/critique-mustfix] an artifact-level defect is not a decision-level harm — open the decision input of record before crying false-safe](../learnings/1785780014953-approver-critique-mustfix-an-artifact-level-defect.md)).

**Endpoint split #3 — maintainer directives arrive on `issues/N/comments` with no review state, and they outrank everything with one.** On slang-rhi#803 a scan reported "7 reviews, all COMMENTED — no CHANGES_REQUESTED, no standing blocker" indefinitely, while skallweitNV (MEMBER) had posted *"we want to keep slang-rhi free from using git submodules. Can you fetch TinyBVH through FetchContent as we do for the other dependencies?"* — a concrete change request invalidating the PR's approach, carrying no review state because it is not a review object. `pulls/N/reviews` **structurally cannot see** it, so any "is there a standing blocker?" check built on review states has a permanent blind spot for the highest-authority feedback on the PR. Treat the standing-blocker question as a **union over all three endpoints, filtered by `author_association`** (`MEMBER`/`OWNER`/`COLLABORATOR` on a plain issue comment outranks a bot's `COMMENTED` review object — authority is `author_association`, never API shape), paginate all three, and record which endpoint each directive came from so a later scan doesn't "retire" it by re-checking only reviews. Two comments from one maintainer can differ in kind: a bandwidth note is a tripwire, "do X instead of Y" is a directive a later revision must satisfy ([[approver/clause-gap] maintainer directives live on issues/N/comments and carry no review state — union all three endpoints, authority from author_association](../learnings/1785780753487-approver-clause-gap-maintainer-directive-on-issues.md)).

**For a join, `issues/N/timeline` is the one endpoint that orders everything together.** Joining slang#11667 (`pr_merged`) against a row pinned at `ad924934`, `pulls/N/reviews` showed only bot reviews at the pinned commit plus one human `approved` at a later commit — which reads as "no human ever objected to my head, and the merge is APPROVED-equivalent." Both halves were wrong: the decisive signal was a plain issue comment rejecting the approach (a CHANGES_REQUESTED-equivalent invisible to `/reviews`), and the head had been force-pushed between that comment and the approval. `gh api repos/O/R/issues/N/timeline?per_page=100 --paginate` interleaves `committed`, `head_ref_force_pushed`, `reviewed`, `merged`, `closed` **in one timestamp ordering**, which is what proves whether an objection predated or postdated a head move — here establishing the maintainer objected AT the pinned head, making the row scorable as CHANGES_REQUESTED rather than a neutral "head moved, no signal." On a force-pushed fork branch `compare A...B` is misleading (`ahead_by=108` from a rebase); confirm content by **sha256 of the file blobs** via `contents/<path>?ref=<sha>`, never by ahead/behind. Never infer "a human considered X" from "a human approved" — compare timestamps ([[approver/clause-gap] score a join with issues/N/timeline — it interleaves force-pushes with reviews, and maintainer vetoes arrive as plain issue comments](../learnings/1785782777239-approver-clause-gap-score-a-join-with-issues-n-tim.md)).

### The general rule: a predicate written in one endpoint's vocabulary silently excludes the others

The harvester bug and the resume-trigger bug are the same defect: **the data model of the query quietly became the definition of the thing**, and both times the omission was invisible because the query succeeded. A hold/resume trigger recorded as *"a non-bot **review** with an actionable **state** lands"* is a `review`+`state` predicate — and the directive that was the entire reason for holding lives only on `issues/N/comments` with no review object and no state, so the check could never fire on its own stated blocker and would report "nothing actionable" indefinitely. When writing any gate, trigger, or blocker predicate, ask **which surfaces can carry this signal, and does my predicate's vocabulary reach all of them?** — and prefer semantic terms ("actionable feedback from a non-bot") over schema terms ("review with state X"), because schema terms inherit one endpoint's blind spots. The same section carries a companion trap: **a deletion is not a disposition.** An approvals queue reading `[]` with `get` returning "approval not found" for each prior id does not mean "all three were rejected" — rows delete on approve OR reject OR expiry and `get` returns the identical `not found` for all three, so absence identifies the *set*, not the member (same shape as `mergeable_state`, which names *that* something is unmet, never *which*). Before reading absence as evidence, enumerate every path that produces the same absence; if more than one does, mark the disposition **unknowable** rather than inferring it. And naming an unreachable authoritative field is a *complete* answer, not a hedge — "cannot read; field F is authoritative; the tier that can read it should map it" beats deriving an id by timestamp adjacency, because **a wrong identifier is worse than no identifier**: the next reader re-verifies *through* it and lands somewhere else ([[approver/clause-gap] a deletion is not a disposition, and a predicate in one endpoint's vocabulary silently excludes the others](../learnings/1785819644171-approver-clause-gap-a-deletion-is-not-a-dispositio.md)).

⚠️ **Widening that never-fires predicate produced an always-fires one — and the missing discriminator was the ADDRESSEE, not the endpoint.** The fix *"actionable non-bot feedback in ANY of the three endpoints"* is **already satisfied literally** by the directive being waited on, so the resume condition read as *already met*: it would wake a fresh decision immediately, forever, with nothing new to decide. The maintainer comment opens `@<contributor>` — it is a change request **to the PR author**, actionable for the PR and not an inbound to the approval decision. Endpoint told you where to look; state told you the API shape; neither told you **who is being asked to act**. Ordered test for "is this a decision-relevant inbound?": (1) non-bot author, via `author_association` — not a login-suffix heuristic, since `gh pr view --json reviews` strips `[bot]`; (2) addressed to the contributor, or to the reviewer/decision?; (3) does it change a load-bearing input (size total, a gate/ABI premise, CI state, a standing verdict)? All three, or it is not a resume trigger. **After any predicate fix, evaluate it against the KNOWN CURRENT STATE: if it fires now with nothing new, it is wrong** — one lookup, catches the whole class. The always-fires direction is the worse half: a never-fires predicate sits inert, an always-fires one burns a re-decision on every check and trains the reader to ignore the signal ([[approver/clause-gap] widening a never-fires predicate produced an always-fires one — the missing discriminator was the addressee](../learnings/1785821476819-approver-clause-gap-widening-a-never-fires-predica.md)).


## Clauses read proxies — `ci_green_on_sha`, `commit_match`, `tier_eligible`

Three of the six mechanical clauses evaluate a *proxy* for the thing they name, and in each case the failure direction is toward the answer that permits an approval.

**`ci_green_on_sha` reads the legacy combined status, which is blind to GitHub Actions.** `eval-clauses.py:187` derives CI state exclusively from `commits/{sha}/status`; `grep -cF check-runs eval-clauses.py` = **0** in both the slang and slangpy approvers. On slangpy#1090 that combined status returned `state:success` with `n:2` — and those two statuses were **`license/cla` and `CodeRabbit`**, no build at all — while `commits/{sha}/check-runs` carried every real `build (...)` matrix job ([[approver/clause-gap] ci_green_on_sha reads combined status only — blind to Actions check-runs, and defaults to require_ci_green=true](../learnings/1785787076247-approver-clause-gap-ci-green-on-sha-reads-combined.md)). At the moment of first evaluation the same commit had **6 of 12 builds `in_progress`**, including both macOS legs — the only Metal-capable platform and the one the PR targets — so a clause keyed on combined status would have reported green over a build that had not finished and could still fail ([[approver/clause-gap] ci_green_on_sha says success while every build is still in_progress](../learnings/1785786196856-approver-clause-gap-ci-green-on-sha-reads-combined.md)). The perverse part: `require_ci_green` **defaults to `true`** when the key is absent, and the mounted `v0-shadow-relaxed` policy sets it `false` — so the defect is inert in shadow mode and activates for anyone *tightening* the gate or running without a mounted policy. **The stricter setting is strictly less trustworthy than the lax one; do not read "we require CI green" as "we verified CI."** Correct behavior: AND both surfaces, `unevaluable` on any non-terminal run (⇒ `ABSTAIN_INFRA:CLAUSE_UNEVALUABLE:ci_green_on_sha`), `fail` on any adverse conclusion, and — critically — **absence of build signal on both surfaces must be `unevaluable`, not `pass`**, since the current code has no path distinguishing "no builds ran" from "all builds passed."

**`commit_match` reads `reviews[].commit_id`, which GitHub retroactively rewrites to the current head.** On slangpy#1068 the only human review was `ccummingsNV APPROVED submitted_at=2026-07-29T10:05:29Z`, while the decided head `266b2072e621` is a merge commit dated **2026-08-03T19:22:22Z** — five days later. `reviews[].commit_id` nonetheless reported `266b2072e621`, so `commit_match` passed *"review commit_id == pinned"* on a stale approval: a false-safe in the approve direction, reporting freshness it never verified. The obvious spot-check cannot save you — other PRs retained *historical* `commit_id`s, so inequality is not a reliable staleness signal either; the rewrite is silent and inconsistent. **Compare timestamps, not SHAs**: a review is stale iff its `submitted_at` precedes the author date of the commit it claims to review (independent control: slangpy#1084's `jkwak-work APPROVED` at `21:13:48Z` carries a commit dated `22:22:34Z`). Keep the two questions apart — "is the approval record stale?" is not "did the reviewed code change?" (on #1068 the net diff vs `main` was 1 file / +8 / −0, so the reviewed tree was byte-identical) — or the finding reads as more alarming than the evidence supports. A timestamp-inverted review should route to the Devin-only tier like harvest exit 10; and a human approval, fresh or stale, is a **JOIN signal**, never grounds to lift an independent abstain ([[approver/clause-gap] commit_match reads reviews[].commit_id, which GitHub retroactively rewrites to the current head](../learnings/1785786613151-approver-clause-gap-commit-match-reads-reviews-com.md)).

**`tier_eligible` is a threshold on a total, so re-evaluate the predicate on every head — never reason about the delta.** slang-rhi#803 R1/R2 failed at 12,754 LOC, of which 9,376 was one vendored third-party header; at R3 the author de-vendored it to a submodule gitlink, and the intuitive read — "the blocking bulk is gone, the cap must clear" — is **wrong**: the new total is **3,391 LOC**, still 1.70× over the 2,000 cap. Any change to the diff invalidates the prior evaluation in *both* directions, and the old evidence string ("12,754 lines > cap 2000") describes a revision that no longer exists. Re-run the evaluator per head, read the LOC figure it computes for *that* head (`gh pr view <n> --json additions,deletions,changedFiles` is the cheap check), and say out loud when the cap now fires on the PR's own hand-written code rather than third-party bulk — materially different and more legitimate, and a human will otherwise dismiss it as "just the vendored blob again." Two adjacent traps from the same round: **a retiring *reinforcing* reason doesn't move the verdict but must stop being relayed** — the R1/R2 rows cited "fork PR CI never ran (`action_required`)", stale by R3 once a maintainer approved the run (17 success / 2 in_progress), while the other reason (feature compiles out ⇒ green builds are zero coverage) was re-verified and HELD; and **`gh pr view --json reviews` strips the `[bot]` suffix**, rendering `coderabbitai[bot]` as plain `coderabbitai`, so an `endswith("[bot]")` filter false-negatives and a bot review gets miscounted as human — which directly drives `mode=live`/`live_late` and whether a human blocker exists. Use GraphQL `__typename: Bot` or REST `issues/<n>/timeline` (which carries `type: Bot` and the suffixed login) ([[approver/clause-gap] re-test the size cap against the NEW TOTAL after a vendored-blob removal, not the delta](../learnings/1785777122746-approver-clause-gap-re-test-the-size-cap-against-t.md)).

**Count check-runs by predicate, and reconcile the parts against `total_count`.** Two reviewers independently miscounted the same 16-entry response on slangpy#1090 while both arguing correctly about it: "16 real build check-runs" conflated all check-runs with build check-runs, and the correction's "13 `build (...)` legs" was self-detectably wrong (13+1+1+2 = **17** against a stated total of 16). Ground truth by `jq` predicate: `total_count` 16, `startswith("build (")` → **12**, remainder `pre-commit` + `add-to-project` + `Claude Code Assistant` ×2 **skipped**. Summing components against `total_count` catches an off-by-one for free — do it before transmitting any breakdown — and watch pagination (`check-runs` pages at 30; `total_count > (.check_runs|length)` means your count is short). The substantive detail both counts buried: the 2 `skipped` runs are the **review bot**, never a skipped build, and must not be cited as missing build coverage; while the macOS legs are **present and `success`** yet executed no Metal GPU test because the job runs on paravirtual `macos-latest`. So a green macOS check-run is affirmative evidence *for* the Metal coverage gap, not against it — the "builds but does not execute" signature that a reviewer skimming for a red or skipped entry reads backwards ([[approver/clause-gap] count check-runs by predicate, not by eyeball — and green ≠ exercised on accelerator-gated paths](../learnings/1785787407011-approver-clause-gap-count-check-runs-by-predicate-.md)).
