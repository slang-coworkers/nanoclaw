# PR-Approver: Decision Procedure, Clauses, Infra-Abstain & Critique-Gate

# PR-Approver: Decision Procedure, Clauses, Infra-Abstain & Critique-Gate

The `*-pr-approver` coworkers (slang / slangpy) turn a PR review into ONE auditable, scoreable decision drawn from a closed four-state enum — `WOULD_APPROVE | ABSTAIN_POLICY | ABSTAIN_INFRA | BLOCK` — recorded to the host `approval_decisions` ledger. The approver **never writes to GitHub** (shadow mode) and never rounds up to approve. This page covers the *deterministic procedure*: the Step-1 eligibility clauses and their precedence, when an abstain is POLICY vs INFRA, how a GraphQL-only auth outage does NOT block a REST-served decision, and the codex critique-gate recording mechanics that must be satisfied before `record_decision`. It also covers what the procedure *cannot* measure — clauses that read proxies, a review-signal harvest that queries one of three endpoints, a Devin tier that exits 0 on nothing — and the evidence discipline a recorded row demands, since almost every approver defect on record is a correct measurement over an unverified scope rather than a wrong verdict. The sibling page [[wiki/concepts/review-approver-challenger-calibration.md]] covers the challenger's verified-BLOCK catches and human-agreement calibration.

## TL;DR


## The procedure is a deterministic order, not a menu

The skill is a **procedure of record**, executed in a fixed order: Step 1 = deterministic eligibility clauses (`eval-clauses.py`), Step 2 = parse the harvested review-doc verdict, Step 3 = the adversarial challenger runs ONLY if Steps 1–2 pass. A `[approver/clause-gap]` incident made the ordering explicit: on slang#12136 a `tier_eligible` clause FAILed (3355 lines > 2000 cap) while the challenger had *already verified* a real red-CI build break, and the approver initially recorded BLOCK reasoning "BLOCK is strictly more conservative." The DECISION_REVIEW critique flagged it must-fix — SKILL.md says any clause FAIL ⇒ ABSTAIN_POLICY and the challenger runs only if Steps 1–2 pass, so a clause fail **short-circuits to `ABSTAIN_POLICY:CLAUSE_FAIL` before the challenger** ([[approver/clause-gap] A Step-1 clause FAIL short-circuits to ABSTAIN_POLICY even when the challenger has already verified a BLOCK-class defect — the skill ordering governs, don't invent a "BLOCK overrides clause-fail" exception](../learnings/1784271165403-approver-clause-gap-a-step-1-clause-fail-short-cir.md)). "More conservative" is not license to pick the verdict you think truthful — inventing a "BLOCK overrides clause-fail" exception would make the procedure non-deterministic and un-auditable. ABSTAIN is still safe (it routes to a human and never rounds up); the verified defect is preserved as human-facing CONTEXT in the challenger/next-action fields, not as the decision reason.

The same "read the whole procedure, not one step" discipline governs the BLOCK/ABSTAIN split for a doc's 🔴. Step 2's mechanical "any 🔴 ⇒ BLOCK" sets only the doc's PRIOR (BLOCK-shaped); Step 4 defines BLOCK with a load-bearing qualifier — "the review found a **VERIFIED** 🔴 Bug" — and Step 3 explicitly presupposes 🔴 docs reach the challenger ("investigation can only add caution, never upgrade a doc's 🔴 toward approval"). So a **refuted/false-positive bot-🔴 lands at ABSTAIN_POLICY (CHALLENGER_CONCERN, or an independent verified gap carries it), not BLOCK** — the bar is asymmetric (a refuted 🔴 may not be rounded UP to WOULD_APPROVE, but it is not auto-BLOCK either). Recording a false-BLOCK on a CI-green, textbook-correct change is exactly the miscalibration the accuracy join punishes; when the DECISION_REVIEW gate demands BLOCK on a refuted 🔴 (observed: 8 rounds on slang-rhi#800 R2), cite Step 4's "verified" + Step 3's prior-not-verdict/never-upgrade clauses together, not Step 2 alone, and keep a "Why ABSTAIN not BLOCK" reconciliation in investigation.md to pre-empt the loop [[approver/critique-mustfix] A refuted/false-positive bot-🔴 lands at ABSTAIN_POLICY, not BLOCK — Step 4's "verified" qualifier governs, not Step 2's mechanical map](../learnings/1784814885802-approver-critique-mustfix-a-refuted-false-positive.md).

**Clause precedence — a FAIL dominates a co-present UNEVALUABLE.** When a clause FAIL and a clause UNEVALUABLE co-occur, the FAIL (policy) wins by *dominance*, not by which "ran first." On slang#11847 (self-merged before resume, review artifacts GC'd, GraphQL 401), the missing review doc tempted an `ABSTAIN_INFRA:NO_REVIEW_SIGNAL` — but `no_protected_paths` FAILed (16/16 files under `.github/**`), which dominates: even a fully-healthy pipeline would land ABSTAIN_POLICY here because `.github/**` demands a human ([[approver/clause-gap] protected-path FAIL dominates a co-present missing-review-doc — decision is ABSTAIN_POLICY, not ABSTAIN_INFRA](../learnings/1784271261294-approver-clause-gap-protected-path-fail-dominates-.md)). Run `eval-clauses.py` even on a stood-down/merged PR (it works off git+REST, no review doc needed); its FAIL is the honest decision-of-record, and an orchestrator "moot/merged" framing is a claim to verify, not the mapping.


## The protected-path clause is terminal — a clean review never overrides it

`.github/**` (and repo-specific globs like `**/slang-tag-version.h`) are protected paths whose FAIL is a terminal ABSTAIN_POLICY that short-circuits before the challenger — *regardless* of how safe the diff looks or how clean the bot review is. On slang#11957 (CUDA prelude vec1 helpers) the primary production review was CLEAN (0 findings) but the PR also edited `.github/workflows/ci-slang-test.yml`; the decision was `ABSTAIN_POLICY:CLAUSE_FAIL:no_protected_paths`, and it was VINDICATED — an external COLLABORATOR (expipiplus1) later reviewed and approved the CI-config change, exactly the human review the policy exists to trigger ([[approver/human-agreement] clean-primary-review still abstains on protected .github path — human review it defers to is real](../learnings/1784286359675-approver-human-agreement-clean-primary-review-stil.md)). The same class held for single-file `.github/workflows/*.yml` one-liners: slang#12149's obviously-correct `sed` version-regex fix and slang#12140-adjacent release-plumbing all abstain on protected-path before any code reasoning ([[approver/calibration] macOS release.yml version-regex fix → ABSTAIN_POLICY on protected path (fix was correct)](../learnings/1784326170528-approver-calibration-macos-release-yml-version-reg.md)). Do the correctness read for the audit trail, but never let a clean fix or clean bot review tempt a WOULD_APPROVE.

The board-sync onboarding rollout is the canonical multi-repo recurrence of this class: PRs that onboard a repo to slang's shared `pr-board-sync.yml` reusable workflow touch ONLY `.github/workflows/*.yml` thin callers (secret wiring `SLANG_PR_BOT_TOKEN`, `permissions: {}`, fork-review bridge→apply privilege split). slang-rhi#804 (MEMBER, same-repo head, +193/−126) had a clean review — CodeRabbit head-current with 2 nits, Devin exit-0 — and 5 of 6 clauses passed, but the single `no_protected_paths` FAIL is dispositive: `gh pr view --json files` up front, and if every changed path matches `.github/**` the outcome is fixed as ABSTAIN_POLICY:CLAUSE_FAIL:no_protected_paths regardless of author trust, review cleanliness, or small size ([[approver/clause-gap] workflow-only PR with clean review is still protected-path ABSTAIN](../learnings/1785455194475-approver-clause-gap-workflow-only-pr-with-clean-re.md)). Two follow-on subtleties recur on the `synchronize` revisions of these PRs: (1) the CodeRabbit/zizmor `unpinned-uses` finding (the callers pin `@master`, a mutable branch, while forwarding a token) is typically NOT resolved by pinning to a 40-hex SHA — the idiomatic fix is a `.github/zizmor.yml` `ref-pin` **exception** declaring `@master` acceptable for the same-org first-party reusable workflow, so don't read the CodeRabbit nag disappearing as the ref being hardened; and that exception file *itself* lives under `.github/**`, so the clause still FAILs (now on N+1 files) and the outcome is unchanged ([[approver/clause-gap] board-sync unpinned-uses finding gets a zizmor ref-pin exception, not an SHA pin — and the fix stays under .github/**](../learnings/1785514070974-approver-clause-gap-board-sync-unpinned-uses-findi.md)); (2) on each push a prior head's human review is auto-DISMISSED — stamp it on the prior revision's ledger row via `record_human_verdict` before deciding the new head. Expect identical protected-path ABSTAINs on every companion (slangpy#1084, slangpy-samples#57) and every revision.


## Out-of-scope repo/content: class determination BEFORE the review-signal mapping

A content PR from a non-compiler repo (website/blog/docs, e.g. `shader-slang/shader-slang.github.io`) sails through all six mechanical clauses as PASS (they carry **no repo-class predicate**) and harvest returns exit 20 `{found:false}` (production `claude-pr-review.yml` doesn't run there) — which looks identical to the `ABSTAIN_INFRA:NO_REVIEW_SIGNAL` trigger. The correct call is a **class determination first**, prior to and overriding the review-signal mapping: out-of-scope content is a POLICY boundary → `ABSTAIN_POLICY` with `reason_code=OUT_OF_SCOPE:<class>`, NOT INFRA (the pipeline didn't fail; the PR is out of domain) and NEVER WOULD_APPROVE (no applicable code-review signal — don't round up even if a human already approved) ([[approver/clause-gap] Non-compiler repo (website/blog/docs) is ABSTAIN_POLICY:OUT_OF_SCOPE, not INFRA — clauses have no repo-class predicate](../learnings/1784270359274-approver-clause-gap-non-compiler-repo-website-blog.md)). This was confirmed by a genuine human join: a website PR (#207, course-slides link) decided `ABSTAIN_POLICY:OUT_OF_SCOPE:website-content` later got a real (non-self) human APPROVED + merge by a different actor — a *vindicated* withhold, distinct from a self-merge which is neither agreement nor disagreement ([[approver/human-agreement] website-content PR OUT_OF_SCOPE confirmed by genuine human approval+merge](../learnings/1784325987979-approver-human-agreement-website-content-pr-out-of.md)). The four-state enum is closed; `reason_code` carries the free-form detail.


## INFRA-abstain: a GraphQL 401 is not a GitHub outage — probe REST first

The lab container's `GH_TOKEN` is a OneCLI *routing* token, not a GitHub PAT. When the GraphQL surface is unconnected, `gh`'s GraphQL-backed commands (`gh pr view`, `gh api graphql`, `gh api rate_limit`) 401 with `app_not_connected` and `gh auth status` reports "token invalid" — but **plain REST `gh api repos/...` still works**, and `harvest-reviews.py` / `eval-clauses.py` use REST internally so they run fine ([[approver/infra] gh GraphQL 401 at OneCLI gateway but REST gh api works — probe REST before ABSTAIN_INFRA; critique-gate hook false-positives on read-only /pulls reads](../learnings/1784270369731-approver-infra-gh-graphql-401-at-onecli-gateway-bu.md)). Before recording ABSTAIN_INFRA on a `gh pr view` 401, probe a REST endpoint; fetch PR metadata, reviews, files, `/commits/{sha}/status`, `/check-runs`, `/contents/...?ref=<sha>` all via REST, and anonymous `git ls-remote` / `git fetch refs/pull/N/head` also serves head+diff for public repos with no auth ([[approver/infra-abstain] GraphQL 401 gateway outage ≠ no GitHub access — gh REST still serves the clauses; don't over-ABSTAIN_INFRA](../learnings/1784271281635-approver-infra-abstain-graphql-401-gateway-outage-.md)). Only ABSTAIN_INFRA when the *specific datum a clause needs* is served solely by the failing transport. (The underlying 07-16/07-17 outages were later confirmed RESOLVED — a migration cred regression, now fixed — so the split is a diagnostic *technique*, not a standing state; see [[wiki/concepts/agent-infra-github-auth-operations.md]].)

**When INFRA is genuinely unavoidable — and when it isn't.** Bot-authored PRs (dependabot) get harvest exit 20 *by design* (production review skips bot authors), leaving Devin as the SOLE review signal; if Devin also times out (exit 3, 30-min), there is nothing to decide from → `ABSTAIN_INFRA:NO_REVIEW_SIGNAL` is unavoidable and correct — never self-review the bump, never round up ([[approver/infra-abstain] dependabot bot-authored PR: harvest exit 20 + Devin timeout = guaranteed NO_REVIEW_SIGNAL](../learnings/1784302351197-approver-infra-abstain-dependabot-bot-authored-pr-.md)). But that verdict is correct **only for the run where Devin actually times out** — it must not become a reason to *pre-emptively skip* Devin on a re-sync (a timeout is a transient per-run flake, harvest-20 is the structural fact; see the challenger page). Two mechanisms convert a would-be INFRA abstain into a scoreable decision: (1) **patience on a running primary bot** — exit 22 means the bot is IN_PROGRESS, and the 6-min poll window is a floor not a ceiling; on slang#12147 the production review took ~34 min, and falling to Devin-only (which also timed out) would have produced a false INFRA abstain instead of the real PRIMARY-tier BLOCK ([[approver/infra-abstain] exit-22 patience pays: hold for the primary bot even at ~30min when Devin will time out anyway](../learnings/1784316416216-approver-infra-abstain-exit-22-patience-pays-hold-.md)); (2) **retry a timed-out Devin once** — a 30-min exit-3 is transient (browser/analysis hang), and a fresh relaunch often completes in ~1 min, flipping `NO_REVIEW_SIGNAL` (excluded from scoring) into a scoreable POLICY row — high-value when the not-approved outcome is already over-determined by a standing human CHANGES_REQUESTED ([[approver/infra-abstain] Devin retry after 30m timeout flips NO_REVIEW_SIGNAL to a scoreable POLICY row](../learnings/1784326417365-approver-infra-abstain-devin-retry-after-30m-timeo.md)). Gotchas: `devin-error.txt` from run 1 is not cleared by a successful retry (confirm from the retry's own log), and compound `pkill;rm;nohup` one-liners abort before the launch — run the retry standalone.


## A webhook is a notification, never evidence of state

The payload selects which PR to look at; it is not the evidence. This holds for every event class, and the failure is symmetric — a webhook can overstate a change (`synchronize` on an unmoved head) or overstate a terminal outcome (`pr_closed` on a PR that is live `open`).

**`pr_closed` with `merged:false` can be a seconds-long close/reopen bounce.** On slang#12080 a `pr_closed` inbound arrived against two recorded `WOULD_APPROVE / CLEAN` rows — textbook false-safe shape (closed-unmerged + standing approve ⇒ `human_verdict=CHANGES_REQUESTED`, the highest-severity calibration finding). The author had closed and reopened **4 seconds apart** (the classic CI re-trigger), and at the payload level a bounce is byte-indistinguishable from a terminal close: same event name, same `merged:false`, no `reopened_at`. Scoring off the payload would have fabricated a `CHANGES_REQUESTED` against clean rows and permanently poisoned calibration (atoms are append-only). So on **every** `pr_closed` / `pr_merged` / `pr_review` join: re-read live `state` + `closed_at` first (`state=open` / `closed_at=null` ⇒ **no join, stop**; `closed_at==null` on a `pr_closed` inbound is the cheap tell it was reopened), then read the `closed`/`reopened` pair on `issues/{n}/timeline` — which is often *not* page 1 (on #12080 it sat on page 2 of 3 behind 100 force-push/review events), so paginate to the end or the bounce is invisible. A close/reopen pair seconds apart **by the author** is a CI re-trigger; no human judged the code. Never round a `merged=false` webhook up to a false-safe — a fabricated false-safe is worse than a missed one, because it teaches the challenger to distrust a procedure that was correct ([[approver/clause-gap] a pr_closed webhook can be a seconds-long close/reopen bounce — verify live state, not the payload, before scoring any join](../learnings/1785741585307-approver-clause-gap-a-pr-closed-webhook-can-be-a-s.md)).

A `synchronize` / `pr_ready_for_review` webhook can fire on a comment-driven `updated_at` bump with the head byte-identical. On slang#12138 the emitter fired `synchronize` twice off a human issue comment while the head stayed at `9f5ce276` (no force-push, no new check-runs) ([synchronize-webhook-can-fire-on-comment-updated-at-bump](../learnings/1784269837644-synchronize-webhook-can-fire-on-comment-updated-at.md)). Before honoring a synchronize-triggered re-decision, verify the head ACTUALLY moved via ≥2 independent signals — `.head.sha` vs last decided commit, a `head_ref_force_pushed`/`committed` timeline event, fresh check-run `started_at` — because acting blindly records a DUPLICATE ledger row for an identical commit (violating one-decision-per-revision) and burns a full harvest+Devin+challenger cycle ([[approver/clause-gap] Verify the head actually moved before honoring a "synchronize" re-decision tasking — a comment can masquerade as a push](../learnings/1784269782291-approver-clause-gap-verify-the-head-actually-moved.md)). If the head is unchanged, don't re-run; read whatever *did* change (the comment) and re-evaluate on its merits — a substantive human comment can re-open a chain even when no code changed.

**A rebase-onto-newer-master synchronize: classify by the PR's OWN merge-base…head diff.** When a branch is rebased/has-master-merged, `compare/<prior-head>...<new-head>` includes ALL intervening base commits (on slang#12034 R5: 62 commits, hundreds of files, `.github/**` + `external/` churn) — which would wrongly trip `no_protected_paths` and blow the size caps. The PR's true footprint is `compare/<merge-base>...<head>` where merge-base = `compare(base,head).merge_base_commit.sha`; an `ahead_by:1` single squashed commit confirms rebase/squash, not new content ([[approver/clause-gap] a synchronize that rebased onto newer master: classify by the PR's OWN merge-base…head diff, never the compare-vs-prior-head](../learnings/1784289530976-approver-clause-gap-a-synchronize-that-rebased-ont.md)). `eval-clauses.py` computes changed paths for the pinned commit correctly — but the human/agent classification step must reduce the synchronize to merge-base…head first, never eyeball the raw compare-vs-prior-head. A stale local `main` fabricates the same illusion from the other direction: on slang-rhi#807 a `main...HEAD` diff showed **8 phantom `.github/**` files** — enough to trip the protected-path clause — purely because the local branch was 3 weeks behind, so always diff against the PR's *real* base SHA ([[approver/clause-gap] verify a workaround PR's premise against the PINNED dependency version, never master](../learnings/1785764986434-approver-clause-gap-verify-a-workaround-pr-s-premi.md)).

**A stale dispatch on an already-joined PR is a no-op-plus-report, never a re-decision — resolve live and grep your own store BEFORE staging.** A `[PR-APPROVE]` dispatch for slang#12142 (`synchronize`) was *delivered* 2026-08-04 but timestamped Jul 17 — ~18 days stale — on a PR that had been MERGED since 2026-07-29 and that I had **already decided** on 07-19 at the exact same head `2a61c227a2ca`, with the human verdict already stamped (kaizhangNV APPROVED, `commit_id == head`). Walking the workflow literally (stage → harvest → Devin → synthesize → decide) would have produced a *second* decision on an already-joined row, and because `record_decision` is **idempotent per `(repo, pr, commit_sha)` — a re-run on the same commit REPLACES the row** — this is actively destructive, not merely wasted work: it would overwrite a terminal, human-joined calibration datapoint (the join is the product; the decision alone is worthless) with a verdict contaminated by post-hoc information that did not exist at decision time (here the fork CI gate released 4 days *after* the abstain, so a fresh decision would "see" green CI and plausibly flip a correct conservative abstain into a fake agreement). The destructive operation looks identical in the tool surface to the constructive one, so the guard has to live upstream, in the decision to stage at all. Two API calls before staging anything: `gh pr view <pr> --json state,isDraft,headRefOid,mergedAt` for live state, plus `grep -rn "<pr>"` your own memory dir and `ls -d work/<pr>-*` for prior rows/workspaces. Then branch — MERGED/CLOSED with a row at that same head ⇒ **no-op, report the existing decision + its join**; a row at the same head with the PR still open ⇒ no new decision (head hasn't moved); head has MOVED ⇒ a genuinely new revision, decide `live_late` with a full re-gate; no row ⇒ normal path. Cheap tell: dispatch timestamp vs `date -u` — any gap of more than a few hours means re-resolve everything. This is the same family as scoring a join off a stale SHA: never let a relayed fact stand in for a live read of the thing it describes ([a stale webhook dispatch on an already-joined PR is a no-op; `record_decision` idempotency will clobber the join](../learnings/1785841360069-approver-procedure-a-stale-webhook-dispatch-on-an-.md)).

**A standing "let it ride if codegen-inert" decision expires when the author pivots design — check the subject-of-record still EXISTS, not just that it is unchanged.** slang#12080 carried a standing rule (re-run the gated procedure only when codegen changes; on an inert synchronize let the recorded row ride) that worked for ~6 heads of nit-polish. ~18 heads later the naive inert check said "everything changed" (rebase drift touched all 11 files) — alarming and uninformative — while the real finding was worse: **the approved construct no longer existed.** Both rows had approved one specific lowering (`builder.emitGetAddress(builder.getPtrType(arg->getDataType()), arg)`); the author had pivoted to a third design (retype the parameter via `getBorrowInParamType` + a new `AddressSpace::CudaKernelParam` enumerator, an explicit `IRLoad`, a new `lowerImmutableBufferLoadForCUDA` pass) — a lineage whose *first* design had been approved 4× and then reported to MISCOMPILE. A diff-shaped question assumes both sides still describe the same construct; a pivot breaks that premise, so make the inert check **existence-first**: grep the new head for the specific call/predicate the row approved (absent ⇒ the row is stale, retire it and re-gate), grep for new load-bearing vocabulary (a new `AddressSpace`, `SLANG_PASS`, IR op, `setFullType` call — new vocabulary means new design, not polish), neutralize rebase contamination by diffing file content at both SHAs via `raw.githubusercontent.com` rather than `gh compare`, and cap the standing rule by head count. Write the row's subject-of-record as a **greppable anchor** so the existence check is mechanical, re-derive `mode` (reviews accumulated during the ride ⇒ `live` became `live_late`), and rewrite the memory-index line too — a stale "standing / re-gate only on genuine emit change" index entry is itself load-bearing and invites the next session to ride a row whose subject is gone ([[approver/clause-gap] a standing "let it ride if codegen-inert" decision expires on a design pivot — assert the subject-of-record still exists](../learnings/1785741613665-approver-clause-gap-a-standing-let-it-ride-if-code.md)).


## Review-resume: don't blindly finish a torn-down run

When a `/slang-pr-review` or `-approve` session is torn down mid-run and resumed days later, check three invalidators before re-running: (1) **PR merge/close state** via REST (`{state,merged,merged_at}`) — a merged PR makes a live review moot and any approval historical-only; re-running the full ~$60 live pipeline is a fresh spend the parent should authorize, not an autonomous default; (2) **token validity** — after a multi-day gap `GH_TOKEN` often rotates invalid (GraphQL 401 while REST works), so the standard `pr`-mode scripts hard-fail at their head-SHA preflight — fall back to `patch`-mode off a REST-fetched diff; (3) **artifact survival** — reviewer run-dirs under `.claude/skills/*/transcripts/` can be GC'd, and a log with hundreds of assistant messages but no `final-review.md` was never synthesized ([Review-resume sweep: check merge-state, token validity, artifact survival before re-running](../learnings/1784270233557-review-resume-sweep-check-merge-state-token-validi.md)). Report the changed facts + a concrete viable path (e.g. patch-mode historical review) to the parent for a go/no-go.
