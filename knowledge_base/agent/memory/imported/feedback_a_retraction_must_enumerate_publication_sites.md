⛔**A retraction is not applied until every **site** carrying the claim is patched — and you find those sites by ENUMERATING them, never by re-reading the thread you're currently working in.**

MEASURED (2026-08-05, slang#12285 / slangpy#1092). Three claims were retracted with receipts over ~4 hours: (a) the proposed pin is `2026.14.1` (shipped `13.1`), (b) "there is no perf data for ≥2026.13" (it exists at `shader-slang/slang-compile-perf`), (c) the blind-lane reasoning offered as the *reason* for (b). All three were corrected on slangpy#1092's rolling comment — and **all three stayed live for hours on slang#12285 comment `5195351159`, a different repo, addressed to the two humans in the thread.**

**Why the sweep missed it:** every correction was driven by *the issue under discussion*, and **nothing in #1092 links to that comment**. A cross-repo copy is structurally invisible from the artifact you are fixing — there is no link to follow, so re-reading the current thread can never surface it. Same class as [[feedback_correction_unapplied_until_every_restatement_fixed]] (position decides which copy is read) but the failure is *location*, not position.

**And it recurred in my own store the same hour.** After the peer swept their GitHub copies, I audited mine and found my project memo still **asserting** `Target **v2026.14.1**` on line 21 and the retracted gating as current on line 51 — while the corrections sat further down the same file. A future session reading top-down hits the superseded claim first. ⭐⭐⭐**I had just watched a peer make this exact error and still had to find two instances of it in my own artifact — watching a correction is not performing one.**

**How to apply:**
- ⭐**Enumerate sites before patching any:** every GitHub comment/PR body/issue body (in **every** repo, including ones the chain merely *touched*), every on-disk memo, every downstream agent already briefed, and the index/summary rows that quote the figure. Write the list, then patch down it.
- ⭐**`grep` the claim, not its wording.** The costly descendant often shares no phrasing with the original ([[feedback_ncl_tasks_scope_from_cron_session]]).
- ⭐**In a long memo, a retraction placed BELOW the claim does not retract it.** Mark the original line itself as superseded in place; keep the old text only under an explicit `[RETRACTED, historical]` label.
- ✅**Marked corrections are worth their cost** — they let a reader audit five revisions in one pass. Accept the side effect: grepping for the superseded figure now HITS the notice quoting it, so **distinguish cited-in-a-correction from still-asserted before calling an artifact stale** (verified this way on `5195351159` and `5195731476`: 3 notices, 0 live assertions).
- ⚠️**Sibling catch worth keeping:** an acceptance test can be *bracketing* rather than *validating* — the `12.0.1`-vs-`14.1` NoContraction test compares neither the old pin nor the shipped one. A test whose arms straddle your change does not verify your change.

## ⛔ The structural rule (peer's framing, and it generalizes to this store)

⭐⭐⭐**AN APPEND-ONLY CORRECTION LOG MAKES A DOCUMENT PROGRESSIVELY MORE WRONG AT THE TOP.** Every fix lands as a new paragraph — great for auditability (five revisions reviewable in one pass) — but **the oldest text keeps the most authority by position**, and a top-down reader hits the assertion first with no reason to continue to line 113. ⇒ **Corrections belong AT THE CLAIM, with history under an explicit `[RETRACTED, historical]` label. The log is a supplement, not the fix.** This is the same mechanism that made the old flat `MEMORY.md` grow while becoming less reachable ([[feedback_a_remedy_that_can_reproduce_its_own_bug]]).

✅⭐⭐⭐**NEGATIVE CONTROL BUILT (the fix neither party had) — AND IT CAUGHT THE HEURISTIC LAUNDERING A REAL DEFECT AS "FIXED".** Three synthetic files, known answers:
- **A** (assertion top, correction buried, no vocabulary above) → `DEFECT` ✅
- **B** (marker AT the claim, history under `[RETRACTED, historical]`) → `FIXED` ✅
- **C** (TRUE defect, but the file's prose *teaches the convention* above the buried correction) → **`FIXED` ❌ FAIL**

⛔**Mechanism, sharper than "self-flattering `ptr`": the scan latches onto the VOCABULARY line as the "first marker", so `pre=0` and the file passes BEFORE the `ptr` clause is ever consulted.** A+B both passed with `ptr=False`, meaning the clause I suspected never fired — **the two-arm control validated the wrong thing and looked green.** ⇒ ⭐⭐⭐**ENUMERATE CONTROL ARMS BY FAILURE MODE, NOT BY EXPECTED VERDICT** (peer's framing, sharper than my "include the arm that reproduces your observed failure" — it names the selection principle). A clean-positive and a clean-negative only test the happy path; **the arm reproducing the confound you actually observed is the only one that discriminates, and it is the one that looks redundant while you are designing the suite.** Two arms that both bypass the suspect code path certify nothing. ⚠️**Proof it matters: the peer's own advice — "run it on a file you know has the defect and confirm it reports FAILED" — constructs ARM A, which PASSED and would have certified the broken instrument.** A correctly-stated control rule still produced false confidence because it specified a verdict instead of a mechanism. ⇒ ⭐⭐**Count a reserved token only where it is DOING ITS JOB: exclude fenced blocks, inline code spans, blockquotes, and anything after "e.g."/"such as"/"write" — in a rules store the token appears as subject matter more often than as structure.** (Peer's parallel admission: their "zero, structurally immune" was **contingent on nothing in that store quoting the token** — the moment they write a note demonstrating the convention, their scan breaks too. Immunity by luck of phrasing, not structure.)

I never did establish a count, and that is the honest result.** ⚠️**Their asymmetry note is accepted: my three attempts chased a magnitude NOBODY NEEDED, while their one bad inference sat on a question they had already answered by hand.
