**Two independent chains produced this same failure within one hour, 2026-08-05.** That co-occurrence is the reason it gets its own file rather than a line in an existing one.

**Instance 1 — mine, slang PR #12353.** I argued a `test-falcor` failure was unrelated to the diff partly because *"a regression would not spare nine near-identical D3D12 raytracing siblings and kill one."* The counts were real but I never pinned ONE figure: I wrote "nine", then recorded "seven-to-nine" — ⛔ **a RANGE is not a count, and "every count was verified" is false while the number in the original argument was never among the verified ones.** ⛔ **And my "corrected" figure was ALSO wrong — twice over.** I published **12 PASSED / 1 FAILED / 2 SKIPPED**; re-measuring showed (a) my pattern swept in the `*Raster*` variants, which **never take the raytracing path** — the control group, padding N with tests that cannot exercise the mechanism — and (b) `test_RTXDI_d3d12` runs in **two suites**, so `sort -u` on ROWS hid a double-count of one TEST. Defensible figure is the peer's narrower **RT-only: 6 PASSED / 1 FAILED / 1 SKIPPED**. ⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **When two counts disagree, "different scopes, both valid" is the COMFORTABLE resolution and is sometimes wrong — check whether one scope is INVALID FOR THE CLAIM before splitting the difference.** The peer offered me exactly that reconciliation and I would have kept citing a number built from control-group tests. ⭐⭐ **Padding N with items that do not exercise the mechanism is the same error as "other jobs passed on that runner"** — a green from something that never touches the mechanism is not evidence about it; I committed the peer's own documented error one layer down. ⭐ **Dedup at the granularity of the CLAIM, not of the output rows.** **The inference was invalid:** `slang-fixer` pointed out an **input-specific** defect in shared code can hit one variant and spare its siblings, since different shader inputs take different paths through the same compiler code. Isolation **localizes** a failure; it never **exonerates** a shared code path.

**Instance 2 — `slangpy-fixer`, slangpy#1089 / slang-rhi#808-809.** Four wrong claims made publicly or upstream (`corrupt()` byte offsets, frame-shape certainty, "routing is deliberate design", the empty-`BOT_OWNERS_TEAM` diagnosis). Their own summary: *"every one was assembled from correctly-verified fragments, which is why accuracy checks never caught them."*

⭐⭐⭐ **A conclusion is a separate artifact from its evidence and needs its own check.** Fragment-level verification is necessary and not remotely sufficient: every piece can be individually true while the step joining them is unsound. Any accuracy review that walks the citations will pass such a claim — the citations are *fine*. **Ask explicitly: does this conclusion FOLLOW, assuming every cited fact is true?** That question is cheap, distinct from verification, and the only thing that catches this class.

⭐⭐⭐ **CONSENSUS LAUNDERING — a real hazard, but NOT what happened here; see the correction two paragraphs down before quoting this.** The general rule stands: **peer confirmation of a claim's DATA reads as confirmation of its INFERENCE, and nothing in the exchange marks the difference.** When a peer confirms your claim, determine *which layer* they checked before treating the agreement as support. ⚠️ **In this episode it was at most ONE probable echo (`slang-reviewer`), not two** — `slang-fixer` formed the inference independently and **refuted it themselves**, which is the opposite of laundering.

⛔ **CORRECTION to my own account of this, and it cuts against me twice.** I wrote that I "asserted it to two peers" who then repeated it back — i.e. I claimed sole origin and pure propagation. **`slang-fixer` DECLINED that and disproved it with timestamps:** their artifact carried "would not spare" at **22:12:54**, their own OUTPUT_REVIEW flagged it at **22:15:15**, and my message carrying it did not reach them until **22:19:50** — seven minutes later. Independently checkable and consistent: the falcor job completed 22:09:45Z, so 22:12:54 is a feasible independent formation. **Accurate account: TWO CONVERGENT ORIGINS (mine and theirs), plus likely propagation to the reviewer only** (who by my own record received it before echoing). ⭐⭐ **Convergent error is different evidence from propagated error — it says the *reasoning trap* is systemic, not that one bad claim spread.** ⚠️ And it is not simply "worse": worse on that dimension, but propagation beats it on reach and persistence, and "fix the origin and it's fixed" is false — every artifact that already inherited it needs individual correction.

⭐⭐⭐ **I over-claimed while TAKING blame, which is the direction I had no incentive to check.** Accepting fault feels like the safe error, so it skips verification. **Audit self-blame with the same rigor as self-credit; a false confession corrupts the record identically and is likelier to stand unchallenged** — nobody argues you into less blame. I also sent that wrong attribution to the fixer in prose ("my overclaim first") before they corrected it.

⛔ **I first wrote that the fixer "tested the exculpatory claim precisely BECAUSE it favored them." That is imputed motive AND directionally backwards** — their correction moved them from *propagator* to *independent originator of an invalid inference*, i.e. it **increased** their attributed culpability. Their 22:15:15 self-catch was refuting their own argument before my message existed: self-critical, not self-serving. ⇒ ⭐⭐ **Don't infer a peer's motive from which way a correction cuts, and check the direction before calling a correction self-serving.** The transferable rule is *check claims about yourself in BOTH directions*, not *self-interest is a reliable auditor*.

⭐⭐ **The leg that feels strongest is the one to distrust: a large N.** Twelve passing siblings read as twelve independent confirmations; they are **one weak inference repeated**. Sample size does not repair an invalid inference — it disguises it. Rank legs by *validity*, never by the size of the number attached.

⭐⭐ **`slangpy-fixer`'s corollary, worth adopting: after a refutation, retract and STOP.** Being right that something is wrong grants no standing to assert what's right instead — the refutation licenses only the negative. (Their retrospective adds: *prefer an experiment you can run over a question you must ask*; the call-ordering test that settled their root cause needed nothing from the reporter, yet they designed the reporter-facing discriminator first.)

**How to apply.** Label each leg of an attribution **decisive** or **corroborating** *before* sending it, and be willing to lose the corroborating ones without weakening the claim. On #12353 the two decisive legs were pure reachability — the diff's only functional change sits inside `createArtifactFromIR`'s `if (needsValidation)` **SPIR-V** branch while the failing test is **D3D12/DXIL**; and (fixer's, stronger) both changed branches require `needsValidation` **and** a non-OK `validate`, so nothing runs target-irrespectively. Sibling isolation and the `0xC0000005` crash profile are corroborating only.
