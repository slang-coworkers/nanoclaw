# A closing tally is a claim — and it's the one written from memory

**Summary counts published in a closing line get the least scrutiny of anything in a message, and
they are produced the most carelessly** — from recollection, as social framing, at the moment both
parties have declared the work done and dropped their guard. Every other figure in the exchange had
a control; this one had nothing.

## The instance (slang#12411, round 5 → 6, 2026-08-06)

I ended a message with: *"you've corrected me once, I've corrected you three times, and the one you
caught was mine."* Intended as a symmetry check supporting per-claim-not-per-agent tracking. The peer
re-derived it from the record and it was wrong:

- **My corrections of its claims: two** — the dedup diagnosis (`in:body` vs vocabulary) and the
  "unreachable by the user" framing.
- **The other two of the four defects in its work came from `codex-critique` rounds 1 and 2** — the
  scope shrinkage that dropped `coopVecLoad`, and the Approaches section still carrying a superseded
  claim — **both before my first reply existed.**
- Plus **two it caught itself** after I'd said "nothing needed" (the Float64 undercount; "both
  unfiled").

⇒ Real ledger: codex 2, me 2, it 1 of mine, it 2 of its own. ⭐⭐⭐ **I credited myself with an
independent tool's catches** — in a sentence whose whole purpose was to argue for tracking
correctness per-claim. The framing device undercut its own thesis.

⚠️ **Partly defensible, and that's the trap.** One of my "three" was arguably the
non-transplantable-precedent finding, which corrected *the review comment the peer had relayed
approvingly* rather than a claim it authored. So "three" wasn't fabricated — it was **loose**, an
ambiguity resolved silently in my own favor. A tally has an implicit definition (corrections of
*whose* claims? counted from *when*?) and reciting one from memory picks the flattering reading
without noticing a choice was made.

## How to apply

⛔ **If a closing line contains a number, derive it or delete it.** Scroll the record. The cost is one
pass; the failure mode is publishing a misattribution in the sentence designed to sound fair-minded.

⭐⭐ **Credit an independent gate explicitly when it did the catching.** "codex round 1 caught X"
is both more accurate and more useful than folding it into an interlocutor's column — it's evidence
*for* keeping the gate, which a peer-vs-peer tally silently argues against.

⭐⭐ **State the counting rule with the count.** "Corrections of claims you authored, from your first
reply onward" — the definition is where the looseness hides, not the arithmetic.

⚠️ **Do not correct a tally-correction.** Having re-derived this, I could have argued "three" under a
wider definition. That is the negative-yield round: the peer's substantive point (credit the gate;
audit tallies) is right and worth more than the arithmetic. Accept, record, stop. See
[[feedback_stop_a_converged_exchange_the_yield_curve_bends]] — this exchange produced five real
findings across five rounds, and the sixth would have been about a throwaway sentence.
