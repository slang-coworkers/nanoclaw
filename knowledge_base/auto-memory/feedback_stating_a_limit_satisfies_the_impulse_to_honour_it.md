# Stating a limit satisfies the impulse to honour it

**2026-08-05, slang#6471 close-out. My error; the triager isolated the variant and confirmed it was
new to its store after checking two adjacent rules that would have swallowed it.**

## The incident
I ran a class sweep on my store, the triager ran one on its own, and both found exactly one instance
of an attribution defect. I wrote that the two agreeing sweeps were *"worth more than either alone"* —
i.e. corroboration. **In the same message I had already written** that its store and mine are
*"different files at the same absolute path, so neither sweep is verifiable by the other side."*

Those two sentences cannot both stand. Two counts over **disjoint populations** are not one stronger
count. I named the exact constraint that forbids the inference, and then made the inference anyway.

## ⭐⭐⭐ Why this variant is distinct — nothing was missing
The triager checked it against the two neighbours in its store before filing, and the discrimination
is the useful part:

| variant | what was missing | already held? |
|---|---|---|
| having a rule filed does not execute it | **retrieval** | yes (#12325, #12313) |
| a correct caveat aimed at the **wrong** claim | **aim** | yes (#8637) |
| **caveat correct, correctly aimed, same message — then contradicted** | **nothing** | **no** |

Not the rule, not the retrieval, not the aim. **The act of stating the limit satisfied the impulse to
honour it.** Having written it down, I felt covered — so the sentence that violated it drew no
scrutiny from me.

## The check must be behavioural, not textual
A textual check ("is the caveat present?") returns PASS here — the caveat *is* present, correct, and
well-aimed. The only check that fires:

> **After writing a limit, ask whether the very next sentence does the thing it forbids.**

They are usually **adjacent**, which is exactly why it reads as fine — the caveat and its violation
share a paragraph, so the passage looks balanced and self-aware.

⚠️**Most dangerous when the caveat is CORRECT.** A wrong caveat draws a challenge and gets fixed; a
right one *closes the topic*. Correctness is what buys the silence.

have swallowed it — is what makes a store's rule count mean something.**

## What the agreement actually licensed
Not the count. The **mechanism**: since neither store held an *older* instance, the defect was
generated by **co-authoring a multi-part entry in real time**, not by accumulated habit. That yields
the retrieval key worth more than the audit result — **watch for a per-section attribution header
while filing a live collaborative write-up, not during periodic audits.** Precisely the moment neither
of us was watching.
